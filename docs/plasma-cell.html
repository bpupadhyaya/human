Plasma Cell

Plasma cells are terminally differentiated B lymphocytes specialised for massive immunoglobulin secretion. They lose surface BCR and MHC-II expression, downregulate B cell identity factors (PAX5), and massively expand rough ER to accommodate antibody synthesis. Short-lived plasmablasts (days) arise early in the response; long-lived plasma cells (LLPCs) in bone marrow survival niches secrete high-affinity IgG for years–decades — the cellular basis of durable humoral memory after vaccination.

Overview

Plasma cells are the terminal effectors of humoral adaptive immunity — the cells that do the work the B cell lineage is built toward: synthesizing and secreting vast quantities of high-affinity antibody. They are produced when B cells are activated by antigen, receive T cell help (Tfh-derived CD40L + IL-21), and undergo the germinal center reaction.

Two fundamentally different plasma cell populations exist, with profoundly different lifespans:

Plasmablasts (short-lived)

Arise within 3–5 days of B cell activation (extrafollicular response). Lifespan days–weeks. Produce mainly IgM initially, then IgG after class switching. The "rapid-response" factory — peak titers fast but low-affinity, short-lived serum antibody.

Long-lived plasma cells (LLPCs)

Arise from GC-selected, affinity-matured B cells. Home to bone marrow survival niches (CXCL12+VCAM1+ stromal cells provide IL-6, APRIL, BAFF, SDF-1). Produce high-affinity class-switched IgG for decades without further antigen stimulation. The cellular basis of lasting vaccine protection.

The durability of antibody-mediated immunity — whether from natural infection or vaccination — depends on the number and quality of LLPCs established during the primary immune response. A vaccine that fails to generate a robust GC reaction produces poor LLPCs, and serum antibody titers wane rapidly.

Competition for bone marrow niches: Bone marrow LLPC niches are limited. Booster vaccination can establish new LLPCs with higher-affinity antibody (from secondary GC reactions using memory B cells) that outcompete older LLPCs occupying niches — providing a mechanistic basis for dose 3 improvements in antibody quality and breadth seen with mRNA COVID-19 vaccines.

Pathology

Multiple Myeloma

Clonal expansion of malignant plasma cells in bone marrow. Secretes monoclonal immunoglobulin (M-protein, "myeloma protein") detectable by serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) and immunofixation. Bone destruction (osteoclast activation via RANKL from myeloma cells), renal impairment (light chain cast nephropathy, AL amyloidosis), anemia (bone marrow suppression), hypercalcemia. Diagnosis: CRAB criteria + bone marrow >10% clonal plasma cells. Treatment: proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib, carfilzomib), IMiDs (thalidomide, lenalidomide), anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody (daratumumab), BCMA CAR-T (idecabtagene, ciltacabtagene). Not curable by standard therapy; autologous SCT extends remission.

MGUS and Smoldering Myeloma

Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS): M-protein <30 g/L, <10% bone marrow plasma cells, no CRAB. Risk of progression ~1%/year. Smoldering MM: M-protein ≥30 g/L or ≥10% bone marrow plasma cells, no end-organ damage. Risk of progression to active MM ~10%/year initially. Requires surveillance but not treatment unless progresses.

AL Amyloidosis (Primary Amyloidosis)

Clonal plasma cells secrete amyloidogenic light chains (most often lambda) that misfold and deposit as β-sheet fibrils (AL amyloid) in heart (restrictive cardiomyopathy — the major cause of death), kidneys (nephrotic syndrome), liver, peripheral nerves. Diagnosed by fat pad biopsy or bone marrow biopsy + mass spectrometry to confirm AL type. Treated with clone-directed therapy (bortezomib + dexamethasone ± daratumumab) and autologous SCT in eligible patients.

Rituximab and LLPC persistence

Rituximab (anti-CD20) depletes B cells and short-lived plasmablasts but does NOT deplete long-lived plasma cells, which are CD20-negative. This explains why serum IgG titers and vaccine-induced antibody levels can persist even months after rituximab therapy. However, the B cell compartment cannot replenish LLPCs during rituximab treatment, so secondary responses to new antigens are severely impaired — critical for vaccination timing in patients starting rituximab-based therapy (vaccinate ≥4 weeks before rituximab if possible).

Antibodies produced by plasma cells exert protection through multiple molecular mechanisms, each dependent on antibody isotype and Fc interactions:

IgG subclass differences: IgG1 (dominant vaccine response, strong FcγRIII, good complement); IgG2 (polysaccharide antigens, weaker Fc effector); IgG3 (strongest complement activation, short half-life ~7 days); IgG4 (weak complement, no ADCC, half-life ~21 days; chronic antigen exposure → IgG4 class switch = "blocking" antibody; pemphigus target antigen).
