Cytokine Storm

A life-threatening dysregulated hyperinflammatory response characterized by pathological amplification of cytokine production, loss of immunological brakes, macrophage-lymphocyte positive feedback, and progressive multi-organ damage. The final common pathway of severe viral infections, CAR-T therapy, and several autoimmune triggers.

Scale: L04 Cellular/Molecular System: Immune System (Innate + Adaptive) Type: Hyperinflammatory Syndrome Status: Draft · reviewed 2026-06-05

Definition and Clinical Spectrum

The term cytokine storm (also: cytokine release syndrome, CRS; hypercytokinemia) describes a clinical state in which the immune system loses physiological control over cytokine production, generating self-amplifying inflammatory circuits that cause systemic toxicity disproportionate to — and increasingly independent of — the original trigger. The key distinction from a normal immune response is not merely elevated cytokine levels but the failure of regulatory mechanisms that ordinarily terminate inflammation once the threat is cleared.

Cytokine storm exists within a clinical spectrum of overlapping syndromes that share core immunopathological mechanisms:

Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)
Rare hyperinflammatory syndrome defined by the HLH-2004 diagnostic criteria: fever, splenomegaly, cytopenias in ≥2 lineages, hypertriglyceridemia and/or hypofibrinogenemia, hemophagocytosis on bone marrow/liver/lymph node biopsy, low/absent NK cell activity, ferritin >500 μg/L, and elevated sCD25 (>2400 U/mL). Primary (familial/genetic) HLH involves mutations in perforin pathway genes (PRF1, UNC13D, STX11, STXBP2); secondary HLH is triggered by infections (EBV most common), malignancy, or autoimmune conditions.
Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS)
The form of secondary HLH occurring in the context of rheumatological disease, most commonly systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis (sJIA) and adult-onset Still’s disease. Classified separately from HLH due to distinct triggers, subtly different cytokine profiles (IL-18 predominance), and response to IL-1β blockade. MAS-2016 classification criteria for sJIA-associated MAS: ferritin >684 μg/L plus ≥2 of: platelet count ≤181×109/L; AST >48 U/L; triglycerides >156 mg/dL; fibrinogen ≤360 mg/dL.
CRS (Cytokine Release Syndrome)
Clinical entity defined in the context of immunotherapy (CAR-T cells, bispecific antibodies, checkpoint inhibitors) and characterized by fever, hypotension, and end-organ toxicity temporally related to immune effector cell activation. Graded by ASTCT 2019 consensus criteria: grade 1 (fever alone) through grade 4 (life-threatening vasopressors + intubation required). Neurotoxicity (ICANS) often accompanies severe CRS in CAR-T settings.

Triggers

  • Viral infections: EBV (most common trigger of secondary HLH), SARS-CoV-2 (severe COVID-19), influenza A (H1N1/H5N1), CMV, HHV-8, dengue, HIV
  • CAR-T cell therapy: CD19-directed CAR-T (axicabtagene ciloleucel, tisagenlecleucel); BCMA-directed (idecabtagene vicleucel); high tumor burden and rapid T cell expansion are primary risk factors
  • Bispecific antibodies: Blinatumomab (CD3×CD19), teclistamab (CD3×BCMA) — CRS risk correlates with target antigen density
  • Sepsis: Dysregulated systemic macrophage activation in response to disseminated PAMPs and DAMPs; both NLRP3-dependent (IL-1β) and NF-κB-dependent (TNF-α, IL-6) axes
  • Autoimmune disease: sJIA, adult-onset Still’s, SLE flares, Kawasaki disease, MIS-C (multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children, post-SARS-CoV-2)
  • Malignancy-associated: Hematological malignancies (T/NK cell lymphomas, aggressive B cell lymphomas) via direct macrophage activation; iatrogenic (checkpoint inhibitors)

Feedback Amplification and Loss of Immunological Brakes

Macrophage–Lymphocyte Amplification Circuit

The central positive feedback loop driving cytokine storm was first described in the context of HLH/MAS and operates as follows:

  1. An inciting trigger (viral antigen, autoreactive T cell, CAR-T target engagement) leads to macrophage activation and antigen presentation.
  2. Activated macrophages secrete IL-12 and IL-18 → NK cells and Th1/CTL produce IFN-γ.
  3. IFN-γ further activates macrophages → hemophagocytosis, increased IL-12, IL-18, IL-6, and TNF-α production, and upregulation of MHC II → enhanced antigen presentation.
  4. Enhanced antigen presentation drives further T cell and NK cell activation → more IFN-γ.
  5. Simultaneously, IL-6 from macrophages suppresses Treg function, removing a key brake on T cell activation.

In health, this circuit is terminated by: (a) NK cell and CTL-mediated killing of the activating antigen source (clearing infection); (b) Treg-mediated IL-2 competition and IL-10/TGF-β suppression; (c) feedback inhibition of IL-12 production by IL-10; (d) SOCS proteins dampening JAK-STAT signaling. In cytokine storm, one or more of these brakes fail.

Ferritin as Acute-Phase Marker

Ferritin is both an iron-storage protein and an acute-phase reactant secreted by activated macrophages. In cytokine storm:

  • IL-1β and IL-6 induce ferritin synthesis in hepatocytes and macrophages
  • IFN-γ directly stimulates ferritin secretion from activated macrophages
  • Serum ferritin >500 μg/L raises suspicion for HLH; >10,000 μg/L is diagnostic in adults (95% specificity for HLH in one meta-analysis); children may exceed 500,000 μg/L in severe primary HLH
  • Ferritin itself may be immunosuppressive (via ferritin receptors on lymphocytes), contributing to NK/CTL dysfunction

Treg Exhaustion and IL-10 Paradox

Regulatory T cells (CD4+FOXP3+ Tregs) normally curtail effector T cell responses through IL-10, TGF-β, and competitive IL-2 consumption. In cytokine storm, three mechanisms converge to impair Treg function:

  • IL-6 trans-signaling: STAT3 activation downstream of sIL-6R/gp130 in T cells converts FOXP3+ Tregs into pro-inflammatory Th17-like cells that secrete IL-17 instead of IL-10
  • TNF-α-mediated Treg killing: TNFR2 on Tregs mediates TNF-dependent apoptosis at high TNF concentrations
  • Lymphopenia: CD4+ Treg depletion (common in severe COVID-19, HLH) eliminates the suppressor cell pool

IL-10 is paradoxically elevated despite Treg exhaustion because macrophages and monocytes produce IL-10 as an attempted counter-regulatory response. However, this macrophage-derived IL-10 may contribute to cytotoxic lymphocyte dysfunction (NK and CTL impairment), worsening the failure to clear the inciting trigger and perpetuating the storm.

Impaired Cytotoxic Lymphocyte Function

A critical insight from primary HLH genetics is that defective cytotoxic granule exocytosis is the root cause: mutations in perforin (PRF1), Munc13-4 (UNC13D), Syntaxin-11 (STX11), or STXBP2 prevent NK cells and CTLs from killing virally infected cells or APC. Unable to eliminate the activating stimulus, the immune system enters a futile amplification state. In secondary HLH and COVID-19 cytokine storm, functional exhaustion (PD-1/LAG-3/TIM-3 upregulation) on NK and CD8+ T cells produces a phenotypically similar state of impaired cytotoxicity without genetic defect.

The perforin paradox: Cytokine storm arises not from excessive killing but from insufficient killing. NK cells and CTLs that cannot eliminate their targets continue to secrete cytokines in a frustrated activation state, maintaining the amplification circuit indefinitely. This counterintuitive principle explains why HLH patients have both the highest inflammatory markers and the most impaired lymphocyte cytotoxicity simultaneously.

Organ Damage Patterns

ARDS and Pulmonary Failure

The lung is the primary target in COVID-19 cytokine storm and influenza-associated CRS. Alveolar capillary leak — driven by IL-6 (VEGF upregulation, tight junction disruption), TNF-α (TNFR1-mediated endothelial apoptosis), and IL-1β (prostaglandin-mediated vascular permeability) — floods alveoli with protein-rich exudate. Neutrophil recruitment via CXCL8/IL-8 and CXCL10 drives NETosis (neutrophil extracellular trap formation), adding amplified oxidative injury and coagulation pathway activation. The histological result is diffuse alveolar damage (DAD), and the clinical result is ARDS (Berlin criteria: bilateral infiltrates + PaO2/FiO2 <300 mmHg).

Cytokine-Mediated Coagulopathy

Cytokine storm drives a hypercoagulable state through multiple convergent mechanisms:

  • TNF-α and IL-6 upregulate tissue factor (TF) expression on macrophages and endothelium → extrinsic coagulation cascade activation
  • IL-6 induces hepatic fibrinogen, factor VIII, and vWF synthesis (acute phase response)
  • IFN-γ and TNF-α suppress thrombomodulin expression on endothelium → reduced protein C activation → impaired fibrinolysis
  • Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) provide a scaffold for platelet aggregation and fibrin deposition → microvascular thrombosis
  • In severe HLH, paradoxical hypofibrinogenemia occurs (fibrinogen consumed by extensive hemophagocytosis and plasminogen activator release from activated macrophages) → DIC-like bleeding

Cardiac Injury

Cytokine storm causes cardiac injury through direct and indirect mechanisms: TNF-α and IL-1β are directly cardiodepressant (impair myocyte calcium handling and reduce contractility via iNOS-derived NO); IL-6 drives STAT3 activation in cardiomyocytes promoting pathological hypertrophy; coronary microvascular thrombosis and spasm cause myocardial ischemia. Clinically: troponin elevation in 8–12% of severe COVID-19 and 15–20% of HLH; stress cardiomyopathy (Takotsubo pattern); fulminant myocarditis in MIS-C and CAR-T-related CRS.

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

Cytokine-driven AKI results from tubular injury (IL-6- and TNF-α-mediated tubular apoptosis), renal vasoconstriction (thromboxane A2, endothelin-1 upregulation), and glomerular thrombotic microangiopathy. In COVID-19 severe AKI, complement deposition (C3b, MAC) on glomerular capillaries contributes. Kidney injury in HLH may be severe enough to require dialysis; ferritin nephrotoxicity (tubular iron deposition) is an additional mechanism in extreme hyperferritinemia.

CNS Involvement

Cytokine-mediated encephalopathy is increasingly recognized as a complication of severe cytokine storm. IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β disrupt the blood-brain barrier (BBB) via tight junction protein downregulation and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) upregulation → cerebral edema and inflammatory cell infiltration. In COVID-19, microglial activation, perivascular inflammation, and fibrin microclots have been documented at autopsy. CAR-T-associated neurotoxicity (ICANS) is mediated by CSF cytokine elevations (IL-6, IL-8, IFN-γ), seizures, and in severe cases cerebral edema.

Diagnostic Criteria

HScore for HLH Probability

The HScore (Fardet et al. 2014) is a validated clinical tool assigning points across 9 variables to calculate the probability of reactive HLH. A score ≥169 has ~93% sensitivity and ~86% specificity for HLH. Variables include:

  • Temperature (≥38.4°C: 33 pts; ≥39.4°C: 49 pts)
  • Organomegaly: hepatomegaly or splenomegaly (23 pts each, 38 pts for both)
  • Number of cytopenias (1 lineage: 0; 2: 24 pts; 3: 34 pts)
  • Serum ferritin (<2000: 0; 2000–6000: 35 pts; >6000: 50 pts)
  • Serum triglycerides (<132 mg/dL: 0; 132–354: 44 pts; >354: 64 pts)
  • Fibrinogen (>250 mg/dL: 0; ≤250: 30 pts)
  • AST (<30 U/L: 0; ≥30: 19 pts)
  • Hemophagocytosis on bone marrow biopsy (no: 0; yes: 35 pts)
  • Known underlying immunosuppression (no: 0; yes: 18 pts)

MAS-2016 Classification Criteria (for sJIA-associated MAS)

A patient with sJIA is classified as having MAS if: serum ferritin >684 μg/L AND any 2 or more of the following:

  • Platelet count ≤181×109/L
  • AST >48 U/L
  • Triglycerides >156 mg/dL
  • Fibrinogen ≤360 mg/dL

ASTCT CRS Grading (CAR-T/bispecific contexts)

Grade 1: Fever ≥38°C only. Grade 2: Fever + hypotension responsive to fluids OR O2 requirement <low-flow nasal cannula. Grade 3: Fever + hypotension requiring vasopressors OR high-flow O2. Grade 4: Life-threatening (pressors + intubation). Treatment escalation (tocilizumab, corticosteroids) is typically initiated at Grade 2 in CAR-T settings.

Emapalumab
(Gamifant)		 Human anti-IFN-γ monoclonal antibody; neutralizes IFN-γ, preventing macrophage activation and hemophagocytosis; targets the central amplification loop of HLH NI-0501 trial (primary HLH): 63% response rate (reduction of HLH activity) in patients refractory/intolerant to conventional HLH-2004 therapy; FDA approved 2018 for primary HLH in pediatric patients FDA-approved for primary HLH (pediatric); investigational in refractory secondary HLH and MAS; serum IFN-γ levels used to monitor treatment response; distinct from IL-6R/IL-1 blockade in that it directly interrupts the macrophage-lymphocyte circuit rather than downstream effector cytokines

Treatment sequencing principle: Corticosteroids are universal first-line therapy in cytokine storm. Add IL-6R blockade (tocilizumab) for COVID-19 CRS and CAR-T CRS. Prefer IL-1 blockade (anakinra) for MAS/sJIA. Add JAK inhibition (baricitinib, ruxolitinib) for refractory cases. Reserve etoposide for confirmed HLH or life-threatening refractory disease. Use emapalumab for primary HLH or severe refractory secondary HLH. Avoid over-immunosuppression in infection-driven storms where residual pathogen control is needed.

Connections

  • driven byNLRP3 Inflammasome — caspase-1 activation produces the key storm amplifiers IL-1β and IL-18; NLRP3 activation in macrophages by viral viroporins (SARS-CoV-2 E protein), PAMPs, or DAMPs is an upstream initiator of cytokine storm in viral infections; pyroptosis propagates DAMPs that activate neighboring cells
  • contextCOVID-19 Disease — the hyperinflammatory phase of severe COVID-19 (~5% of infections) is the most studied contemporary example of cytokine storm; dexamethasone, tocilizumab, and baricitinib were validated as treatments in COVID-19 trials and subsequently adopted more broadly
  • cytokineIL-6 — the primary amplifier cytokine in COVID-19 and CAR-T cytokine storm; IL-6 trans-signaling via sIL-6R enables tissue-wide STAT3 activation; elevated serum IL-6 correlates with storm severity and is the biomarker guiding tocilizumab use
  • mediatesAntibody — autoantibodies generated during cytokine storm (anti-IFN-α, anti-adrenergic, anti-nuclear) contribute to Long COVID and MAS pathophysiology; IVIG addresses Fc-receptor-mediated amplification; therapeutic mAbs (tocilizumab, emapalumab) are among the most precise storm-targeting agents
  • antigenIgG — IgG Fc receptor (FcγR) signaling on macrophages can amplify storm; IVIG FcγRIIB upregulation (inhibitory signaling) is a key anti-inflammatory mechanism in Kawasaki and MIS-C; anti-spike IgG in COVID-19 can activate FcγR on macrophages in the lung
  • antigenMHC Class II — IFN-γ-driven MHC II upregulation on macrophages and non-professional APCs enhances antigen presentation to CD4+ T cells, amplifying the macrophage-lymphocyte circuit; disrupting this loop (via JAK inhibition blocking IFN-γ signaling) is a key mechanism of baricitinib and ruxolitinib
  • triggerCAR-T Therapy (car-t.html) — CD19/BCMA-directed CAR-T cells trigger CRS through rapid release of IL-6 and IFN-γ upon tumor antigen engagement; CRS incidence and severity correlate with tumor burden and T cell expansion kinetics; tocilizumab is approved as first-line treatment
