Troponin Complex

The heterotrimeric Ca²⁺ switch on the cardiac thin filament — TnC binds Ca²⁺ and transduces the signal; TnI inhibits actomyosin in the absence of Ca²⁺; TnT anchors the trimer to tropomyosin. Together they translate the cytosolic Ca²⁺ transient into the on/off state of the thin filament, gating every heartbeat. Cardiac-specific TnI and TnT isoforms are the diagnostic biomarkers of acute myocardial infarction.

Genes: TNNC1, TNNI3, TNNT2 UniProt: P63316 (TnC), P19429 (TnI), P45379 (TnT) Aliases: cardiac troponin, Tn complex, TnC-TnI-TnT Scale: 03-Molecular

Overview

The troponin complex is a three-subunit Ca²⁺ switch on the thin filament of striated muscle. In the absence of Ca²⁺, it holds tropomyosin over the myosin-binding sites on actin — the muscle is off. When cytosolic Ca²⁺ rises and binds TnC, the entire complex undergoes a conformational shift that exposes those binding sites — the muscle is on. Every heartbeat is one cycle of this switch, repeated synchronously in every sarcomere of every cardiomyocyte.

The three subunits are named for their roles: TnC (Ca²⁺-binding), TnI (inhibitory), and TnT (tropomyosin-binding). The cardiac isoforms (TNNC1, TNNI3, TNNT2) are expressed exclusively or predominantly in cardiac muscle — this tissue specificity is the basis for the serum cardiac troponin assay for acute MI diagnosis.

The troponin assay: Cardiac TnI (cTnI) and cardiac TnT (cTnT) are released into the bloodstream when cardiomyocytes die. High-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays detect rises within 1–3 hours of ischemic onset, with specificity close to 99% for myocardial injury. This is the cornerstone of the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (ESC/ACC/AHA/WHF 2018).

Structure

The Trimer

Each troponin complex is one TnC + one TnI + one TnT, sitting on the thin filament approximately every seven actin monomers, alongside one tropomyosin dimer. The thin filament presents a regularly repeating regulatory unit along its length.

TnC — the Calcium Sensor

TnC is an EF-hand Ca²⁺-binding protein with four EF-hand domains. In the cardiac isoform, site I is non-functional (evolutionary amino acid substitution eliminates Ca²⁺ coordination). Only sites II, III, and IV bind Ca²⁺:

  • Site II — the low-affinity regulatory site in the N-terminal lobe (Kd ~2–5 µM). This is the calcium switch that gates contraction. Ca²⁺ binding here in systole (peak ~1 µM) is the trigger for all downstream conformational events.
  • Sites III and IV — high-affinity structural sites in the C-terminal lobe, occupied by Mg²⁺ at rest; exchange Ca²⁺ slowly; structural role anchoring TnC to TnI.

TnI — the Inhibitory Subunit

Cardiac TnI has a unique N-terminal extension (~30 aa) absent from skeletal isoforms, containing Ser23 and Ser24 — the PKA phosphorylation sites linking β-adrenergic signaling to myofilament Ca²⁺ sensitivity. The C-terminal half contains the inhibitory peptide (engages actin in off state) and the switch peptide (captured by TnC hydrophobic patch in on state).

Mechanism

Ca²⁺-Triggered Thin-Filament Activation

── Troponin complex — Ca²⁺ switch mechanism ────────────── DIASTOLE ([Ca²⁺]i ~100 nM — OFF state): TnC site II: EMPTY TnI inhibitory peptide: bound to ACTIN → holds it still TnI switch peptide: NOT captured by TnC Tropomyosin: BLOCKED position (over myosin-binding sites on actin) → No cross-bridge formation. No force. SYSTOLE ([Ca²⁺]i rises to ~1 µM — ON state): ↓ TnC site II binds Ca²⁺ → Hydrophobic patch opens in TnC N-terminal lobe → Hydrophobic patch captures TnI switch peptide → TnI inhibitory peptide releases from actin → Tropomyosin slides azimuthally: BLOCKED → CLOSED → OPEN ↓ Myosin heads can now bind actin (cross-bridge formation) ATP hydrolysis → power stroke → thin filament pulled inward → Sarcomere shortening → cell contraction RELAXATION (Ca²⁺ falls — return to OFF state): SERCA2a (~70%) + NCX1 (~28%) remove Ca²⁺ TnC site II releases Ca²⁺ TnC hydrophobic patch closes → releases TnI switch peptide TnI inhibitory peptide re-engages actin Tropomyosin returns to blocked position Cross-bridges detach → relaxation β-AR MODULATION — PKA phosphorylates TnI Ser23/24: Phospho-TnI has LOWER affinity for Ca²⁺ at TnC site II → TnI-actin interaction releases Ca²⁺ sooner (faster TnI release) → FASTER relaxation (lusitropy) → Matched with PLN Ser16-P (faster SERCA2a) for high-HR performance

The Hill coefficient for thin-filament Ca²⁺ sensitivity is ~3–5 in cardiac muscle — highly cooperative. This means a twofold change in Ca²⁺ transient amplitude can produce a several-fold change in developed force, giving the heart enormous dynamic range over Ca²⁺ concentrations.

Pathology

DiseaseMechanism
Acute myocardial infarctionCardiomyocyte necrosis releases cTnI and cTnT into circulation. High-sensitivity assays detect rises within 1–3 hours. Basis of the Fourth Universal Definition of MI. Serial troponin measurement (0h/3h/6h protocol) is standard of care in ACS workup.
Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)Mutations in TNNT2 and TNNI3 (and sarcomeric genes: MYH7, MYBPC3) destabilize the complex, often increasing myofilament Ca²⁺ sensitivity → hypercontractility → hypertrophy, myocyte disarray, interstitial fibrosis, VT risk. Most TNNT2 mutations produce severe hypertrophy with high SCD risk despite modest hypertrophy.
Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM)Some TNNT2 and TNNI3 mutations cause decreased force generation and cooperative activation → chamber dilation, reduced EF. Distinct from HCM mutations that increase Ca²⁺ sensitivity.
Restrictive cardiomyopathy (RCM)Rare TNNI3 mutations produce restrictive physiology with near-normal chamber size; difficult to distinguish from constrictive pericarditis clinically.
Cardiac troponin in non-ischemic causesElevated troponin is not specific to MI — also seen in myocarditis, PE, sepsis, hypertensive emergency, cardiac contusion, chronic kidney disease. The clinical context determines diagnosis (Type 1 vs. Type 2 MI vs. acute non-ischemic myocardial injury per 4th Universal Definition).
