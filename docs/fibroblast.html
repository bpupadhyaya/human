Fibroblast

Mesenchymal spindle-shaped cells found throughout connective tissue (dermis, organ stroma, tendon, joint capsules, periosteum). Core function: continuous synthesis, secretion, and remodelling of the extracellular matrix (collagen I/III/V, fibronectin EDA, elastin, proteoglycans). Activated by TGF-β1 into αSMA+ myofibroblasts — the central effectors of wound repair (beneficial, acute) and pathological fibrosis (harmful, chronic) in liver, lung, heart, kidney, and tumour stroma.

Overview

The fibroblast is the principal architect of connective tissue — a mesenchymal spindle-shaped cell found throughout the body (dermis, organ stroma, tendons, joint capsules, submucosa, periosteum) whose core function is the continuous synthesis, secretion, and remodelling of the extracellular matrix (ECM). In healthy tissue, fibroblasts are largely quiescent (fibrocytes); injury or inflammation activates them, and in pathological states their persistent activation drives organ fibrosis in virtually every tissue.

Fibroblasts lack a single definitive positive marker and are identified by context and a combination: vimentin (intermediate filament), absence of lineage markers (CD45 for haematopoietic, CD31/PECAM for endothelial, EpCAM for epithelial), together with PDGFRα and FSP1/S100A4 in activated states.

The myofibroblast — the contractile, highly secretory fibroblast variant induced by TGF-β1 — is the central effector of both physiological wound repair and pathological fibrosis. Myofibroblasts are defined by expression of α-smooth muscle actin (αSMA, ACTA2) assembled into stress fibres connected to the ECM via fibronexus (supermature focal adhesions). The balance between myofibroblast activation and resolution (apoptosis) is one of the most clinically important problems in medicine.

Tissue-Specific Variants

Hepatic Stellate Cells (HSC / Ito Cells)

Perisinusoidal mesenchymal cells in the Space of Disse. Quiescent: store vitamin A (retinyl esters, 80% total body retinol); GFAP+, desmin+. Activated by TGF-β1, PDGF, ET-1, LPS: lose retinol stores, upregulate αSMA, PDGFRβ → collagen I/III secretion → pericellular/perisinusoidal fibrosis → cirrhosis. Primary fibrogenic cell in NAFLD/NASH, viral hepatitis, alcoholic liver disease.

Cardiac Fibroblasts

~60–70% of cardiac cells by number (far outnumber cardiomyocytes; contribute ~15% cardiac mass). Maintain myocardial ECM; communicate with cardiomyocytes via Cx43 gap junctions and paracrine IL-6/TGF-β/FGF. Post-MI: activated to myofibroblasts → collagen scar (beneficial acute structural integrity) → ongoing activation → interstitial fibrosis → diastolic dysfunction, HFpEF, arrhythmia.

Cancer-Associated Fibroblasts (CAFs)

Heterogeneous population in tumour stroma (resident, BM-derived, EMT). Functions: ECM remodelling (dense collagen capsule — physical barrier to drug penetration and T cell infiltration), growth factor secretion (FGF, HGF, EGF → tumour proliferation), immunosuppression (TGF-β, CXCL12, IL-10 → T cell exclusion, MDSC recruitment), angiogenic support (VEGF, bFGF). High CAF abundance: poor prognosis in pancreatic, breast, colorectal cancers.

Lung Fibroblasts (IPF)

In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, pathological TGF-β signalling from injured type II alveolar epithelial cells drives fibroblast foci — dense αSMA+ myofibroblast aggregates with collagen I deposition → honeycombing pattern (UIP). Nintedanib (PDGFR/FGFR/VEGFR inhibitor) and pirfenidone (TGF-β/PDGF inhibitor) slow progression; median survival from diagnosis ~3 years without treatment.

Pathology

Liver Cirrhosis

HSC activation (NASH, alcohol, HBV, HCV) → myofibroblast transformation → perisinusoidal collagen I/III deposition → progressive portal-bridging fibrosis (Metavir F1→F4) → cirrhosis. Portal hypertension → varices, ascites, SBP, HRS, HE. Anti-fibrotic targets in clinical trials: TGF-βR1 inhibitors, PDGFR antagonists, ROCK inhibitors, LOXL2 inhibitors (simtuzumab — failed), FXR agonists (obeticholic acid — ↓fibrosis score). NASH regression possible with sustained weight loss and GLP-1 agonists.

Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)

Pathological TGF-β-myofibroblast activation in the lung alveolar interstitium. UIP pattern on HRCT (honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis, basilar/subpleural predominance). Fibroblast foci are pathological hallmark. Prognosis poor (~3 yr median survival untreated). Nintedanib (anti-PDGFR/FGFR/VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor) and pirfenidone (anti-TGF-β/antifibrotic) slow FVC decline. Lung transplantation for advanced disease.

Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)

Autoimmune TGF-β-driven widespread fibroblast activation. Anti-Scl-70 (topoisomerase I) or anti-centromere antibodies. Diffuse cutaneous SSc: progressive skin thickening + ILD + PAH + renal crisis (scleroderma renal crisis, anti-RNA polymerase III Ab; manage with ACEi). Limited SSc (CREST): calcinosis, Raynaud, oesophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia. Treatment: mycophenolate/cyclophosphamide (ILD), nintedanib (ILD progression), bosentan (PAH), ACEi (renal crisis).

Keloid and Hypertrophic Scar

Failed apoptosis of dermal myofibroblasts following wound healing. Hypertrophic scar: excessive collagen I accumulation within wound margins; regresses partially. Keloid: myofibroblast expansion beyond wound margins; genetically predisposed (African descent); does not regress. Mechanisms: persistent TGF-β1, reduced myofibroblast apoptosis (Bcl-2 ↑), ↑connective tissue growth factor (CTGF/CCN2). Treatment: intralesional steroids (triamcinolone), pressure garments, 5-FU, cryotherapy, surgery + adjuvant radiation.

Cancer Stroma — Desmoplasia

CAF-mediated dense collagen I/HA matrix in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) physically excludes T cells and blocks drug penetration. CXCL12 from CAFs binds CXCR4 on T cells, redirecting them around the tumour (not into it) — a primary immune exclusion mechanism reversed by CXCR4 inhibitors (plerixafor + anti-PD-L1 in trials). Dual strategies: stroma depletion (PEGPH20 — hyaluronidase) vs stroma normalisation (anti-TGF-β + checkpoint blockade).
