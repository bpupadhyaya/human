Stomach

J-shaped muscular organ positioned between oesophagus and duodenum in the left upper quadrant. Resting capacity 1–1.5 L, distensible to ~4 L. Three essential roles: mechanical trituration of food into fine particles (<2–4 mm), chemical digestion via HCl (parietal cell H⁺/K⁺-ATPase) and pepsin (chief cell pepsinogen → activated at pH <5), and regulated delivery of chyme to duodenum. Six distinct secretory cell types in gastric glands. Parietal cells produce both HCl (pH 0.8 at canalicular surface) and intrinsic factor (IF — essential for B12 absorption). Three-phase acid regulation: cephalic (~30%), gastric (~60%), intestinal (~10%). Gastric mucosal barrier: mucus-bicarbonate gel + PGE2/PGI2 (COX-1 constitutive). H. pylori infection of gastric mucosa is the leading cause of peptic ulcer disease and Correa cascade to gastric adenocarcinoma.

Overview

The stomach is a J-shaped muscular organ located in the left upper quadrant and epigastric region of the abdomen, positioned between the oesophagus and duodenum. It is the most dilatable segment of the gastrointestinal tract — resting capacity 1–1.5 L, distensible to approximately 4 L after a large meal. The stomach performs three essential roles: mechanical trituration of food into fine particles, chemical digestion via hydrochloric acid and pepsin, and regulated delivery of chyme into the duodenum. Its gastric glands contain at least six distinct secretory cell types, each tightly regulated by neural, hormonal, and paracrine signals.

The stomach is central to both nutrition (protein digestion initiation, intrinsic factor secretion for vitamin B12) and disease: Helicobacter pylori infection of the gastric mucosa is the leading cause of peptic ulcer disease worldwide, and gastric adenocarcinoma is the fifth most common cancer and second most common cause of cancer-related death globally.

Function — Parietal Cell Proton Pump and Three-Phase Acid Regulation

PARIETAL CELL — H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (Proton Pump) Mechanism
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
At rest: H⁺/K⁺-ATPase sequestered in cytoplasmic tubulovesicles
Stimulation (histamine/gastrin/ACh) → tubulovesicles fuse with apical canalicular membrane
  → H⁺/K⁺-ATPase actively pumps H⁺ into lumen
  → pH 0.8 at canalicular surface → equilibrates to intragastric pH 1.5–3.5
  → Alkaline tide: blood/urine pH transiently rises postprandially

Intracellular ion cycle (to sustain H⁺ secretion):
  CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻  (carbonic anhydrase II)
  H⁺ → pumped into lumen by H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (antiports K⁺ back)
  HCO₃⁻ → exits basolateral membrane → blood (alkaline tide)
  Cl⁻ → enters lumen via CFTR/apical Cl⁻ channels (with H⁺ → HCl)

PPI (proton pump inhibitor) mechanism: omeprazole, lansoprazole
  → irreversibly bind cysteine residues on α-subunit of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase
  → require activated (secreting) parietal cell → best taken before meals
  → ~80–90% inhibition of maximal acid output (most potent acid suppressant)

THREE-PHASE ACID REGULATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CEPHALIC PHASE (~30% of acid secretion)
  Sight/smell/taste/thought of food → vagal activation
  → Preganglionic → enteric ganglia → postganglionic ACh
  → Parietal cells (M3R) + G cells → gastrin + ECL histamine → HCl

GASTRIC PHASE (~60% of acid secretion)
  Gastric distension (mechanoreceptor → vagovagal reflex)
  + Luminal protein/peptides (direct G-cell stimulation)
  → G-17 from antral G cells → CCK-2R on parietal cells → Ca²⁺ → H⁺/K⁺-ATPase
  → Gastrin → ECL → histamine → H2R → cAMP → PKA → additive stimulation

INTESTINAL PHASE (~10% acid secretion)
  Stimulatory: protein in duodenum → intestinal G-34 (gastrin)
  Inhibitory: acid/fat/osmolarity in duodenum → secretin (S-cells) + CCK (I-cells)
    + GIP (K-cells) + somatostatin → enterogastric reflex → ↓gastric acid + motility

NEGATIVE FEEDBACK:
  Luminal pH <3 in antrum → D-cell somatostatin → paracrine inhibition of G cells + ECL
  → ↓gastrin, ↓histamine → reduced acid output → protects mucosa from over-acidification

Gastric Emptying and Motility

Fundus: Receptive relaxation (vagally mediated via VIP + NO) → fundal accommodation of food bolus without significant pressure rise; proximal fundal tone then slowly increases providing the pressure gradient for emptying.

Antrum/pylorus: 3 peristaltic slow waves/min from ICC pacemaker → propulsive contractions triturate food against a transiently closed pylorus → retropulsion of larger particles → grinding → eventually 2–4 mm particles permitted through pylorus as chyme. Liquids empty faster (~20 min) than solids (~2–4 h). Fat delays emptying most significantly (duodenal I-cells CCK + ileal brake GLP-1 + PYY → ↓gastric contractility + ↑pyloric tone).

Ghrelin: The "hunger hormone" — secreted from fundal X/A-like cells during fasting → acts on hypothalamic NPY/AgRP neurons → ↑appetite + ↑GH secretion. Ghrelin falls postprandially. The only known circulating appetite stimulant hormone. Post-bariatric surgery (sleeve gastrectomy removes most fundal ghrelin cells) → ↓ghrelin → reduced appetite contributes to weight loss beyond mere restriction.

Pathology

Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD) — H. pylori and NSAIDs

H. pylori (CagA/VacA strains): Urease → NH₃ → neutralises local acid → bacterial colonisation of antrum → CagA injected via T4SS → ↑NF-κB, IL-8, gastrin → mucosal inflammation → disruption of mucus barrier → ulceration. Duodenal ulcers (antral gastritis → ↑gastrin → ↑acid) more common than gastric ulcers. Triple therapy: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin (7–14 days); bismuth-quadruple in resistant areas. NSAIDs: systemic COX-1 inhibition → ↓PGE2/PGI2 → ↓mucus barrier → ulceration; co-prescribe PPI for high-risk patients.

Gastric Adenocarcinoma

5th most common cancer worldwide, 2nd leading cause of cancer death. Correa cascade: normal mucosa → H. pylori chronic gastritis → atrophic gastritis → intestinal metaplasia → dysplasia → carcinoma (non-cardia intestinal type; TP53, ARID1A, CDH1, KRAS mutations). H. pylori eradication at early stages reduces cancer risk ~40%. Cardia/GEJ type: GERD, obesity, HER2 amplification (~15%, targeted by trastuzumab). Stage-specific treatment: endoscopic resection (early), perioperative chemotherapy (FLOT) + gastrectomy (localised), trastuzumab + chemotherapy (HER2+), pembrolizumab + chemotherapy (PD-L1 CPS ≥5).

Autoimmune Gastritis and Pernicious Anaemia

Autoimmune attack on parietal cells (anti-H⁺/K⁺-ATPase antibodies + anti-IF blocking antibodies) → achlorhydria + loss of intrinsic factor → megaloblastic anaemia (B12 deficiency) + subacute combined degeneration of the cord (dorsal column + corticospinal tract demyelination). Associated with other autoimmune diseases (thyroid, T1DM, vitiligo). Treat: parenteral hydroxocobalamin lifelong. ↑Risk of gastric carcinoid (ECL cell hyperplasia due to ↑gastrin from achlorhydria).

GORD (Gastro-Oesophageal Reflux Disease)

LOS incompetence (transient LOS relaxations, ↓resting pressure, hiatus hernia) → acid/bile reflux into oesophagus → heartburn, regurgitation. Chronic → Barrett's oesophagus (columnar intestinal metaplasia of squamous oesophageal epithelium, premalignant) → oesophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC). Treat: lifestyle (weight loss, head elevation, avoid triggers), PPI (gold standard), surgical fundoplication (Nissen).

Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome

Gastrinoma (gastrinoma triangle: duodenum > pancreas > other) → unregulated gastrin → massive gastric acid hypersecretion → multiple, refractory peptic ulcers (often distal duodenum/jejunum), diarrhoea, oesophagitis. Secretin stimulation test: paradoxical gastrin rise (diagnostic). Associated with MEN-1 in 20–25%. Treat: high-dose PPI; surgical resection if localised; somatostatin analogues for metastatic disease.

Gastroparesis

Delayed gastric emptying without mechanical obstruction. Commonest causes: diabetes mellitus (autonomic neuropathy → ICC dysfunction → loss of pacemaker activity), post-vagotomy/surgery, idiopathic. Symptoms: nausea, vomiting, early satiety, erratic glycaemic control. Diagnose: scintigraphic gastric emptying study (>10% retention at 4 hours). Treat: dietary modification (small, low-fat, low-fibre meals), prokinetics (metoclopramide, domperidone, erythromycin/motilin agonist), gastric electrical stimulation (refractory cases).
