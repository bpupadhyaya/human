RyR2 — Ryanodine Receptor 2

The cardiac SR Ca²⁺ release channel (gene RYR2) — a massive ~2.2 MDa homotetramer on the junctional SR membrane, positioned precisely opposite L-type Ca²⁺ channels at T-tubule dyads. A small trigger Ca²⁺ current through Cav1.2 activates RyR2 clusters via CICR, releasing ~75% of the Ca²⁺ that drives each heartbeat. Mutations cause CPVT and arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy; hyperphosphorylation drives Ca²⁺ leak in heart failure.

Gene: RYR2 · 1q43 UniProt: Q92736 Aliases: RyR2, cardiac ryanodine receptor, type 2 RyR Scale: 03-Molecular

Overview

RyR2 (Ryanodine receptor type 2) is the cardiac SR Ca²⁺ release channel — the molecular amplifier at the heart of every heartbeat. Encoded by RYR2, each ~560 kDa subunit assembles as a homotetramer (~2.2 MDa) on the junctional SR membrane, positioned precisely opposite L-type Ca²⁺ channels (Cav1.2) in the T-tubule at the 10–15 nm dyadic cleft. RyR2 is one of the largest ion channel proteins in biology.

During each action potential, a small Ca²⁺ current through Cav1.2 (~6 pA for a few hundred milliseconds) raises local [Ca²⁺] in the dyadic cleft to ~10–100 µM, triggering the opening of RyR2 tetramers. This calcium-induced calcium release (CICR) amplifies a tiny trigger into a full-scale SR Ca²⁺ flood — the mechanism that converts each action potential into a forceful, whole-cell contraction.

CPVT — the RyR2 disease: Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT1) is caused by >200 known gain-of-function RYR2 mutations. Increased diastolic Ca²⁺ leak generates delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs) under adrenergic stress → triggered ventricular tachycardia. Autosomal dominant; often lethal without beta-blocker or flecainide therapy.

Diastolic Sparks and Arrhythmia Risk

Even at diastole, individual RyR2 clusters occasionally open spontaneously — producing diastolic Ca²⁺ sparks (~1–3 per cell per second at rest). This low spark rate is controlled by FKBP12.6/calstabin, CASQ2 luminal sensing, and Mg²⁺ competition. If diastolic spark frequency rises excessively (CPVT, heart failure CaMKII hyperphosphorylation), cumulative Ca²⁺ release activates NCX forward mode → net inward current → delayed afterdepolarization (DAD) → triggered arrhythmia.

CPVT1>200 known gain-of-function RYR2 mutations; increased diastolic Ca²⁺ leak at rest and under adrenergic stress → DADs → bidirectional VT → VF. Autosomal dominant. Diagnosis: exercise stress test showing bidirectional or polymorphic VT at HR >120–130 bpm.
Heart failureCaMKII hyperactivation → Ser2814 hyperphosphorylation → chronically elevated diastolic Ca²⁺ leak → depleted SR Ca²⁺ load (impaired contractility) + increased DAD frequency (arrhythmia risk). One of the principal Ca²⁺-handling defects in HFrEF.
Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (ACM)Rare RYR2 mutations (distinct from classic CPVT mutations) associated with structural myocardial remodeling + arrhythmia; mechanism includes disrupted RyR2 gating + altered protein interactions.
