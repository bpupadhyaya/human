Secretory IgA

Secretory IgA (SIgA) is the dominant immunoglobulin at mucosal surfaces — ~3 g/day secreted into the gut lumen alone — providing the first line of adaptive immune defense against inhaled and ingested pathogens without triggering inflammation. A unique dimeric structure with J-chain and secretory component makes SIgA resistant to protease digestion in the hostile mucosal environment.

Structure: dimer (2× IgA + J-chain + secretory component) Concentration: ~2.5 mg/mL in saliva ~400 mg/day in breast milk (colostrum)

Overview

SIgA is produced by IgA-committed plasma cells in the lamina propria — not by circulating plasma cells. These cells originate from IgA class-switching events in Peyer's patches and mesenteric lymph nodes after antigen encounter, then migrate to the lamina propria via gut-homing integrins and chemokine receptors.

The journey from plasma cell to lumen is unique among antibody isotypes: dimeric IgA (dIgA) exits plasma cells with J-chain disulfide-linked to both Fc regions → dIgA binds the polymeric immunoglobulin receptor (pIgR) on the basolateral surface of intestinal/mucosal epithelial cells → receptor-mediated transcytosis through the epithelial cell → pIgR is proteolytically cleaved at the apical surface → the extracellular portion of pIgR (secretory component, SC) remains covalently bound to the dIgA → SIgA is released into the lumen.

Two subclasses exist: IgA1 (~90% of serum IgA; extended hinge region with O-linked glycans — susceptible to IgA1 proteases from some pathogens) and IgA2 (~50% of intestinal SIgA; shorter hinge, more resistant to bacterial proteases — better suited for the protease-rich gut environment).

Scale: The gut produces more IgA each day (~3 g) than all other antibody isotypes combined systemically. Mucosal immunity is the body's largest immune compartment by antibody mass.

Biosynthesis and Transcytosis Pathway

IgA Class Switching in the Gut

  • Peyer's patch M cells sample luminal antigens → pass to subepithelial dome DCs → germinal center reaction in Peyer's patch B cell follicles
  • T-dependent IgA switching: TGF-β (essential cytokine for α-chain switch) + IL-10 + IL-5; CD40L on T helper cells; produces high-affinity, somatically hypermutated IgA for pathogens
  • T-independent IgA switching: APRIL + BAFF (from DCs and epithelial cells); drives IgA class switching directly in B cells without T cell help; major pathway for commensal bacteria-directed SIgA (lower affinity but high volume); innate IgA provides broad coverage of microbiome

Lamina Propria Migration

  • IgA-switched B cells upregulate gut-homing receptors: CCR9 (binds CCL25/TECK from small intestine) + α4β7 integrin (binds MAdCAM-1 on gut high endothelial venules) → home specifically to small intestinal lamina propria
  • α4β7/MAdCAM-1 axis is the same pathway targeted by vedolizumab (anti-α4β7) in IBD treatment

Transcytosis

  1. Plasma cell secretes dIgA (with J-chain) into lamina propria extracellular space
  2. dIgA binds pIgR (poly-Ig receptor) on basolateral membrane of enterocytes via J-chain interaction with pIgR domain 1
  3. Endocytosis → transcytotic vesicle → apical membrane
  4. Serine protease cleaves pIgR transmembrane domain → SC remains covalently attached → SIgA released into lumen; free pIgR stub recycled
Retrograde transport: pIgR can also capture luminal dIgA and IgM that have crossed the epithelium and transport them back into the lumen — a mechanism to remove immune complexes that formed within the epithelium. pIgR also exports IgA immune complexes containing intracellular pathogens during transcytosis (intracellular neutralization).

Functions

Immune Exclusion

SIgA in the mucus layer binds pathogens and toxins → prevents adhesion to epithelial receptors → entraps organisms in mucus → cleared by peristalsis and mucociliary action. This is the primary function: non-inflammatory neutralization. Unlike IgG and IgM, SIgA does not efficiently activate the classical complement pathway and has no functional FcγR binding on myeloid cells → no inflammatory tissue damage at the mucosal barrier.

Intracellular Neutralization

During pIgR-mediated transcytosis, IgA can intercept and neutralize pathogens within the endosomal compartment of the epithelial cell — demonstrated for rotavirus and influenza. IgA also extracts LPS and other PAMPs from the cytoplasm during transcytosis.

Microbiome Shaping

SIgA-coated bacteria (identifiable by flow cytometry of stool — "IgA-SEQ") are enriched for pathobionts and immunogenic species. Coating controls the composition of the luminal microbiome: SIgA limits bloom of individual species (immune exclusion of pathogens), promotes adherence of beneficial commensal bacteria to mucus through specific high-affinity recognition, and controls Proteobacteria overgrowth. IgA-deficient mice have dysbiotic microbiomes.

Breast Milk Protection

SIgA is the dominant immunoglobulin in colostrum (~400 mg/day in the first days postpartum) and mature breast milk. It provides passive mucosal immunity to the neonate before the infant's own SIgA system matures (~2 years). Breast milk SIgA reflects the maternal mucosal immune memory — antibodies against maternal gut and respiratory pathogens are transferred to protect the infant's mucosal surfaces. IgA in breast milk is not significantly absorbed; it acts locally in the neonatal gut lumen.

IgA Fc Receptor (FcαRI / CD89)

FcαRI (CD89) is expressed on myeloid cells — neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, eosinophils, and DCs — but is not expressed at mucosal surfaces (preventing inflammatory activation by the constant stream of luminal SIgA). Its functions are systemic:

  • Opsonization: IgA1-opsonized bacteria → FcαRI → phagocytosis and respiratory burst; efficient for Streptococcus pneumoniae, H. influenzae
  • ADCC: FcαRI-mediated antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity; investigated in anti-tumor immunotherapy (IgA-based mAbs targeting tumor antigens may be superior to IgG for neutrophil-mediated killing)
  • IgA1 protease susceptibility: S. pneumoniae and N. gonorrhoeae produce IgA1 proteases that cleave the extended IgA1 hinge → release Fab fragments (still bind but can no longer signal through FcαRI → "blocking Fab" strategy used by pathogens)
