Vancomycin

Class: Glycopeptide Antibiotic  ·  Route: IV (systemic); PO (C. difficile only — not absorbed)  |  Brand: Vancocin  |  Spectrum: Gram-positive only (too large for gram-negative OM)

Vancomycin (isolated 1952 from Amycolatopsis orientalis) is a tricyclic glycopeptide (MW 1449 Da) that forms five hydrogen bonds with the D-Ala-D-Ala C-terminus of lipid II and nascent peptidoglycan in the bacterial periplasm. This steric clamp physically blocks both transglycosylation (glycan chain elongation) and transpeptidation (cross-linking) without acylating PBPs directly — explaining why MRSA (PBP2a-mediated beta-lactam resistance) remains fully susceptible. VanA-type VRE substitutes D-Ala-D-Lac, losing one critical H-bond and reducing affinity 1000×. MRSA bacteremia treatment, C. difficile (oral), and meningitis. TDM now AUC₀₋₂₄/MIC 400–600 per 2020 guidelines.

Why inactive against gram-negatives? Vancomycin (MW 1449 Da, hydrophilic) cannot penetrate the outer membrane lipopolysaccharide barrier of gram-negative bacteria. It only reaches its target (periplasmic D-Ala-D-Ala) in gram-positive organisms, which have no outer membrane.

VanA Resistance — D-Ala-D-Lac Mechanism

VanA-type resistance (vancomycin MIC ≥32; teicoplanin MIC ≥8) is encoded by the transposon Tn1546 carrying seven genes:

GeneFunction
vanHD-lactate dehydrogenase — produces D-lactate from pyruvate
vanAD-Ala-D-Lac ligase — synthesizes D-Ala-D-Lac (instead of D-Ala-D-Ala)
vanXD,D-dipeptidase — hydrolyzes residual D-Ala-D-Ala (removes susceptible target)
vanYD,D-carboxypeptidase — removes terminal D-Ala from any remaining precursors
vanR/vanSTwo-component regulatory system — vancomycin sensing and VanA operon induction
vanZTeicoplanin resistance (mechanism unclear)

Clinical impact: VRE (VanA/B/C types) are major nosocomial pathogens in ICUs globally. VRSA (vancomycin-resistant S. aureus) has emerged rarely via horizontal transfer of VanA operon from VRE to MRSA — the nightmare scenario of dual beta-lactam + glycopeptide resistance.

Therapeutic Drug Monitoring — 2020 Guidelines

The 2020 ASHP/IDSA/SIDP consensus guideline replaced trough-only monitoring with AUC/MIC-guided dosing for serious infections:

Adverse Effects

  • Nephrotoxicity (most important, ~10–15%): Risk factors: high trough concentrations (>20 mg/L), prolonged therapy (>7 days), concurrent nephrotoxins (aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, contrast), baseline CKD; AUC-guided dosing reduces nephrotoxicity compared with trough-guided (higher AUC targets previously drove high troughs); monitor SCr and urine output daily in ICU patients
  • Red man syndrome (not true allergy): Histamine release from mast cells during rapid IV infusion → flushing, erythema, pruritus of face/neck/chest ± hypotension; dose-rate dependent, not immune-mediated; prevent with slow infusion rate (1 g over ≥60 min, 1.5–2 g over 90 min) and diphenhydramine pre-medication; NOT a contraindication to vancomycin therapy
  • Ototoxicity (rare at therapeutic doses): Cochlear (sensorineural hearing loss) and vestibular; risk increased by concurrent loop diuretics and aminoglycosides, and possibly by sustained high AUC concentrations; audiological monitoring for prolonged high-dose courses
  • Thrombophlebitis: Peripheral IV vancomycin causes significant vein inflammation; use central line for courses >2–3 days; dilute adequately (maximum 5 mg/mL for peripheral IV)
  • Leukopenia / neutropenia (rare): Typically after prolonged treatment; monitor CBC weekly for courses >2 weeks
