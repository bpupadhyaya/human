Targeted Therapy

Scale: L02 Molecular  |  System: Oncology (multi-tumor)  |  Type: Small molecule TKIs, mAbs, ADCs, PARP inhibitors  |  Companion diagnostics required

Targeted therapies are drugs designed to interfere with specific molecular alterations that drive cancer cell proliferation, survival, and invasion. Unlike cytotoxic chemotherapy, which damages all rapidly dividing cells, targeted agents exploit vulnerabilities specific to cancer cells — oncogene dependence, synthetic lethality, and tumor-specific surface antigens. The concept was validated by imatinib's dramatic efficacy in BCR-ABL-driven CML (Drucker and Lydon, 2001), establishing "oncogene addiction" as the central principle of precision oncology. All targeted therapies require a companion diagnostic (CDx) to identify eligible patients.

Overview — Concept and Principles

The concept of "oncogene addiction" — that cancer cells can become critically dependent on the continued activity of a single oncogene, even when normal cells tolerate its inhibition — underpins all targeted therapy. When such an oncogene is pharmacologically inhibited, the cancer cell cannot compensate (lacking the redundant survival pathways available to normal cells) and undergoes apoptosis or growth arrest. Normal cells with the same pathway affected have redundant survival signals, yielding a therapeutic window.

Key historical milestone: Imatinib mesylate (Gleevec) — developed by Brian Drucker and Nicholas Lydon — inhibits the constitutively active BCR-ABL kinase in CML with nanomolar affinity. The IRIS trial (2001) showed 87% complete cytogenetic response (CCyR) at 18 months vs 34% with interferon-α, and ~10-year event-free survival of 83%. Imatinib transformed CML from a disease requiring bone marrow transplant to one managed with a daily oral pill, demonstrating that a single kinase could be the Achilles' heel of an entire cancer.

Targeted agents are categorized by mechanism: tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) — small molecules that compete with ATP in the kinase active site; monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) — large biologics targeting extracellular domains; antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) — mAb-cytotoxic payload hybrids; and PARP inhibitors — exploiting DNA repair deficiencies via synthetic lethality.

Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs)

Small molecules that competitively inhibit the ATP-binding pocket of receptor and non-receptor tyrosine kinases. Kinase selectivity is determined by the DFG loop conformation (Type I inhibitors: active conformation; Type II: inactive DFG-out; Type III: allosteric). Resistance commonly arises from mutations in the "gatekeeper" residue or activation loop.

PARP Inhibitors

Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) 1 and 2 are enzymes that sense and repair single-strand DNA breaks (SSBs) via base excision repair. PARP inhibitors (PARPi) exploit the concept of synthetic lethality in tumors with homologous recombination (HR) deficiency — most commonly due to germline or somatic BRCA1/2 mutations.

Mechanism — Synthetic Lethality: In normal cells, when PARP is inhibited → unrepaired SSBs → stall at replication forks → double-strand breaks (DSBs) → repaired by HR using BRCA1/2. In BRCA1/2-mutant cells, HR is deficient → DSBs cannot be repaired → replication catastrophe → cell death. PARP trapping (the PARP enzyme becomes covalently bound to DNA) is a key mechanism separate from simple catalytic inhibition — trapped PARP-DNA complexes are more cytotoxic than unrepaired SSBs alone. Talazoparib has the highest PARP trapping potency; niraparib and olaparib intermediate; rucaparib lower.

The concept extends beyond BRCA1/2 to broader HRD (homologous recombination deficiency) — including PALB2, RAD51C, RAD51D, BRIP1, CHEK2 mutations and "HRD signature" by genomic scarring (LOH + TAI + LST score). The Myriad myChoice CDx assay quantifies HRD score; HRD+ tumors respond even without BRCA mutation (PRIMA trial niraparib — PFS 21.9m vs 10.4m in HRD+ ovarian, including non-BRCA).

Resistance Mechanisms

All targeted therapies eventually face resistance — the central limitation of oncogene-directed therapy. Mechanisms fall into five categories:

Secondary Target Mutation

Mutation in the drug-binding site reduces binding affinity. Classic examples: EGFR T790M (gatekeeper mutation → osimertinib); BTK C481S (ibrutinib resistance in CLL → non-covalent BTK inhibitors: zanubrutinib, acalabrutinib have lower C481S resistance rate); BCR-ABL T315I (imatinib/dasatinib/nilotinib resistance → ponatinib).

Target Amplification

Increased copy number of the oncogene outcompetes drug binding. MET amplification in 5–10% of EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC (bypass of EGFR signaling via MET-GAB1-PI3K); HER2 amplification in trastuzumab resistance; FGFR2 amplification in FGFR inhibitor resistance.

Bypass Pathway Activation

Upregulation of alternative kinases or downstream effectors circumvents the blocked pathway. MET amplification → EGFR-independent RAS/PI3K activation; RAS mutation (KRAS G12C/G13D) in EGFR-TKI resistance; PIK3CA mutation in HER2-targeted therapy resistance; epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) with AXL upregulation.

Phenotypic Transformation

Cancer undergoes cell-lineage transformation to a resistant state. EGFR NSCLC transformation to SCLC (5–10% of EGFR-TKI resistance) — loss of Rb, TP53 gain, neuroendocrine marker expression; EGFR NSCLC transformation to squamous cell carcinoma; EMT with loss of E-cadherin and gain of vimentin.

Tumor Heterogeneity

Pre-existing resistant clones selected under drug pressure (Darwinian selection). Liquid biopsy (ctDNA) reveals heterogeneous resistance mutations in CML (ABL kinase domain mutations at multiple residues simultaneously). Geographic heterogeneity in primary tumor + metastases may mean resistance is pre-existing at diagnosis.

Clinical response to resistance: Sequential generation TKIs exploit resistance mutations as druggable targets (T790M → osimertinib; C797S → allosteric EGFR inhibitors investigational). Combination strategies (e.g., EGFR-TKI + MET inhibitor at resistance — SAVANNAH/MARIPOSA-2) or alternating regimens aim to prevent or delay resistance by targeting heterogeneous mechanisms simultaneously.
