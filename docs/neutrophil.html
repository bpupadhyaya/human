Neutrophil

Neutrophils (PMNs) are the most abundant leukocyte — 50–70% of circulating WBC — and the central first-responders of innate immunity produced at ~10¹¹/day. They deploy a multi-layered antimicrobial arsenal: phagocytosis of opsonised targets, NADPH oxidase (NOX2) oxidative burst generating HOCl via MPO, hierarchically ordered granule degranulation (azurophil → specific → gelatinase → secretory), and suicidal/vital NETosis. Dysregulation causes chronic granulomatous disease (NOX2 mutations), ANCA-associated vasculitis, and drives COVID-19 microvascular thrombosis.

Overview

The neutrophil (polymorphonuclear leukocyte, PMN) is the most abundant circulating white blood cell, comprising 50–70% of all leukocytes (1.8–7.7×10⁹/L in adult blood). It is the central first-responder of innate immunity, recruited within minutes of tissue infection or injury. Neutrophils are short-lived but produced in vast numbers — approximately 10¹¹ cells per day — from myeloid progenitors in the bone marrow. Their name reflects the neutral (weakly eosinophilic) staining of their cytoplasm with Romanowsky-type dyes; "poly" refers to their multilobed nucleus — 3–5 lobes connected by thin chromatin filaments visible on blood smear.

Neutrophils are the primary defense against bacteria and fungi. Their dysregulation contributes to sterile inflammatory injury (ARDS, ischaemia-reperfusion), autoimmune vasculitis (ANCA-associated vasculitides), and immunodeficiency (chronic granulomatous disease, CGD). The NET-COVID-19 connection — where SARS-CoV-2 drives excessive neutrophil extracellular trap formation contributing to microvascular thrombosis and ARDS — has made neutrophil biology a major focus of critical care immunology.

NETs (Neutrophil Extracellular Traps): Stimulated by PMA, LPS, IL-8, or fungi, neutrophils expel decondensed chromatin scaffolded with histones (H3Cit4 by PAD4), MPO, elastase, and LL-37 — extracellular mesh structures that immobilize and kill bacteria/fungi. "Suicidal" NETosis: cell lysis (ROS-dependent PAD4 activation). "Vital" NETosis: NET release via vesicle extrusion while cell survives. NETs are also pathological in COVID-19 thrombosis, autoimmune vasculitis, and stroke.

Pathology

Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD)

X-linked (gp91phox/CYBB mutations, 70%) or autosomal recessive (p47phox/NCF1, p67phox/NCF2, p22phox/CYBA subunit mutations). NOX2 NADPH oxidase non-functional → absent oxidative burst → recurrent infections with catalase-positive organisms (Staphylococcus aureus, Aspergillus, Nocardia, Burkholderia cepacia) and granuloma formation. Granulomas form because macrophages cannot kill engulfed organisms. Diagnose with dihydrorhodamine (DHR) flow cytometry. Treat with TMP-SMX + itraconazole prophylaxis + IFN-γ (2×/week). Cure with HSCT or gene therapy (lentiviral vector correcting gp91phox or p47phox).

Neutropenia

Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) below 1.5×10⁹/L; severe neutropenia (ANC <0.5×10⁹/L) causes febrile neutropenia — life-threatening susceptibility to gram-negative bacteremia (Pseudomonas, E. coli, Klebsiella) and invasive fungal infections (Aspergillus, Candida). Causes: chemotherapy myelosuppression, aplastic anemia, cyclic neutropenia (ELANE mutations, 3-week cycle), autoimmune neutropenia. G-CSF (filgrastim, pegfilgrastim) stimulates BM production and mobilization — standard prophylaxis with myelosuppressive chemotherapy.

ANCA-Associated Vasculitis (AAV)

NET-derived MPO and PR3 serve as autoantigens for ANCA (antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies): anti-MPO (p-ANCA) in microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) and eosinophilic GPA (EGPA); anti-PR3 (c-ANCA) in granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA). ANCA binds primed neutrophil surface → FcγR activation → NETosis → vascular injury → glomerulonephritis, alveolar hemorrhage, pauci-immune vasculitis. Treat with rituximab or cyclophosphamide + steroids; avacopan (C5aR1 antagonist) FDA-approved as steroid-sparing adjunct.

COVID-19 neutrophil hyperactivation and NETs

SARS-CoV-2 infection drives excessive NETosis via TLR7/TLR8 sensing and complement activation; NET-derived DNA/histones trigger endothelial damage, complement activation (C3/C5), platelet aggregation (via HMGB1, citrullinated histones), and microvascular thrombosis. NET burden correlates strongly with COVID-19 severity, ARDS, and D-dimer elevation. Low-dose corticosteroids (dexamethasone) suppress NET-mediated inflammation — the mechanistic basis of the RECOVERY trial mortality benefit.

MPO deficiency

Most common primary neutrophil disorder (1:2000–4000); usually asymptomatic — compensatory prolonged respiratory burst compensates for absent MPO. Increased risk of Candida infections specifically in concurrent diabetes mellitus (dual defect: impaired killing + hyperglycemia feeding fungus). Detected incidentally by automated haematology analysers (MPO fluorescence channel shows absent signal). No treatment generally needed.

NETs — Formation, Function, and Pathology

Neutrophil Extracellular Traps (NETs) are extracellular web-like DNA/protein structures first described by Brinkmann et al. in 2004. They represent a third killing mechanism distinct from phagocytosis and degranulation:

  SUICIDAL NETOSIS (cell death, 2–4 hours):
  Trigger: PMA, LPS, activated platelets, C3b, fungi (Candida/Aspergillus)
         │
         ▼
  NADPH oxidase (ROS) → PAD4 activation (peptidylarginine deiminase 4)
         │
         ▼
  PAD4 citrullinates H3 at Arg2, Arg8, Arg17 → chromatin decondensation
  + Nuclear envelope breakdown
  + Granule fusion (MPO + elastase translocate to nucleus)
         │
         ▼
  Cell lysis → NET release
  Scaffold: dsDNA + citrullinated histones (H3Cit4)
  Armed with: MPO, neutrophil elastase, LL-37, granulysin, lactoferrin
         │
         ▼
  NET immobilizes bacteria/fungi → killing via ROS and AMPs

  VITAL NETOSIS (cell survives, ~minutes):
  Triggered by: platelet interaction, low-level LPS, complement
  NETs released via mitochondrial DNA extrusion (anuclear vesicles)
  Neutrophil retains functional capacity after NET release
         │
  NET RESOLUTION:
  DNase I (serum) degrades NET scaffold
  Macrophage efferocytosis of NET remnants
  C1q + histones → NET complement activation (pathological)

  NET PATHOLOGY:
  ├── ANCA-AAV: NET-derived MPO/PR3 become autoantigens → ANCA → re-activates PMNs
  ├── COVID-19: SARS-CoV-2 NET hyperactivation → microvascular thrombosis, ARDS
  ├── SLE: NET DNA/citrullinated histones activate pDCs (TLR9) → type I IFN loop
  ├── Atherosclerosis: NETs in plaque macrophages → foam cell formation, instability
  └── Stroke: neutrophil NETs in cerebral vasculature → thrombus formation

Therapeutic targeting: DNase I (dornase alfa, nebulized) breaks up NET-rich mucus in cystic fibrosis. PAD4 inhibitors (BB-Cl-amidine) being investigated for ANCA vasculitis and SLE. Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) in COVID-19 reduces NET-mediated coagulopathy. Gasdermin D inhibitors (in development) may block both NET and pyroptosis pathways.
