Norepinephrine (NE)

The primary sympathetic neurotransmitter — maintains vascular tone via α1 receptors, modulates CNS arousal and attention via locus coeruleus projections, and is the first-line vasopressor in septic shock.

MW 169.18 g/mol. Synthesised from dopamine by DβH (intra-vesicular, Cu²⁺/ascorbate/O₂) in sympathetic terminals and locus coeruleus neurons. Precursor to epinephrine (via adrenal PNMT). Three main receptor classes: α1 (Gq, vasoconstriction), α2 (Gi, autoreceptor/sedation), β1 (Gs, cardiac inotropy/chronotropy). Reuptake: NET (SLC6A2) — target of TCAs, SNRIs, atomoxetine. Degradation: MAO-A + COMT → VMA.

Norepinephrine (NE) / Noradrenaline

Class: Catecholamine  ·  Sympathetic neurotransmitter  ·  CNS neuromodulator  |  Biosynthesis: DβH (VESICULAR; dopamine precursor)  |  Reuptake: NET (SLC6A2)  |  Degradation: MAO-A → DHPG → VMA; COMT → normetanephrine

Norepinephrine (noradrenaline; NE; MW 169.18 Da) is the primary neurotransmitter of sympathetic postganglionic neurons throughout the body and the principal neuromodulator of CNS arousal states via diffuse projections from the locus coeruleus (LC) — the largest noradrenergic nucleus in the brain (A6 cell group, pontine brainstem). NE is structurally identical to epinephrine except for its primary amine (NH₂) versus epinephrine's N-methyl secondary amine — a single difference that reduces β₂ affinity substantially. It acts predominantly at α₁ (vasoconstriction → ↑TPR → ↑BP), α₂ (presynaptic autoreceptor inhibition + CNS sedation/analgesia), and β₁ (cardiac positive inotropy/chronotropy + renin release) adrenergic receptors. Its norepinephrine transporter (NET/SLC6A2) is the pharmacological target of tricyclic antidepressants, SNRIs, and atomoxetine. Clinically, IV norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor in septic shock.

Structure & Chemistry

NE is a catecholamine (catechol ring + aminoethanol side chain). Catechol ring (3,4-dihydroxybenzene) is essential for receptor binding and is O-methylated by COMT at the 3-OH → normetanephrine. The benzylic chiral centre (R-configuration in natural L-NE) confers stereoselective receptor binding — synthetic racemic mixtures are less potent. The primary amine (−NH₂) distinguishes NE from epinephrine (−NHCH₃) and is responsible for its higher α₁/α₂ and lower β₂ selectivity relative to epinephrine.

NE is stored in dense-core vesicles in sympathetic terminals co-packaged with ATP, chromogranin A, and neuropeptide Y (NPY). In the LC, NE is synthesised and packaged in synaptic vesicles that release NE in a firing rate-dependent fashion as volume transmission throughout the forebrain.

NE vasopressor dosing guidance: At therapeutic doses in septic shock, NE (0.01–3 mcg/kg/min IV) primarily exerts α₁-mediated vasoconstriction → ↑MAP. β₁ stimulation is present but baroreflex counteracts direct chronotropic effect — resulting in little change or slight reflex bradycardia. At very high doses, vasoconstriction can reduce cardiac output by ↑afterload — watch for peripheral ischemia (fingers, gut, kidneys). Titrate to MAP ≥65 mmHg, not to achieve arbitrary NE dose.
