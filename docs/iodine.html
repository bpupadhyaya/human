Biological Roles

Thyroid hormone synthesis — from iodide uptake to active hormone secretion

Thyroid Hormone Synthesis Pathway

  Blood I⁻
      │
      ▼  NIS (SLC5A5) — 2 Na⁺ : 1 I⁻ symport (basolateral)
  Follicular cell cytoplasm
      │
      ▼  Pendrin (SLC26A4) — apical exit into follicular lumen
  Follicular lumen
      │
      ▼  TPO (thyroid peroxidase) + DUOX2-generated H₂O₂
  Organification: I⁻ → I⁰ → MIT (monoiodotyrosine)
                                 → DIT (diiodotyrosine)
      │
      ▼  Coupling reaction (TPO)
  MIT + DIT ────────────────────────► T3 (triiodothyronine)
  DIT + DIT ────────────────────────► T4 (thyroxine / tetraiodothyronine)
      │
      ▼  Stored as thyroglobulin (Tg) colloid
  Secretion: Tg endocytosis → lysosomal proteolysis → T4/T3 release
      │
      ▼  Peripheral deiodination (selenoproteins)
  T4 ──DIO1/2──► T3 (active; ~80% of circulating T3)
  T4 ──DIO3────► rT3 (inactive; inactivation)
  T3 ──DIO3────► T2 (inactive; degradation)

T4 is the predominant secretory product (~90% of output). DIO2 in liver, kidney, brain, and brown adipose tissue converts T4 → T3. DIO3 inactivates T3 and T4, protecting the fetus from maternal thyroid hormones.

Wolff-Chaikoff Effect

Acute pharmacological iodide loading transiently inhibits TPO organification ("Wolff-Chaikoff block"), protecting against thyrotoxicosis. Normal thyroid "escapes" within 24–48 h by downregulating NIS expression. Escape failure leads to iodide-induced hypothyroidism in susceptible individuals.

Absorption & Metabolism

NIS-mediated active accumulation, pendrin apical transport, deiodination recycling

Dietary iodide is rapidly absorbed in the proximal small intestine and enters plasma. The thyroid actively concentrates iodide via NIS, achieving a thyroid:plasma ratio of 20–40:1 (up to 100:1 in iodine deficiency). TSH (thyrotropin) upregulates NIS expression through the cAMP/PKA pathway.

~30–40 µg iodide is recycled daily from thyroid hormone deiodination by DIOs. Renal excretion accounts for >90% of iodide excretion; urinary iodine concentration (UIC) is the standard epidemiological biomarker (median UIC <100 µg/L indicates deficiency).

Deficiency & Toxicity

Iodine deficiency is the world's leading preventable cause of intellectual disability

StatusUIC (µg/L)SignsTreatment
Severe deficiency<20Cretinism (neurological: deaf-mutism, spasticity; myxoedematous: profound hypothyroidism, stunted growth); goitreIodine supplementation; iodised salt programs
Moderate deficiency20–49Endemic goitre; subclinical hypothyroidism; impaired cognition in childrenIodine supplementation

Sufficient100–199Normal thyroid function—
Excess (acute)>300Wolff-Chaikoff hypothyroidism; iodide goitre; thyroiditis (rare)Withdraw excess iodine

Congenital hypothyroidism affects ~1:3000–4000 neonates globally; neonatal screening programs (TSH + T4 heel-prick) allow early levothyroxine treatment, preventing cognitive damage if initiated within 2 weeks of birth.
