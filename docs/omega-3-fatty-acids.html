Omega-3 Fatty Acids

Source: Cold-water fish, algae, flaxseed  |  Active forms: EPA (pharmacological anti-inflammatory/CV), DHA (structural neurology), ALA (dietary essential)  |  Category: n-3 PUFA · Nutraceutical · Prescription lipid agent

Long-chain n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids: EPA (C20:5n-3) and DHA (C22:6n-3) from marine sources; ALA (C18:3n-3) from plants. EPA/DHA are incorporated into cell membrane phospholipids, displacing arachidonic acid and shifting eicosanoid synthesis from high-potency pro-inflammatory products (PGE₂, TXA₂, LTB₄) toward weaker EPA-derived equivalents and specialised pro-resolving mediators. At pharmacological doses, EPA reduces triglycerides 30–50% via PPARα activation and SREBP-1c suppression. REDUCE-IT (2019, n=8,179): icosapentaenoic acid 4 g/day → −25% MACE and −20% cardiovascular death in statin-treated hypertriglyceridaemic patients over 4.9 years.

Overview

Omega-3 fatty acids (n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids) are a family of long-chain lipids characterised by the position of the first carbon-carbon double bond at the omega-3 position from the methyl end. The three clinically relevant members are ALA (alpha-linolenic acid, C18:3n-3), an essential plant-derived omega-3 found in flaxseed (~55% ALA), chia seeds (~60% ALA), walnuts, and canola oil; EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid, C20:5n-3), the primary anti-inflammatory and cardiovascular-active form concentrated in cold-water oily fish; and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid, C22:6n-3), a critical structural lipid accounting for ~40% of fatty acids in the brain's grey matter. ALA can in principle be elongated and desaturated to EPA and DHA in humans, but conversion efficiency is very low (typically <5% to EPA, <0.5% to DHA), making dietary marine sources or algae the only reliable path to adequate EPA/DHA.

Western diets have dramatically shifted the n-6:n-3 ratio — estimated at 15–20:1 in contemporary diets versus an evolutionary ratio of 1:1 to 4:1. This imbalance shifts arachidonic acid (AA, n-6) substrate toward pro-inflammatory eicosanoid synthesis, forming the biochemical rationale for therapeutic omega-3 supplementation. The pharmacological landscape ranges from over-the-counter fish oil capsules (variable quality; typically 180 mg EPA + 120 mg DHA per 1 g capsule) to FDA-approved prescription agents: pure EPA ethyl ester (Vascepa/icosapentaenoic acid, 4 g/day) for cardiovascular risk reduction in hypertriglyceridaemic patients, and combined EPA+DHA ethyl esters (Lovaza/Omacor) for severe hypertriglyceridaemia (≥500 mg/dL). Algal-derived omega-3 provides a vegan-equivalent marine source with comparable bioavailability.

The distinction between pure EPA and combined EPA+DHA formulations is clinically important. DHA (but not pure EPA) raises LDL-C by ~5–10% at high doses — a consequence of increased LDL particle size and reduced LDL catabolism. This mechanistic difference likely underlies the divergent trial outcomes: REDUCE-IT (pure EPA, 2019) demonstrated 25% MACE reduction, while STRENGTH (EPA+DHA combination, 2020) and OMEMI showed no significant cardiovascular benefit. Formulation selection therefore matters critically when using omega-3s for cardiovascular risk reduction.

  1. Membrane displacement: EPA/DHA incorporate into phospholipid sn-2 positions, displacing arachidonic acid in proportion to dietary EPA/DHA intake and inversely proportional to n-6 intake — the ratio matters, not absolute omega-3 dose alone.
  2. Competitive substrate shift: PLA₂-mediated release of EPA instead of AA redirects COX-1/2 and 5-LOX toward 10–100× weaker EPA-derived eicosanoids (PGE₃, TXA₃, LTB₅), reducing platelet aggregation, vasoconstriction, and cytokine amplification.
  3. Triglyceride lowering (PPARα/SREBP-1c): EPA/DHA activate PPARα in hepatocytes → ↑fatty acid β-oxidation; suppress SREBP-1c → ↓de novo lipogenesis; reduce VLDL secretion; enhance LPL activity → 30–50% triglyceride reduction at 4 g/day.
  4. SPM biosynthesis: EPA and DHA are precursors for resolvins, protectins, and maresins — lipid mediators that actively resolve inflammation by promoting macrophage efferocytosis, inhibiting neutrophil transmigration, and restoring tissue homeostasis. This active resolution is distinct from anti-inflammatory suppression.

Pleiotropic Cardiovascular Actions

Anti-arrhythmic

EPA/DHA in cardiomyocyte membranes alter Nav1.5 gating kinetics → ↓sodium current → ↓automaticity and triggered activity; proposed basis for sudden cardiac death reduction in post-MI epidemiological data

Endothelial function

↑Nitric oxide bioavailability; ↓endothelial oxidative stress; ↓VCAM-1 expression → anti-atherosclerotic effects that extend beyond triglyceride lowering alone

Antiplatelet

TXA₂ → TXA₃ substrate shift reduces platelet aggregation; additive with aspirin; clinically significant bleeding risk at doses ≥3 g/day in patients on anticoagulants

IL-6 / cytokine reduction

Reduced PGE₂ (which amplifies IL-6 and TNF-α transcription) lowers systemic inflammatory burden; effect meaningful at ≥3 g/day EPA+DHA and modest at dietary doses

Clinical Evidence

Study Design n Key Result
REDUCE-IT (2019) RCT; icosapentaenoic acid (Vascepa) 4 g/day vs. mineral oil placebo; 4.9 yr; statin-treated, TG 135–499 mg/dL, CVD or diabetes+RF 8,179 −25% MACE (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.68–0.83; p<0.001); −20% CV death; −31% fatal/nonfatal MI; −28% stroke. NNT for primary endpoint = 21 over 5 years.
STRENGTH (2020) RCT; EPA+DHA combination (carboxylic acids) 4 g/day vs. corn oil placebo; 3.5 yr 13,078 No significant MACE reduction (HR 0.99; p=0.84). Combined EPA+DHA fails where pure EPA succeeded — important formulation distinction.
GISSI-HF (2008) RCT; omega-3 FA 1 g/day in systolic heart failure; 3.9 yr 6,975 −9% all-cause mortality (HR 0.91; p=0.041); modest but significant survival benefit added to standard HF therapy.
VITAL (2019) RCT; omega-3 FA 1 g/day in primary prevention; 5.3 yr 25,871 No significant MACE reduction in general primary prevention population. Significant reduction in cancer mortality (unexpected secondary finding). Dose and CV risk stratification are critical determinants of benefit.
Meta-analysis (Mozaffarian 2011) Meta-analysis of RCTs and prospective studies >40,000 EPA+DHA ≥2 g/day: TG reduction −25 to −35%; at 4 g/day: −40–50% in severe hypertriglyceridaemia. Effects dose-dependent and consistent.

Evidence quality note: REDUCE-IT represents high-quality evidence for pure EPA 4 g/day in a specific high-risk population (statin-treated, elevated TG, established CVD or diabetes). The mineral oil placebo debate has not fully resolved, but the magnitude of benefit (NNT=21) is compelling. The critical lesson is that 1 g/day OTC fish oil in unselected primary prevention populations does not replicate REDUCE-IT outcomes — evidence is specific to dose, formulation (pure EPA vs. combined), and baseline cardiovascular risk level.

Safety & Interactions

  • GI symptoms (common, dose-dependent): Fishy breath, GI upset, and burping with standard fish oil capsules. Enteric-coated formulations and refrigerating capsules reduce these effects substantially. Pure EPA preparations (Vascepa) have better tolerability than combined EPA+DHA products.
  • Bleeding risk — clinically significant at ≥3 g/day: Omega-3s inhibit TXA₂-mediated platelet aggregation. Additive bleeding risk with warfarin (monitor INR), aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors (clopidogrel, ticagrelor), and NSAIDs. Discontinue 5–7 days before elective surgery at pharmacological doses. In REDUCE-IT, serious bleeding rates were numerically slightly higher in the EPA arm (2.7% vs. 2.1%) but not statistically significant and judged favorable vs. the cardiovascular benefit.
  • LDL-C increase with DHA (formulation-specific): DHA raises LDL-C by ~5–10% at 4 g/day via increased LDL particle size; pure EPA (Vascepa) does not raise LDL-C. Select pure EPA when optimising LDL-C alongside triglyceride management.
  • Antihypertensive interaction: Omega-3s modestly lower blood pressure (−2 to −4 mmHg SBP in meta-analyses). Additive effect with antihypertensive medications — generally beneficial; monitor in patients on intensive BP-lowering regimens.
  • Orlistat: Reduces fat absorption and consequently omega-3 bioavailability; separate dosing by at least 2 hours.
