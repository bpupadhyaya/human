Angiotensin II

RAAS effector octapeptide generated by ACE from Ang I. AT1R activation drives vasoconstriction (Gq/IP3/Ca²⁺), cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis (MAPK/TGF-β/SMAD), aldosterone secretion, and sympathetic potentiation. AT2R counter-regulates via NO/cGMP and phosphatases. ACE2 converts Ang II → Ang 1-7 (Mas receptor, vasodilatory) — the counter-axis exploited by SARS-CoV-2. Therapeutic blockade with ACEi or ARBs is standard of care in hypertension, HFrEF, post-MI, and CKD.

Overview

Angiotensin II is the principal effector of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) — the dominant neurohumoral axis controlling blood pressure, fluid balance, and vascular tone. It is generated from the 10-amino-acid precursor angiotensin I by the zinc-dependent dipeptidyl carboxypeptidase ACE, removing the C-terminal His-Leu dipeptide.

Ang II acts through two GPCRs: AT1R (the predominant receptor in adults, mediating vasoconstriction, aldosterone, ADH, Na⁺ retention, cardiac hypertrophy, and fibrosis) and AT2R (largely counter-regulatory — vasodilation, anti-fibrosis, anti-proliferation via NO/cGMP). The ratio of AT1R:AT2R signalling determines cardiovascular outcome.

Chronic AT1R over-activation is a central driver of end-organ damage in hypertension, heart failure, and diabetic nephropathy. The ACE2/Ang 1-7/Mas axis provides endogenous opposition. SARS-CoV-2 hijacks ACE2 as its entry receptor, down-regulating surface ACE2 and shifting the Ang II:Ang 1-7 balance toward injury.

ACE structure: ACE exists in two isoforms — somatic ACE (1306 aa; two catalytic domains, N and C) expressed on lung/renal endothelium (responsible for bulk circulating Ang II generation) and germinal ACE (732 aa; single catalytic domain) on testicular spermatids. ACE also inactivates bradykinin; ACEi simultaneously raises bradykinin → B2R → PGE2/substance P → dry cough (up to 15%) and angioedema (0.1–0.3%).

Bilateral renal artery stenosis and ACEi/ARBs: In bilateral RAS (or single functioning kidney with RAS), GFR is maintained by preferential efferent arteriole constriction driven by Ang II. ACEi or ARBs block this efferent constriction → ↓GFR → acute kidney injury. Creatinine rise >30% after starting an ACEi/ARB should prompt investigation for renovascular disease. ACEi and ARBs are absolutely contraindicated in confirmed bilateral RAS and in pregnancy (teratogenic: fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, neonatal death).
