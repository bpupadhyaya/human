Osteoclast

Large terminally differentiated multinucleated cells arising from the haematopoietic monocyte/macrophage lineage. Attach to bone via an actin sealing zone, acidify the enclosed Howship's lacuna with V-ATPase–generated protons (pH 4.5), dissolve hydroxyapatite mineral, and proteolytically digest type I collagen with cathepsin K. RANKL (from osteoblasts) → RANK (on osteoclast precursors) → TRAF6 → NF-κB + NFATc1 governs differentiation. TRAP5b (serum) and CTX/NTX (urine) are clinical bone resorption markers. Dysregulation underlies osteoporosis, RA erosions, Paget's disease, and lytic metastases.

Overview

Osteoclasts are large, terminally differentiated, multinucleated cells that are the principal mediators of bone resorption. Unlike osteoblasts (mesenchymal origin), osteoclasts arise from the haematopoietic monocyte/macrophage lineage, forming by fusion of circulating osteoclast precursors (OCPs) in response to RANKL and M-CSF. They attach to bone surfaces via an actin sealing zone, acidify the enclosed Howship's lacuna with vacuolar-ATPase–generated protons (pH 4.5), and dissolve the mineral phase before proteolytically digesting the organic collagen matrix with cathepsin K (CTSK).

The RANK–RANKL–OPG axis couples osteoclast activity to osteoblast signals, making the system tunable by systemic hormones (PTH, oestrogen, calcitonin) and local immune signals (IL-1, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17). NFATc1 is the master transcription factor of osteoclast differentiation, activated by RANKL → TRAF6 → NF-κB + ITAM co-stimulatory signalling → Ca²⁺ oscillations → calcineurin dephosphorylation of NFATc1 → nuclear translocation → CTSK, TRAP, CLC-7, αvβ3, RANK gene programme.

Lifecycle — Monocyte to Activated Osteoclast

HSC → CMP → GMP → monocyte precursor (bone marrow) → blood monocyte (CD14⁺⁺CD16⁻) → osteoclast precursor (OCP: CCR2⁺CX₃CR1⁺RANK⁺) → homes to endosteum via CCL2 + RANKL gradients → RANKL + M-CSF stimulate proliferation and commitment → OCP–OCP fusion (DC-STAMP/OC-STAMP, CD9, CD47-SIRPα) → multinucleated pre-osteoclast → bone attachment → activated osteoclast (ruffled border + sealing zone assembled).

Lifespan: 1–2 weeks. Survival signals: RANKL → NFκB (RelB/p52), PI3K/Akt, Bcl-xL; M-CSF → Mcl-1. Pro-apoptotic signals: OPG, calcitonin, oestrogen, bisphosphonates (N-containing → FPP synthase inhibition → ↓Ras/Rho prenylation → caspase activation). After resorption, coupling factors (TGF-β, BMP, IGF-1, Slit3 from resorbed matrix) recruit osteoblast precursors to initiate the formation phase.

Pathology

Osteoporosis

↑Osteoclast activity relative to osteoblast output → net bone loss → fragility fractures (vertebral compression, hip, Colles'). Primary (postmenopausal: oestrogen withdrawal → ↑RANKL/OPG), secondary (glucocorticoid-induced: ↑osteoclast lifespan via Bcl-2; hyperparathyroidism; myeloma). Treatment: bisphosphonates (alendronate, zoledronate → FPP synthase inhibition → osteoclast apoptosis), denosumab (anti-RANKL mAb, 60 mg SC 6-monthly).

Rheumatoid Arthritis Bone Erosion

Synovial fibroblasts, Th17 cells, and macrophages at the pannus produce RANKL, TNF-α, and IL-17 → osteoclastogenesis at the cartilage-bone interface → periarticular cortical erosions (hallmark of RA). Anti-TNF biologics, tocilizumab (anti-IL-6R), abatacept, and denosumab reduce radiographic progression (Sharp/van der Heijde score).

Paget's Disease of Bone

Focal uncontrolled osteoclast activation (SQSTM1/p62 mutations → NF-κB dysregulation; paramyxoviral inclusions in osteoclast nuclei, controversial) → lytic phase (↑ALP, ↑CTX, bone pain) → compensatory ↑osteoblast → disorganised coarse woven bone (mosaic lamellar pattern). Complications: deformity (bowing tibia "sabre shin"), pathological fracture, high-output cardiac failure, hearing loss, osteosarcoma (<1%). Treatment: zoledronate 5 mg IV (suppresses TRAP5b and CTX for years).

Giant Cell Tumour of Bone (GCT)

Osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells within a background of mononuclear stromal cells (H3F3A H3.3 K36M mutation) driving osteoclastogenesis via RANKL. Locally aggressive; distal femur/proximal tibia. Treatment: curettage + cement; denosumab (anti-RANKL) for unresectable GCT → converts osteoclast-rich tumour to fibrous stromal tissue.

Multiple Myeloma Bone Disease

Myeloma plasma cells secrete DKK1 (↓osteoblast Wnt), RANKL, MIP-1α (CCL3) → ↑osteoclast + ↓osteoblast → extensive osteolytic lesions, vertebral compression fractures, hypercalcaemia (↑PTHrP). Zoledronate (bisphosphonate IV) and denosumab reduce skeletal-related events.
