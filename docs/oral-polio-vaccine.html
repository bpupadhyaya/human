OPV — Oral Polio Vaccine (Sabin Trivalent)

Platform: Live-attenuated poliovirus  |  Route: Oral  |  Developer: Albert Sabin (1957–1961)  |  Manufacturer: Multiple (Bio Farma, SIIPL, Panacea Biotec)

Live-attenuated trivalent oral vaccine containing Sabin poliovirus strains types 1 (LSc-2ab), 2 (P712/ch/2ab), and 3 (Leon 12a1b). Administered as 2 drops on the tongue; replicates in intestinal epithelium and Peyer's patches, inducing secretory IgA (sIgA) at the gut mucosal surface and systemic neutralising IgG. Only vaccine that can interrupt poliovirus fecal-oral transmission — the property essential for eradication. Has reduced global wild poliovirus cases from 350,000/year (1988) to fewer than 10 (2023). Carries a small risk of vaccine-associated paralytic poliomyelitis (VAPP, ~1:750,000 first doses) and, in under-vaccinated communities, circulating vaccine-derived poliovirus (cVDPV).

tOPV (trivalent) bOPV (bivalent, types 1+3) mOPV1 / mOPV2 / mOPV3 (monovalent) Sabin vaccine

Overview

OPV was developed by Albert Sabin at the University of Cincinnati through years of serial passage of poliovirus in non-human primate cells, selecting for variants with intestinal tropism but severely reduced neurovirulence. First licensed in 1961, OPV was preferred over Salk's inactivated polio vaccine (IPV) for mass campaigns because it (1) induces gut mucosal sIgA that interrupts transmission, (2) can be administered by non-medical personnel, (3) spreads to unvaccinated contacts, and (4) is substantially cheaper per dose.

The WHO Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI), launched in 1988, used OPV as its primary tool. The initiative has reduced annual wild poliovirus cases from an estimated 350,000 to fewer than 10. Wild poliovirus type 2 was declared eradicated in 2015 (last detected 1999, India); type 3 in 2019 (last detected 2012, Nigeria). Only wild type 1 remains in two endemic countries: Afghanistan and Pakistan.

In 2016, a simultaneous global switch from trivalent OPV (tOPV) to bivalent OPV (bOPV, types 1+3) was executed in 155 countries — removing the type 2 Sabin strain from routine use after WPV2 eradication, to prevent type 2 cVDPV emergence. Monovalent OPV2 (mOPV2) is now stockpiled by WHO/UNICEF for outbreak response against cVDPV2.

Platform & Antigen Design

Sabin Strain Attenuation — Molecular Basis

Each Sabin strain carries specific point mutations that reduce neurovirulence while preserving intestinal replication. The key attenuating mutations reside in the 5′ non-translated region (5′NTR), which controls IRES-mediated translation efficiency in cell-type-specific fashion:

  POLIOVIRUS ATTENUATION MECHANISM (Sabin strains)

  Wild-type poliovirus (neurovirulent):
  5'NTR IRES translates efficiently in:
    ├─ Gut epithelium (primary replication site)
    └─ Motor neurons (CNS invasion → paralysis)

  Sabin attenuation (point mutations in 5'NTR):
    Type 1: C→U at nt 480   (IRES domain V)
    Type 2: A→G at nt 481   (IRES domain V)
    Type 3: U→C at nt 472   (IRES domain V) + Ser→Phe at VP3 codon 91

  Effect:
    Gut epithelium:  translation preserved → replication → IgA induction
    Motor neurons:   translation impaired  → no CNS invasion → no paralysis
    (IRES is structured differently in neural vs. intestinal cells;
     mutations selectively disrupt neural IRES function)

  Reversion risk:
    Type 1 — Low (multiple attenuating events)
    Type 2 — Moderate (single major mutation; most frequently reverts)
    Type 3 — High (nt 472 mutation genetically unstable)
    → Drives VAPP and cVDPV type 3 risk profile
  1. Oral administration: 2 drops (0.1 mL) on tongue or on a sugar cube; Mg²+ stabiliser protects virus from gastric acid. Virus binds CD155 (PVR / Nectin-5) on enterocyte apical surfaces.
  2. Gut replication: Attenuated virus replicates in small intestine epithelium and M cells overlying Peyer's patches; virus spreads to lamina propria and mesenteric lymph nodes (without causing systemic viraemia or CNS invasion in immunocompetent hosts).
  3. Mucosal IgA induction: B cells in GALT undergo IgA class-switch; polymeric IgA secreted into intestinal lumen via poly-Ig receptor; sIgA blocks wild-type poliovirus at intestinal entry portal.
  4. Systemic IgG: Transient low-level viraemia from mesenteric lymph node spread primes systemic IgG against all three serotypes; neutralising IgG protects against CNS invasion if wild-type poliovirus is ever encountered.
  5. Shedding and community spread: Attenuated OPV virus shed in stools for 4–6 weeks; may spread to unvaccinated household contacts, immunising them passively — amplifying herd immunity beyond direct vaccination.

Immunogenicity

Mucosal sIgA

Secretory IgA at the intestinal surface — the only immune response that blocks poliovirus replication in the gut. Critical for transmission interruption and eradication. Not generated by IPV. Type-specific sIgA correlates with resistance to wild-type poliovirus intestinal infection.

Systemic Neutralising IgG

Serum neutralising Ab titres ≥1:8 (typically ≥1:64 after 3 doses). Protects against viraemia and CNS invasion. Correlate of protection against paralytic disease — the endpoint measured in immunogenicity trials. Both OPV and IPV generate equivalent systemic IgG.

T-Cell Responses

CD4&sup+ Th1/Th2 and CD8&sup+ CTL responses to poliovirus capsid proteins (VP1–VP4); provide long-lived memory. Not the primary correlate of protection (humoral responses dominate), but contribute to memory B-cell help and rapid anamnestic responses on re-exposure.

LMIC Seroconversion Gap

In tropical LMIC settings, OPV seroconversion after 3 doses can be 70–75% per serotype (vs. >95% in HIC). Causes: maternal antibody interference, enteric co-infections competing for gut replication niches, gut microbiome differences, malnutrition impairing mucosal immunity. Addressed by repeated NIDs (National Immunisation Days) and birth-dose OPV.

Safety Profile

  • Expected No systemic adverse events in immunocompetent hosts — OPV replicates only in the gut in normal individuals; no injection-site reactions (oral administration); no fever; no systemic illness. The simplest adverse-event profile of any live vaccine.
  • Rare / Serious VAPP (Vaccine-Associated Paralytic Poliomyelitis) — Sabin strain mutations revert during gut replication; if reversion is sufficient, virus recovers neurovirulence. Causes flaccid paralysis clinically identical to wild polio. Rate: ~1 per 750,000 first doses; subsequent doses ~1 per 5.1 million doses. Risk highest for: first dose, type 3 serotype, children with B-cell immunodeficiency (agammaglobulinemia, CVID).
  • Serious (population-level) cVDPV (Circulating Vaccine-Derived Poliovirus) — If OPV circulates in an under-vaccinated community (<80% coverage) for >12 months, sequential replication and mutation can restore full neurovirulence. The resulting cVDPV causes paralytic outbreaks. cVDPV2 has become the dominant cause of paralytic polio globally since the 2016 bOPV switch removed routine type 2 OPV, leaving type-2 immunity gaps in under-immunised areas.
  • Contraindication Known immunodeficiency — particularly B-cell disorders (agammaglobulinemia, CVID) and severe combined immunodeficiency. OPV can cause prolonged VAPP (months to years of viral replication) in B-cell-deficient individuals. Use IPV instead in immunocompromised patients and their household contacts.
