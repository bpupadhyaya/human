Omeprazole (Prilosec)

Proton pump inhibitor — a benzimidazole prodrug activated in parietal cell canaliculi, where it covalently inactivates H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (the gastric proton pump), suppressing acid secretion by ≥95%.

First PPI approved (1988), now one of the world's most prescribed drugs. Cornerstone of GERD, peptic ulcer disease, and H. pylori eradication regimens — with an important CYP2C19 pharmacogenomics story.

Omeprazole

Class: Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) / Benzimidazole Prodrug  ·  Route: Oral (enteric-coated)  |  Brand: Prilosec, Losec  |  Status: WHO Essential Medicine; OTC + prescription

Omeprazole is a prodrug benzimidazole that selectively concentrates in the acidic secretory canaliculi of gastric parietal cells, where it undergoes acid-catalyzed rearrangement to a sulfenamide that forms covalent disulfide bonds with Cys813 and Cys892 of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase, permanently inactivating the proton pump. First-line for GERD, peptic ulcer disease, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, and H. pylori eradication. Bioavailability 40–65%; metabolized by CYP2C19 (significant pharmacogenomic variability); half-life 1–2 h but biological effect lasts 24+ h due to irreversible enzyme binding.

Why the prodrug design? Omeprazole carboxylate is too reactive to administer directly — it would be activated randomly throughout the body. The prodrug strategy achieves targeted activation only in the uniquely acidic environment of the parietal cell canaliculus — a pH found nowhere else in the human body at therapeutically relevant concentrations.

Mechanism of Action

H⁺/K⁺-ATPase — The Gastric Proton Pump

  1. H⁺/K⁺-ATPase biology: The gastric proton pump is a P-type ATPase located on the apical membrane of parietal cells (gastric corpus); exchanges 2H⁺ into the stomach lumen for 2K⁺ per ATP hydrolyzed, generating the extreme pH gradient (stomach pH ~1–2 vs. cell pH ~7.2) — the steepest H⁺ gradient in the human body
  2. Final common pathway: The proton pump is the final step in acid secretion regardless of the stimulus — acetylcholine (vagal), histamine (ECL cells), gastrin (G cells) all converge on H⁺/K⁺-ATPase; omeprazole blocks ALL acid secretion simultaneously
  3. Covalent inactivation: The sulfenamide forms a stable disulfide bond with Cys813 (luminal-accessible) and, at higher concentrations, Cys892 — permanently inactivating that pump molecule; new acid secretion requires de novo pump protein synthesis
  4. Onset kinetics: Single 20 mg dose inhibits ~70–80% of actively secreting pumps; maximal effect at day 3–5 (not all pumps actively translocating H⁺ at any given time → subsequent doses catch more pumps); pH >4 maintained 14–16 h/day at steady state

Pharmacogenomics — CYP2C19

Omeprazole is primarily metabolized by CYP2C19 (hydroxylation to 5-OH-omeprazole) and secondarily by CYP3A4. CYP2C19 polymorphisms create dramatic inter-individual variability:

Clopidogrel interaction: Omeprazole is a potent CYP2C19 inhibitor; reduces clopidogrel activation by ~40–50% → potential reduced antiplatelet effect. Clinical significance debated; pantoprazole (weaker CYP2C19 inhibitor) preferred with clopidogrel.

Adverse Effects & Long-Term Safety

  • Short-term (common, mild): Headache (5–7%), nausea (3–5%), diarrhea (2–4%), abdominal pain; generally well-tolerated
  • Hypomagnesemia (long-term ≥1 year): Clinically significant in ~1%; mechanism uncertain (reduced TRPM6/TRPM7 transporter activity in gut); risk of tetany, arrhythmia; monitor Mg²⁺ in long-term users, especially on digoxin or diuretics
  • Vitamin B12 deficiency (long-term): Reduced gastric acid → impaired pepsin-mediated release of protein-bound B12; risk increases with duration; annual B12 monitoring in long-term users >50 years
  • C. difficile infection: Elevated gastric pH allows C. difficile spore germination; ~1.7× increased risk; avoid unnecessary prolonged PPI use
  • Fracture risk (osteoporosis): ~20–30% increased relative risk of hip/wrist/spine fractures; mechanism unclear; modest absolute risk; ensure adequate calcium/vitamin D in long-term PPI users
  • Acute interstitial nephritis (rare): Idiosyncratic; may present as unexplained AKI; resolve on stopping PPI
  • Community-acquired pneumonia: Elevated gastric pH → bacterial overgrowth → aspiration; small increased relative risk (~1.3×)
