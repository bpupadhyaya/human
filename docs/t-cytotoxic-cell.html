Cytotoxic T Cell (CD8+)

CD8+ αβ T lymphocytes constitute 20–35% of circulating T cells. They recognize peptide:MHC-I on all nucleated cells; killing proceeds via directed degranulation of perforin + granzyme B at the immunological synapse (SMAC), and via Fas/FasL induction of apoptosis. Forms long-lived memory subsets (TCM, TEM, TRM) and exhausts under chronic antigen — PD-1 blockade partially reinvigorates exhausted CTLs, the basis of checkpoint immunotherapy.

Overview

Cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs, CD8+ T cells) are the primary cellular effectors of adaptive immunity against intracellular pathogens and neoplastic cells. They constitute 20–35% of circulating CD3+ T lymphocytes and are defined by surface expression of the CD8αβ co-receptor, which binds the α3 domain of MHC class I molecules. Because MHC class I is expressed on virtually all nucleated cells (~10¹³ in the human body), CTLs can survey the entire organismal proteome for signs of viral infection, oncogenic transformation, or intracellular bacterial invasion.

CTLs are generated in the thymus by positive and negative selection, producing a repertoire estimated at >10⁷ distinct specificities. Upon antigen encounter, naïve CD8+ T cells undergo massive clonal expansion (one cell → 10⁴–10⁶ effector progeny), mediate cytolytic effector function, then contract — leaving 5–10% long-lived memory cells capable of rapid reactivation on secondary exposure.

Selectivity: The immunological synapse architecture (cSMAC/pSMAC/dSMAC) ensures directed degranulation exclusively into the synaptic cleft, protecting bystander cells. A single CTL can serially kill multiple targets.

IFN-γ secretion: CTLs also produce IFN-γ (upregulates MHC-I on targets improving recognition; activates macrophages) and TNF-α (synergistic killing), bridging cellular and innate immunity.

Exhaustion: Chronic antigen (HIV, HBV/HCV, tumors) drives progressive CTL exhaustion — TOX transcription factor, PD-1hi/TIM-3hi/LAG-3hi expression, reduced cytotoxicity and IFN-γ. PD-1:PD-L1 blockade (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) partially reinvigorates exhausted CTLs — the major basis of checkpoint immunotherapy in cancer.

Pathology

HIV/AIDS — CTL impairment and exhaustion

HIV-1 depletes CD4+ T helper cells required for CTL priming and IL-2 support. HIV-specific CTLs are initially generated but become exhausted (PD-1hi, TIM-3hi). HIV immune evasion via Nef downregulates MHC-I, reducing CTL recognition. CD8+ T cell functional capacity is a key correlate of protection in HIV elite controller phenotypes. ART restores CD4+ counts but CTL memory quality remains suboptimal.

Tumour immunology and CAR-T cell therapy

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are often PD-1hi and functionally exhausted due to chronic tumor antigen exposure and suppressive tumour microenvironment (TGF-β, IDO, Treg-derived IL-10). Checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4) reinvigorates exhausted CTLs. CAR-T cells (chimeric antigen receptor, e.g., anti-CD19 for B-ALL, anti-BCMA for myeloma) redirect CTLs to surface tumour antigens in an MHC-independent manner, achieving deep remissions.

Autoimmune CTL pathology

CD8+ T cells infiltrate and destroy islet β-cells in type 1 diabetes (HLA-A2-restricted insulin B chain peptides); contribute to synovitis in rheumatoid arthritis; mediate allograft rejection (allorecognition of donor MHC-I peptides). EBV-specific CTL exhaustion allows persistence and transformation of EBV-infected B cells → lymphoma.

Chronic viral hepatitis — CTL exhaustion

HBV and HCV drive CTL exhaustion; virus-specific CTLs are PD-1hi/TIM-3hi and fail to clear hepatic infection. PD-1 blockade partially restores HCV-specific CTL function. Complete HBV cure strategies require combination CTL reinvigoration with antiviral therapy (nucleos(t)ide analogues), possibly with therapeutic vaccines.
