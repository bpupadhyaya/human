Pathogenesis Mechanisms

  • Candidalysin — Pore-forming peptide toxin ECE1-encoded 31-amino-acid amphipathic α-helical peptide cleaved from the Ece1 polyprotein inside hyphae. Inserts into epithelial membranes → pore formation → Ca²⁺ influx → cell lysis. Only produced by true hyphae (not yeast), linking morphogenesis directly to virulence. Also activates MAPK/EGFR signalling → innate cytokine response.
  • Als3 / Hwp1 — Adhesin and invasin network Als3 (agglutinin-like sequence 3) is a GPI-anchored hypha-surface glycoprotein that binds E-cadherin and N-cadherin on epithelial and endothelial cells → induced endocytosis. Hwp1 acts as a covalent substrate for host transglutaminases, cementing hyphal attachment. Als3 also binds ferritin for iron acquisition and mediates biofilm maturation.
  • Yeast-to-hypha transition — master virulence switch In bloodstream and tissues, hyphae penetrate through endothelium and cross tissue barriers. Hyphae secrete hydrolytic enzymes: Sap (secreted aspartyl proteases) 1–10, Lip lipases, Plb phospholipases degrade host extracellular matrix, immunoglobulins, and cell membranes. Sap4/5/6 are hypha-associated and most tissue-destructive.
  • Biofilm formation — drug and immune refuge On indwelling devices (catheters, prosthetic valves), C. albicans forms multilayer biofilms: yeast basal layer → hyphal scaffold → extracellular matrix (β-1,3-glucan, mannoproteins, eDNA). β-glucan matrix sequesters fluconazole and echinocandins, raising effective MICs 1000-fold. Macrophage and neutrophil killing is impaired within biofilm. Biofilm dispersal seeds bloodstream with planktonic cells.
  • Immune modulation — Dectin-1/Dectin-2 evasion and exploitation Yeast β-1,3-glucan is shielded under mannan layer → reduced Dectin-1 recognition in resting yeast. Hyphae expose more glucan, triggering Dectin-1 on macrophages/DCs → CARD9-BCL10-MALT1 → NF-κB → IL-6, IL-23, TNF-α → Th17 polarization (protective at mucosa). Mannan/O-mannan activates Dectin-2 and TLR4. Overactivation drives damaging inflammation in immunocompetent hosts (thrush).
  • Commensal colonization — GI/mucosal reservoir Colonizes oral cavity, GI tract, and vaginal mucosa in ~50% of healthy adults. Commensal form is predominantly yeast. Dysbiosis (antibiotics), local immune defects (IL-17 pathway mutations → CMC), or systemic immunosuppression (neutropenia, corticosteroids, HIV CD4 <200) tips the balance to pathogenic hyphal form. GI translocation is the predominant source of candidaemia in ICU patients.
  • Azole resistance — ERG11 mutations + efflux pumps Fluconazole targets Erg11 (CYP51, lanosterol 14α-demethylase). Resistance mechanisms: (1) ERG11 gain-of-function mutations Y132F, K143R, F126L reduce azole binding; (2) CDR1/CDR2 (ATP-binding cassette) efflux pumps — transcriptional upregulation via Tac1; (3) MDR1 (MFS transporter) — upregulated via Mrr1. Echinocandin resistance: FKS1 hot-spot mutations (S645P, F641S) reduce β-1,3-glucan synthase affinity for drug.
