Fluoxetine (Prozac)

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) — blocks SERT (SLC6A4), prolonging synaptic 5-HT; presynaptic 5-HT1A autoreceptor desensitization over 2–4 weeks drives antidepressant effect via BDNF/TrkB neuroplasticity.

First major SSRI (1987). The world's most culturally prominent antidepressant — 40+ million users. Exceptionally long half-life (norfluoxetine t½ up to 16 days) protects against discontinuation syndrome. CYP2D6 inhibitor with important drug interactions.

Fluoxetine

Class: SSRI (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor)  ·  Route: Oral  |  Brand: Prozac, Sarafem  |  FDA Approvals: MDD (adults + children ≥8), OCD, panic disorder, bulimia nervosa, PMDD, bipolar depression (+ olanzapine)

Fluoxetine selectively inhibits SERT (serotonin transporter) with Ki ~0.8 nM and >1000× selectivity over norepinephrine and dopamine transporters. Acute SERT block raises synaptic 5-HT immediately, but the antidepressant effect emerges only after 2–4 weeks of 5-HT1A autoreceptor desensitization — allowing sustained serotonergic tone. Chronic elevation drives BDNF upregulation in hippocampus via CREB, promoting neuroplasticity as the core therapeutic mechanism. Metabolized by CYP2D6 to norfluoxetine (active); is also a potent CYP2D6 inhibitor — pharmacokinetically important interactions with codeine, tamoxifen, TCAs.

Mechanism — SERT Inhibition to Neuroplasticity

  Serotonergic neuron (dorsal raphe → prefrontal cortex / limbic)

  ACUTE (days 1–14):
  Fluoxetine → blocks SERT → ↑ synaptic 5-HT
         │
         ├──→ Postsynaptic 5-HT1A/2A activation (mood, cognition)
         │
         └──→ Presynaptic 5-HT1A AUTORECEPTOR activation
                    │  Negative feedback: ↓ firing rate
                    │  Counteracts SERT blockade acutely
                    ▼
               PARTIAL antidepressant effect only

  CHRONIC (weeks 2–6):
  Sustained 5-HT → 5-HT1A autoreceptors DESENSITIZE / downregulate
         │  Loss of negative feedback
         ▼
  Net ↑↑ serotonergic tone (SERT blocked + autoreceptor inhibition gone)
         │
         ▼
  CREB phosphorylation → BDNF expression ↑ (hippocampus)
         │
         ▼
  TrkB activation → MAPK/ERK + PI3K/Akt
         │
         ▼
  Dendritic spine density restoration + hippocampal neurogenesis
         ▼
  FULL antidepressant effect emerges (2–6 week delay explained)

The BDNF/TrkB neuroplasticity pathway is now understood as the actual mechanism of antidepressant action — depression correlates with hippocampal atrophy (stress-induced BDNF suppression), and antidepressants reverse this. The serotonin elevation is the upstream trigger, not the endpoint.

CYP2D6 — Metabolism & Drug Interactions

Fluoxetine is both a substrate of and a potent inhibitor of CYP2D6 — creating clinically significant drug interactions that persist for weeks after stopping due to the long half-life.

  • Codeine → morphine conversion (blocked): CYP2D6 converts codeine to the active opioid morphine; fluoxetine blocks this → codeine becomes ineffective as an analgesic in patients on fluoxetine
  • Tamoxifen → endoxifen conversion (reduced): CYP2D6 converts tamoxifen to endoxifen (the active antiestrogen); fluoxetine reduces endoxifen levels ~65%; avoid concurrent use in breast cancer patients on tamoxifen — use sertraline or venlafaxine instead
  • TCAs (amitriptyline, nortriptyline) — levels elevated: CYP2D6 inhibition → TCA accumulation → QTc prolongation, anticholinergic effects; TCA dose may need 50–75% reduction if combination unavoidable
  • MAOIs — absolute contraindication (serotonin syndrome): Allow 14 days washout after MAOI before starting fluoxetine; allow 5 weeks after stopping fluoxetine (due to long half-life) before starting MAOI

Adverse Effects

  • GI effects (most common, early): Nausea 20–30% (take with food; transient, resolves week 1–2), diarrhea, appetite reduction — useful in overweight patients; weight gain possible with long-term use
  • Sexual dysfunction (20–40%, dose-dependent): Delayed ejaculation, difficulty achieving orgasm (anorgasmia), decreased libido; most common reason patients stop SSRIs; management: lower dose, drug holiday, switch to bupropion or mirtazapine, or add sildenafil/bupropion as augmentation
  • Insomnia / activation: Especially at initiation; morning dosing, temporary dose reduction, or short-term sleep aid
  • Serotonin syndrome: Risk with MAOIs, triptans, tramadol, linezolid, methylene blue; features: hyperthermia, clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation, tachycardia; potentially fatal; treat with cyproheptadine and supportive care
  • Black box — suicidality (children/adolescents <25 years): Class effect of antidepressants; increased suicidal ideation risk in 2–4 weeks of initiation; clinical benefit generally outweighs risk in moderate-severe MDD; monitor closely, especially in first month
  • QTc prolongation (mild): Fluoxetine causes modest QTc prolongation; rare clinically significant arrhythmia; caution in pre-existing QT prolongation or with QT-prolonging agents
