Microglia

CNS-resident macrophages (~5–20% of CNS cells) of haematopoietic (myeloid) origin — unique among glia. Yolk-sac progenitors seed the CNS before BBB formation; maintained by self-renewal (CSF1R/IL-34). Ramified microglia survey ~1000 µm³/min; prune synapses (C1q/C3→CR3); clear amyloid-β; and drive neuroinflammation via TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome pathways.

Overview

Microglia are the resident macrophages of the central nervous system, constituting approximately 5–20% of all CNS cells depending on region — with higher densities in the substantia nigra, hippocampus, and basal ganglia compared with white matter. They are unique among CNS glial cells in their haematopoietic (myeloid) rather than neuroectodermal origin, and unique among macrophage populations in their embryonic seeding and lifelong self-renewal without monocyte replacement under homeostatic conditions.

Unlike astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and ependymal cells (all neuroectoderm-derived), microglia descend from primitive macrophage precursors of the embryonic yolk sac that colonise the developing CNS before blood-brain barrier formation and before definitive haematopoiesis begins in the bone marrow. These early progenitors establish the long-lived parenchymal microglial pool, which persists throughout life predominantly via local self-renewal.

Homeostatic microglia display a characteristic ramified morphology: a small cell body bearing numerous thin, highly branched processes that extend and retract continuously, sampling approximately 1000 µm³ of parenchyma per minute and making transient contact with synapses, blood vessels, and neuronal soma. Upon detection of injury, infection, or cellular stress, microglia rapidly retract processes, assume an amoeboid morphology, and adopt effector programs ranging from phagocytosis to neuroinflammatory cytokine secretion.

Function — M1/M2 Polarisation Spectrum and Key Pathways

Microglial activation is a spectrum rather than a binary switch. The classical M1/M2 framework (from peripheral macrophage biology) is an approximation — in vivo microglia adopt a continuum of states best captured by single-cell RNA-seq. Nonetheless the poles illustrate the key effector programs:

PRO-INFLAMMATORY (M1-like / DAM-inflammatory) Triggers: LPS (TLR4), IFN-gamma, Abeta fibrils, alpha-synuclein, HMGB1 | TLR4 --> MyD88 --> IRAK4 --> TRAF6 --> NF-kB --> TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6, CCL2 NLRP3 inflammasome (ATP/P2X7, Abeta, uric acid) --> caspase-1 --> IL-1beta/IL-18 + pyroptosis iNOS (NOS2) --> NO --> ONOO(-) (peroxynitrite) --> neuronal protein nitration NOX2 --> superoxide --> H2O2 --> lipid peroxidation | v [chronic activation] Neuronal damage, synaptic loss, disease progression (AD, PD, ALS, TBI) ANTI-INFLAMMATORY / NEUROPROTECTIVE (M2-like) Triggers: IL-4, IL-13, IL-10, TGF-beta1, TREM2 ligation (ApoE-lipid, PS, Abeta) | STAT6 (IL-4/13) --> Arg1, CD206, CD163, Ym1 TREM2/DAP12 --> Syk --> PI3K --> Akt --> enhanced phagocytosis + mTOR-driven MBP/anabolic metabolism IL-10 --> STAT3 --> suppression of TNF-alpha, IL-1beta BDNF, IGF-1, NT-3 secretion --> neuronal survival and synaptic support

Single-cell transcriptomics reveals additional states: homeostatic microglia (HM), proliferating microglia, disease-associated microglia (DAM), lipid-associated microglia (LAM), and IFN-microglia (IRM) — each with distinct gene signatures and functions.

Function — Synaptic Pruning and Surveillance

Homeostatic surveillance. Ramified microglia perform continuous, non-directed surveillance driven by P2RY12 (purinergic receptor detecting extracellular ATP/ADP from stressed cells, creating chemotactic gradients) and CX3CR1 (fractalkine receptor, constitutively activated by neuronal CX3CL1, maintaining a non-inflammatory surveillant state). Surveillance rate: ~1000 µm³/min per cell — the entire brain parenchyma is sampled every few hours.

Complement-mediated synaptic pruning. During development and in disease:

Less active / supernumerary synapses --> C1q deposition (C1qA/C/B from microglia, also neurons) + C3 cleavage --> C3b opsonisation --> Microglial CR3 (CD11b/CD18) binds C3b-tagged presynaptic terminal --> Phagocytic engulfment and lysosomal degradation --> Synapse eliminated Normal: Refinement of retinogeniculate, hippocampal, and other circuits during critical periods Disease: Abeta oligomers activate complement --> excessive synapse tagging --> early synaptic loss in AD

Phagocytosis targets. Microglia phagocytose: apoptotic neurons (phosphatidylserine via TREM2, MERTK, AXL); myelin debris (TREM2 → essential for remyelination); amyloid-β plaques/fibrils (TREM2-dependent, limited capacity in established AD); pathogens (TLRs, CLEC7A, FcγRs).

Function — TREM2-ApoE-DAM Axis (Alzheimer's Disease)

In Alzheimer's disease, microglia transition from homeostatic to disease-associated microglia (DAM) via a stereotyped two-stage program:

Stage 1 DAM (TREM2-independent)

Downregulation of homeostatic genes (P2RY12, TMEM119, CX3CR1, SALL1); upregulation of TYROBP/DAP12, ApoE, B2m. Occurs in response to sensing amyloid pathology without TREM2 engagement.

Stage 2 DAM (TREM2-dependent)

TREM2 binds ApoE-lipid complexes or Aβ → DAP12 → Syk → PI3K → mTOR; upregulates CST7, LPL, SPP1, CLEC7A, ITGAX (CD11c), APOE. DAM cells cluster around plaques and attempt phagocytic clearance — overwhelmed in established AD.

AD GWAS risk genes are strongly enriched in microglia: TREM2 (R47H → ~3× AD risk), CLU, CR1, BIN1, PICALM, MS4A cluster, ABCA7, PLCG2, ABI3 — all expressed in microglia and implicated in phagocytosis, lipid metabolism, or endolysosomal trafficking.

Lifecycle

Embryonic origin. Primitive macrophage progenitors arise in the yolk sac (E8.5 mouse equivalent) and migrate to the developing CNS during E9.5–E12.5, before BBB closure and before definitive bone-marrow haematopoiesis. These precursors express Runx1, PU.1 (SPI1), and C-kit at early stages; transitional cells express CX3CR1 as they enter the brain parenchyma. The equivalent process occurs in the first trimester of human development.

Maturation and identity. From E13 through postnatal day 3 (mouse), immature microglia differentiate into ramified adult microglia under CNS-derived factors: TGF-β1 (maintains SALL1 expression and homeostatic identity), IL-34 and M-CSF (CSF1R ligands). Key transcription factors: IRF8, PU.1, SALL1 (unique to microglia vs. peripheral macrophages — maintained by CNS environment; SALL1 deletion converts microglia to a peripheral macrophage-like identity).

Homeostatic maintenance. Adult microglia are maintained predominantly by local self-renewal (Ki67⁺ cycling microglia, half-life of several months in mice). Under homeostatic conditions, circulating monocytes do NOT substantially contribute to the resident microglial pool — a key distinction from most other tissue macrophage populations.

Injury response and CSF1R inhibitor depletion. After TBI, stroke, or severe systemic infection, Ly6C-hi classical monocytes can transiently enter via disrupted BBB. CSF1R inhibitors (PLX5622) deplete >95% of microglia within 1–2 weeks in rodents; rapid repopulation from residual progenitors occurs upon withdrawal within 7–14 days.

Pathology

Alzheimer's Disease

TREM2 loss-of-function variants (R47H ~3× risk) impair phagocytosis and DAM transition → failure of amyloid plaque containment + unchecked tau spread. NLRP3 inflammasome activation by Aβ fibrils → IL-1β/IL-18 → complement-mediated synaptic pruning → early synaptic loss and cognitive decline. Microglia around plaques are dystrophic with impaired phagocytic capacity.

Parkinson's Disease

α-Synuclein aggregates from degenerating SNc neurons are recognised by TREM2, TLR1/2/4, CD36 → NF-κB → TNF-α, IL-1β → feedforward dopaminergic neuron injury. LRRK2 gain-of-function mutations (most common familial PD) → increased TLR4-driven inflammatory signalling in microglia.

Multiple Sclerosis

Activated microglia and infiltrating macrophages at active MS lesion borders produce TNF-α, IL-1β, NO, ROS → oligodendrocyte death and demyelination. Conversely, M2-like foamy macrophages that have phagocytosed myelin debris are essential for remyelination — the microglial balance between inflammatory and phagocytic states determines lesion progression vs. repair.

ALS

TDP-43 and C9orf72 dipeptide repeat aggregates from degenerating motor neurons activate microglia via cGAS/STING and NF-κB → sustained TNF-α and IL-6 → motor neuron injury. Microglial NF-κB activation (SOD1-G93A model) accelerates disease progression.

Nasu-Hakola Disease (PLOSL)

Loss-of-function mutations in TREM2 or TYROBP (DAP12) → polycystic lipomembranous osteodysplasia with sclerosing leukoencephalopathy — early-onset progressive dementia + bone cysts. Demonstrates that TREM2-DAP12 is essential for CNS microglial homeostasis and osteoclast function.

TBI / Neuroinflammation

Acute microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation after TBI provides initial clearance but chronic activation perpetuates secondary injury and contributes to CTE (chronic traumatic encephalopathy). Primed microglia after TBI show exaggerated responses to subsequent insults.
