Overview

Leptin is a 16 kDa cytokine-like adipokine encoded by the LEP (ob) gene, discovered in 1994 by Friedman and colleagues through positional cloning of the mouse obesity gene. It is secreted proportionally to adipose mass and functions as a long-term satiety signal to the hypothalamus, signalling that fat stores are adequate. In doing so, leptin suppresses appetite, increases energy expenditure, and coordinates neuroendocrine function — suppressing reproductive (GnRH) and growth (GHRH) axes during starvation.

Leptin acts primarily on hypothalamic arcuate nucleus (ARC) neurons via the long-form receptor LepRb (LEPR). Two antagonistic ARC populations mediate its effects: NPY/AgRP neurons (orexigenic) are inhibited by leptin, while POMC/CART neurons (anorexigenic) are activated. The net result is reduced food intake, increased BAT thermogenesis, and enhanced insulin sensitivity.

Paradoxically, the majority of obese individuals have high circulating leptin yet display leptin resistance — the hypothalamus does not respond appropriately. Resistance mechanisms include impaired blood-brain barrier transport, SOCS3-mediated receptor desensitisation, and ER stress in hypothalamic neurons. This resistance is a key target for obesity pharmacotherapy.

Structure

Leptin is a 4-helix bundle cytokine (class I cytokine superfamily), structurally homologous to IL-6, IL-11, and G-CSF. It contains a single disulfide bond (Cys96–Cys146) essential for bioactivity. The ob/ob mouse mutation encodes a truncated, non-secreted protein. The human receptor (LepRb) belongs to the class I cytokine receptor superfamily — a single-pass transmembrane protein with extracellular cytokine-binding domains (CRH1, CRH2) and an intracellular domain that constitutively associates with JAK2.

Mechanism — ARC Circuit & JAK/STAT Signalling

Leptin triggers LepRb dimerisation, JAK2 auto-transphosphorylation, and activation of three major downstream pathways: JAK2/STAT3 (dominant anorexigenic signal), PI3K/Akt/FoxO1 (acute insulin sensitisation), and MAPK/ERK (proliferation/survival). SOCS3 and PTP1B are the principal negative feedback regulators — both are upregulated in leptin resistance states.

  1. Adipose mass expands → LEP transcription increases (C/EBPα, PPARγ2 drive adipocyte-specific expression); leptin is constitutively secreted in pulses with diurnal rhythm (peak at night).
  2. Circulating leptin crosses the blood-brain barrier via LepRa-mediated transcytosis in tanycytes of the median eminence and choroid plexus.
  3. LepRb on ARC NPY/AgRP neurons → STAT3 → upregulates SOCS3 which further inhibits NPY/AgRP; net reduction in neuropeptide Y release → reduced hunger.
  4. LepRb on POMC neurons → STAT3 → ↑POMC transcription → α-MSH post-processing → MC4R activation in paraventricular nucleus → ↑sympathetic outflow → ↑UCP1 in brown adipose tissue thermogenesis.
  5. SOCS3 (cytokine-inducible SH2-containing protein 3) and PTP1B (protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B) act as negative regulators — both elevated in diet-induced obesity, causing cellular leptin resistance.

Pharmacology & Clinical Use

Metreleptin (Myalept) — recombinant human leptin analogue (Met-Leu-His tag added for stability); approved for congenital or acquired generalised lipodystrophy. Remarkable: restores near-normal metabolic control in patients with absent adipose and near-zero leptin; reduces hepatic steatosis, hypertriglyceridaemia, and HbA1c dramatically. Also used compassionately for hypothalamic amenorrhoea due to low leptin.

Experimental targets — PTP1B inhibitors (ertiprotafib; trodusquemine/MSI-1436): reduce leptin receptor de-phosphorylation → restore leptin sensitivity in DIO mice; ongoing clinical translation. SOCS3 antisense / hypothalamic gene delivery approaches are preclinical.

GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) reduce body weight substantially; partly by restoring central leptin sensitivity (reducing ER stress, SOCS3) — mechanistic overlap demonstrated in animal models. Tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1) may additionally work via leptin sensitisation through GIP receptor pathways.

Clinical pearl: Serum leptin is NOT a useful clinical test in typical obesity — levels are already high. Leptin is diagnostic only for congenital leptin deficiency (frameshift/nonsense LEP mutation: profound early-onset hyperphagia, hypogonadism, normal or low leptin). One copy of leptin replacement → dramatic weight loss and restoration of reproductive function in these rare patients.

Connections

Leptin is tightly integrated with insulin signalling (both activate PI3K/Akt in hypothalamus; insulin resistance and leptin resistance co-occur); AMPK (leptin activates hypothalamic AMPK in food intake, inhibits peripheral AMPK in fatty acid oxidation — context-dependent); thyroid hormones (T3 stimulates leptin secretion; hypothyroidism reduces leptin); glucocorticoids (oppose leptin in hypothalamus); adiponectin (anti-inflammatory adipokine whose ratio to leptin is a metabolic health marker).
