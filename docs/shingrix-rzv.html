Shingrix (RZV)

Shingrix (RZV, recombinant zoster vaccine; GSK) is the most efficacious vaccine against herpes zoster (shingles) ever developed. By pairing a recombinant VZV glycoprotein E (gE) antigen with the AS01B adjuvant system, it achieves 97.2% efficacy against shingles in adults ≥50 years and sustains durable protection ≥10 years — a dramatic advance over the earlier live-attenuated Zostavax.

Scale: L02 Molecular Type: Recombinant subunit + adjuvant Developer: GSK FDA approval: Oct 20, 2017 Schedule: 2 doses (0, 2–6 months)

Overview

Shingrix (RZV; recombinant zoster vaccine) is a non-live adjuvanted subunit vaccine manufactured by GlaxoSmithKline (GSK). It was approved by the FDA on October 20, 2017 for prevention of herpes zoster (shingles) in adults aged ≥50 years, superseding the older live-attenuated Zostavax (ZVL) for this indication. A subsequent FDA approval on July 23, 2021 extended the indication to immunocompromised adults aged ≥18 years, including those with hematologic malignancies, HIV, and solid organ or autologous stem cell transplant recipients.

The vaccine is administered as a two-dose intramuscular series (deltoid), with dose 2 given 2–6 months after dose 1. For immunocompromised individuals, an accelerated schedule of 1–2 months between doses is permissible. The Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommends Shingrix for all immunocompetent adults ≥50 years, regardless of prior episode of zoster or prior receipt of Zostavax. Prior vaccination with Zostavax is not a contraindication; a minimum 2-month interval is recommended before transitioning.

Because Shingrix contains no live virus, it is safe to administer in immunocompromised patients — a population for whom Zostavax is absolutely contraindicated.

Antigen and Adjuvant System

VZV Glycoprotein E (gE) Antigen

The antigen component is a recombinant form of VZV glycoprotein E (gE) — specifically the ectodomain of gE, produced in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells and delivered at 50 μg per dose. gE is the most abundant glycoprotein on the surface of VZV-infected cells and virions. It is the dominant target of both humoral and T-cell immunity during natural VZV infection and reactivation. gE is highly conserved across clinical VZV strains, making it an ideal vaccine antigen with minimal escape potential.

gE forms a heterodimer with gI on the viral envelope and is expressed on the surface of latently infected dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons during viral reactivation, making it accessible to immune effectors. CD4+ T cells recognizing gE-derived peptides presented by MHC class II are the principal correlate of protection against zoster progression.

AS01B Adjuvant System

The AS01B adjuvant system is the defining pharmacological innovation of Shingrix. It consists of two immunostimulatory components co-formulated in a liposome bilayer:

  • MPL (3-O-desacyl-4′-monophosphoryl lipid A) — a chemically detoxified derivative of Salmonella minnesota R595 lipopolysaccharide; acts as a TLR4 agonist; activates innate immune cells (dendritic cells, macrophages) via the MyD88/TRIF signaling pathways; drives IL-12 production → CD4+ Th1 polarization; quantity: 50 μg per dose
  • QS-21 — a purified saponin fraction derived from the bark of Quillaja saponaria Molina; activates antigen-presenting cells and promotes cross-presentation to CD8+ T cells; quantity: 50 μg per dose; co-formulation in liposomes reduces QS-21’s intrinsic hemolytic activity while preserving its adjuvant potency

Together, MPL and QS-21 within the liposome create a synergistic immunostimulatory environment that simultaneously promotes antigen-specific humoral immunity (high-titer, class-switched anti-gE IgG) and robust CD4+ Th1 T-cell responses (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2, CD107a co-expression). The AS01B system drives approximately 30-fold greater gE-specific CD4+ T-cell responses compared to unadjuvanted gE alone, and is responsible for the unique durability of the Shingrix immune response.

Why AS01B matters: The VZV-specific CD4+ T-cell pool declines with age — exactly the demographic at highest risk for shingles. AS01B compensates for this immunosenescence by dramatically amplifying both T-cell and antibody responses even in older and immunocompromised adults, explaining why Shingrix maintains >90% efficacy in individuals ≥70 years old where Zostavax falls to ~38%.

Immunogenicity

Humoral Response

Following the two-dose series, anti-gE IgG titers are 11–12-fold higher than those induced by Zostavax and approximately 4-fold higher than titers seen following natural VZV primary infection in adults. The anti-gE antibody response is predominantly IgG1 and IgG3, consistent with Th1 polarization. Antibody titers peak at approximately 1 month post-dose 2 and decline gradually thereafter but remain substantially elevated above pre-vaccination baseline.

Long-term follow-up from the ZOE-50 trial demonstrates that anti-gE IgG titers remain ≥6-fold above pre-vaccination levels at 10 years post-vaccination (Cunningham et al., JID 2022), providing serological evidence supporting the durability of protection observed in ongoing efficacy follow-up studies.

Cellular (T-cell) Response

The CD4+ T-cell response is particularly critical for zoster protection and is the primary mechanistic differentiator from Zostavax. Shingrix induces gE-specific CD4+ T cells expressing two or more of the functional markers IFN-γ, IL-2, TNF-α, and CD107a (a degranulation marker, surrogate for cytotoxic activity) — a polyfunctional Th1 phenotype associated with durable protective immunity. These multifunctional CD4+ T cells are detected in >97% of vaccine recipients at 1 month post-dose 2 and persist above pre-vaccination levels at ≥9 years of follow-up.

CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocyte responses are also induced, though of smaller magnitude than CD4+ responses. CTLs contribute to direct killing of VZV-reactivating neurons, complementing the CD4+ Th1-mediated protective mechanisms.

Efficacy Trials

ZOE-50 (Lal et al., N Engl J Med 2015)

The pivotal efficacy trial enrolled 15,411 adults ≥50 years at 18 countries in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. The primary endpoint was confirmed herpes zoster by PCR and/or Tzanck preparation. Among evaluable participants, Shingrix demonstrated 97.2% vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster (95% CI 93.7–99.0%; 6 cases in vaccine vs. 210 in placebo, median follow-up 3.2 years). Efficacy against postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) was 100% in the ZOE-50 population (0 cases vs. 18 in placebo).

ZOE-70 (Cunningham et al., N Engl J Med 2016)

A companion trial enrolling 13,900 adults ≥70 years demonstrated 91.3% vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster (95% CI 86.8–94.5%). Efficacy against PHN was 88.8%. The pooled ZOE-50/ZOE-70 analysis confirmed 89.8% efficacy against PHN and demonstrated that efficacy did not wane materially across the five-year follow-up period.

Immunocompromised Populations

Several trials evaluated Shingrix in high-risk immunocompromised individuals, populations for whom shingles risk is dramatically elevated and Zostavax is contraindicated:

  • ZOSTER-006 (autologous SCT recipients): 1,846 patients post-autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant; 68.2% efficacy against herpes zoster (95% CI 55.7–77.5%; Bastidas et al., JAMA 2019)
  • ZOSTER-015 (HIV patients): 94.0% CD4 response rate; vaccine efficacy 74.5% in HIV-positive patients with CD4 ≥200 cells/μL (Berkowitz et al., JID 2015)
  • ZOSTER-039 (renal transplant recipients): 68.4% efficacy (Bastidas 2019 follow-up data)
  • Hematologic malignancy studies: ≥70% reduction in confirmed zoster in NHL and CLL patients receiving chemotherapy

Comparison with Zostavax (ZVL)

Zostavax (live attenuated varicella zoster vaccine, ZVL; Merck) was the first licensed zoster vaccine (FDA 2006). It is a high-potency (~19,400 PFU) formulation of the same Oka-strain live-attenuated VZV used in Varivax (varicella vaccine), intended to boost waning VZV-specific immunity. However, its performance characteristics differ substantially from Shingrix in every relevant dimension:

ZVL is no longer recommended by ACIP; Shingrix is the preferred and only routinely recommended zoster vaccine in the United States. For adults who have previously received Zostavax, the ACIP recommends Shingrix at least 2 months after ZVL administration.

Mechanism of Protection

VZV establishes lifelong latency in the cell bodies of sensory neurons of dorsal root ganglia (DRG) after primary varicella infection. Reactivation — herpes zoster — occurs when VZV-specific cell-mediated immunity declines below a threshold, typically with advancing age, immunosuppression, or physiological stress. Shingrix intervenes in this process at multiple immunological levels:

  1. CD4+ Th1 cells: Recognize gE peptides presented by MHC class II on antigen-presenting cells and VZV-infected keratinocytes. Polyfunctional CD4+ T cells secreting IFN-γ + TNF-α + IL-2 simultaneously are the strongest immunological correlate of zoster protection. These cells provide both direct cytokine-mediated antiviral activity and helper function for CD8+ CTLs and B cells.
  2. CD8+ CTLs: Kill VZV-reactivating neuronal or epithelial cells displaying gE peptides on MHC class I. CTL activity is critical for limiting viral spread within ganglia and preventing centrifugal virion transport to skin.
  3. Antibody (anti-gE IgG): Neutralizes cell-free virions and mediates antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) against gE-expressing infected cells. Anti-gE IgG concentrations correlate with protection in epidemiological studies, but the cellular immune response is considered the primary protective mechanism for control of established latency.
  4. AS01B amplification: TLR4 signaling via MPL in draining lymph node dendritic cells drives IL-12 production, polarizing naive CD4+ T cells to Th1. QS-21 promotes cross-presentation, activating CTLs. The liposomal co-delivery maximizes local innate stimulation while containing QS-21 toxicity.

Adverse Effects

Shingrix is notable for a higher rate of local and systemic reactogenicity compared to Zostavax — a direct consequence of the potent AS01B adjuvant. However, since no live virus is present, there is no risk of vaccine-strain infection in immunocompromised recipients.
