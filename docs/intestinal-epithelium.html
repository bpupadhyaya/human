Intestinal Epithelium

Single-cell-thick columnar epithelial lining of the small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) and large intestine (caecum, colon, rectum). Despite its minimal physical thickness (~20–25 µm per cell height), it fulfils extraordinary roles: nutrient and water absorption, mucus secretion, microbial sensing, and immune education. Presents ~32 m² of mucosal surface via the villus-crypt architecture. Most rapidly renewing adult tissue: complete turnover in 3–5 days (small intestine) driven by Lgr5⁺ intestinal stem cells at crypt bases.

Overview

The intestinal epithelium is the single-cell-thick columnar epithelial lining of the small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) and large intestine (caecum, colon, rectum). It presents the largest mucosal surface in the human body — approximately 32 m² when fully extended by villi and microvilli — far exceeding skin (~1.7 m²) or lung epithelium (~70 m²). The gut epithelium is the most rapidly renewing tissue in the adult human body, with a complete cellular turnover every 3–5 days in the small intestine and 5–7 days in the colon, driven by intestinal stem cells (ISCs) — Lgr5⁺ crypt-base columnar cells — residing at the base of crypts of Lieberkühn. Approximately 10¹⁰ epithelial cells are shed into the intestinal lumen per day.

The intestinal epithelium forms the primary barrier separating ~38 trillion luminal microorganisms (gut microbiome) and their metabolites from the sterile internal milieu. Barrier integrity depends on tight junctions, mucus layers (MUC2-rich dense inner layer in the colon), and secretory IgA. Barrier breach — via tight junction dysregulation, cell death, or mucus layer disruption — triggers inflammatory responses, and chronic dysfunction underlies conditions ranging from inflammatory bowel disease to systemic endotoxaemia. More than 70% of the body's immune cells reside in gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) immediately subjacent to the epithelium; the epithelium is the primary conduit for immune education and tolerance induction toward commensal bacteria and dietary antigens.

The epithelium is not a simple absorptive sheet — it is a multifunctional immunological interface. Specialised cell types (M cells, tuft cells, enteroendocrine cells) sense the luminal environment and relay signals to sub-epithelial immune cells, the enteric nervous system, and the pancreatic islets. The enteroendocrine cells collectively constitute the largest endocrine organ in the body by cell number: L-cell-derived GLP-1 and GIP account for ~50–70% of the post-meal insulin response (incretin effect), and are the targets of the most successful modern class of anti-obesity/diabetes drugs (GLP-1 receptor agonists: semaglutide, liraglutide).

Anatomy & Structure

Villus-crypt axis (small intestine). Finger-like projections (villi, 0.5–1.5 mm) interspersed with tubular invaginations (crypts of Lieberkühn, ~100–300 µm deep). Terminally differentiated cells migrate from crypt → villus tip over 3–5 days, then are shed (~10¹⁰ cells/day). Apical microvilli (brush border): 1–3 µm tall, ~3,000 per enterocyte, ×30 surface amplification; stabilised by F-actin cross-linked by villin, fimbrin, eps8. The brush-border glycocalyx (400–500 nm) anchors digestive enzymes (sucrase-isomaltase, lactase-phlorizin hydrolase, aminopeptidase N, dipeptidyl peptidase IV) in the apical membrane.

Tight junctions and the paracellular barrier: Claudins (1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 15), occludin, tricellulin, and junction adhesion molecules (JAMs) seal adjacent cells. Claudin-2 forms leaky cation channels (increased in IBD and Th2 states). ZO-1/2/3 scaffold proteins link tight-junction strands to the perijunctional actomyosin ring. MLCK phosphorylation contracts this ring → junction opening — the dominant mechanism of inflammatory barrier dysfunction (TNF-α, IL-13, IFN-γ all activate MLCK pathway). Mucus layers (colon): dense, sterile inner layer (~150 µm, MUC2 cross-linked by FCGBP/CRS) + loose bacteria-permissive outer layer (~700 µm) as the microbial habitat. Bacteria penetrating the inner layer trigger innate immune activation.

Function

Nutrient Absorption

~9 L fluid absorbed/day (2 L dietary, 7 L GI secretions). SGLT1: Na⁺-coupled active glucose/galactose uptake (apical). GLUT5: facilitated fructose. PepT1 (H⁺-coupled): di/tripeptides. NPC1L1: cholesterol absorption (ezetimibe target). FATP4: long-chain fatty acids. Vitamins B₁₂ via cubam receptor (intrinsic factor complex, terminal ileum only); fat-soluble ADEK in bile-salt micelles. Butyrate: primary colonocyte energy (70% of oxidative fuel); activates GPR109A/GPR41 → anti-inflammatory epigenetic effects via HDAC inhibition.

Barrier Function

Physical + chemical multilayer barrier: tight junctions + mucus gel layer + secretory IgA (SIgA transcytosed by pIgR) + antimicrobial peptides (defensins α/β, RegIIIγ) + peristalsis (luminal flushing). Pattern recognition receptors positioned basolaterally (TLR4 — LPS, TLR5 — flagellin, NOD1/2 — peptidoglycan fragments) respond to microbial penetration without constitutive activation by luminal commensals. Polarity maintenance (apical vs. basolateral membrane domains) is essential for vectorial transport and compartmentalised signalling.

Immune Education and Tolerance

TSLP (epithelial alarmin) → tolerogenic DCs (RA-producing, IDO-expressing) → Treg induction. APRIL + BAFF + TGF-β → IgA class switching in lamina propria B cells → secretory IgA (SIgA) binds commensal bacteria (immune exclusion without inflammation). RALDH2-expressing epithelial cells convert retinol → retinoic acid → imprints gut-homing (α4β7 + CCR9) on T and B cells. IL-25 (tuft cells) + IL-33 → ILC2 activation → Th2 responses (helminth expulsion) + tissue repair via amphiregulin.

Enteroendocrine Signalling

L-cell GLP-1 (released by glucose, fatty acids, bile acids post-prandially) → pancreatic β-cell insulin secretion (50–70% of post-meal insulin response — incretin effect). Targets: GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide). Serotonin (5-HT, EC cells): ~95% of body 5-HT; activates intrinsic sensory neurons of ENS → peristaltic reflex; sensed by 5-HT3R (cisapride target, nausea) and 5-HT4R (prokinetics). CCK (I-cells): pancreatic enzyme + bile release + satiety signal via vagal afferents.

Pathology

Celiac Disease

Immune-mediated enteropathy: gliadin peptides (from wheat, rye, barley) + HLA-DQ2 or HLA-DQ8 → tissue transglutaminase (tTG) modifies deamidated gliadin peptides → potent HLA-DQ2/8-restricted CD4⁺ T cell activation in lamina propria + CD8⁺ intraepithelial lymphocyte (IEL) cytotoxicity against enterocytes → villous atrophy, crypt hyperplasia (Marsh grade III). Consequences: malabsorption of fat (steatorrhoea), iron, calcium, B₁₂, and fat-soluble vitamins. Serology: anti-tTG IgA (sensitivity ~98%, specificity ~98%); confirmation by duodenal biopsy. Treatment: lifelong strict gluten-free diet. Refractory celiac (RCD type II): aberrant IEL expansion (sCD3⁻CD103⁺ NKT-like) → high risk of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL).

Crohn's Disease and Ulcerative Colitis (IBD)

Both share a core mechanism: barrier dysfunction + microbiome dysbiosis + aberrant mucosal immune activation in genetically susceptible individuals. In Crohn's disease, transmural granulomatous inflammation with skip lesions; NOD2 (CARD15) mutation impairs Paneth cell antimicrobial peptide production and innate immune sensing of intracellular bacteria → luminal microbes breach the mucosa → Th1/Th17-driven transmural inflammation. In ulcerative colitis, the barrier defect is prominent — reduced MUC2 secretion, claudin-2 upregulation (leaky tight junctions), ↓Paneth cell defensins — with continuous superficial mucosal inflammation from rectum proximally. Anti-integrin vedolizumab blocks α4β7-MAdCAM-1 interaction → gut-selective T-cell homing blockade without systemic immunosuppression.

Colorectal Cancer

Originates in crypt stem cells via the APC/Wnt → KRAS → TP53 → SMAD4 sequential mutational progression in adenoma precursor lesions (adenoma → carcinoma sequence, ~10–15 years). APC loss → unrestrained Wnt/β-catenin → crypt hyperproliferation → adenoma. MSI-H (Lynch syndrome, sporadic MLH1 hypermethylation) → hypermutation → immunogenic → responds to checkpoint blockade (pembrolizumab). Sessile serrated adenomas: BRAF V600E + MLH1 methylation pathway. Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (E-cadherin loss) enables basement membrane invasion and metastasis. Screening: colonoscopy removes adenomas before malignant transformation.

Infectious Diarrhoea and Epithelial Disruption

Vibrio cholerae: CT toxin ADP-ribosylates Gs → ↑cAMP → CFTR-mediated Cl⁻ hypersecretion → massive watery diarrhoea (up to 20 L/day); treatment: ORS + doxycycline. ETEC: heat-labile toxin (mimics CT) + heat-stable toxin (↑cGMP → CFTR). Rotavirus: infects mature villus enterocytes → NSP4 (viral enterotoxin) → Ca²⁺-dependent Cl⁻ secretion + tight junction disruption; vaccine-preventable (RV1, RV5). C. difficile: TcdA (enterotoxin) + TcdB (cytotoxin) glucosylate Rho GTPases → tight junction loss + apoptosis → pseudomembranous colitis; treat with vancomycin/fidaxomicin; FMT for recurrent CDI.

Short Bowel Syndrome and Necrotising Enterocolitis

Short bowel syndrome (SBS): <200 cm of viable small intestine after surgical resection (Crohn's, mesenteric ischaemia, volvulus) → chronic malabsorption, fluid/electrolyte loss, parenteral nutrition dependence. GLP-2 analogue teduglutide stimulates epithelial proliferation + crypt cell survival via GLP-2 receptor on sub-epithelial myofibroblasts; reduces PN requirements by ~30%. Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC): predominantly preterm infants; immature tight junctions + deficient antimicrobial peptides + dysbiotic microbiome → microbial translocation → TNF-α-driven transmural necrosis → systemic sepsis and perforation. Treatment: bowel rest, antibiotics, surgical resection of necrotic bowel.
