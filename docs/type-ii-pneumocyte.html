Type II Pneumocyte (AT2)

Cuboidal alveolar epithelial cells at alveolar corners, identified by lamellar bodies (lysosome-related organelles containing tightly wound DPPC phospholipid membranes and surfactant proteins SP-B, SP-C, SP-A, SP-D). Three critical functions: surfactant synthesis and secretion (prevents alveolar collapse by reducing surface tension from ~70 mN/m to <5 mN/m), alveolar epithelial repair after AT1 injury (alveolar stem cell: proliferates and transdifferentiates to AT1), and active Na⁺/fluid transport (ENaC + Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase). TTF1/NKX2.1 and SP-C are definitive lineage markers. ACE2 expression makes AT2 the primary SARS-CoV-2 entry point in the alveolus.

Overview

The alveolar type II (AT2) pneumocyte is the secretory, regenerative, and immunologically active cell of the alveolar epithelium. While the AT1 cell — a vast, extremely flat cell — covers approximately 95% of the alveolar surface area and is the primary site of gas diffusion, AT2 cells occupy only ~5% of the surface area but constitute approximately 60% of all alveolar epithelial cells by number.

AT2 cells have three indispensable functions: (1) surfactant synthesis and secretion — preventing alveolar collapse at end-expiration; (2) alveolar epithelial repair (stem cell function) — proliferating and differentiating into AT1 cells after injury; and (3) active ion/fluid transport — maintaining a dry gas-exchange surface by driving alveolar fluid reabsorption via ENaC and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. AT2 cells also express ACE2, making them the principal SARS-CoV-2 entry site in the alveolus.

Structure

Morphology. Cuboidal, ~8–10 µm diameter, located at the corners and junctions of alveolar walls ("nooks") on the basement membrane. The apical surface bears abundant microvilli (unlike flat AT1 cells). Well-formed apical tight junctions (claudin-3, claudin-4, ZO-1) maintain the epithelial barrier against alveolar flooding.

Characteristic organelles. Lamellar bodies — the defining organelle (0.2–1 µm diameter): lysosome-related organelles containing tightly wound DPPC phospholipid membranes and surfactant proteins SP-B, SP-C (essential for compaction and stability) + SP-A, SP-D (innate immune collectins). Exocytosis of lamellar bodies onto the apical surface releases surfactant into the alveolar hypophase. Abundant mitochondria (high metabolic demand for continuous surfactant synthesis); extensive rough ER and Golgi apparatus for surfactant protein processing.

Function — Surfactant Synthesis and Secretion

SURFACTANT SYNTHESIS CYCLE (AT2 cell)

  1. LIPID SYNTHESIS (ER)
     Choline + CDP-choline pathway → Phosphatidylcholine
     Dipalmitoylation → DPPC (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine, ~40% of surfactant lipid)
     ABCA3 transports phospholipids into lamellar body lumen

  2. PROTEIN SYNTHESIS (ER → multivesicular bodies → lamellar bodies)
     Prepro-SP-B/C → ER processing → proSP-B/C → cleavage to mature SP-B/C
     SP-A, SP-D: secreted glycoproteins (innate immune collectins)

  3. LAMELLAR BODY SECRETION (regulated exocytosis)
     Stimuli: ↑[Ca²⁺]i (stretch, β-agonists, ATP → P2Y receptors)
     Lamellar body → fuses with apical membrane
     → Tubular myelin forms in alveolar hypophase (SP-B + SP-A essential)
     → Surfactant monolayer at air-liquid interface

  4. SURFACE TENSION REDUCTION
     LaPlace law:  P = 2γ/r
     Without surfactant: γ ≈ 70 mN/m → enormous collapsing pressure at r ~0.1 mm
     With surfactant:    γ < 5 mN/m at end-expiration → alveolus stays open
     Normal lung compliance: ~200 mL/cmH₂O (↓ dramatically without surfactant)

  5. RECYCLING
     ~75% of secreted surfactant is recaptured by AT2 cells
     Receptor-mediated endocytosis → recycled into new lamellar bodies
     SP-A + SP-D promote macrophage phagocytosis of excess surfactant

ALVEOLAR FLUID CLEARANCE (AT2 Na⁺ transport)
  Apical:       ENaC (epithelial Na⁺ channel) + CFTR (Cl⁻)  → Na⁺ in
  Basolateral:  Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → Na⁺ out → osmotic water follows
  → Maintains dry alveolar surface; failure → alveolar oedema (ARDS)

Alveolar Repair — Stem Cell Function

AT2 cells are self-renewing progenitors of the alveolar epithelium. Under homeostasis, AT2 cells divide slowly (~turnover 80–120 days). After AT1 cell injury (viral pneumonitis, toxic exposure, mechanical injury), AT2 cells proliferate and transdifferentiate into AT1 cells, regenerating the gas-exchange surface. This repair capacity is the cellular basis for recovery from ARDS when the causative injury is resolved.

A subset of AT2 cells marked by HopX or transitional KRT8/KRT17 states acts as the activated progenitor pool during injury repair. Dysregulation of this process — hyperplastic AT2 cells failing to properly differentiate into AT1 cells — contributes to the fibrotic remodelling of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), particularly when SFTPC mutations impair AT2 proteostasis.

Pathology

ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome)

Diffuse alveolar damage — extensive AT1 and AT2 cell death → alveolar flooding with protein-rich exudate (hyaline membranes) → loss of surfactant → severely reduced compliance → hypoxaemic respiratory failure. Triggers: pneumonia (COVID-19, influenza), sepsis, aspiration, trauma. Mortality 30–50% in severe ARDS. AT2 repair capacity determines recovery trajectory.

Neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)

Surfactant deficiency due to developmental immaturity of AT2 cells in premature infants (<32 weeks gestational age; SP-C and ABCA3 expression incomplete). Presents within hours of birth: grunting, intercostal recession, cyanosis. Treat: antenatal corticosteroids (accelerate AT2 maturation via TTF1/NKX2.1), exogenous surfactant instillation (beractant, calfactant, poractant alfa), CPAP or ventilation.

SARS-CoV-2 / Influenza A

SARS-CoV-2 binds AT2 ACE2 (with TMPRSS2 priming) → viral entry → AT2 destruction → loss of surfactant production + loss of repair capacity → ARDS. Influenza A infects AT2 cells via α-2,3 and α-2,6 sialic acid receptors → diffuse alveolar damage. Both viruses destroy the alveolar stem cell pool, prolonging recovery.

Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)

Repeated subclinical AT2 injury → aberrant repair → AT2 cells adopt a transitional KRT8⁺ state → excessive fibroblast activation → progressive fibrosis. SFTPC mutations (dominant negative misfolded SP-C → ER stress → UPR → AT2 apoptosis) cause familial IPF. Usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern on HRCT (honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis). Anti-fibrotics (nintedanib, pirfenidone) slow progression.

Lung Adenocarcinoma

Malignant transformation of AT2 cell lineage (NKX2.1+ AT2-like progenitors harbouring KRAS or EGFR mutations) is the predominant pathway to pulmonary adenocarcinoma. Peripheral location, ground-glass opacities on CT; targetable with EGFR inhibitors (osimertinib) or KRAS G12C inhibitors (sotorasib).
