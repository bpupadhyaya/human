Norovirus

The "stomach flu" pathogen. A 38 nm non-enveloped icosahedral calicivirus that can infect a host with as few as 18 particles — and causes more foodborne disease globally than any other pathogen.

Norovirus (family Caliciviridae, genus Norovirus) is the dominant cause of acute epidemic gastroenteritis worldwide, accounting for ~685 million cases and 200,000 deaths per year. Genogroup II genotype 4 (GII.4) has driven successive global pandemics since the mid-1990s via antigenic drift in the VP1 P2 subdomain. Despite enormous disease burden, no approved antiviral or vaccine exists, and the virus resists alcohol-based hand rubs and many disinfectants.

Norovirus

Non-enveloped icosahedral virus with a ~7.7 kb positive-sense ssRNA genome encoding three ORFs. VP1 capsid protein (ORF2) carries the hypervariable P2 subdomain that mediates histo-blood group antigen (HBGA) binding and is the target of neutralizing antibodies. The NS6 3C-like protease cleaves host STAT1/STAT2, disabling interferon signaling for the duration of peak viral replication. With an HID50 of approximately 18 particles and fecal titers exceeding 1011/gram, norovirus is among the most efficiently transmitted human pathogens — and no approved therapeutic exists.

Infection Mechanism

1 · HBGA-mediated attachment

The VP1 P2 subdomain engages fucosylated histo-blood group antigens (HBGAs) displayed on intestinal epithelial cell surfaces and in secretions. Different strains show distinct HBGA binding specificity: GI.1 binds H type 1 and Lewis b; GII.4 binds Lewis b and H3; P[II] strains vary. HBGA binding concentrates virus at the apical surface of villus tip enterocytes but does not appear to directly drive endocytosis. Bile acids (especially chenodeoxycholic acid) act as additional entry cofactors for certain strains by inducing conformational changes in the P domain that facilitate receptor engagement.

2 · Endocytosis and genome delivery

Following HBGA-mediated surface concentration, norovirus enters villus tip enterocytes via receptor-mediated endocytosis. Within the endosomal compartment, low pH and possible protease cleavage facilitate VP1 conformational change and genome release into the cytoplasm. The positive-sense genome carries a covalently linked VPg at its 5' end rather than a conventional m7G cap; VPg interacts with eIF4E-family translation initiation factors, enabling direct ribosomal recruitment without cap-dependent scanning.

3 · Replication and assembly

The ORF1 polyprotein is autocatalytically cleaved by NS6 3Cpro into 6 non-structural proteins. The NS7 RdRp generates negative-sense RNA intermediates on ER-associated membrane complexes that serve as templates for new positive-sense genomes and a shorter subgenomic RNA encoding VP1 and VP2. VPg is covalently linked to nascent RNA molecules by VPg nucleotidylation (NS5 activity), enabling ribosome recruitment to progeny genomes. VP1 and VP2 self-assemble around newly synthesized genomic RNA; virions released by cell lysis can reach titers exceeding 1011 particles per gram of feces during peak infection.

4 · Innate immune evasion — NS6 cleavage of STAT1/STAT2

The NS6 3C-like protease directly cleaves host STAT1 and STAT2, dismantling the Jak-STAT signaling cascade downstream of both the type I IFN receptor (IFNAR1/2) and the IFN-γ receptor. This prevents transcription of hundreds of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) and blocks establishment of the antiviral state in bystander epithelial cells. NS3 NTPase additionally disrupts NF-κB signaling in some experimental models, and NS1-2 membrane complex remodeling sequesters replication complexes from cytosolic PRR detection.

5 · Microbiome interactions and environmental stability

Commensal gut bacteria expressing HBGA-like antigens on their cell surfaces may enhance norovirus stability and attachment in the intestinal lumen, potentially acting as "Trojan horse" carriers. Murine norovirus (MNV) persistence in germ-free mice is dramatically reduced, underscoring the microbiome dependency of norovirus biology. Environmentally, the non-enveloped capsid is highly stable: survives on surfaces for days to weeks, resists 60 °C heating, and is not reliably inactivated by 60–70% ethanol — soap-and-water mechanical removal is required.

Treatment & Prevention

Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) — mainstay of treatment

WHO low-osmolarity ORS (sodium 75 mmol/L, glucose 75 mmol/L, potassium 20 mmol/L, citrate 10 mmol/L; osmolarity 245 mOsm/L) is first-line. ORT exploits the sodium-glucose cotransporter (SGLT1) which remains functional in crypt-derived cells even when villous tips are disrupted. IV fluid replacement (Ringer's lactate or normal saline) is used for severe dehydration or inability to tolerate oral intake. Antiemetics (ondansetron, 5-HT3 antagonist) may reduce vomiting and facilitate ORT. Zinc supplementation (10–20 mg/day for 10 days) benefits children in low-income settings.

No approved antiviral — investigational agents

Nitazoxanide showed marginal benefit (approximately 1.5-day reduction in illness duration) in one small RCT in immunocompetent adults; not broadly recommended and not licensed for norovirus. NS6 3Cpro protease inhibitors are in preclinical development as antivirals. In immunocompromised patients with chronic norovirus infection, ribavirin, IVIG, and immune reconstitution (reducing immunosuppression) have been used with anecdotal success.

No approved vaccine — development pipeline

Virus-like particle (VLP) bivalent vaccines (GI.1 + GII.4 VLPs) and P-domain nanoparticle vaccines are in Phase II clinical trials. Challenges include short-lived and genogroup-restricted immunity, limited correlates of protection, and the requirement to reformulate for emerging GII.4 variants. No product has reached licensure as of 2026.

Infection control

Soap-and-water handwashing is superior to alcohol-based hand rubs, which do not reliably inactivate non-enveloped norovirus. Surface decontamination requires bleach at ≥1000 ppm (0.1% sodium hypochlorite). Symptomatic individuals should be excluded from food handling and patient care for at least 48 h after symptom resolution. During healthcare outbreaks, cohorting of symptomatic patients and thorough environmental disinfection of aerosolized vomitus contamination zones are critical control measures.
