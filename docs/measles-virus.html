Measles Virus

The most contagious human pathogen known — and the only one proven to erase immunological memory of every infection you’ve ever survived.

Measles virus (MeV) is a non-segmented (−)ssRNA Morbillivirus with a basic reproduction number (R₀) of 12–18 — the highest confirmed among human pathogens. Its H protein binds CD150/SLAM on lymphocytes and DCs (immune entry) and Nectin-4 on airway epithelium (viral shedding). V protein targets STAT1/2 for proteasomal degradation, blocking all IFN signalling. Beyond acute febrile illness with rash, MeV causes profound immune amnesia: VDJ sequencing shows 11–73% depletion of pre-existing B cell receptor diversity, leaving survivors susceptible to all previously mastered pathogens for 2–3 years. MMR vaccine (≥93% efficacy per dose) is the only intervention; ≥95% population coverage is required for herd immunity.

Measles Virus (MeV)

Enveloped non-segmented negative-sense ssRNA Morbillivirus with a ~16 kb genome encoding 6 structural genes and 3 P-gene products (P, V, C). H protein (haemagglutinin) binds three confirmed receptors: CD150/SLAM (activated lymphocytes, monocytes, DCs — primary immune entry; wild-type and vaccine strains), Nectin-4/PVRL4 (basolateral airway epithelium — viral egress and respiratory shedding), and CD46 (vaccine/laboratory strains only). The V protein is the master IFN antagonist: it hijacks DDB1-Cullin4A E3 ubiquitin ligase to target STAT1 and STAT2 for proteasomal degradation, blocking all type I, II, and III IFN signalling. C and P proteins provide additional IRF3 and RIG-I blockade. Infection proceeds via DC seeding in lung, lymph node amplification, viremia, and Nectin-4-mediated epithelial shedding. The most clinically distinctive long-term consequence — immune amnesia — is driven by direct memory B cell depletion, abolishing recall immunity to previously mastered pathogens for 2–3 years and making secondary bacterial pneumonia the leading cause of measles mortality. MMR vaccine (live-attenuated; 2 doses) is the only countermeasure; ≥95% population coverage required for herd immunity given the extraordinary R₀.

Infection Mechanism

1 · Dendritic cell infection and lymph node amplification

Infectious droplet nuclei (airborne particles <5 µm) reach the lower respiratory epithelium. Alveolar and submucosal dendritic cells expressing CD150/SLAM are the first cells infected: MeV H protein engages CD150/SLAM with high affinity, triggering F protein-mediated membrane fusion and cytoplasmic delivery of the viral RNP. Infected DCs migrate via lymphatics to regional (mediastinal) lymph nodes, where they infect additional CD150/SLAM-expressing activated lymphocytes. Over 4–6 days, massive productive viremia develops — the prodromal phase. Virus-laden droplet nuclei persist in enclosed spaces for up to two hours after an infectious person has left — a unique epidemiological property explaining the extraordinary R₀ of 12–18.

2 · Nectin-4 receptor — airway epithelial shedding and contagion

During the viremic phase, MeV reaches the basolateral surface of airway epithelial cells where Nectin-4 (PVRL4) is expressed. H protein binds Nectin-4; F protein drives basolateral-to-apical transcytosis; progeny virions are shed apically into respiratory secretions. This Nectin-4-mediated epithelial egress is the primary source of respiratory contagion. Importantly, MeV infection of airway epithelial cells occurs during viremia (after immune cell infection) — not as the initiating event. CD46 is a receptor only for vaccine/laboratory-adapted strains (Edmonston) and is not used by wild-type clinical isolates.

3 · V protein — STAT1/2 proteasomal degradation and IFN shutdown

The MeV V protein is encoded by the P gene via cotranscriptional RNA editing (+1 G insertion). V protein hijacks the DDB1-Cullin4A (DCAF1) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex to polyubiquitinate both STAT1 and STAT2, targeting them for proteasomal degradation. Loss of STAT1 blocks IFN-α/β (type I) signalling via JAK-STAT1/2; loss of STAT2 additionally blocks IFN-λ (type III) signalling; and STAT1 is also required for IFN-γ (type II) signalling. This single viral protein thus comprehensively silences all three IFN type signalling axes. Additionally, C protein binds STAT1 directly and inhibits IFN-β promoter activation; N protein binds MDA5, interfering with dsRNA sensing; P protein blocks IRF3 phosphorylation. The combined effect is a broad innate immune evasion window that enables the extremely efficient person-to-person transmission underlying R₀ = 12–18.

4 · Immune amnesia — depletion of pre-existing immunological memory

MeV directly infects CD150+ activated lymphocytes — both CD4+ T helper cells and B cells — via H-CD150 binding and F-mediated fusion. Infected lymphocytes undergo apoptosis; bystander lymphocytes are suppressed from proliferating by FasL upregulation and FcγR cross-linking on antigen-presenting cells. Most critically, MeV depletes pre-existing memory B cells and plasma cells that carry immunological recall to previously encountered pathogens. VDJ receptor deep sequencing studies demonstrate that natural measles infection eliminates 11–73% of pre-existing B cell receptor diversity in unvaccinated children — the serological equivalent of erasing years of immunological history. This immune amnesia leaves survivors susceptible to all previously mastered pathogens for 2–3 years post-infection, converting measles into a gateway to mortality from common childhood infections (pneumonia, diarrhoea).

5 · F protein syncytium formation (Warthin-Finkeldey giant cells)

MeV F protein drives cell-cell membrane fusion of CD150+ immune cells in lymph nodes, lungs, and tonsils. The resulting Warthin-Finkeldey multinucleated giant cells are the histological hallmark of measles and represent immunopathological fusion products rather than productive viral factories. In immunocompromised patients (HIV, haematological malignancy, primary immune deficiency), giant cell pneumonia develops in the lung — Warthin-Finkeldey syncytia in alveoli with progressive respiratory failure — which is frequently fatal. The maculopapular rash is immune-mediated (CD8+ CTL cytotoxicity against MeV-infected dermal immune cells + small vessel vasculitis) — not due to direct viral cytolysis of skin cells.

Treatment & Prevention

MMR vaccine (live-attenuated)Measles-mumps-rubella combined live-attenuated vaccine; Edmonston-Zagreb or Moraten measles strain; 2 doses; dose 1 at 12–15 months: ~93% efficacy; dose 2 at 4–6 years: cumulative ~97% efficacy; generates long-lived neutralising anti-H antibodies and cellular immunity; does NOT cause immune amnesia (vaccine strain does not infect memory lymphocytes via CD150/SLAM as efficiently as wild-type); ≥95% population coverage required for herd immunity given R₀ = 12–18
Vitamin A supplementationWHO recommendation for all hospitalised measles cases (children); reduces pneumonia severity and mortality, especially in vitamin A-deficient populations by restoring epithelial and mucosal immune function; two doses (200,000 IU/day × 2 days for children >12 months); reduces mortality by 50–90% in severe disease in deficient populations
Ribavirin (off-label)Nucleoside analogue RdRp inhibitor; used off-label in SSPE and severe measles in immunocompromised patients; IV ribavirin for MeV encephalitis; limited clinical evidence for benefit in acute immunocompetent measles; generally not recommended for uncomplicated disease
AntibioticsIndicated only for confirmed secondary bacterial pneumonia (S. pneumoniae, S. aureus, H. influenzae) or acute otitis media; empiric coverage based on local susceptibility patterns; no benefit for viral pneumonitis
Post-exposure prophylaxisImmune globulin (IG) IM within 6 days of exposure: protects immunocompromised, infants <12 months, pregnant women; MMR vaccine within 72 h of exposure for unvaccinated healthy individuals ≥12 months; neither measure prevents infection reliably once symptoms develop
No specific antiviralNo approved direct antiviral for wild-type measles in immunocompetent hosts. Supportive care: antipyretics, oral rehydration, vitamin A. Prevention via universal MMR vaccination is overwhelmingly more effective than any treatment-based strategy.
