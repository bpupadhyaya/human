Overview

Arginine vasopressin (AVP), also called antidiuretic hormone (ADH), is a 9-amino-acid cyclic peptide synthesised in magnocellular neurons of the hypothalamic supraoptic nucleus (SON) and paraventricular nucleus (PVN). It is packaged with its carrier protein neurophysin II and transported axonally to the posterior pituitary for storage and release. It is also synthesised in parvocellular PVN neurons, where it acts as a CRH co-secretagogue for ACTH release.

AVP is the master regulator of water homeostasis. Its release is controlled primarily by plasma osmolality (detected by circumventricular osmoreceptors, threshold ~280–285 mOsm/kg) and secondarily by blood volume/pressure (detected by baroreceptors — volume depletion >10% triggers a steep AVP rise). The two inputs are integrated in the SON/PVN and modulated by angiotensin II, nausea (potent stimulus), and endocannabinoids. Release follows a steep dose-response: 1% rise in osmolality → 1 pg/mL AVP → ~500-fold concentration of urine.

Structure & Receptors

Vasopressin sequence: Cys¹-Tyr²-Phe³-Gln⁴-Asn⁵-Cys⁶-Pro⁷-Arg⁸-Gly⁹-NH₂ (C-terminal amide). A disulfide bond between Cys1–Cys6 forms the cyclic ring essential for bioactivity. Oxytocin differs at positions 3 (Ile for Phe) and 8 (Leu for Arg), with remarkably different receptor selectivity despite near-identical sequences.

  1. Plasma hyperosmolality detected by intrinsic osmosensitive neurons in SON/OVLT (organum vasculosum laminae terminalis) — mechanosensitive Trpv1/Trpv4 channels; cell shrinkage activates firing → AVP release.
  2. AVP reaches renal collecting duct basolateral V2R → Gαs → cAMP → PKA → phosphorylates AQP2 at Ser256 in cytoplasmic tail → AQP2-containing vesicles (subapical pool) fuse with apical membrane.
  3. AQP2 at apical membrane creates a transcellular water channel; osmotic gradient drives water from hypotonic urine into hypertonic medullary interstitium → AQP3/AQP4 at basolateral side returns water to capillary.
  4. Sustained V2R stimulation → PKA → CREB phosphorylation → CRE elements in AQP2 promoter → new AQP2 synthesis over 12–24 h.
  5. V2R also stimulates: NKCC2 in thick ascending limb (maintains medullary hypertonicity for countercurrent multiplication); ENaC in cortical collecting duct (Na⁺ reabsorption); urea transporter UT-A1 (inner medullary collecting duct — allows urea accumulation, enhances concentrating mechanism).
  6. V1aR stimulation: Gαq → PLC-β → IP₃ → ER Ca²⁺ + DAG → PKC → MLCK → VSM contraction → peripheral vasoconstriction; at pharmacological doses used in vasodilatory shock.

Pharmacology & Clinical Use

Desmopressin (DDAVP) — synthetic AVP analogue: deaminated at Cys1 + D-Arg8 → V2-selective (no V1a vasoconstriction), prolonged action (~8–24 h). Indications: central diabetes insipidus (intranasal 10–40 μg or oral 0.1–0.4 mg); primary nocturnal enuresis (oral); von Willebrand disease type 1 (releases vWF + FVIII from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies via V2R/cAMP); haemophilia A mild cases (avoids exogenous factor).

Vasopressin (Vasostrict) — non-selective V1a+V2R agonist; used in septic shock as a norepinephrine-sparing agent (0.03–0.04 U/min IV); hepatorenal syndrome (with albumin and midodrine); refractory variceal bleeding (splanchnic V1a vasoconstriction); cardiac arrest (ACLS protocol — limited evidence vs epinephrine).

Terlipressin — V1a-preferring prodrug (glycyl-lysine-vasopressin); hepatorenal syndrome type 1: dramatically reduces creatinine and 90-day mortality (CONFIRM trial); also oesophageal variceal haemorrhage.

Vaptans (V2R antagonists) — tolvaptan (oral), conivaptan (IV): block V2R → aquaresis (electrolyte-free water excretion, unlike diuretics). Indications: euvolaemic hyponatraemia (SIADH) and hypervolaemic hyponatraemia (heart failure, cirrhosis); contraindicated in liver disease (tolvaptan hepatotoxicity). Tolvaptan (Jynarque) specifically slows PKD progression by blocking V2R-cAMP-driven cyst growth in ADPKD.

Clinical pearl: Central DI (AVP deficiency) vs nephrogenic DI (V2R/AQP2 defect) can be distinguished by water deprivation test followed by desmopressin: central DI responds (urine osmolality rises >50%), nephrogenic DI does not. Copeptin (C-terminal AVP precursor, more stable in plasma) is increasingly used as a surrogate for AVP measurement — copeptin + osmolality threshold can diagnose DI and SIADH rapidly.

Connections

AVP is co-released with oxytocin from the posterior pituitary (structurally homologous neuropeptides). V1bR on α-cells amplifies glucagon release during stress. Angiotensin II (RAAS) is a powerful stimulus for AVP release and has overlapping vasoconstrictor effects — together they defend perfusion pressure. Aldosterone (downstream of RAAS) retains Na⁺ in the collecting duct while AVP retains water — complementary volume defence. Natriuretic peptides (ANP/BNP) antagonise AVP action and suppress its release.
