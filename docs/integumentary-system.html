Integumentary System

Skin, hair, nails, and glands — ~1.5–2.0 m² and ~4 kg of living barrier that simultaneously defends against pathogens, regulates temperature, synthesises vitamin D, transduces sensory input, and heals wounds through a precisely choreographed four-phase programme.

The largest organ by surface area. The epidermis renews completely every ~28 days. Barrier integrity connects to systemic immune tone — filaggrin deficiency triggers the atopic march from eczema through asthma to rhinitis.

Integumentary System

The integumentary system encompasses the skin and its derivatives — hair follicles, sebaceous glands, eccrine and apocrine sweat glands, and nails. As the body's interface with the environment, it performs functions unmatched in breadth: physical barrier (stratum corneum brick-and-mortar; TEWL <5 g/m²/h), chemical barrier (acid mantle pH ~5.5, AMPs, skin microbiome), immune surveillance (Langerhans cells, dermal DCs, mast cells), thermoregulation (eccrine sweating + cutaneous vasomotion handles up to 60% of cardiac output in heat), vitamin D photosynthesis (UVB 290–320 nm + 7-DHC → pre-D₃), and wound healing (haemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodelling).

Overview

The critical insight about skin is that it is not a passive physical wall. The epidermis actively manufactures a multi-layered impermeability barrier — the cornified envelope, lipid lamellae (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids), and tight junctions — whose integrity requires ongoing LGR5/KRT5+ stem cell renewal and keratinocyte differentiation every ~28 days. Simultaneously, the skin houses a dense resident immune network that is constantly sampling environmental antigens and calibrating systemic immune tone.

The skin is the site of the atopic march — the epidemiological sequence of atopic dermatitis → food allergy → allergic asthma → allergic rhinitis — driven by filaggrin (FLG) barrier defects releasing epithelial alarmins (TSLP, IL-25, IL-33) that polarise systemic immunity toward Th2. This makes skin biology central to understanding the global allergy epidemic.

Epidermal Layers (Superficial to Deep)

LayerKey features
Stratum corneum15–30 layers of dead anucleate corneocytes embedded in lipid lamellae (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids). Primary permeability barrier. Transepidermal water loss (TEWL) <5 g/m²/h normal; ↑↑ in eczema, psoriasis, burns.
Stratum lucidumPresent only in thick skin (palms, soles). Densely packed homogeneous cells.
Stratum granulosumKeratohyalin granules (filaggrin, loricrin, involucrin → cornified cell envelope). Lamellar body exocytosis → lipid lamellae. Tight junctions (claudin-1/4) — inner permeability barrier.
Stratum spinosumKRT1/KRT10. Abundant desmosomes (desmoglein 1/3 — target of pemphigus antibodies). Langerhans cells reside here — CD1a+, CD207+/langerin+, MHCII+.
Stratum basaleProliferating basal keratinocytes (KRT5/KRT14, Ki67+). Hemidesmosomes (integrin α6β4 → laminin-332 → basement membrane). Melanocytes (1:10 ratio; melanin = UV shield). Merkel cells (mechanoreceptors; neuroendocrine).

Dermis and Skin Appendages

Papillary dermis

Thin, loose connective tissue; dermal papillae interdigitating with epidermal rete ridges → fingerprints; type III collagen, fine elastic fibres; capillary loops; Meissner corpuscles (rapidly adapting, discriminative touch, fingers/lips).

Reticular dermis

Dense irregular connective tissue; type I collagen along Langer's lines; coarse elastin; fibroblasts secreting collagens + hyaluronic acid; mast cells (IgE-armed); Pacinian corpuscles (vibration); Ruffini endings (stretch); free nerve endings (pain, temperature [TRPV1/TRPM8], itch [TRPA1]).

Eccrine sweat glands

2–4 million; densest on palms, soles, forehead. Cholinergic (M3) innervation under hypothalamic thermoregulatory control. Primary isotonic secretion → duct reabsorbs NaCl (ENaC + CFTR) → hypotonic sweat. Capacity: 1–2 L/h/m² at maximum heat stress.

Sebaceous glands and hair follicles

Sebaceous glands: androgen-sensitive (testosterone → DHT via 5α-reductase → sebaceous hyperplasia → acne). Sebum: antimicrobial fatty acids, acid mantle pH ~5.5. Hair cycles: anagen (2–7 yr scalp) → catagen (2–3 wk) → telogen (3 mo), controlled by WNT/BMP, IGF-1, thyroid hormone, androgens.

Key Functions

Physical barrier: Stratum corneum brick-and-mortar structure (corneocytes + lipid lamellae) creates a tortuous diffusion path. TEWL increases dramatically with filaggrin loss, burns, and psoriasis (rapid turnover). Skin surface pH ~5.5 (acid mantle) inhibits pathogen colonisation.

Thermoregulation: Preoptic nucleus of anterior hypothalamus detects rising core temperature → sympathetic cholinergic fibres → eccrine sweating (evaporative cooling: ~580 kcal/L evaporated) + cutaneous vasodilation (AV anastomoses — glomus bodies in fingertips, toes, nose). At maximum heat load, up to 60% of cardiac output redirected to skin. Cold → sympathetic vasoconstriction → ↓heat loss; piloerection.

Vitamin D synthesis: UVB photons (290–320 nm) → 7-dehydrocholesterol → pre-vitamin D₃ → vitamin D₃ → liver (25-OH-D₃) → kidney (1,25(OH)₂D₃/calcitriol) → VDR → ↑intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption, ↑muscle function, immune modulation. Synthesis declines with age, high latitude, dark skin, and sun avoidance.

Sensory transduction: ~1 million cutaneous sensory receptors of five mechanoreceptor classes transduce discriminative touch (Meissner), vibration (Pacinian), sustained pressure (Merkel), stretch (Ruffini), and pain/temperature/itch (free nerve endings via TRPV1/TRPA1/TRPM8).

Wound Healing — Four Overlapping Phases

  PHASE 1 — HAEMOSTASIS (0–2 h)
  Vessel injury → vasoconstriction (TXA₂)
  → platelet adhesion (collagen → vWF → GpIb-IX-V →
    GpIIb/IIIa → fibrin clot + platelet plug)
  → PDGF, TGF-β, EGF from α-granules → recruit repair cells

  PHASE 2 — INFLAMMATION (days 0–7)
  Neutrophils (day 0–3): débridement, NET release
  Macrophages (day 3+):
    M1 phase: IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, CXCL8 → kill bacteria
    M2 phase (day 5+): TGF-β, VEGF, PDGF, IGF-1 → repair

  PHASE 3 — PROLIFERATION (days 3–21)
  Re-epithelialisation: basal keratinocytes migrate across
    fibrin matrix (↓E-cadherin, integrin switch to αvβ6)
  Fibroplasia: fibroblasts → type III collagen (granulation);
    myofibroblasts (α-SMA+, TGF-β-driven) → wound contraction
  Angiogenesis: VEGF-A/C → VEGFR2 → new capillaries

  PHASE 4 — REMODELLING (weeks–years)
  Type III → type I collagen (MMPs/TIMPs)
  ↓cellularity, ↓vascularity → white avascular scar
  Pathological: keloid (excess TGF-β, extends beyond margins)
    or hypertrophic scar (within margins)

Immunological Barrier — The Atopic March

The skin is a primary immunological organ. The three canonical epithelial alarmins — TSLP, IL-25 (IL-17E), and IL-33 — are released by barrier-disrupted keratinocytes and activate ILC2s, dendritic cells, and mast cells to initiate type 2 immune responses:

  Filaggrin (FLG) loss-of-function mutation (chr 1q21)
           │ ↑TEWL; ↑allergen penetration
           ▼
  Keratinocyte TSLP + IL-33 + IL-25 release (alarmins)
           │
           ▼
  ILC2 activation + LC/DC maturation + mast cell priming
           │
           ▼
  Th2 polarisation → IL-4/IL-13 (→ ↓FLG, vicious cycle)
                   → IL-31 (itch via IL-31RA on DRG neurons)
                   → ↑IgE → mast cell sensitisation
           │
           ▼
  ATOPIC MARCH:
  Atopic Dermatitis (infancy)
    → Food allergy (toddler)
      → Allergic asthma (childhood)
        → Allergic rhinitis (adolescent/adult)

Langerhans cells form a continuous antigen-surveillance network in the stratum spinosum. They are responsible for allergic contact sensitisation (e.g., nickel, poison ivy urushiol → Th1/Th17 contact hypersensitivity). On activation they migrate via dermal lymphatics to draining lymph nodes to prime naïve T cells.

Pathology

Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

FLG loss-of-function mutations (15–20% of children, 7–10% adults) weaken barrier → ↑TSLP/IL-33/IL-25 → Th2 → IL-4/IL-13 (↓FLG further, vicious cycle) + IL-31 (itch via DRG IL-31RA/OSMR). Standard: topical corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus). Biologics: dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα → blocks IL-4 + IL-13 simultaneously). JAK inhibitors: baricitinib, upadacitinib (JAK1/2-STAT6).

Psoriasis

Chronic immune-mediated disease; Th17 cells + IL-17A → keratinocyte IL-17RA → NF-κB → AMPs + chemokines → neutrophils → Munro microabscesses. Epidermis renews in 4 days instead of 28 → plaques. Systemic: psoriatic arthritis (30%), ↑CVD risk. Therapy: topical corticosteroids/vitamin D analogues → NB-UVB → methotrexate/acitretin/ciclosporin → anti-TNF (adalimumab), anti-IL-17A (secukinumab, ixekizumab), anti-IL-23 (risankizumab).

Melanoma

UV-induced DNA damage → BRAF V600E (~50%), NRAS (~20%), NF1 (~15%), CDKN2A, TERT mutations. Targeted therapy: BRAF/MEK inhibitors (dabrafenib + trametinib). Immunotherapy: anti-CTLA4 (ipilimumab) + anti-PD-1 (nivolumab) → ~50% ORR; ~40% 5-year OS metastatic. Sentinel node biopsy for staging. ABCDE criteria: asymmetry, border irregularity, colour variation, diameter >6 mm, evolution.

Burns

Depth classification: superficial (epidermal, heals 3–5 days), partial-thickness superficial (papillary dermis, heals 7–21 days), partial-thickness deep (reticular dermis, often needs grafting), full-thickness (all layers, always grafts required). Large burns (>20% TBSA): massive fluid shifts → Parkland formula (4 mL × weight kg × %TBSA in 24 h), hypermetabolic state (↑cortisol/catecholamines), immunosuppression → sepsis (Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus, Candida).

Basal and Squamous Cell Carcinoma

BCC (~3 million/year USA): UV + PTCH1 loss → constitutive Hedgehog/Smoothened → locally invasive, rarely metastasises. Vismodegib/sonidegib (Smo inhibitors) for advanced. SCC: UV-induced TP53/TP63 mutations; risk: immunosuppression, HPV-16/18, chronic wounds. Anti-PD-1 (cemiplimab/pembrolizumab) for advanced/metastatic SCC.
