NCX1 — Na⁺/Ca²⁺ Exchanger

NCX1 (gene SLC8A1) — the cardiac sarcolemmal Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger. In forward mode it extrudes one Ca²⁺ by exchanging 3 Na⁺ inward, removing ~28% of cytosolic Ca²⁺ per beat. In reverse mode (Na⁺ overload, depolarized Vm) it brings Ca²⁺ into the cell. Also generates a depolarizing inward current that contributes to the Ca²⁺-clock pacemaker mechanism in SA node cells.

Gene: SLC8A1 Aliases: NCX1, cardiac Na/Ca exchanger, sodium-calcium exchanger 1 Scale: 03-Molecular Status: Draft · 2026-06-05

Overview

NCX1 (Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger 1, encoded by SLC8A1) is the major sarcolemmal Ca²⁺ transport protein of cardiomyocytes, responsible for approximately 28% of cytosolic Ca²⁺ removal following each systole in human ventricular myocytes (with SERCA2a handling ~70%). It is electrogenic: each cycle exchanges 3 Na⁺ for 1 Ca²⁺, generating a net charge movement of +1 per cycle. The direction of ion flow is determined by the electrochemical driving forces for Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ and the membrane potential.

NCX1 serves three distinct roles in cardiac physiology: (1) forward mode Ca²⁺ extrusion during diastole (primary role; contributes to cardiac relaxation); (2) reverse mode Ca²⁺ entry during action potential phase 2 and in ischemia (a pathological contribution when Na⁺ is elevated); and (3) depolarizing inward current (INCX) that contributes to the Ca²⁺ clock of SA node pacemaker cells via NCX operating in forward mode after spontaneous SR Ca²⁺ sparks.

Ischemia paradox: In ischemia, [Na⁺]i rises (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is inhibited by ATP depletion), driving NCX into reverse mode and loading the cell with Ca²⁺ — contributing to ischemic Ca²⁺ overload, contracture, and cell death. This is one mechanism why Na⁺ overload amplifies ischemic injury.

Regulatory Ca²⁺-Binding Domains

The large intracellular f-loop contains two Ca²⁺-binding domains: CBD1 (high-affinity primary Ca²⁺ sensor; binds Ca²⁺ with Kd ~0.2 µM; allosteric activation of exchanger activity) and CBD2 (lower affinity; modulates regulatory response). Ca²⁺ binding to CBD1/CBD2 relieves secondary Na⁺-dependent inactivation and promotes NCX activity — an autoactivation mechanism ensuring NCX operates maximally when cytosolic Ca²⁺ is elevated (i.e., during systole, when the exchanger is most needed).

Mechanism

Bidirectional Transport and Driving Forces

── NCX1 — bidirectional operation ──────────────────────── FORWARD MODE (normal Ca²⁺ extrusion during relaxation) [Na⁺]i = ~10 mM [Na⁺]o = ~140 mM → strong Na⁺ inward gradient [Ca²⁺]i = ~1 µM (systole) → ~100 nM [Ca²⁺]o = ~1.2 mM Vm = −85 mV (diastole, below E_NCX) ───────────────────────────────────────────────── 3 Na⁺ IN + 1 Ca²⁺ OUT → net +1 charge inward → small inward current Extrudes ~28% of Ca²⁺ per beat in human ventricle REVERSE MODE (Ca²⁺ entry — physiological brief, pathological in ischemia) Vm depolarized (phase 0–2, or ischemia + Na⁺ overload) [Na⁺]i elevated (ischemia: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase fails → [Na⁺]i rises to 20–30 mM) ───────────────────────────────────────────────── 3 Na⁺ OUT + 1 Ca²⁺ IN → net −1 charge outward Loads cytosol with Ca²⁺ → Ca²⁺ overload → contracture / cell death CA²⁺ CLOCK ROLE (SA node pacemaker cells) Spontaneous SR Ca²⁺ spark during late diastole ↓ [Ca²⁺]i rises locally ↓ NCX1 forward mode activated → 3 Na⁺ in, 1 Ca²⁺ out ↓ Net inward current → additional late-diastolic depolarization ↓ Assists in triggering next action potential (Ca²⁺ clock contribution)

Interaction with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase

NCX1 activity is critically dependent on the Na⁺ gradient maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (NKA). NKA pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP, keeping [Na⁺]i at ~10 mM. When NKA is inhibited (cardiac glycosides like digoxin, or ischemia), [Na⁺]i rises, reducing the Na⁺ gradient driving NCX forward mode — reducing Ca²⁺ extrusion and elevating [Ca²⁺]i. This is the mechanistic basis of digoxin's positive inotropic effect (mild [Na⁺]i rise → less NCX extrusion → higher [Ca²⁺]i → greater SR Ca²⁺ load → stronger contraction).
