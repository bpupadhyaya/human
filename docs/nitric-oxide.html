Overview

Nitric oxide (NO) is a diatomic free radical gas that serves as a potent biological signalling molecule. It was identified as the endothelium-derived relaxing factor (EDRF) by Furchgott, Ignarro, and Murad — work recognised by the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Unlike classical signalling molecules, NO is not stored in vesicles but synthesised on demand from L-arginine by nitric oxide synthase (NOS) enzymes, freely diffuses through cell membranes (lipophilic diradical), and acts locally within its brief biological half-life (~1–5 seconds in tissue; rapidly quenched by reaction with oxyhemoglobin to form metHb + nitrate).

Three NOS isoforms with distinct biology: eNOS (NOS3, endothelial) — constitutive, Ca²⁺/CaM-dependent, generates low-level "physiological" NO for vascular tone; nNOS (NOS1, neuronal) — constitutive, Ca²⁺/CaM-dependent, synaptic and central NO production; iNOS (NOS2, inducible) — Ca²⁺-independent, NFκB-driven, generates high-level "pathological" NO in inflammation (macrophages, hepatocytes). All three require the cofactor BH4 (tetrahydrobiopterin) — BH4 deficiency uncouples eNOS → superoxide production instead of NO.

  1. eNOS: L-arginine → L-citrulline + NO (5 electron oxidation); electrons flow from NADPH through FAD → FMN (reductase domain) across calmodulin bridge to heme (oxygenase domain); O₂ activated and transferred to L-Arg guanidinium N.
  2. BH4 critical: stabilises eNOS oxygenase dimer; electron source for O₂ activation; BH4 oxidation to BH2 → eNOS "uncoupling" → superoxide (O₂•⁻) instead of NO; seen in hypertension, diabetes, ageing; folic acid and sepiapterin can replete BH4.
  3. sGC: heterodimer (α+β); haem Fe²⁺ in β-subunit binds NO → 5-coordinate NO-Fe complex → breaks His105 bond → activates catalytic site; riociguat (sGC stimulator) and vericiguat (sGC stimulator) can activate sGC independently of NO or sensitise to NO.
  4. PKG: activated by cGMP → phosphorylates MLCP regulatory subunit (MYPT1) activating phosphatase; phosphorylates BKCa channel Cβ1 subunit (K⁺ efflux → hyperpolarisation); phosphorylates phospholamban → SERCA relief → Ca²⁺ re-uptake.
  5. PDE5 (phosphodiesterase 5) degrades cGMP in VSM and corpus cavernosum; sildenafil/tadalafil/vardenafil inhibit PDE5 → cGMP accumulates → sustained erection (penile sinusoidal smooth muscle relaxation) or pulmonary vasodilation.
  6. iNOS: calmodulin permanently bound even at low Ca²⁺; NFκB drives transcription within 4–6 h of LPS/cytokine; produces 100–1000× more NO than eNOS; combined with NADPH-oxidase superoxide → peroxynitrite ONOO⁻ — nitrating agent causing tissue damage and pathogen killing.

Pharmacology & Clinical Use

Nitrates (nitroglycerin, isosorbide mononitrate) — prodrugs bioactivated by ALDH2 (mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase) to NO → sGC → cGMP → venodilation (↓preload) and coronary vasodilation. Tolerance develops with continuous use (BH4 depletion, ALDH2 downregulation, peroxynitrite) — patch-free intervals prevent tolerance. Sublingual GTN for acute angina onset; oral isosorbide for prophylaxis.

Inhaled NO (iNO) — 5–40 ppm inhaled selective pulmonary vasodilator; rapidly inactivated by hemoglobin in pulmonary circulation → no systemic hypotension. Indications: PPHN (persistent pulmonary hypertension of newborn), ARDS-refractory hypoxaemia (improves V/Q matching), post-cardiac surgery right heart failure.

PDE5 inhibitors — sildenafil (Viagra, Revatio), tadalafil (Cialis, Adcirca), vardenafil, avanafil: ↑cGMP in VSM by blocking cGMP breakdown; licensed for ED and PAH; tadalafil also for benign prostatic hyperplasia (urethral/bladder neck smooth muscle). Not in nitrate users (synergistic hypotension risk).

sGC stimulators/activators — riociguat (Adempas): stimulates sGC independently of NO and sensitises it to low NO; approved for PAH and CTEPH. Vericiguat (Verquvo): sGC stimulator for HFrEF — improves outcomes in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

eNOS enhancers — statins (upregulate eNOS by stabilising eNOS mRNA), exercise (shear stress), ACE inhibitors (bradykinin → endothelial Ca²⁺ → eNOS), omega-3 FAs — pleiotropic NO-augmenting mechanisms.

Clinical pearl: iNOS-derived NO is central to septic shock vasodilation — massive NFκB activation → iNOS in vascular smooth muscle → profound vasoplegia refractory to catecholamines. Low-dose vasopressin (0.03 U/min) is used as a vasopressor-sparing catecholamine adjunct in septic shock partly by acting independently of NO/sGC. Non-selective NOS inhibition (L-NAME) trials in sepsis increased mortality — some NO is essential for organ perfusion.

Connections

NO and PGI₂ cooperate in vascular homeostasis (both vasodilate; both inhibit platelet aggregation; both are lost in atherosclerotic endothelial dysfunction). NO inhibits COX-1/2 via S-nitrosylation in some contexts, creating regulatory cross-talk. Hemoglobin rapidly scavenges NO (oxyHb + NO → nitrate + metHb) — erythrocyte NOS (eNOS in RBCs) and SNO-Hb may serve as local NO buffer/delivery system. AMPK phosphorylates and activates eNOS at Ser1177 — a mechanism linking energy sensing to vascular tone. Leptin stimulates eNOS in endothelium but excess ADMA (asymmetric dimethylarginine) in obesity competitively inhibits NOS.
