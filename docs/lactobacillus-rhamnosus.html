Lactobacillus rhamnosus

The most clinically studied probiotic bacterium in the world; strain LGG (ATCC 53103) has been tested in >300 clinical trials across antibiotic-associated diarrhoea, childhood eczema, and rotavirus diarrhoea prevention.

Long-chain SpaCBA pili mediate tight mucus adhesion enabling prolonged gut residence; the only lactobacillus with robust randomised trial evidence (NNT ~7 for AAD prevention) accumulated over 35 years of research.

Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG

Gram-positive, acid-tolerant probiotic bacterium with exceptional mucus adhesion via SpaCBA sortase-assembled pili; produces lactic acid, acetate, and bioactive peptides that competitively exclude pathogens, modulate mucosal immunity, and reduce gut permeability; the reference strain for evidence-based probiotic medicine with RCT-proven efficacy in antibiotic-associated diarrhoea, paediatric infectious diarrhoea, and atopic eczema prevention.

Mechanisms of Benefit

  • Competitive exclusion & colonisation resistance SpaCBA pili anchor LGG firmly to intestinal mucus, occupying attachment sites that enteropathogens (Salmonella, E. coli, C. difficile, rotavirus) require for colonisation. Lactic acid production lowers local pH to <5; combined with hydrogen peroxide and bacteriocin production, this creates a hostile microenvironment for pathogen establishment. LGG also competes for nutrients (glucose, carbohydrates) in the mucus niche.
  • Gut barrier reinforcement LGG supernatant activates EGF receptor → PI3K/Akt → Rac1 signalling in colonocytes, promoting tight junction reassembly after cytokine-mediated disruption. Reduces paracellular permeability (measured by TEER) in Caco-2 models. In vivo: LGG reduces LPS translocation and serum zonulin (leaky gut marker) in clinical trials of antibiotic-treated patients and post-surgical patients.
  • Immunomodulation — Th1 skewing & IgA induction LGG cell wall components (LTA, peptidoglycan) and secreted factors activate TLR2 and NOD2 on DCs and macrophages → IL-12 and IL-18 production → Th1 polarisation (IFN-γ↑, IL-4↓). Enhances secretory IgA production in gut; SIgA coats pathogens, neutralising them in the lumen before mucosal invasion. IgA induction particularly relevant for rotavirus and respiratory virus prevention.
  • Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea (AAD) prevention mechanism Antibiotics disrupt indigenous microbiota → overgrowth of diarrhoea-causing species (C. difficile, Klebsiella, Staphylococcus) → diarrhoea. LGG taken concurrently: (1) partially resists antibiotic killing (intrinsic resistance to erythromycin/ampicillin); (2) competes with C. difficile for intestinal niche; (3) lactocepin degrades CXCL10 attracting inflammatory cells to mucosa; (4) accelerates microbiota recovery post-antibiotic. Meta-analysis (n=12,494): RR 0.58, NNT ~7 for AAD prevention.
  • Atopic eczema prevention in infants Landmark ALLERGYFLORA trials (Kalliomäki 2001, Kuitunen 2009): LGG (10⁹ CFU) given to mothers during last 4 weeks of pregnancy + to newborns for 6 months → 50% reduction in eczema incidence at 2 years in at-risk infants. Mechanism: maternal LGG alters breast milk cytokine/IgA composition; infant LGG colonisation shifts Th2-biased neonatal immunity toward balanced Th1/Th2/Treg; reduces intestinal permeability reducing allergen sensitisation.

Research & Formulation Status

  • Commercial formulations LGG (ATCC 53103) marketed as Culturelle (USA), Valio LGG (Finland), and dozens of generic preparations at 10⁹–10¹⁰ CFU doses. Recommended dose for AAD: 10¹⁰ CFU/day during antibiotic course + 7 days post-completion. Refrigerated and lyophilised (shelf-stable) formats available. Note: strain identification matters — generic "Lactobacillus rhamnosus" products without LGG strain designation may lack SpaCBA pili and have different efficacy profiles.
  • LGG JB-1 and psychobiotic research Separately, L. rhamnosus JB-1 (a different strain) has shown anxiolytic and antidepressant-like effects in mice via vagus nerve-dependent GABA receptor modulation (Foster lab, 2011). Human trials have not fully replicated the anxiolytic effect; brain–gut microbiome research continues. JB-1 ≠ LGG in mechanisms or clinical indications.
  • Safety in immunocompromised patients Rare cases (<0.05%) of LGG bacteraemia in immunocompromised patients (haematopoietic transplant, premature neonates, central line users). Standard guidance: avoid in immunosuppressed patients and preterm neonates <32 weeks gestation or <1500g birthweight without physician approval. Safe in healthy adults and term infants.
