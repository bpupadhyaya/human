Immunoglobulin G

The most abundant serum antibody — a Y-shaped 150 kDa glycoprotein (2 heavy + 2 light chains) and the primary mediator of long-term humoral immunity, vaccine-elicited protection, and placental passive immunity.

IgG circulates at 10–15 mg/mL (~75% of all serum immunoglobulins), persists for ~21 days via FcRn-mediated endosomal recycling, and mediates protection through antigen neutralization, opsonization, complement activation, and ADCC. Four subclasses (IgG1–4) differ in Fc effector functions. The goal of virtually every modern vaccine is to elicit high-affinity, durable IgG against the target antigen.

Atlas One · Molecular · Antibody / Immune Effector

Immunoglobulin G (IgG)

Class: Antibody (immunoglobulin)  ·  Humoral immune effector  |  Encoded by: IGH locus (chr 14q32) + IGK/IGL loci  |  Produced by: Plasma cells (terminally differentiated B cells)

Immunoglobulin G is the central molecular effector of adaptive humoral immunity. Secreted by long-lived plasma cells in the bone marrow, IgG circulates at concentrations of ~10–15 mg/mL in healthy adults and mediates protection against pathogens through four primary mechanisms: neutralization (Fab arms block receptor-binding domains), opsonization (Fc tail recruits FcγR on macrophages and neutrophils), complement activation (IgG1/IgG3 bind C1q → classical pathway → MAC), and ADCC (Fc–FcγRIII engagement on NK cells → perforin/granzyme release → killing of antibody-coated targets). Its extraordinarily long half-life (~21 days) is maintained by FcRn-mediated rescue from lysosomal degradation — the same pathway that extends the half-life of albumin and of every therapeutic monoclonal antibody in clinical use.

Structure — The Y-Shaped Tetramer

IgG is a tetrameric glycoprotein consisting of two identical heavy chains (γ-chains, ~50 kDa each) and two identical light chains (κ or λ, ~25 kDa each), joined by disulfide bonds and non-covalent interactions. The total molecular weight is ~150 kDa. Its Y-shaped architecture reflects three functional regions:

The Asn297 glycan — essential for effector function

A single N-linked oligosaccharide at Asn297 of each CH2 domain is required for Fc effector functions. The glycan composition (sialylation, fucosylation, galactosylation) modulates C1q binding, FcγR affinity, and the pro- vs. anti-inflammatory balance of IgG. Afucosylated IgG has ~50-fold higher affinity for FcγRIII on NK cells and macrophages, massively amplifying ADCC — this is the basis of afucosylated therapeutic antibodies (mogamulizumab, obinutuzumab).

FcRn — the half-life recycling receptor

The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) binds IgG Fc at histidines 310 and 435 of each CH2–CH3 interface at acidic pH (~6.0) in endosomes of vascular endothelial cells and macrophages. This rescues IgG from lysosomal degradation; at neutral pH at the cell surface, lower affinity releases IgG back into circulation. IgG not recycled is degraded, limiting the half-life from the theoretical ~200+ days to ~21 days. Fc engineering (YTE, LS, M428L/N434S mutations) enhances FcRn binding affinity 5–10-fold → extended half-life to ~4–6 weeks for therapeutic antibodies (e.g., nivolumab-YTE).

Pharmacology / Clinical Use

Therapeutic monoclonal antibodies (mAbs): Modern mAbs are almost exclusively IgG1 or IgG4 format. IgG1 is chosen when ADCC/complement killing is desired (rituximab in B-cell lymphoma; trastuzumab in HER2+ breast cancer; nivolumab in cancer immunotherapy). IgG4 is chosen when effector functions are undesired (sarilumab anti-IL-6R; dupilumab anti-IL-4Rα — blocking cytokine signalling without cytotoxicity of the signalling cell).

Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG): Pooled IgG from thousands of donors (~10–15 g/L protein, >95% IgG); used for: primary immunodeficiencies (replacement therapy), Kawasaki disease (prevents coronary aneurysms; mechanism unclear but FcγRIIB inhibitory receptor engagement is hypothesised), Guillain-Barré syndrome (anti-idiotypic neutralization of pathogenic autoantibodies), immune thrombocytopenia (ITP: FcRn saturation → accelerates clearance of anti-platelet IgG autoantibodies).

Subcutaneous immunoglobulin (SCIG): Alternative to IVIG for primary antibody deficiency; 10–20% IgG solutions; better quality of life; home administration; comparable efficacy; preferred in European centres.

IgG as vaccine correlate of protection: For essentially every approved vaccine, the correlate of protection is a threshold serum IgG titer against the pathogen target antigen (e.g., anti-HBs ≥10 mIU/mL for hepatitis B; anti-Hib polysaccharide ≥0.15 μg/mL; anti-SARS-CoV-2 spike RBD IgG ≥264 BAU/mL for 50% protection against symptomatic COVID-19). mRNA vaccines (BNT162b2, mRNA-1273) elicit peak serum IgG titers 2–4 weeks after completion of primary series, with geometric mean titers declining ~6-fold over 6 months, then stabilising — maintained by long-lived plasma cells in bone marrow niches.
