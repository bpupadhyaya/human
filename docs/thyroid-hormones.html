Overview

Thyroid hormones (THs) — thyroxine (T4, tetraiodothyronine) and 3,5,3'-triiodothyronine (T3) — are iodinated tyrosine derivatives synthesised in thyroid follicular cells. T4 is the primary secretory product (~93% of thyroid output) but is a prohormone with relatively low receptor affinity. Peripheral deiodination by selenoenzymes DIO1 and DIO2 converts T4 to the biologically active T3, which binds thyroid hormone receptors (TRα, TRβ) with ~10–15× higher affinity. DIO3 inactivates both T4 and T3 by 5-deiodination to rT3 (reverse T3) and T2.

T3 and T4 regulate a vast range of physiological processes by binding to TRα/TRβ nuclear receptors, which dimerize on thyroid hormone response elements (TREs) in target gene promoters. TH increases basal metabolic rate (BMR) by inducing mitochondrial biogenesis, uncoupling protein UCP3, and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase expression. Thyroid function is precisely controlled by the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis — TRH → TSH → thyroid; negative feedback by T3/T4 occurs at both hypothalamic and pituitary levels.

Thyroid hormones are transported in plasma bound to thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG, ~70%), transthyretin (TTR, ~20%), and albumin (~10%). Only free T4 (~0.02%) and free T3 (~0.3%) are biologically active — "free" fractions measured in thyroid function tests.

  1. TRH is released from hypothalamic PVN in a pulsatile pattern; stimulated by cold (via NE from locus coeruleus), fasting, and leptin deficiency; inhibited by dopamine, somatostatin, T3/T4 negative feedback.
  2. TSH binds TSHR on thyroid follicular cells → Gαs → cAMP → PKA → phosphorylates NIS, DUOX2, TPO → ↑iodide organification and TG proteolysis → T3/T4 secretion within hours.
  3. T4 has 7-day plasma half-life (TBG-bound); DIO2 in target tissues provides local T3 — important in brain (local conversion ensures adequate T3 despite circulating T4; DIO2 upregulated in hypothyroidism).
  4. T3 binds TRβ1/TRβ2 (dominant in liver, pituitary, cochlea, retina) or TRα1 (dominant in heart, muscle, bone); TR-RXR heterodimer on TRE shifts from corepressor to coactivator complex upon T3 binding.
  5. Non-genomic T3/T4 actions: αvβ3 integrin receptor (cell surface) → ERK/MAPK → PI3K → rapid effects on SERCA, Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger, cytoskeletal changes (within minutes); important in cardiac and neuronal context.
  6. T3 induces mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α, upregulates uncoupling protein (UCP3) in skeletal muscle — the biochemical basis of heat generation and ↑BMR; increases O₂ consumption in all metabolically active tissues except brain, testes, spleen.

Pharmacology & Clinical Use

Levothyroxine (L-T4) — first-line hypothyroidism replacement; oral bioavailability ~80%; requires DIO2 conversion to T3; TSH titration target 0.4–4.0 mU/L; pregnancy target TSH 0.1–2.5; administered fasting 30–60 min before breakfast (calcium/iron/PPIs ↓absorption). Synthetic identical to human T4.

Liothyronine (L-T3) — short-acting T3; used in myxoedema coma (IV), T3/T4 combination therapy for residual symptoms in some patients; in thyroid cancer preparation for RAI scan (rapid hypothyroidism induction).

Thionamides (propylthiouracil, carbimazole/methimazole) — inhibit TPO → block organification of iodide → ↓TH synthesis; PTU also inhibits DIO1 (reduces T4→T3 conversion — useful in thyroid storm). Takes 4–8 weeks to deplete pre-formed TH. Used for Graves' disease, toxic nodules, pre-surgical/RAI preparation.

Radioactive iodine (¹³¹I) — concentrated by NIS in thyroid; emits β radiation → follicular cell destruction → hypothyroidism in 6–12 months; first-line in Graves' (US) and toxic nodular goitre; contraindicated in pregnancy, severe ophthalmopathy.

Thyroid hormone receptor β agonists — resmetirom (Rezdiffra): selective TRβ agonist; approved 2024 for NASH (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis) with liver fibrosis; ↓liver fat, ↓liver enzymes, ↓LDL-C without cardiac TRα effects (↑HR, AF). Major advance in MASH/NASH pharmacotherapy.

Clinical pearl: In subclinical hypothyroidism (elevated TSH, normal free T4), treat if TSH >10 mU/L, or TSH 4–10 with symptoms, dyslipidaemia, or pregnancy. Screening TSH: thyroid disease prevalence 5–10% in women >50. Sick euthyroid (non-thyroidal illness syndrome) — low T3 with high rT3, low/normal TSH — do NOT treat; the thyroid is normal; illness suppresses DIO1 and induces DIO3 as an adaptive response to reduce metabolic demand.

Connections

Thyroid hormones regulate cholesterol metabolism via TRβ (↑LDL receptor) — hypothyroidism is a major secondary cause of dyslipidaemia. T3 is permissive for leptin's full metabolic effects; hypothyroidism associates with leptin resistance. Thyroid hormones ↑AMPK activity in skeletal muscle (partially mediating ↑fatty acid oxidation). T3 is essential for full glucocorticoid and GH effects (permissive hormonal interactions). Selenium is required for DIO selenoenzymes — selenium deficiency in iodine-replete areas mimics hypothyroidism by impairing T4→T3 conversion.

Cholesterol (TRβ drives LDL receptor) AMPK (T3 activates skeletal muscle AMPK) Leptin (permissive for metabolic signalling) Glucagon (hepatic metabolic synergy) EPO (thyroid status affects erythropoiesis)
