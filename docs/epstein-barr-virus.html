Epstein-Barr Virus

The gammaherpesvirus that infects 95% of adults worldwide, transforms B cells through mimicry of CD40, BCR, and Notch signalling — and is causally linked to six distinct human malignancies.

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV; HHV-4) is the only known human lymphotropic herpesvirus with proven oncogenic potential. Primary infection causes infectious mononucleosis in adolescents; EBV then establishes lifelong latency in resting memory B cells with minimal gene expression. Under immunosuppression or in specific genetic/geographic contexts, EBV drives Burkitt lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, PTLD, EBV+ gastric carcinoma, and CAEBV. No approved antiviral has proven clinical efficacy.

Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV / HHV-4)

Enveloped dsDNA gammaherpesvirus with a ~170 kb genome encoding 80+ genes. Entry into B cells requires gp350/220 binding of CD21 (complement receptor 2); gHgL and gB mediate membrane fusion. The viral latency programme in newly infected B cells (Latency III) expresses 9 EBNAs + LMPs + EBERs + BART miRNAs — collectively mimicking constitutive CD40 (LMP1→NF-&kB;), BCR (LMP2A→PI3K/Akt), and Notch (EBNA-2→c-Myc) signalling to drive polyclonal B cell immortalisation. EBNA-1’s glycine-alanine repeat domain evades MHC-I presentation by resisting proteasomal degradation — enabling long-term immune escape. After the acute CTL response of infectious mononucleosis clears most infected blasts, EBV retreats to Latency 0 in resting memory B cells — a silent, lifelong reservoir. Reactivation under immunosuppression or oncogenic co-factors drives the spectrum of EBV-associated malignancies.

Infection Mechanism

1 · Entry into B cells via gp350/220 – CD21

gp350/220 (the most abundant envelope glycoprotein) binds CD21 (CR2; complement receptor 2) on B cells with high affinity; CD35 co-receptor stabilises initial attachment. The gHgL complex then engages an uncharacterised B cell surface receptor, triggering gB-mediated membrane fusion. The naked capsid is released into the cytoplasm, transported to the nuclear pore, and the linear dsDNA genome is injected into the nucleus, where it circularises into an episome.

2 · Epithelial cell entry — oropharyngeal replication and saliva shedding

EBV also infects oropharyngeal epithelial cells and salivary gland ductal cells. Here, entry proceeds via gHgL binding αv integrins (αvβ5, αvβ6, αvβ8) and BMRF2 binding β1/β5 integrins — distinct from the CD21 B cell route. Lytic replication in epithelial cells produces infectious virions shed into saliva, explaining the “kissing disease” transmission route and the lifelong asymptomatic salivary shedding in seropositive adults.

3 · Latency III — B cell immortalisation programme

Newly infected B cells enter Latency III (growth programme), expressing all 9 EBNAs, LMP1/2A/2B, EBERs, and BART miRNAs. This co-opts three key B cell activation pathways: EBNA-2 mimics Notch signalling (→ RBP-Jκ → c-Myc, cyclin D2, CD23); LMP1 constitutively activates CD40-TRAF signalling (→ NF-&kB; → Bcl-2, XIAP, anti-apoptotic programme); LMP2A mimics constitutive BCR signalling (→ Syk → PI3K/Akt → survival without antigen). The result is polyclonal blast transformation — proliferating B cell lymphoblasts that are the substrate of the disease in infectious mononucleosis.

4 · Latency 0 — the silent reservoir in resting memory B cells

Following resolution of infectious mononucleosis, the overwhelming CD8+ CTL response eliminates most Latency III blasts, forcing EBV into the silenced Latency 0 state in resting CD27+ memory B cells — expressing only BART miRNAs and no viral proteins detectable by immune surveillance. The EBV episome is replicated with the cell’s own DNA during mitosis, maintaining the latent reservoir indefinitely. Estimated frequency: 1–50 EBV+ B cells per 10⁶ peripheral blood B cells in healthy seropositive adults.

5 · EBNA-1 GA repeat — invisible to MHC-I surveillance

EBNA-1 contains a large glycine-alanine (GA) repeat domain (~800–1,800 bp) that confers remarkable resistance to ubiquitin-proteasome degradation, blocking the generation of peptides for MHC class I loading. EBNA-1 is the only protein expressed in Latency I (Burkitt lymphoma), and — precisely because it evades CTL recognition — it is the ideal protein for maintaining the episome with minimum immune cost. EBNA-1 expression is universal across all EBV-associated tumours.

6 · LMP1 — the six-transmembrane oncoprotein

LMP1 is a constitutively active signalling molecule with six transmembrane domains that form a cluster in lipid rafts, mimicking an activated CD40 receptor without requiring ligand. Its two cytoplasmic activation domains (CTAR1 and CTAR2) recruit TRAF2/3/5/6, TRADD, and IRAK1, activating NF-&kB;, JNK, PI3K/Akt, JAK/STAT3, and MAPK pathways. LMP1 upregulates anti-apoptotic proteins (Bcl-2, XIAP, A20), adhesion molecules (ICAM-1, LFA-3), and immune checkpoints. LMP1 expression in Reed-Sternberg cells is the molecular basis of the survival advantage of EBV+ Hodgkin lymphoma.

Treatment & Prevention

AntiviralsNo approved antiviral has proven clinical efficacy in well-powered randomised trials for IM or EBV-associated malignancies. Aciclovir/ganciclovir are active against lytic-phase replication (VCA+ cells) but have no effect on latently infected cells (which carry the oncogenic latency programme). Ganciclovir is occasionally used for EBV post-transplant reactivation.
Infectious mononucleosisSupportive care: rest, analgesics, antipyretics. Avoid aspirin (Reye’s risk in children). Avoid ampicillin/amoxicillin (drug-EBV maculopapular rash in ~80%). Corticosteroids for severe airway compromise from tonsillar hypertrophy or severe haematological complications (not routine). Activity restriction 3–4 weeks for splenomegaly (contact sport/heavy lifting prohibition).
PTLD1) Reduce immunosuppression (if safely possible); 2) Rituximab (anti-CD20 mAb depletes EBV+ B cells) — first-line for PTLD; 3) Chemotherapy (CHOP-based) for rituximab-refractory or T cell PTLD; 4) EBV-specific CTL infusion (donor-derived or third-party; clinical trials and compassionate use).
EBV-associated cancersStandard tumour-directed therapy (chemoradiotherapy for NPC, ABVD for HL, CHOP for BL); EBV-specific immunotherapy approaches (LMP1/LMP2-specific CTL infusion) in clinical trials for NPC and PTLD. CAR-T cells targeting EBV antigens under investigation.
VaccineNo approved vaccine. Phase II trial of gp350 subunit vaccine (Baxter) reduced IM incidence ~78% but did not prevent EBV infection. mRNA-based vaccines targeting gp350, gHgL, and gB in Phase I development (2024–2025).
