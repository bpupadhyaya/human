Immune system

Two-layer defense: innate (fast, pattern recognition, complement, IFN) and adaptive (antigen-specific T/B lymphocytes, immunological memory). Peak adaptive response 7–14 days on first exposure; memory recall 2–3 days. Organized around primary lymphoid organs (thymus, bone marrow) for cell development and secondary organs (lymph nodes, spleen, MALT) for response initiation. This is also the system that can turn against the host — autoimmunity, allergy, and cytokine storm are all failures of regulation.

Overview

The immune system is the body's multilayered defense against pathogens, malignant cells, and foreign substances — and simultaneously the regulatory network that maintains tolerance to self. It is not a single organ but a distributed system spanning every tissue: cells circulate in blood and lymph, patrol tissues as resident populations, and communicate through soluble mediators (cytokines, chemokines, antibodies, complement proteins).

Two functionally distinct but deeply interconnected arms cooperate:

Arm 1 — Innate immunity

Rapid (minutes to hours). Broad-specificity, non-adaptive detection of PAMPs (pathogen-associated molecular patterns) and DAMPs (damage-associated molecular patterns) via TLRs, NLRs, RIG-I, cGAS/STING. Effectors: neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells, dendritic cells, complement, type I interferons.

Arm 2 — Adaptive immunity

Slow first response (7–14 days). Exquisitely antigen-specific via rearranged TCR and BCR. Capable of immunological memory — on re-encounter, memory recall peaks in 2–3 days with higher magnitude and affinity. Effectors: CD4+ T helper cells, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, regulatory T cells, B lymphocytes, antibodies.

This two-layer architecture is why vaccination works: a vaccine primes the adaptive immune system without disease, establishing memory cells and long-lived plasma cells that enable rapid, protective responses on subsequent natural exposure.

The immune system surveils approximately 37 trillion cells in the human body via constitutive MHC-I self-presentation — any cell that fails to display normal MHC-I with normal self-peptides is detected as abnormal by NK cells and cytotoxic T cells. This surveillance is the primary defense against intracellular pathogens and cancer.

Lymphoid Organs — Primary

Soluble Mediators

Complement system

30+ plasma proteins; three activation pathways (classical, lectin, alternative) converging on C3 cleavage → C3b opsonization + MAC lysis + C5a anaphylatoxin/chemoattractant.

Cytokines & interferons

Interleukins (IL-1 through IL-38+); type I interferons (IFN-α/β) for antiviral state; type II (IFN-γ) for macrophage activation; TNF, TGF-β, colony-stimulating factors.

Chemokines

~50 small cytokines guiding cell migration via concentration gradients. CXCL8/IL-8 for neutrophils; CCL19/21 for DC/T cell homing; CXCL13 for B cell follicle formation.

Antibodies (immunoglobulins)

IgM (first response, complement), IgG (most abundant, long half-life, placental transfer), IgA (mucosal secretory), IgE (allergy, anti-parasite), IgD (B cell surface co-receptor).

Immunological Memory

After primary response contraction, long-lived memory cells persist — the molecular basis of lasting protective immunity:

Memory T cells

Memory CD4+ and CD8+ T cells distributed in lymphoid and non-lymphoid tissues (including tissue-resident memory TRM cells in lung, gut, skin). Respond within hours of re-exposure — orders of magnitude faster than naïve T cell priming.

Memory B cells & long-lived plasma cells

Memory B cells circulate; respond within 1–3 days on re-encounter with rapid secondary germinal center reactions and high-affinity IgG. Long-lived plasma cells (LLPCs) in bone marrow continuously secrete IgG for years, maintaining serum antibody titers that provide immediate neutralization on re-exposure.

Pathology

Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID)

Absence of both T and B cell function; causes include ADA-SCID (adenosine deaminase deficiency), X-SCID (IL-2Rγ chain mutation), RAG1/2 mutations. Untreated, invariably fatal in infancy. Curable by HSCT or gene therapy. ADA-SCID was the first human gene therapy success (1990).

HIV/AIDS

HIV-1 retrovirus selectively infects and depletes CD4+ T cells (via CCR5/CXCR4 co-receptors). CD4+ count <200 cells/µL defines AIDS; opportunistic infections (PCP, CMV retinitis, cryptococcal meningitis, MAI) are the proximate causes of death. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) can fully reconstitute CD4+ counts and prevent progression.

Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

Loss of tolerance to nuclear antigens (dsDNA, histones, Sm); anti-dsDNA antibodies form immune complexes; complement-mediated damage in kidneys (lupus nephritis), skin, joints, brain. HLA-DR2/DR3 strongly associated. Type I IFN signature is pathognomonic.

Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

Anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA/anti-CCP) + rheumatoid factor (RF) attack synovial joint membranes; Th17-driven neutrophilic/macrophage synovitis → cartilage and bone destruction. HLA-DRB1 shared epitope is the major genetic risk factor.

Anaphylaxis & allergy

Systemic mast cell degranulation (IgE crosslinking by allergen → FcεRI → histamine, tryptase, LTC4) causes airway edema, bronchospasm, and circulatory collapse. Type I hypersensitivity (Gell-Coombs). Treatment: epinephrine reverses bronchospasm and vasoconstriction. Atopy (asthma, rhinitis, eczema) shares the IgE-mast cell axis without systemic collapse.

Cytokine storm

Uncontrolled amplification of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF, IL-18, IFN-γ) — seen in severe COVID-19, hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy. Results in multi-organ failure from immunopathology rather than direct pathogen damage. Targeted by tocilizumab (anti-IL-6R) and dexamethasone.

Pathogen Evasion

PathogenKey evasion mechanisms
SARS-CoV-2NSP1 blocks mRNA translation of innate immune genes; ORF3b and ORF6 suppress IFN-β; N protein prevents TRIM25-mediated RIG-I ubiquitination; spike downregulates MHC-I. Severe disease linked to defective early IFN response and subsequent hyperinflammation.
Influenza ANS1 binds TRIM25, blocking RIG-I/IFN-β; PB1-F2 promotes mitochondrial apoptosis in immune cells; neuraminidase cleaves sialic acids that facilitate IgA binding; annual antigenic drift in HA/NA evades prior antibody responses.
M. tuberculosisESAT-6 perforates phagosomal membrane; LAM blocks phagosome maturation (blocks Rab7, preventing lysosome fusion); coronin-1A retains mitochondria near phagosome; bacterium persists for decades in granulomas. Reactivation highest with HIV co-infection or anti-TNF therapy.
