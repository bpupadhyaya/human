Atlas One · Scale 06 — Organ

Thymus

The thymus is the sole site of T lymphocyte development and education. It imposes two sequential quality-control checkpoints on every developing T cell: positive selection ensures TCRs can recognise self-MHC (enabling antigen recognition), and negative selection eliminates T cells with dangerously high affinity for self-antigens (preventing autoimmunity). Failure of either checkpoint has catastrophic immunological consequences.

Overview

The thymus is a bilobed lymphoepithelial organ occupying the anterior superior mediastinum, anterior to the great vessels and posterior to the manubrium of the sternum. It derives from the third pharyngeal pouch (epithelial scaffold from endoderm) and is colonised by bone-marrow-derived T cell progenitors throughout foetal development and childhood. At birth it weighs 12–15 g; it reaches its peak of ~30–40 g at puberty before undergoing progressive involution driven by sex steroids, with replacement of parenchyma by adipose tissue. By age 45 roughly 70% of the functional cortex is gone, though a remnant thymus persists throughout life and its output — though diminished — remains measurable (TRECs, T-cell receptor excision circles, are a biomarker of recent thymic emigrants).

The thymus is the only lymphoid organ that lacks afferent lymphatics and does not directly respond to peripheral antigens. Its entire function is the generation and education of the T cell repertoire. Each day in childhood and young adulthood, ~10⁶–10⁷ naive T cells egress from the thymus into blood via S1PR1-mediated migration, seeding the peripheral T cell pool of secondary lymphoid organs. The price of this output is extreme selectivity: only ~2–5% of incoming progenitors complete development and are exported; the other ~95% die by apoptosis, either having failed positive selection (no recognition of self-MHC) or having been deleted by negative selection (too-high affinity for self-antigens). This stringent education is the mechanistic basis of MHC restriction and central tolerance.

The thymic stromal scaffold — predominantly thymic epithelial cells (TECs) — plays an indispensable instructive role. Cortical TECs (cTECs) express the thymoproteasome (incorporating the unique subunit β5t) that generates a distinct peptide repertoire for presenting on MHC I to DP thymocytes during positive selection. Medullary TECs (mTECs) are distinguished by expression of the Autoimmune Regulator (AIRE) transcription factor, which drives ectopic transcription of >10,000 peripheral tissue-restricted antigens (TRAs) — including insulin, thyroglobulin, retinal arrestin — in the medulla. This "promiscuous gene expression" by mTECs projects a shadow of the entire body's self-antigen repertoire into the thymus, enabling deletion of T cells that would cause organ-specific autoimmunity if they reached the periphery.

Function

The thymus accomplishes three linked tasks: commitment of multipotent progenitors to the T cell lineage, selection of a self-MHC-restricted T cell repertoire, and purging of autoreactive T cells that would cause autoimmune disease. A small fraction of high-affinity T cells escapes deletion and is diverted instead into the regulatory T cell (Treg) lineage, producing FoxP3⁺ CD25⁺ CD4⁺ Tregs that enforce peripheral tolerance.

AIRE and Promiscuous Gene Expression
  • AIRE (Autoimmune Regulator) is a transcription factor expressed exclusively in mature mTECs (CCR7⁺CD80⁺UEA-1⁺ subset)
  • AIRE binds unmethylated H3K4 (an "open" chromatin mark) and activates transcription of TRAs whose epigenetic landscape is typically repressive in non-cognate tissues
  • TRAs include: insulin (INS), thyroglobulin (Tg), retinal arrestin (S-Ag), glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD65), and >10,000 others
  • Biallelic AIRE loss-of-function → APECED/APS-1 syndrome: candidiasis + hypoparathyroidism + Addison's disease + type 1 diabetes + alopecia + multiple autoimmune manifestations
  • IFN-α autoantibodies are pathognomonic for APECED and predict severe COVID-19 (these autoantibodies can also arise somatically in elderly)
Treg Generation in the Thymus
  • ~5% of T cells with high self-affinity (that would otherwise be deleted) are diverted to FoxP3⁺ regulatory T cell fate
  • Requirements: high-affinity TCR signal + IL-2 signal + TGF-β; Hassall's corpuscle-derived TSLP activates DCs that produce IL-2 and TGF-β for Treg induction
  • Thymic Tregs (tTregs) are distinguished from peripherally-induced Tregs (pTregs) by Helios + Neuropilin-1 co-expression
  • tTregs are essential for organ-specific self-tolerance: in mouse models where Foxp3 is absent (scurfy), lethal multi-organ autoimmunity develops by 4 weeks of age
  • tTreg specificity is biased toward self-antigens expressed in the thymus, including many AIRE-driven TRAs
Thymic Involution & Immunosenescence
  • Sex steroids (testosterone, oestradiol) drive thymic involution post-puberty: androgens accelerate cortical thymocyte apoptosis and reduce cTEC/mTEC numbers; castration reverses involution in experimental models
  • Involution → progressive decline in thymic export → peripheral T cell pool maintained increasingly by homeostatic proliferation of existing memory cells (IL-7/IL-15 driven)
  • Consequences: narrowing TCR repertoire diversity (oligoclonal expansion), impaired responses to novel antigens (poor vaccine efficacy in elderly), chronic inflammation ("inflammageing")
  • Clinical relevance: thymic atrophy post-chemotherapy/radiation → prolonged lymphopenia; thymic transplantation in complete DiGeorge syndrome restores T cell production; IL-7 therapy under investigation for immune reconstitution
Thymic Hormones
  • Thymulin (FTS — facteur thymique sérique): zinc-dependent nonapeptide; active only when zinc-bound; promotes T cell differentiation and IL-2 production; serum levels decline with age and thymic involution
  • Thymopoietin: 49 aa peptide; promotes DP → SP transition; synthetic pentapeptide thymopentin (TP-5) explored clinically for immunostimulation
  • Thymosin α1: 28 aa N-terminal fragment of prothymosin-α; promotes T cell maturation and IFN-γ production; thymalfasin (recombinant Tα1) used in HBV/HCV treatment and as adjuvant in some cancer immunotherapies in Asia
  • Thymosin β4: G-actin sequestering peptide; promotes tissue repair and angiogenesis beyond T cell biology; explored for cardiac and wound healing applications

Pathology

DiGeorge Syndrome (22q11.2 Deletion Syndrome)

Most common microdeletion syndrome (~1:4,000 live births); ~90% have del22q11.2 detectable by FISH/chromosomal microarray. Haploinsufficiency of TBX1 (T-box transcription factor) disrupts pharyngeal arch development → classic triad: conotruncal cardiac defects (tetralogy of Fallot, interrupted aortic arch, truncus arteriosus), hypoparathyroidism (parathyroid aplasia/hypoplasia → neonatal hypocalcaemia + seizures), and T cell immunodeficiency (thymic aplasia/hypoplasia). Spectrum from complete DiGeorge (absent thymus, profound T cell lymphopenia, SCID-like) to partial (small thymus, CD4 >500/µL). Complete DiGeorge (~1–2%): absolute T cell deficiency → recurrent infections, inability to reject allografts; treatment: thymic transplantation (cultured postnatal thymus tissue implants in quadriceps) restores T cell production and has been curative. Psychiatric features (schizophrenia, psychosis ~30%), learning disabilities, facial dysmorphology (tubular nose, low-set ears, micrognathia) are common.

APECED / APS-1 (AIRE Mutation Syndrome)

Autoimmune Polyendocrinopathy-Candidiasis-Ectodermal Dystrophy; caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in AIRE; most common in Finnish (~1:25,000), Sardinian, and Iranian Jewish populations. Classical triad: chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis (often first manifestation, ages 1–5), hypoparathyroidism (ages 5–15), Addison's disease (adrenal cortex autoimmunity, ages 5–20); >50% also develop T1DM, premature ovarian/testicular failure, hypothyroidism, pernicious anaemia, alopecia, vitiligo, hepatitis. Pathognomonic finding: high-titre IFN-α and IFN-ω autoantibodies — these neutralise Type I interferon antiviral defences → ~40× increased risk of severe/fatal COVID-19. Diagnosis: clinical triad + AIRE sequencing; autoantibody panel (anti-21-hydroxylase, anti-NALP5, anti-IFN-α/ω). Management: immunosuppression (corticosteroids, mycophenolate, rituximab for specific manifestations); antifungal prophylaxis; hormone replacement for each insufficiency; COVID-19 special surveillance and early treatment.

Thymoma & Thymic Carcinoma

Thymic epithelial tumours; thymoma is the most common anterior mediastinal mass in adults (>40 years). WHO classification: Type A (spindle cell, indolent, excellent prognosis), AB (mixed, good prognosis), B1 (lymphocyte-rich, like normal thymus histology), B2 (cortical type, most common), B3 (epithelial dominant, high risk), and thymic carcinoma (WHO C, aggressive). ~30–50% of thymoma patients develop myasthenia gravis (MG) due to autoantibodies against acetylcholine receptor (AChR) — ectopic mTEC expression of AChR ± loss of normal thymic tolerance for AChR antigens; thymectomy improves MG prognosis (MGTX trial, NEJM 2016). Paraneoplastic associations: pure red cell aplasia, Good's syndrome (combined B and T cell immunodeficiency), polymyositis, SLE. Treatment: resection (curative for stages I–II); adjuvant radiotherapy for stage III/IV; cisplatin-based chemotherapy ± immune checkpoint inhibitors (with caution — risk of flare of autoimmune co-morbidities). PD-L1 expression common in thymic carcinoma → pembrolizumab.

Thymic Lymphoid Hyperplasia & Myasthenia Gravis

In ~80% of AChR antibody-positive MG patients without thymoma, the thymus shows lymphoid follicular hyperplasia — germinal centres containing B cells producing anti-AChR autoantibodies. This "non-thymomatous" MG thymus is enlarged and histologically resembles a lymph node. Pathophysiology: defect in central tolerance to AChR + ectopic germinal centre formation in thymic medulla → ongoing autoreactive B cell activation. Thymectomy removes the ongoing germinal centre activity and leads to clinical improvement over months to years: MGTX trial (NEJM 2016) — extended thymectomy vs. prednisone alone in generalised AChR⁺ MG: thymectomy + prednisone group had significantly better 3-year clinical status scores and required lower steroid doses. Thymectomy is now standard of care for non-elderly generalised AChR⁺ MG. Effect is delayed (peak benefit 2–3 years post-surgery) reflecting slow decline of long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells.

Thymic Involution & COVID-19 Risk

Age-related thymic involution progressively reduces naive T cell export and TCR repertoire diversity, contributing to immunosenescence — impaired vaccine responses, increased susceptibility to novel pathogens, chronic low-grade inflammation. The critical importance of thymic output was dramatically illustrated during COVID-19: individuals with thymic dysfunction (APECED/APS-1 with anti-IFN autoantibodies; elderly with severely involuted thymuses; patients post-haematopoietic stem cell transplant with poor immune reconstitution) had dramatically worse outcomes. Anti-IFN-α/ω autoantibodies — found in ~4% of people >70 years — account for an estimated 15–20% of life-threatening COVID-19 cases (Bastard et al., Science 2020). Strategies to reverse thymic involution under investigation include sex-steroid blockade (GnRH agonists/antagonists) and IL-7 supplementation.
