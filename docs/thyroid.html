Thyroid Gland

The thyroid is a bilobed gland at levels C5–T1 weighing ~25 g. Two defining properties: (1) the only endocrine organ storing large hormone quantities extracellularly (iodinated thyroglobulin in follicular colloid, ~100 days supply), and (2) the only tissue concentrating iodide >30-fold via NIS. It secretes T4 (prohormone, ~80 µg/day) and T3 (active, ~8 µg/day from thyroid; ~80% of circulating T3 from peripheral T4→T3 deiodination by DIO1/DIO2). C-cells secrete calcitonin (↓Ca²⁺). The HPT axis (TRH→TSH→T3/T4) governs output with tight negative feedback. Hashimoto's thyroiditis and Graves' disease are the dominant autoimmune disorders.

Overview

The thyroid gland is the principal regulator of basal metabolic rate — a 25 g organ that sets the metabolic tempo of virtually every cell in the body. Its hormones T3 and T4 bind nuclear receptors TRα1 (heart, bone, brain) and TRβ1/TRβ2 (liver, kidney, pituitary), modulating the transcription of hundreds of metabolic genes. The spectrum of physiological effects is remarkable: BMR, cardiac chronotropy and inotropy, CNS myelination, bone turnover, gluconeogenesis, LDL receptor expression, and thermogenesis all fall under thyroid hormone control.

The thyroid is unique in its iodine metabolism. Dietary iodide is actively transported against a >30-fold gradient via NIS (Na⁺/I⁻ symporter). This dependency on dietary iodine has made iodine deficiency the world's most common preventable cause of intellectual disability. It also makes the thyroid the one organ where imaging and therapy exploit the element-specific uptake: radioactive ¹³¹I is both the gold-standard diagnostic agent and a definitive therapy for hyperthyroidism and differentiated thyroid cancer.

Anatomy and Histology

The thyroid is the most vascular organ per unit weight (~5 mL/min/g, vs ~1 mL/min/g for kidney). The fundamental structural unit is the thyroid follicle (~3 million per gland; diameter 50–500 µm):

Follicular epithelial cells (thyrocytes)

Single monolayer surrounding colloid lumen. Flat in hypostimulated state, columnar when TSH-stimulated. Polarised: apical membrane faces colloid (organification, resorption); basolateral membrane faces capillaries (iodide uptake, hormone secretion). Rich in rough ER, Golgi, lysosomes.

Colloid lumen

Contains thyroglobulin (Tg, 660 kDa homodimer; 2748 aa/monomer; 4–8 iodinated tyrosines form T3/T4). Pale eosinophilic on H&E. Stores ~100 days of hormone — a unique buffer against dietary iodine fluctuations. "Scalloping" of colloid margins = TSH stimulation on histology.

Parafollicular C-cells

Neural crest-derived cells between follicles. Larger, polygonal, pale-staining. Secrete calcitonin (32-aa peptide; lowers Ca²⁺ by inhibiting osteoclasts + ↑renal Ca²⁺ excretion). Cell of origin of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC); calcitonin is the tumour marker.

Surgical landmarks

Recurrent laryngeal nerves (RLN) in tracheo-oesophageal groove: injury → hoarseness (unilateral), respiratory distress (bilateral). Four parathyroid glands embedded in posterior capsule — at risk during total thyroidectomy → post-op hypoparathyroidism/hypocalcaemia. Berry's ligament anchors thyroid to cricoid.

Pathology

Hashimoto Thyroiditis (Autoimmune Hypothyroidism)

Most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-replete countries (~5% prevalence; F:M = 8:1). HLA-DR3/DR5 associated. T-cell-mediated thyrocyte destruction + anti-TPO antibodies (>90%) + anti-thyroglobulin antibodies (~60%). Histology: lymphocytic infiltration with germinal centres, Hürthle cell (oxyphilic) change, fibrosis. Course: goitre → subclinical hypothyroidism → overt hypothyroidism → atrophy. Symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, bradycardia, constipation, depression, myxoedema, delayed tendon reflexes. Treatment: levothyroxine (L-T4) once daily; target TSH 0.5–2.5 mIU/L.

Graves' Disease (Autoimmune Hyperthyroidism)

Most common cause of hyperthyroidism (~75%; F:M = 7:1). Thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins (TSI/TRAb-stimulating) bind TSH-R → constitutive Gs activation → autonomous T3/T4 synthesis (TSH suppressed to <0.01 mIU/L). Classic triad: diffuse goitre + ophthalmopathy (anti-IGF-1R antibodies on orbital fibroblasts → GAG → proptosis, lid lag) + pretibial myxoedema. Treatment: antithyroid drugs (methimazole preferred; PTU in first trimester/thyroid storm), radioactive iodine (¹³¹I), or surgery. β-blockers (propranolol) for acute symptom control (also inhibits DIO1 → ↓T4→T3). Thyroid storm: PTU + Lugol's iodine + dexamethasone + propranolol + ICU.

Thyroid Cancer

Papillary (PTC, 80–85%): BRAF V600E (50%), RET/PTC fusions; excellent prognosis (>95% 10-year survival low-risk). Follicular (FTC, 10–15%): PAX8/PPARγ, RAS; vascular invasion = poor prognostic feature. Medullary (MTC, 3–5%): C-cell origin; RET mutation (hereditary: MEN-2A/2B — prophylactic thyroidectomy in RET+ families); calcitonin as tumour marker. Anaplastic (<2%): catastrophic, median OS 3–5 months; BRAF/RAS/TP53/TERT mutations. Management (DTC): total thyroidectomy ± ¹³¹I ablation → TSH-suppressive levothyroxine → surveillance (Tg + neck US).

Myxoedema Coma

Life-threatening decompensated hypothyroidism — hypothermia, bradycardia, hypoventilation, hyponatraemia, altered consciousness. Precipitants: cold exposure, infection, drugs (sedatives, amiodarone), surgery. Treatment: IV liothyronine (L-T3) + IV hydrocortisone (adrenal insufficiency co-exists in 5–10%) + supportive ICU care + passive rewarming.

Sick Euthyroid Syndrome

During critical illness: ↑DIO3 + ↓DIO1 → ↓T3, ↑reverse T3 (rT3), normal or ↓TSH. Reflects adaptive reduction in metabolic rate. Treatment with T3/T4 does not improve outcomes in RCTs — treatment is of the underlying illness, not the thyroid function tests.
