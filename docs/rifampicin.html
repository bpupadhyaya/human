Rifampicin

Rifamycin antibiotic — binds the β-subunit of bacterial RNA polymerase and blocks elongation after the dinucleotide initiation step, sterilising dormant Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacilli that no other first-line TB drug can reach.

The backbone of TB treatment since 1968 and the reason we can cure TB in 6 months instead of 18. Also the most dangerous drug interaction perpetrator in clinical medicine — a nuclear PXR activator that induces CYP3A4, CYP2C9, P-gp and renders dozens of essential drugs ineffective.

Rifampicin

Class: Rifamycin Antibiotic  ·  RNA Polymerase Inhibitor  ·  Antitubercular  |  Route: Oral / IV  |  Also known as: Rifampin (US)  |  Status: Essential TB regimen backbone; also leprosy, meningococcal prophylaxis, MRSA biofilm

Rifampicin is a semisynthetic rifamycin antibiotic derived from Amycolatopsis rifamycinica. Its defining characteristic is binding to the β-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RNAP) at a site adjacent to the active centre — blocking the translocation of the nascent RNA chain after formation of the initial dinucleotide. This bactericidal action extends to dormant, non-replicating M. tuberculosis bacilli ("persisters") sheltering in caseous necrosis and macrophages — a sterilising activity unique among first-line TB drugs that explains why rifampicin reduced TB treatment duration from 18–24 months to 6 months. Its second pharmacologically defining property is potent induction of CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, P-glycoprotein, and other drug-metabolising enzymes via nuclear PXR activation — making it one of the most consequential drug interaction perpetrators in all of clinical medicine.

Mechanism of Action — rpoB Binding and RNA Elongation Block

Bacterial RNA Polymerase — Structure and Rifampicin Binding Site

  Bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RNAP) holoenzyme:
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  α₂ββ'ω core + σ factor (promoter recognition)      │
  │  β-subunit (rpoB gene): contains the active centre   │
  │  β'-subunit (rpoC gene): DNA template binding        │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  Transcription initiation:
  1. σ factor guides RNAP to promoter
  2. DNA melts → template strand enters active cleft
  3. NTP substrates enter → dinucleotide (pppNpN) synthesised
  4. NORMAL: elongation begins → σ released → transcript extends

  Rifampicin binding:
  → Binds β-subunit at a pocket ~12 Å from the active site
     (Asp516, Leu533, Met534, His526, Ser531 — RRDR residues)
  → DOES NOT prevent dinucleotide synthesis
  → BLOCKS: translocation of the 2-3 nucleotide nascent RNA
     → aborts transcript AFTER initiation, BEFORE productive elongation
  → Bactericidal: RNA synthesis completely blocked
  → Selectivity: 10,000–100,000× lower affinity for
     eukaryotic RNA polymerases (different β-subunit structure)

The molecular mechanism has an important implication for resistance: rifampicin-resistant mutations cluster in the 81-base-pair "Rifampicin Resistance-Determining Region" (RRDR) of rpoB (codons 507–533 in E. coli numbering, 426–452 in M. tuberculosis). The most common TB mutations are at codons Asp435 (D435V/Y), His445 (H445Y/D/R), and Ser450 (S450L — formerly Ser531 in older numbering). S450L accounts for ~45–55% of rifampicin-resistant M. tuberculosis globally.

Sterilising Activity vs Dormant Bacilli — Why Rifampicin is Irreplaceable

Replicating Bacilli

All first-line TB drugs (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) kill actively replicating M. tuberculosis. Isoniazid is most potent here — responsible for the rapid early bactericidal activity (EBA) in the first 2 days of treatment

Dormant / Persister Bacilli

Non-replicating bacilli in caseous necrosis and macrophages survive isoniazid and ethambutol but are killed by rifampicin and pyrazinamide. Rifampicin kills slow-growing or non-growing bacilli — the key to preventing relapse. Without rifampicin: 18–24 months treatment needed. With rifampicin: 6 months achieves sterilising cure

Acidic Phagosome Environment

Pyrazinamide is specifically active in the acidic intracellular environment of macrophage phagolysosomes — its sterilising activity is distinct from and complementary to rifampicin. Together, rifampicin + pyrazinamide in the first 2 months constitute the "sterilising backbone" of the HRZE regimen

Pharmacokinetic Properties

Rifampicin is lipophilic — excellent tissue penetration including CSF, caseous lesions, and macrophage interiors. Oral bioavailability ~90–95% (fasting); food reduces absorption. Half-life 2–5 hours; biliary/faecal excretion (no dose adjustment in renal impairment)

HRZE Regimen — Standard TB Treatment

The standard first-line regimen for drug-susceptible TB is the 6-month HRZE/HR regimen — two phases designed to exploit the different bactericidal mechanisms of each drug:

DrugAbbreviationStandard Adult DosePrimary Role in RegimenPhase
Isoniazid H 5 mg/kg/day (max 300 mg) Early bactericidal activity — kills replicating bacilli rapidly; prevents resistance emergence; INH monoresistance risk if used alone Both phases (6 months)
Rifampicin R 10 mg/kg/day (max 600 mg) Sterilising activity against dormant bacilli; prevents relapse; backbone of the regimen; most important single drug in TB treatment Both phases (6 months)
Pyrazinamide Z 25 mg/kg/day (max 2 g) Sterilising activity in acidic intracellular environments; allows shortening from 9 to 6 months; hepatotoxic — liver function monitoring required Initial phase only (2 months)
Ethambutol E 15–25 mg/kg/day Protects against resistance emergence (arabinogalactan synthesis inhibitor); added until sensitivity testing confirms no isoniazid resistance Initial phase only (2 months)

Regimen structure: 2HRZE (2 months all 4 drugs — intensive phase) + 4HR (4 months isoniazid + rifampicin — continuation phase). Resistance testing guides modification: confirmed isoniazid resistance → substitute levofloxacin; rifampicin resistance = presumptive MDR-TB → second-line regimen.

Why rifampicin resistance = MDR-TB proxy: Isolated rifampicin resistance is rare in clinical M. tuberculosis — it almost always occurs on a background of pre-existing isoniazid resistance (because acquiring both is easier than acquiring rifampicin resistance de novo in a drug-naive host). The 2016 WHO End TB strategy endorsed rifampicin resistance (detected by Xpert MTB/RIF) as a proxy for MDR-TB → trigger immediate second-line MDR regimen. rpoB mutation detection by Xpert is >96% sensitive for rifampicin resistance.

Resistance — rpoB RRDR and MDR-TB

Rifampicin resistance in M. tuberculosis arises exclusively from point mutations in the Rifampicin Resistance-Determining Region (RRDR) of the rpoB gene — an 81-bp hotspot. No plasmid-mediated or enzymatic rifampicin resistance is seen in mycobacteria. Key features:

Codon (Mtb numbering)Common MutationsResistance LevelFrequency
Ser450 (S531 old) S450L (most common globally) High-level (>32 µg/mL) ~45–55% of RIF-R strains
His445 (H526 old) H445Y, H445D, H445R High-level ~15–20%
Asp435 (D516 old) D435V, D435G Moderate to high ~5–10%
Leu430 (L511 old) L430P Moderate Rare
Ile491 (I572 old) I491F, I491V Low-level ("borderline") ~5%; may respond to high-dose rifampicin

Xpert MTB/RIF (Cepheid) detects 5 overlapping probes spanning the RRDR — each probe probe hybridises to a different RRDR segment. Probe melt curve analysis identifies mutations. Sensitivity for rifampicin resistance ~96%; specificity ~98%. The 2023 WHO-approved Xpert MTB/XDR additionally detects isoniazid (inhA, katG), fluoroquinolone (gyrA/B), and second-line injectable resistance, enabling rapid XDR-TB diagnosis at point of care.

Drug Interactions — CYP3A4 / P-gp Induction

Rifampicin is one of the most potent inducers of drug-metabolising enzymes and transporters known. Mechanism: rifampicin activates the nuclear pregnane X receptor (PXR/NR1I2), a transcription factor that upregulates CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2B6, UGT1A, P-glycoprotein (P-gp/ABCB1), MRP2, and others. Induction begins within 24 hours and reaches maximum effect in 5–7 days; the effect persists for 2–4 weeks after stopping rifampicin (enzyme synthesis continues until induced enzyme is degraded).

Clinical alert: Every patient starting rifampicin MUST have a full medication review for CYP3A4/P-gp substrates. Failure to detect and manage interactions has caused treatment failures, organ rejection, HIV breakthrough, contraceptive failure, and deaths.

Adverse Effects

  • Hepatotoxicity — most important serious adverse effect; occurs in ~2–10% of patients on HRZE regimen (all four first-line TB drugs are hepatotoxic; rifampicin causes both direct hepatotoxicity and potentiates isoniazid hepatotoxicity via induction of CYP2E1 → more toxic isoniazid metabolites). Monitor LFTs at baseline and monthly; stop ALL TB drugs if transaminases >3× ULN with symptoms or >5× ULN asymptomatic; rechallenge sequentially after recovery
  • Orange colouration of body fluids — rifampicin is orange-red in colour and is excreted in urine, sweat, tears, saliva, and breast milk. Urine turns bright orange/red — warn all patients to prevent alarm; permanently stains soft contact lenses (advise patients to avoid or discard)
  • Flu-like syndrome (intermittent dosing) — fever, chills, myalgia, and malaise occurring 2–8 hours after drug ingestion; associated with irregular ("intermittent") dosing rather than daily dosing; mechanism involves immune complex formation with rifampicin antibodies. Solution: use daily dosing; if already on intermittent, do not switch back to daily (risk of immunological reaction)
  • Gastrointestinal — nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain; take on an empty stomach for maximum absorption (30 minutes before food); if intolerable, take with light food (reduces absorption ~20%)
  • Thrombocytopenia (rare) — immune-mediated; rifampicin-dependent antibodies; platelet count falls precipitously during intermittent therapy; discontinue permanently if occurs
  • Induction-related sub-therapeutic drug levels — as noted above — rifampicin induces its own metabolism (autoinduction via PXR) as well as co-administered drugs; plasma levels fall over the first 2 weeks of treatment as CYP3A4 is induced
