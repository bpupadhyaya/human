Biological Roles

Urea cycle detoxifies ammonia; NO synthases produce the gasotransmitter nitric oxide

Urea Cycle — Hepatic Ammonia Detoxification

  NH₃ (from amino acid catabolism, gut bacteria)
      │
      ▼  CPS I (carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I) + N-acetylglutamate
  Carbamoyl phosphate  [mitochondria]
      │
      ▼  OTC (ornithine transcarbamylase)
  Citrulline  ──→ exported to cytoplasm
      │
      ▼  ASS (argininosuccinate synthetase) + Aspartate (2nd N)
  Argininosuccinate
      │
      ▼  ASL (argininosuccinate lyase) → fumarate (→ TCA)
  Arginine
      │
      ▼  Arginase I
  Urea (excreted in urine) + Ornithine (recycled back)

  Nitrogen output: urea ~30 g/day (adults on normal protein diet)
  BUN (blood urea nitrogen) normal 2.5–7.1 mmol/L

Nitric Oxide Synthesis

  L-Arginine + O₂ + NADPH
      │
      ▼  NOS (eNOS / nNOS / iNOS) — FAD/FMN/BH₄/CaM cofactors
  L-Citrulline + NO•

  eNOS (endothelial): Ca²⁺/CaM-activated; Akt-Ser1177 phosphorylation
      → NO diffuses to VSM → sGC → cGMP → PKG → myosin dephosphorylation
      → vasodilation

  nNOS (neuronal): constitutive in neurons; synaptic plasticity; retrograde messenger
  iNOS (inducible): cytokine-induced; high-output NO for antimicrobial killing

Nucleotide Biosynthesis — Nitrogen Donors

De novo purine biosynthesis: N1 from Asp; N3,9 from Gln; N7 from Gly. De novo pyrimidine: ring N1 from Asp; C2 from CO₂; N3 from Gln (CPS II). These biosynthetic pathways are targets for chemotherapy (methotrexate → DHFR inhibition → depletes formyl-THF for purine N10).

Absorption & Metabolism

Dietary protein digestion, transamination, and nitrogen balance

Dietary proteins are hydrolysed by pepsin (stomach), pancreatic proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase), and brush-border peptidases to free amino acids and di/tripeptides. Amino acids enter portal blood; hepatic first-pass metabolises ~50%.

N in (protein) = N out (urea + feces + skin); positive in growth, negative in catabolism/starvation
