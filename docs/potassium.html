Biological Roles

Resting membrane potential, action potential repolarisation, cardiac rhythm, aldosterone/RAAS

Cardiac K⁺ Currents

  Cardiac Action Potential (ventricular myocyte)

  Phase 0: Nav1.5 (INa) — rapid depolarisation (dV/dt 200–300 V/s)
  Phase 1: Ito (Kv4.3) — early transient outward K⁺ current — notch
  Phase 2: ICaL (Cav1.2) balanced by Ito/IKr/IKs — plateau
  Phase 3: IKr (hERG/KCNH2) + IKs (KCNQ1/minK) — repolarisation
  Phase 4: IK1 (Kir2.1/2.2) — inward-rectifier holds Vm at −80 mV

  SA Node Pacemaking (no Phase 1/2):
  Phase 4 spontaneous depolarisation:
    If (HCN4) — funny current — inward Na⁺/K⁺ mixed current
    ICaT (Cav3.1) — low threshold T-type Ca²⁺
  Phase 0 SA node: ICaL (Cav1.2) — slow depolarisation (~1 V/s)
  Repolarisation: IKr + IKs + IKACh (M2-Kir3.1/3.4)

Absorption & Metabolism

ROMK/ENaC aldosterone axis; K⁺/Na⁺ ratio matters as much as absolute intake

~90% of dietary K⁺ is absorbed passively in the small intestine. Renal excretion is the primary regulator. In the cortical collecting duct (CCD), principal cells: ENaC (apical Na⁺ entry, electrogenic) drives lumen-negative voltage → K⁺ exits via ROMK (Kir1.1). Aldosterone upregulates ENaC and ROMK expression.
