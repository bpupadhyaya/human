Mast Cell

Mast cells are tissue-resident innate immune cells specialised for rapid release of vasoactive, pro-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory mediators in response to allergen–IgE–FcεRI crosslinking, complement anaphylatoxins, neuropeptides, and PAMPs. IgE-FcεRI crosslinking triggers Lyn → Syk → LAT → PLCγ → Ca²⁺ flux + PKCβ → degranulation cascade releasing histamine, tryptase, and LTC4/LTD4 within seconds. Central to anaphylaxis, allergic asthma, and anti-parasitic defense; pathological in allergy and mastocytosis.

Overview

Mast cells are tissue-resident innate immune cells of haematopoietic origin, specialised for rapid release of a vast arsenal of vasoactive, pro-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory mediators in response to allergen–IgE–FcεRI crosslinking, complement anaphylatoxins, neuropeptides, and pathogen-associated signals. They were originally identified by Paul Ehrlich in 1878 ("Mastzellen" — well-fed cells) based on their characteristic large metachromatic cytoplasmic granules, which store pre-formed mediators including histamine, heparin, tryptase, and TNF-α.

Unlike basophils — their circulating haematopoietic cousins — mast cells complete their differentiation within tissues, not in the bone marrow or blood. They are the quintessential "first responders" at host-environment interfaces (skin dermis, respiratory mucosa, GI mucosa, peritoneum, meninges) where they serve dual roles: defending against parasitic helminths and arthropod venoms (protective), and mediating pathological type I (IgE-mediated) hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, allergic asthma, and urticaria.

Pathology

Anaphylaxis

Systemic IgE-mediated FcεRI crosslinking → massive simultaneous mast cell + basophil degranulation within minutes: histamine (↓BP, urticaria), LTD₄ (severe bronchoconstriction), PGD₂ (↓BP, vasodilation), tryptase (PAR-2, vascular permeability), PAF (platelet aggregation, bronchoconstriction). Serum tryptase >11.4 µg/L confirms mast cell activation. Treatment: IM epinephrine (α₁ → vasoconstriction, β₂ → bronchodilation, β₁ → ↑cardiac output; also ↑cAMP → inhibits mast cell degranulation) is the only life-saving first-line agent; H₁-antihistamines and corticosteroids are adjunctive. Biphasic reaction in ~20% of cases (late phase 4–12 h later).

Allergic Asthma

Airway mast cells sensitised by inhaled allergen-specific IgE; re-exposure → crosslinking → histamine, PGD₂, LTC₄/LTD₄ → immediate bronchoconstriction; IL-4, IL-13, IL-5 → late-phase eosinophilic inflammation, goblet cell hyperplasia, airway smooth muscle hypertrophy → airway remodeling. Anti-IgE (omalizumab, binds free IgE Cε3 domain, blocks FcεRI binding) is effective in moderate-severe allergic asthma; dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα) blocks IL-4 and IL-13 downstream of mast cell activation.

Mastocytosis

KIT D816V somatic gain-of-function mutation → constitutive c-Kit signaling → autonomous mast cell proliferation. Cutaneous mastocytosis: orange-brown macules that urticulate with stroking (Darier's sign), peaks in children. Systemic mastocytosis (SM): bone marrow infiltration, CD117+CD25+tryptase+; serum baseline tryptase >20 ng/mL (major criterion). Midostaurin (KIT inhibitor, FDA 2017) and avapritinib (FDA 2021, more potent D816V activity) for advanced SM.

Mast cell involvement in GI and neurological disorders

Mast cells lie anatomically adjacent to sensory nerves: tryptase activates PAR-2 on C-fibres → itch/pain amplification; substance P and CGRP from neurons activate MRGPRX2 → degranulation. This mast cell-nerve cross-talk drives visceral hypersensitivity in irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), neurogenic inflammation in rosacea, and contributes to fibromyalgia pain. Cardiac mast cells (perivascular MCTC) can degranulate in MI/reperfusion → Kounis syndrome (allergic myocardial infarction via LTC₄/LTD₄-induced coronary spasm).

Protective Roles — Anti-parasite and Venom Defense

While allergy dominates the clinical discussion of mast cells, their evolutionary role is host defense against macroparasites (helminths) and arthropod venoms:

ThreatMast cell responseProtective mechanism
Helminth (intestinal worms)IL-4/IL-33-driven MCT proliferation in gut mucosa; IgE-independent and IgE-dependent activationMast cell tryptase degrades helminth proteins; smooth muscle contraction (histamine + LTD₄) drives worm expulsion; mucus hypersecretion traps larvae; IL-5 activates eosinophils
Snake/bee venomMRGPRX2 activation by venom basic peptides (mastoparan, phospholipase A₂ products) → rapid degranulationCarboxypeptidase A (CPA3) degrades venom endothelins and sarafotoxins; chymase degrades endothelin-1; histamine-induced vasodilation may dilute local venom concentration; mast cell-deficient mice are more sensitive to venom lethality
Bacteria (early innate)TLR2/TLR4 sensing of PAMPs → cytokine release (TNF-α pre-formed!) without full degranulation; IL-6, IL-12 productionPre-formed TNF-α release is 10× faster from mast cells than macrophages; immediate local alarm signal; CXCL8 (IL-8) recruits neutrophils; LTB₄ amplifies neutrophil chemotaxis
Tick biteHistamine release at bite site (IgE-independent via complement + salivary proteins)Pruritus drives grooming behavior (anti-tick defense); local vascular changes limit tick engorgement; mast cell-deficient mice show increased tick feeding success

The observation that allergic individuals in high-helminth environments have reduced allergy symptoms suggests the "hygiene hypothesis" — Th2 immune circuits evolved for parasite defense; in the absence of helminths (modern sanitation), the same IgE-mast cell axis is misdirected at harmless allergens (pollen, food), producing allergy. Deliberate helminth exposure (Trichuris suis ova therapy) is under clinical investigation for Crohn's disease and allergic asthma based on this premise.
