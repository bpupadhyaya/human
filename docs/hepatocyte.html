Hepatocyte

Large (~20–30 µm), polygonal, frequently binucleate parenchymal cell constituting ~80% of liver volume. Executes carbohydrate homeostasis, lipid/cholesterol synthesis, plasma protein secretion (albumin, clotting factors, complement), CYP450-mediated drug detoxification, bile acid synthesis, and the urea cycle. Metabolically zonated: Zone 1 (periportal) specialises in gluconeogenesis and β-oxidation; Zone 3 (centrilobular) in glycolysis, lipogenesis, and CYP2E1/CYP3A4 drug metabolism.

Overview

The hepatocyte is the principal parenchymal cell of the liver — a large (~20–30 µm diameter), polygonal, binucleate-capable cell that carries out the overwhelming majority of the liver's metabolic activities. Constituting approximately 60–80% of total liver cell number and ~80% of liver volume, hepatocytes execute more than 500 distinct metabolic functions, making them among the most metabolically diverse cells in the human body.

Key metabolic domains span every major pathway: carbohydrate metabolism (gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis, glycogen synthesis, glycolysis); lipid metabolism (fatty acid β-oxidation, de novo lipogenesis, triglyceride synthesis, VLDL secretion, ketogenesis); protein synthesis (albumin 10–15 g/day, fibrinogen, clotting factors II/V/VII/IX/X, complement C1–C9); detoxification (Phase I CYP450s, Phase II glucuronidation/glutathione conjugation, Phase III transporters); bile acid synthesis (cholesterol → cholic/chenodeoxycholic acid → conjugated with glycine/taurine → bile); and the urea cycle (NH₃ → urea, protecting against hyperammonemia).

Hepatocytes can regenerate extensively after injury — after 70% partial hepatectomy (living-donor transplant standard), the remnant restores full functional mass within 4–8 weeks in humans, driven by TNF-α, IL-6, HGF, and EGF signalling cascades.

The plasma membrane has three functionally distinct domains. The basolateral (sinusoidal) surface (~70% of membrane area) faces the Space of Disse and contains uptake transporters: NTCP (bile salts), OATP1B1/1B3 (bilirubin and drugs), GLUT2 (glucose), amino acid transporters. The lateral surface (~15%) connects adjacent hepatocytes via connexin-32/26 gap junctions essential for coordinating glucagon/insulin signalling. The apical (canalicular) surface (~15%) faces the bile canaliculus and houses secretory transporters: BSEP (ABCB11), MRP2 (ABCC2), MDR3 (ABCB4).

Function — CYP450 Drug Metabolism and Bile Acid Synthesis

The smooth ER of hepatocytes houses the cytochrome P450 monooxygenase system — the principal enzyme family for Phase I drug oxidation, responsible for metabolising ~75% of all clinically used drugs. Bile acid synthesis from cholesterol is the primary route for cholesterol elimination from the body.

Glucose Homeostasis and Metabolic Zonation

Zone 1 — Periportal (pO₂ ~70 mmHg)

Gluconeogenesis (PEPCK, PC), β-oxidation, urea synthesis (CPS-I), primary bile acid secretion, amino acid catabolism. First to receive portal blood. Resistant to ischemia. Wnt signalling low; PEPCK/PC/CPS-I high.

Zone 3 — Centrilobular (pO₂ ~35 mmHg)

Glycolysis, de novo lipogenesis, CYP2E1/CYP3A4 drug metabolism, glutamine synthesis (GS — zone-3 IHC marker), ketogenesis. Most vulnerable to ischemia, APAP toxicity, cardiac hepatopathy. Wnt/β-catenin high.

Postprandial State (insulin-dominant)

GLUT2 glucose uptake → glucokinase (GCK; Km ~10 mM) → glycogen synthesis + de novo lipogenesis → VLDL secretion. Insulin → IRS-1/PI3K/Akt → FoxO1 phosphorylation → suppresses gluconeogenesis genes.

Fasted State (glucagon-dominant)

Glucagon → PKA → glycogen phosphorylase → glycogenolysis → glucose-6-phosphatase → free glucose. Prolonged fasting: gluconeogenesis (lactate, alanine, glycerol) + ketogenesis (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate) for brain and muscle fuel.

Protein Synthesis and the Urea Cycle

The hepatocyte is the primary factory for plasma proteins secreted into the circulation. Albumin (10–15 g/day) maintains oncotic pressure (~80% of total plasma oncotic pressure) and acts as a carrier for fatty acids, bilirubin, calcium, and drugs. Clotting factors II/VII/IX/X require hepatic vitamin K-dependent γ-carboxylation (target of warfarin); PT/INR directly reflects hepatic synthetic function. Complement proteins C1–C9 are predominantly hepatocyte-synthesised. Acute-phase reactants (CRP, fibrinogen, serum amyloid A, haptoglobin) are upregulated within hours by IL-6/JAK1/STAT3 signalling during inflammation.

The urea cycle converts toxic NH₃ (from amino acid catabolism and gut-derived urease activity) to urea: NH₃ + CO₂ → carbamoyl phosphate (CPS-I, zone 1) → citrulline (mitochondria) → argininosuccinate (cytosol) → arginine → urea + ornithine. Urea exits to blood → kidneys → excreted in urine. In hepatocellular failure, urea cycle capacity falls → hyperammonemia → hepatic encephalopathy.

Lipid and Cholesterol Metabolism

Hepatocytes synthesise ~70% of whole-body cholesterol via the mevalonate pathway: acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → (HMG-CoA reductase, HMGCR — statin target) → mevalonate → squalene → cholesterol. Statin inhibition of HMGCR ↓intracellular cholesterol → SREBP2 activation → LDLR upregulation → ↓plasma LDL-C.

VLDL assembly: triglycerides + ApoB-100 + cholesterol ester → VLDL particle (MTP-dependent) → secreted to plasma → lipolysis by LPL → IDL → LDL. Hepatic LDL receptors (LDLR, upregulated by statins) clear ~70% of plasma LDL via PCSK9-regulated endocytosis. PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab, alirocumab) block PCSK9-mediated LDLR degradation → further ↓LDL-C by 50–60%.

Pathology

Acetaminophen (APAP) Hepatotoxicity

Therapeutic doses: ~90% glucuronidated/sulfated safely; ~5–10% CYP2E1/CYP3A4 → NAPQI, immediately neutralised by glutathione (GSH). Overdose: Phase II saturation → CYP2E1 overflow → excess NAPQI → GSH depletion → zone-3 covalent protein adduct formation → mitochondrial dysfunction → necrosis. Antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) replenishes GSH. Leading cause of acute liver failure in Western countries (>50% of ALF cases).

Viral Hepatitis — HBV and HCV

HBV enters hepatocytes via NTCP (SLC10A1) binding of preS1 domain; cccDNA persists as nuclear minichromosome. ~296 million chronic carriers; hepatocyte injury is primarily CTL-mediated; 1–4%/year cirrhosis → HCC risk. HCV infects via CD81/SR-B1/CLDN1/OCLN receptor complex; 55–85% chronicity; now >95% SVR with 8–12 week DAA regimens (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir).

NAFLD / NASH / MASLD

Hepatic steatosis (≥5% hepatocyte fat) → NASH (steatosis + ballooning + lobular inflammation) → fibrosis → cirrhosis. Pathomechanism: insulin resistance → excess adipocyte FFA → hepatocyte lipotoxicity → ER stress + JNK activation + mitochondrial dysfunction + oxidative stress → Kupffer/stellate cell activation → TGF-β → collagen I/III → portal fibrosis. Affects ~25% of global adults (~1.8 billion). Approved treatments: semaglutide, resmetirom (THR-β agonist).

Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI)

Idiosyncratic (amoxicillin-clavulanate, isoniazid, nitrofurantoin, diclofenac) or predictable dose-dependent (acetaminophen). Mechanisms: reactive metabolite formation (Phase I), mitochondrial dysfunction, immune-mediated hepatocyte killing (hapten hypothesis). ALT/AST elevation; R-ratio (ALT/ULN ÷ ALP/ULN) differentiates hepatocellular (R ≥5) vs cholestatic (R ≤2) DILI patterns.

Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)

Third leading cause of cancer death globally. Risk factors: HBV (viral DNA integration), HCV cirrhosis, NASH-cirrhosis, aflatoxin B1 (CYP3A4 → 8,9-epoxide → TP53 R249S mutation), alcohol, haemochromatosis. Surveillance: AFP + liver ultrasound every 6 months in all cirrhotic patients. Systemic therapy: atezolizumab + bevacizumab (preferred first-line); sorafenib, lenvatinib (alternatives).

UGT1A1 Syndromes — Gilbert and Crigler-Najjar

Gilbert syndrome (UGT1A1*28 promoter polymorphism, 5–10% of population): mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia, benign, triggered by fasting/illness/haemolysis. Crigler-Najjar type I: complete UGT1A1 absence → severe unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia from birth → kernicterus without 10–18 h/day phototherapy → liver transplantation curative.
