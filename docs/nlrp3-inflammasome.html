NLRP3 Inflammasome

A cytosolic multiprotein innate immune signaling platform that senses a broad spectrum of danger signals — from crystals to ATP to bacterial toxins — and responds by activating caspase-1 to cleave pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18 into mature cytokines, and by executing pyroptotic cell death.

Gene: NLRP3 (also CIAS1, PYPAF1, cryopyrin) Scale: Molecular complex (~700 kDa) Status: Draft · reviewed 2026-06-05

Overview

The NLRP3 inflammasome is the archetypal and most extensively studied inflammasome — a large cytosolic multiprotein complex (~700 kDa) assembled in myeloid cells (macrophages, dendritic cells, monocytes, neutrophils) in response to DAMP and PAMP signals that indicate cellular stress, damage, or infection.

The canonical NLRP3 inflammasome consists of three components: NLRP3 (sensor protein), ASC (adaptor protein bridging NLRP3 to caspase-1), and pro-caspase-1. Upon assembly into the oligomeric complex, pro-caspase-1 undergoes proximity-induced autocatalytic activation. Active caspase-1 then executes two major effector functions:

  • Proteolytic cleavage of pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18 into their mature, bioactive forms for secretion
  • Cleavage of Gasdermin D (GSDMD) to generate membrane-permeabilizing N-terminal fragments that drive pyroptosis — an inflammatory, lytic form of programmed cell death

A non-canonical inflammasome pathway (mouse caspase-11 / human caspase-4 and caspase-5) directly senses cytosolic lipopolysaccharide (LPS) without requiring NLRP3 or ASC, and triggers GSDMD pore formation and secondary NLRP3 activation via K⁺ efflux.

Central role in sterile inflammation: Unlike most pattern recognition pathways that evolved primarily to detect pathogens, NLRP3 is uniquely activated by endogenous damage signals (urate crystals, cholesterol crystals, β-amyloid, ATP) — making it a pivotal driver of sterile inflammatory diseases including gout, atherosclerosis, and Alzheimer's disease.

Activation Mechanism: Two-Signal Model

Signal 1 — Priming

NLRP3 protein expression is constitutively very low and must be transcriptionally induced before the inflammasome can assemble. Priming stimuli include:

  • TLR ligands (e.g., LPS via TLR4): Activate NF-κB → transcription of NLRP3, IL1B (pro-IL-1β), and IL18
  • TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-18: Cytokine-mediated NF-κB activation provides an amplification loop
  • Post-translational priming: TLR signals also promote NLRP3 deubiquitylation (BRCC3/BRISC deubiquitylase), phosphorylation changes, and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species — "licensing" the complex for assembly independent of new transcription

Signal 2 — Activation

A structurally and chemically diverse set of stimuli all converge on NLRP3 oligomerization. Current evidence places potassium efflux as the near-universal prerequisite:

  • K⁺ efflux (universal signal): Artificially lowering intracellular K⁺ (by raising extracellular K⁺) is sufficient to prevent NLRP3 activation by any stimulus; conversely, K⁺ efflux alone can trigger NLRP3. Low cytosolic K⁺ promotes NEK7-NLRP3 binding and destabilizes the NLRP3 autoinhibited conformation.
  • ATP: Released from damaged/dying cells; activates P2X7 receptor → large ion-permeable pore → K⁺ efflux + Ca²⁺ influx → NLRP3 activation. Also mitochondrial signal via TXNIP.
  • Crystalline and particulate matter: Monosodium urate (gout), calcium pyrophosphate, silica, asbestos, cholesterol crystals (atherosclerotic plaques), β-amyloid fibrils (Alzheimer's). Mechanism: phagocytosis → lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP) → cathepsin B release into cytosol → cytosolic cathepsin B activates NLRP3 (possibly through K⁺ efflux via an indirect route).
  • Reactive oxygen species (ROS): Mitochondrial ROS release (mtROS) promotes NLRP3 via TXNIP dissociation from thioredoxin; oxidized mitochondrial DNA (ox-mtDNA) released from damaged mitochondria can directly bind and activate NLRP3.
  • Pore-forming toxins: Staphylococcal α-toxin (alpha-hemolysin), Listeria listeriolysin O (LLO), aerolysin — plasma membrane pore formation → K⁺ efflux → NLRP3.
  • Viral signals: SARS-CoV-2 E protein viroporin → K⁺ efflux → NLRP3; various viral RNA species may activate NLRP3 via MAVS interaction (disputed).
K⁺ efflux as the unifying mechanism: Despite the striking diversity of activating stimuli — from crystals to pore-forming toxins to ATP — they all appear to converge on cytosolic K⁺ depletion. High extracellular KCl (≥130 mM) blocks NLRP3 activation by virtually every known stimulus, making K⁺ efflux the canonical second messenger of NLRP3 signaling.

Downstream Outputs

IL-1β Processing and Secretion

Pro-IL-1β (31 kDa, biologically inactive) is synthesized as a cytosolic precursor following NF-κB priming. Active caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1β at Asp116 → mature IL-1β (17 kDa). Mature IL-1β lacks a classical signal peptide and cannot enter the conventional ER-Golgi secretory pathway. Instead, IL-1β is secreted via GSDMD pores (direct release through the membrane channel) and possibly via an unconventional secretory pathway involving secretory lysosomes and autophagosomes. Mature IL-1β acts via IL-1R1 on target cells to drive fever (PGE₂ in hypothalamus), acute phase response (IL-6 induction in hepatocytes), neutrophil recruitment (CXCL8), and T cell activation.

IL-18 Processing

Pro-IL-18 is similarly cleaved by caspase-1 at Asp35 → mature IL-18 (18 kDa). IL-18 is a critical IFN-γ-inducing factor, activating NK cells and Th1 cells. IL-18 together with IL-12 drives maximal IFN-γ production. IL-18 is also pro-inflammatory in obesity-associated metabolic disease, inflammatory bowel disease, and Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS/HLH).

Pyroptosis via Gasdermin D

GSDMD is cleaved by caspase-1 (canonical) or caspase-4/5/11 (non-canonical) at Asp275, releasing the N-terminal domain (NT-GSDMD, ~31 kDa) from its autoinhibitory C-terminal domain. NT-GSDMD selectively binds the inner leaflet phospholipids phosphatidylserine and phosphatidylinositol phosphates → inserts into the plasma membrane → oligomerizes to form large (~14–18 nm inner diameter) non-selective pores → cell swelling → osmotic lysis (pyroptosis).

Pyroptosis is a highly inflammatory form of cell death: the pyroptotic cell releases its cytosolic contents including IL-1α, IL-18, HMGB1, mtDNA, and other DAMPs that amplify the inflammatory cascade in neighboring cells. Sublytic GSDMD pore formation (below threshold for lysis) can permit IL-1β secretion without cell death — a "kiss of death" partial activation state.

Other Caspase-1 Substrates

  • Pro-IL-33: Nuclear alarmin; cleavage generates mature IL-33 → ST2/IL-1RAcP → ILC2 and Th2 activation; relevant in asthma and atopic contexts
  • PARP1: Caspase-1 cleavage inactivates PARP1, limiting DNA repair during pyroptosis
  • Cytoskeletal proteins: Cofilin, gelsolin — contribute to cellular morphology changes during pyroptosis
