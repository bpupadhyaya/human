Natural Killer Cell

Innate cytotoxic sentinel — a large granular lymphocyte that kills virus-infected and malignant cells without prior antigen sensitisation, guided by "missing-self" recognition and a balance of activating and inhibitory germline-encoded receptors.

NK cells constitute 5–15% of circulating lymphocytes and are especially enriched in the liver (~30–50% of hepatic lymphocytes). CD56dim CD16&sup+ cells (~90% of blood NK cells) are the cytotoxic effectors; CD56bright cells (~10%) are the primary IFN-γ producers. They bridge innate and adaptive immunity, are the mechanism of action for therapeutic antibodies (ADCC), and — in memory NK cells — blur the classical innate/adaptive boundary. CAR-NK cell therapies are in active clinical trials.

Natural Killer Cell

Lineage: Lymphoid (ILC group 1)  ·  Origin: Bone marrow CLP → NKP → mature NK  |  Scale: 04-Cellular  |  Phenotype: CD3− CD19− CD56&sup+ (NKp46&sup+)

Natural Killer (NK) cells are innate lymphocytes that provide rapid, antigen-independent cytotoxic surveillance against virus-infected, transformed, and cellular-stress-bearing cells. Discovered in 1975 by Kiessling and Herberman (independently), NK cells were named for their ability to kill tumour cell lines spontaneously in vitro without prior immunisation. Their target discrimination relies on the integration of activating and inhibitory signals from germline-encoded receptor arrays, embodying the missing-self hypothesis formulated by Klas Kärre: cells that lose MHC-I expression — as many tumour and virally infected cells do — become NK-cell targets because inhibitory KIR receptors lose their HLA ligands. Beyond direct cytotoxicity, NK cells secrete IFN-γ to activate macrophages and polarise Th1 responses, perform ADCC through CD16/FcγRIIIA (the mechanism of rituximab and trastuzumab), sculpt the dendritic cell pool through NK-DC crosstalk, and under CMV infection form long-lived memory-like cells that challenge the innate/adaptive boundary.

Overview

NK cells constitute approximately 5–15% of circulating peripheral blood lymphocytes (~150–500 million cells per adult) and are enriched in the liver (30–50% of hepatic lymphocytes), spleen, lung, uterine decidua, and bone marrow. They are phenotypically defined as CD3− CD19− CD56&sup+ lymphocytes; the CD56dim CD16&sup+ subset (~90% of blood NK cells) is cytotoxically dominant, while CD56bright CD16−/low NK cells (~10%) are the primary IFN-γ secretors and predominate in secondary lymphoid organs.

Unlike T and B cells, NK cells lack antigen-specific receptors generated by V(D)J recombination. Their target discrimination relies instead on the integration of activating and inhibitory signals from an array of germline-encoded receptors. This embodies the "missing-self" hypothesis formulated by Klas Kärre: cells that lose MHC-I expression (as many tumour cells and virally infected cells do) become NK-cell targets because inhibitory KIR receptors lose their HLA ligands, tipping the balance toward activation. Conversely, stressed cells upregulate activating ligands (MICA, MICB, ULBPs) that engage NKG2D, further promoting NK cell killing.

NK cell biology has major clinical relevance: primary NK deficiency causes recurrent severe herpesvirus infections (CMV, EBV, HSV); tumour immune evasion frequently involves MHC-I upregulation or NKG2D ligand shedding to escape NK surveillance; and allogeneic NK cell infusions and CAR-NK therapies are under active development in haematological and solid tumour trials. ADCC mediated by NK cell CD16 is the dominant effector mechanism of approved therapeutic antibodies including rituximab and trastuzumab.

Structure

FeatureDetail
Size & Shape 10–15 µm diameter. Large granular lymphocytes (LGLs) with kidney-shaped or bilobed nucleus. Abundant cytoplasm densely packed with azurophilic granules (specialised secretory lysosomes) visible on Giemsa stain — a defining morphological feature distinguishing NK cells from small lymphocytes.
Lytic granules Contain pre-formed: perforin (67 kDa, homologous to complement C9; membrane pore-forming), granzyme B (serine protease; cleaves caspase-3, Bid → rapid apoptosis), granzyme A (caspase-independent pathway via SETDB1 complex disruption), granulysin (antimicrobial peptide), LAMP-1/CD107a (granule membrane marker, surface degranulation reporter). Granules traffic to the immune synapse via MTOC reorientation within minutes of target recognition.
Cytoskeletal polarity Upon target recognition, the microtubule-organising centre (MTOC) reorients toward the immune synapse (lytic cleft) within ~1–5 minutes. This directs lytic granule secretion focally at the target cell interface, preventing collateral cytotoxicity to bystander cells. Killing occurs within ~30 minutes; NK cell retracts and can reform a new synapse against a new target within ~20 minutes (serial killing).
Surface markers Pan-NK: CD56, NKp46 (NCR1), CD161. CD56dim: CD16 (FcγRIII), CD57, KIR&sup+, KLRG1. CD56bright: CD94/NKG2A&sup+, CCR7&sup+, CXCR3&sup+, CD62L&sup+. Liver/tissue resident: CXCR6, CD49a (α1 integrin). Uterine dNK: CD56bright CD9&sup+ CD49a&sup+.
Special features Serial killing: NK cells can sequentially kill multiple targets without resetting. Licensing (education): NK cells become fully functional only if they express at least one inhibitory receptor (KIR or NKG2A) recognising a self-MHC-I ligand; un-licensed NK cells are hyporesponsive to self tissue, providing a safety mechanism against autoimmunity.

Function

Perforin-Granzyme Cytotoxicity

NK cell recognises target → forms immune synapse → MTOC reorients → lytic granules dock and fuse with plasma membrane → perforin monomers oligomerise in Ca²&sup+-dependent manner into 16 nm pores in target membrane. Granzyme B enters via pores (or mannose-6-phosphate receptor endocytosis) → cleaves caspase-3 and Bid → apoptosis within ~2 hours. Serial killing possible: NK retracts within ~20 min and re-engages new target.

ADCC & Antibody Cooperation

CD16 (FcγRIIIA) on CD56dim NK cells binds IgG Fc → ITAM activation → perforin-granzyme degranulation. Clinical significance: ADCC is the dominant mechanism of rituximab (B-cell lymphoma), trastuzumab (HER2+ breast cancer), cetuximab (colorectal cancer), and daratumumab (myeloma). CD16 V158F polymorphism affects Fc affinity and clinical response to these biologics.

IFN-γ & Cytokine Production

CD56bright NK cells are major early sources of IFN-γ (driven synergistically by IL-12 + IL-18 from macrophages/DCs). NK IFN-γ: (1) classically activates macrophages → enhanced microbicidal capacity; (2) drives Th1 polarisation of CD4&sup+ T cells; (3) upregulates MHC-I on surrounding cells; (4) promotes DC maturation. NK cells also produce GM-CSF, TNF-α, MIP-1α/β, CCL5/RANTES.

NK-DC Crosstalk

NK cells sculpt adaptive immunity by editing the DC pool: immature/tolerogenic DCs are killed by NK cells (NKp30 and NKG2D-mediated); mature/activated DCs are spared and instead activate NK cells via IL-12, IL-18, and contact-dependent signals. This bidirectional quality-control circuit ensures that only immunogenic DCs drive T-cell responses — NK cells shape both the innate-to-adaptive transition and subsequent immune magnitude.

FasL-Fas Apoptosis Pathway

  • Recognition: NK cell contacts target expressing Fas (CD95) or TRAIL-R1/R2 (DR4/DR5)
  • FasL/TRAIL engagement: NK surface FasL (CD95L) or TRAIL binds target death receptors — particularly important against tumour cells that upregulate Fas during transformation
  • DISC formation: Fas trimerisation → FADD recruitment → procaspase-8 clustering → caspase-8 activation (death-inducing signalling complex)
  • Caspase cascade: Active caspase-8 → caspase-3 (type II cells: Bid cleavage → mitochondrial pathway amplification via BAX/BAK → cytochrome c release → caspase-9 → caspase-3)
  • Apoptosis: Nuclear fragmentation, cell shrinkage, phosphatidylserine exposure, apoptotic body formation — ~4–8 hours (slower than granzyme B pathway)

Pathology

DiseaseMechanismClinical FeaturesTreatment
NK Cell Deficiency Primary NK deficiency (NKD): rare mutations in MCM4, GATA2, IRF8, RTEL1, or GINS1 leading to absent or severely reduced NK cells. NK cells are critical for CMV, EBV, and HSV control — T cells cannot fully compensate for missing NK perforin-mediated early cytotoxicity. Recurrent severe infections with herpesviruses (CMV retinitis, EBV-driven lymphoproliferation, HSV encephalitis); susceptibility to certain cancers. Distinguishable from T-cell deficiency by intact total lymphocyte count with absent NK subset on flow cytometry. Antiviral prophylaxis (ganciclovir for CMV, acyclovir for HSV/VZV); IVIG; HSCT for severe cases; gene therapy under investigation for GATA2 deficiency
Cancer Immune Evasion Tumours evade NK killing via: (1) MHC-I upregulation (restores KIR inhibition); (2) NKG2D ligand shedding (soluble MICA/B decoys); (3) HLA-E upregulation → NKG2A inhibition; (4) TGF-β, IL-10 in TME → NK functional exhaustion (PD-1, TIM-3, TIGIT upregulation). Impaired NK function correlates with poor prognosis in multiple cancer types. Tumour progression despite low mutational burden; resistance to therapeutic antibodies (poor ADCC); NK cell exhaustion markers (NKG2A&sup+, PD-1&sup+, low perforin) on tumour-infiltrating NK cells. NKG2A checkpoint blockade (monalizumab in phase II/III trials); anti-TIGIT (tiragolumab); IL-15 superagonist (N-803) to re-activate NK cells; allogeneic NK cell infusions; CAR-NK cells (anti-CD19, anti-CD70, anti-HER2 in trials)
Haemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) Primary HLH: biallelic mutations in PRF1 (perforin), UNC13D (Munc13-4), STX11, STXBP2, RAB27A, or LYST impair cytotoxic granule exocytosis in NK cells and CD8&sup+ T cells. Unable to kill EBV-infected macrophages → uncontrolled macrophage activation → cytokine storm (IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-18, TNF-α). Fever, splenomegaly, bicytopenia/pancytopenia, hyperferritinaemia (>10,000 ng/mL), hypertriglyceridaemia, absent/low NK cell cytotoxic activity (diagnostic criterion), haemophagocytosis on BM biopsy, elevated sCD25 (IL-2Rα). Untreated primary HLH is fatal; 50–70% 5-year survival with treatment. HLH-94/HLH-2004 protocol: etoposide + dexamethasone + cyclosporin; HSCT for primary HLH (curative); emapalumab (anti-IFN-γ, FDA-approved for primary/refractory HLH) depletes the cytokine driving macrophage activation
Recurrent Miscarriage / Preeclampsia Uterine decidual NK (dNK) cells normally remodel spiral arteries by secreting VEGF, PlGF, and angiopoietin-2 promoting trophoblast invasion. Aberrant dNK number or function correlates with failed placentation. HLA-C KIR compatibility is key: KIR AA mothers carrying KIR2DL1-mismatched HLA-C2 fetuses have elevated preeclampsia risk. Recurrent early pregnancy loss (≥3 consecutive); preeclampsia (hypertension + proteinuria >20 weeks); intrauterine growth restriction. Elevated uterine NK/T-cell ratio may be visible on endometrial biopsy. Progesterone supplementation; low-dose aspirin and LMWH for antiphospholipid syndrome subset; IVIG in selected cases; pre-implantation genetic testing for embryonic aneuploidy

The missing-self hypothesis — the conceptual foundation of NK cell biology: Klas Kärre's 1981 PhD thesis proposed that NK cells survey tissues for absence of self-MHC-I, killing cells that have lost it — the opposite of T-cell recognition (which requires MHC-I presentation). This explains why NK cells kill: (1) tumour cells that downregulate MHC-I to escape CD8&sup+ T cells; (2) virally infected cells where viral proteins interfere with antigen presentation (HCMV US2/US11 degrade MHC-I); (3) allogeneic cells lacking recipient-matching HLA ligands. The hypothesis remains the dominant conceptual framework 45 years after its formulation. Its implications extend to transplantation (KIR-HLA mismatch in BMT exploits missing-self for graft-vs-leukaemia effect) and cancer immunotherapy (NKG2A blockade removes inhibition on NKG2D-activated NK cells, simultaneously freeing NK cells and T cells).
