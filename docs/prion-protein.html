Prion Protein (PrP)

A misfolded host protein that is itself the infectious agent — the prion hypothesis: PrPSc (scrapie form) acts as a template to convert normal PrPC into abnormal β-sheet-rich aggregates that accumulate in the brain and are uniformly fatal.

No nucleic acid. No immune response that can clear it. No treatment. The prion concept, proposed by Stanley Prusiner (Nobel Prize 1997), overturned the central dogma of infectious disease and remains one of biology's most extraordinary mechanisms.

Prion Protein (PrPC / PrPSc)

Host-encoded glycoprotein (PRNP gene, chromosome 20) normally expressed as PrPC — a GPI-anchored copper-binding protein with normal physiological functions in synaptic transmission, neuroprotection, and circadian rhythm; upon misfolding to PrPSc (rich in β-sheets), it nucleates polymerisation of normal PrPC into fibrillar amyloid plaques and toxic oligomers, causing transmissible spongiform encephalopathies (TSEs) with vacuolation, neuronal loss, astrogliosis, and inevitably death.

Classification & Structure

ClassificationNot a conventional microorganism; protein-only infectious agent (prion); encoded by host PRNP gene (chromosome 20p13); no viral envelope, capsid, or nucleic acid; resists UV irradiation, formaldehyde, and most disinfectants
PrPC (normal)253 amino acids (before cleavage of 22-aa signal peptide + 23-aa GPI anchor signal); mature 208-aa protein; GPI-anchored to outer leaflet of plasma membrane; predominantly α-helical (42% α-helix, ~3% β-sheet); expressed in neurons, astrocytes, and peripheral tissues; functions include Cu²⁺ binding via octapeptide repeats, neuroprotection, synaptic maintenance
PrPSc (misfolded)Same primary sequence as PrPC; fundamentally different 3D structure: ~43% β-sheet, ~30% α-helix; insoluble in non-denaturing detergents; partially protease-resistant (PK-resistant core: PrP 27–30 kDa fragment); aggregates into amyloid fibrils and oligomers; acts as structural template for converting PrPC
PRNP geneSingle exon encoding PrP; codon 129 polymorphism (Met/Val) is the critical susceptibility determinant — MM homozygotes are susceptible to sporadic CJD and vCJD; MV/VV are partially protected; octapeptide repeat insertions (5–12 extra) cause inherited prion disease
GlycosylationTwo N-linked glycosylation sites (Asn-181, Asn-197); three glycoform populations (di-, mono-, un-glycosylated); glycoform ratio differs between prion disease subtypes — basis for Western blot "glycoform typing" (MM1, MV1, VV2, MM2C, MM2T, VV1) subtype classification in sCJD
StrainsDifferent PrPSc conformations encode different "strain" properties (incubation time, brain region tropism, glycoform pattern, clinical features) — conformational information passed protein-to-protein without nucleic acid; analogous to different crystal polymorphs
Prion replicationNucleated polymerisation: PrPSc + PrPC → [PrPSc·PrPC] complex → PrPC refolded to PrPSc → fibril growth → fragmentation generates new nucleation seeds (exponential amplification). Rate-limiting step is nucleation (spontaneous or seeded).
Stability / decontaminationExtraordinary resistance to conventional sterilisation: survives 134°C autoclave for 18 min (not 15 min standard), 20% bleach for 2h, 4 M guanidinium for partial inactivation; requires 1M NaOH + autoclaving or 20,000 ppm hypochlorite for safe decontamination of instruments; filter-sterilisation ineffective (no nucleic acid to degrade)

Pathogenesis

  • Prion entry & peripheral neuroinvasion Sporadic: spontaneous PrPC → PrPSc conversion in the brain (extremely rare stochastic event, ~1–2/million/year). Acquired (dietary, surgical, iatrogenic): PrPSc ingested or inoculated; taken up by gut M cells → Peyer's patches → follicular dendritic cells in lymphoid tissue (long peripheral replication phase) → sympathetic nervous system → dorsal root ganglia → spinal cord → brain. Neural spread follows retrograde axonal transport.
  • Nucleated polymerisation cascade in brain PrPSc seeds recruit PrPC molecules from neuronal surfaces; elongating fibrils fragment into smaller oligomers (most neurotoxic species) and new seeds. The rate of conversion and spread is exponential once seeded, but the incubation period spans decades because nucleation efficiency is extremely low until a critical seed concentration is reached. Different brain regions have different PrPC expression levels — determines the distribution of pathology.
  • Cellular toxicity mechanisms Mechanistic consensus remains incomplete; proposed mechanisms include: (1) toxic oligomers disrupting membrane integrity and synaptic function; (2) loss of normal PrPC function (copper binding, neuroprotection); (3) UPR (Unfolded Protein Response) activation → chronic ER stress → translational reprogramming → synaptic loss (proposed by Moreno et al.); (4) misfolded PrP trapped in ER activates PERK/eIF2α → global protein synthesis shutdown → neuronal death. Vacuolation (spongiform change) is the hallmark neuropathological finding.
  • Immune evasion — no adaptive immune response to PrPSc PrPSc is derived from a self-protein; the adaptive immune system does not recognise it as foreign (central tolerance). No antibody response is mounted during prion disease. The brain cannot clear the aggregates. Neuroinflammation (microglial activation) occurs but is largely ineffective and may contribute to pathology. Attempts to generate anti-PrP antibodies for therapy are hampered by tolerance and potential antibody toxicity (anti-PrP antibodies binding neuronal PrPC can cause neuronal apoptosis).
  • Codon 129 susceptibility & strain specificity Met129 homozygosity (MM) at PRNP codon 129 confers susceptibility to sCJD (MM accounts for ~70% of cases despite ~37% population frequency) and to vCJD (100% of definite cases were MM129). The valine allele is protective, possibly by creating a kinetically slower conversion template. Additional PRNP mutations (D178N, E200K, P102L, octapeptide repeats) cause familial prion diseases with distinct clinical features and strain properties.

Treatment & Research

  • No approved treatment exists As of 2026, no drug has demonstrated survival benefit in any human prion disease. Multiple therapeutic approaches have failed or remain early-stage: pentosan polysulfate (intracerebroventricular, ICV — no benefit), quinacrine (clinical trial negative), doxycycline (Italian trial: delayed but did not prevent death), flupirtine (symptom management only). Management is palliative: seizure control, myoclonus suppression (clonazepam), sedation, and supportive care.
  • PrPC lowering strategies — anti-sense oligonucleotides Since PrPSc requires PrPC as substrate, reducing PrPC expression should slow or halt conversion. ASOs targeting PRNP mRNA (IND2201; Ionis/Broad) have shown near-complete PrP knockdown in CNS of non-human primates with excellent tolerability. Phase 1/2 trial (PRION-1-PRNP; NCT05256173) underway in humans with genetic prion disease (2023–2025). This is the most mechanistically compelling approach currently in human trials.
  • Small molecule aggregation inhibitors & anti-PrP antibodies GN8 (binding PrPC to stabilise native fold), cpd-B (IND Japan), and PRN2246 (Prothena/Roche; humanised anti-PrP mAb) have shown some murine efficacy but no human translation yet. Concern about antibody toxicity (anti-PrP IgG binding to neuronal PrPC triggers clustering and apoptotic signalling) limits direct antibody approaches. Fab fragments and allosteric stabilisers are preferred formats.
  • RT-QuIC — ultra-sensitive antemortem diagnosis Real-Time Quaking-Induced Conversion (RT-QuIC) detects femtomolar PrPSc seeds in CSF and olfactory brushing with ~95% sensitivity and ~99% specificity for sCJD — transforming antemortem diagnosis. RT-QuIC is now standard of care at specialist centres. This diagnostic advance is critical for future therapeutic trials requiring early diagnosis.
  • Prevention: instrument decontamination & surveillance No vaccine possible (self-protein). Prevention focused on: neurosurgical instrument decontamination with NaOH/bleach protocols; surveillance networks (UK National CJD Research & Surveillance Unit); blood supply safety (leukodepletion reduces vCJD transfusion risk); cadaveric tissue registry; genetic counselling for PRNP mutation carriers regarding predictive testing.
