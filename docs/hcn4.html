HCN4 — Funny Channel

HCN4 (Hyperpolarization-activated Cyclic Nucleotide-gated channel 4, gene HCN4) — the molecular basis of the cardiac funny current (If). Activated by hyperpolarization, carries mixed Na⁺/K⁺ inward current, and is directly gated by cAMP. The dominant pacemaker channel of the SA node; the target of ivabradine for heart-rate reduction without affecting contractility.

Gene: HCN4 · 15q24.1 UniProt: Q9Y3Q4 Aliases: funny channel, If channel, pacemaker channel Scale: 03-Molecular

Overview

HCN4 is the molecular basis of the cardiac funny current (If) — named "funny" because it activates upon membrane hyperpolarization (opposite to most voltage-gated channels) and carries a mixed inward Na⁺/K⁺ current at physiological potentials. Discovered by Dario DiFrancesco, If is the primary current driving spontaneous diastolic depolarization (phase 4) in SA node pacemaker cells — the slow voltage rise from maximum diastolic potential (~−60 mV) to action potential threshold (~−40 mV).

HCN4's dual sensitivity to both membrane voltage and intracellular cAMP makes it the molecular integrator of autonomic heart rate control: sympathetic stimulation (↑cAMP via β1-AR) shifts the activation curve rightward, opening more channels at any given diastolic potential and speeding pacemaking; vagal stimulation (↓cAMP via M2/Gi) shifts it leftward, slowing pacemaking. This cAMP-mediated shift is the mechanistic basis of the chronotropic response to exercise and stress.

Drug target: Ivabradine is a specific use-dependent open-channel blocker of HCN4 (and HCN1). It reduces heart rate by 10–15 bpm without affecting contractility — approved for stable angina and HFrEF with HR >70 bpm on maximally-tolerated beta-blocker. The first clinically validated If-blocking drug.

HCN Family Context

HCN channels belong to the voltage-gated channel superfamily but are evolutionarily related to both Kv channels and cyclic-nucleotide-gated (CNG) channels. Four isoforms exist (HCN1–4) with different kinetics and cAMP sensitivities. HCN4 is the dominant cardiac isoform (SA node >80% of If); HCN1 is fast (brain/heart); HCN2 is widely distributed; HCN3 is minor.

Ion Selectivity

HCN4 carries Na⁺ and K⁺ inward at diastolic potentials. The reversal potential is approximately −25 to −35 mV (between ENa and EK). At the maximum diastolic potential (~−60 mV), the net current is inward, providing the depolarizing drive. The contribution of If to total diastolic depolarizing current in central SA node cells is approximately 10–30%, with the remainder from ICaT and the Ca²⁺ clock.
