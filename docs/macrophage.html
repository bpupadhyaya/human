Macrophage

Professional phagocyte and master innate immune regulator — a long-lived, tissue-resident sentinel that devours pathogens, clears apoptotic debris, and orchestrates inflammation through cytokine secretion and antigen presentation.

Derived from circulating monocytes or embryonic precursors, macrophages inhabit virtually every tissue. Tissue-resident forms include Kupffer cells (liver), microglia (brain), and alveolar macrophages (lung). Upon TLR or NLR activation, macrophages secrete IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, and reactive oxygen species within minutes, linking pathogen detection directly to systemic inflammation. Their polarisation between M1 (pro-inflammatory) and M2 (tissue-repair) states underpins diverse disease processes from atherosclerosis to cancer.

Macrophage

Lineage: Myeloid  ·  Origin: Monocyte / Embryonic yolk-sac progenitor  |  Scale: 04-Cellular  |  Phenotype: CD14&sup+ CD11b&sup+ MHC-II&sup+ (professional APC)

Macrophages are the most versatile cellular players of innate immunity. Their name — from Greek makros (large) and phagein (to eat) — reflects their defining capacity to engulf and destroy targets ranging from bacterial pathogens to senescent red blood cells. Beyond phagocytosis, macrophages are master regulators of inflammation: they sense danger through an array of pattern-recognition receptors (TLRs, NLRs, scavenger receptors, complement receptors), rapidly produce a burst of pro-inflammatory mediators, present antigens to T cells, and ultimately resolve inflammation and remodel tissue. Their functional identity is profoundly shaped by tissue context and activation state, existing on a spectrum from classically activated M1 (bactericidal, tumouricidal) to alternatively activated M2 (wound-healing, tolerogenic). Dysregulation of this balance underlies atherosclerosis, NAFLD/NASH, cancer immune evasion, and neurodegeneration.

Overview

Macrophages are long-lived, tissue-resident phagocytes and innate immune effectors that form the first line of defence against pathogens, coordinate inflammation, and maintain tissue homeostasis through continuous surveillance and clearance of apoptotic cells, cellular debris, and foreign material. The adult human body harbours an estimated 10¹¹ macrophages. In steady state, macrophages perform constitutive efferocytosis (clearance of ~2×10¹¹ senescent erythrocytes per day), pattern-recognition surveillance via Toll-like receptors (TLRs 1–9), NOD-like receptors (NLRs), scavenger receptors (SR-A, CD36), and complement receptors (CR1, CR3).

In adult humans, macrophages exist in two principal pools: (1) monocyte-derived macrophages that differentiate from circulating Ly6C&sup+ classical monocytes following tissue infiltration, and (2) tissue-resident macrophages (TRMs) seeded during embryonic development from yolk-sac progenitors and fetal liver monocytes. TRMs self-renew locally and are remarkably long-lived; Kupffer cells (liver), microglia (CNS), Langerhans cells (skin), and alveolar macrophages (lung) are paradigmatic examples. This ontological duality — embryonic vs. monocyte-derived — profoundly shapes functional identity and tissue-specific gene expression.

Pathologically, macrophage dysregulation is central to atherosclerotic plaque formation (foam cells), sterile inflammation in NAFLD/NASH (Kupffer cell activation), neurodegeneration (microglial activation in Alzheimer's disease), and tumour immune evasion (tumour-associated macrophages, TAMs). Understanding macrophage biology is therefore essential for cardiovascular medicine, oncology, and metabolic disease.

Function

Macrophages are functionally pleiotropic, acting as phagocytes, secretory cells, antigen-presenting cells, and tissue-repair coordinators. Key functions are illustrated below:

Phagocytosis & Killing

PRR engagement or opsonisation (IgG-FcγR, C3b-CR3) drives pseudopodal engulfment into phagosomes. Phagosome–lysosome fusion generates reactive oxygen species (O²•− via NADPH oxidase → H²O² → HOCl via myeloperoxidase) and reactive nitrogen species (NO via iNOS/NOS2 → peroxynitrite ONOO−). Combined, these kill even intracellular pathogens such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis and Listeria monocytogenes.

Cytokine Secretion

LPS → TLR4 → MyD88/TRIF → IRAK4 → TRAF6 → NF-κB p65/p50 nuclear translocation → TNF, IL6, IL1B, IL12A/B, CXCL8 transcription within 30–60 min. TNF-α triggers endothelial activation, fever, and acute-phase response. IL-12 + IL-18 synergise to activate NK cells and drive Th1 CD4&sup+ T-cell polarisation.

Antigen Presentation

After phagocytosis, peptides are loaded onto MHC-II and transported to the cell surface. MHC-II + peptide + CD86 co-stimulation is recognised by CD4&sup+ T-helper cells, licensing adaptive immunity. Cross-presentation of exogenous antigens on MHC-I also enables CD8&sup+ T-cell priming — critical for anti-tumour responses.

Tissue Homeostasis

Kupffer cells clear immune complexes and gut-derived LPS from portal blood, preventing systemic endotoxaemia. Alveolar macrophages clear surfactant and inhaled particles. Osteoclasts (monocyte/macrophage lineage) remodel bone. Microglia prune synapses during development and clear amyloid-β in the ageing brain. Efferocytosis of apoptotic cells suppresses inflammation via PGE² and TGF-β release.

M1 / M2 Polarisation Spectrum

  • M1 (classical activation): IFN-γ + LPS → STAT1 signalling → pro-inflammatory phenotype: high iNOS, IL-12, IL-23, TNF-α, ROS; low IL-10. Metabolically: aerobic glycolysis (succinate accumulation, HIF-1α active). Kills intracellular pathogens and tumour cells.
  • M2 (alternative activation): IL-4 / IL-13 → STAT6 signalling → anti-inflammatory / tissue-repair phenotype: high arginase-1 (Arg1), IL-10, TGF-β, CD206, VEGF; low TNF-α, iNOS. Supports wound healing, fibrosis, and helminth responses. Tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs) are largely M2-like, promoting immune evasion and angiogenesis.
  • M(IL-10): IL-10 → STAT3 → regulatory/tolerogenic phenotype; suppresses M1-driven inflammation in mucosa and liver; central to resolution of inflammatory episodes.
  • Note on the spectrum: Real-world macrophages exist on a continuum with mixed transcriptional states, especially in chronic disease. The M1/M2 dichotomy is a useful simplification that oversimplifies the full transcriptional diversity observed by single-cell RNA-seq.

Atherosclerosis Monocyte recruitment into sub-intimal space → uptake of oxidised LDL via scavenger receptors (SR-A, CD36) → lipid-laden foam cells. NLRP3 inflammasome activation by cholesterol crystals → IL-1β release → plaque inflammation. Impaired cholesterol efflux (ABCA1/ABCG1) traps macrophages in plaque. Coronary artery disease, angina, myocardial infarction, stroke; vulnerable plaque rupture driven by MMP secretion from macrophages Statins reduce LDL (less oxLDL substrate); PCSK9 inhibitors; canakinumab (anti-IL-1β, CANTOS trial) reduced cardiovascular events; colchicine (NLRP3 inhibition in COLCOT trial)
NAFLD / NASH Hepatic steatosis → Kupffer cell activation (TLR4 by gut-derived LPS, TLR9 by mitochondrial DNA from hepatocytes) → TNF-α, IL-6, ROS → hepatocyte apoptosis and stellate cell activation → fibrosis. Monocyte-derived macrophages infiltrate during NASH, amplifying inflammation. Elevated liver enzymes, steatohepatitis on biopsy, progression to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma in a minority Weight loss (reduces portal LPS flux); semaglutide / tirzepatide (GLP-1 agonists reduce hepatic fat and Kupffer cell activation); anti-fibrotic trials ongoing (CCR2/CCR5 antagonist cenicriviroc)
Sepsis Uncontrolled systemic macrophage / monocyte activation → cytokine storm (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) → vascular leak, hypotension, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), multi-organ failure. HLH (haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis) is the extreme end: macrophage activation syndrome with massive cytokine release and uncontrolled phagocytosis. Fever, hypotension, organ failure, 30–50% mortality in septic shock. HLH: pancytopenia, hyperferritinaemia (>10,000 ng/mL), liver failure, CNS involvement Broad-spectrum antibiotics + source control for sepsis; tocilizumab (anti-IL-6R), anakinra (anti-IL-1R), dexamethasone, etoposide + cyclosporin for HLH/MAS
Tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs) Tumour microenvironment (TME) skews infiltrating macrophages toward M2-like TAM phenotype via IL-10, TGF-β, CSF1, and hypoxia. TAMs suppress cytotoxic T cells (IL-10, TGF-β, PD-L1 expression), promote angiogenesis (VEGF), and remodel ECM (MMP secretion). High TAM density correlates with poor prognosis in breast, colorectal, and lung cancer. Immune evasion, resistance to immunotherapy; high TAM density is an adverse prognostic marker in many cancers Anti-PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors; anti-CSF1R (pexidartinib) depletes TAMs in TGCT; CD47 "don't eat me signal" blockade (magrolimab) promotes macrophage phagocytosis of tumour cells
Alzheimer's Disease Microglia fail to clear amyloid-β plaques efficiently in ageing brain (impaired TREM2-driven phagocytosis); alternatively, hyperactivated microglia produce TNF-α and IL-1β driving neuroinflammation and synaptic pruning dysregulation. TREM2 loss-of-function variants (R47H) increase AD risk ~3-fold. Progressive cognitive decline; amyloid plaques and tau neurofibrillary tangles; neuroinflammation visible on PET imaging (TSPO tracer) Lecanemab / donanemab (anti-amyloid antibodies) engage Fc receptors on microglia to promote amyloid clearance; anti-TREM2 agonist antibodies in clinical trials to restore microglial phagocytic function
