Histamine

The pleiotropic inflammatory/allergic amine — stored preformed in mast cell granules and released within seconds to mediate type I hypersensitivity, gastric acid, CNS wakefulness, and neuroinflammation via four GPCR subtypes.

MW 111.15 Da. Synthesised from L-histidine by histidine decarboxylase (HDC, PLP-dependent) in mast cells, basophils, ECL cells of stomach, and tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN) neurons. Four receptors: H1R (Gq — allergy/wakefulness), H2R (Gs — gastric acid), H3R (Gi autoreceptor — CNS modulation), H4R (Gi — immune chemotaxis). Degraded by HNMT (CNS) and DAO (peripheral).

Histamine

Class: Biogenic amine  ·  Immune mediator  ·  Neurotransmitter (CNS)  ·  Paracrine hormone (stomach)  |  Biosynthesis: HDC (gene: HDC)  |  Degradation: HNMT (CNS), DAO (peripheral)

Histamine (2-(4-imidazolyl)ethylamine; MW 111) is one of the oldest-known chemical mediators, recognised in the early 20th century as the principal driver of immediate hypersensitivity reactions and later appreciated as a far more pleiotropic signal spanning immunity, gastric physiology, CNS arousal, and neuroinflammation. It is stored preformed in the acidic secretory granules of mast cells and basophils at very high concentrations (~0.3–1 mM), allowing instant release upon IgE-FcεRI crosslinking — making histamine the fastest-acting immune mediator. The four histamine GPCRs (H1R–H4R) mediate distinct and sometimes opposing effects: H1R drives the cardinal signs of allergy and maintains cortical wakefulness; H2R stimulates gastric HCl secretion; H3R is a presynaptic autoreceptor brake in the CNS; H4R directs immune cell chemotaxis. This receptor diversity explains why selective antihistamines targeting H1 (allergy), H2 (peptic ulcer), or H3 (narcolepsy/cognition) are among the most-used drugs in medicine.

Structure & Chemistry

Histamine consists of an imidazole ring (with two nitrogens, Nτ and Nπ) attached to an ethylamine side chain. At physiological pH (~7.4), histamine is predominantly present as the monocationic form (amino group protonated, pKa ~9.4; imidazole Nτ protonated, pKa ~5.9). The imidazole ring is critical for receptor binding — the two ring nitrogens engage in hydrogen-bonding and ionic interactions within the H1R, H2R, and H3R binding pockets in distinct orientations. Histamine has no reuptake transporter analogous to monoamines; CNS histamine is primarily inactivated by HNMT (histamine N-methyltransferase) methylation, while peripheral histamine is oxidised by DAO (diamine oxidase).

  Histamine → 4 GPCR subtypes:
  ├─ H1R (Gq → PLC → IP3/DAG → ↑Ca²⁺): allergy, wakefulness, bronchoconstriction
  ├─ H2R (Gs → ↑cAMP → PKA): gastric HCl, positive cardiac chronotropy
  ├─ H3R (Gi, presynaptic autoreceptor): ↓histamine + DA/NE/ACh/5-HT release in CNS
  └─ H4R (Gi/Gq): mast cell/eosinophil chemotaxis; itch; DC trafficking

  DEGRADATION:
  ├─ HNMT (cytosolic, CNS) + MAO-B → N-methylhistamine → N-methylimidazole acetic acid
  └─ DAO (extracellular, gut/placenta) → imidazole acetaldehyde → imidazole acetic acid

Pitolisant H3R inverse agonist/antagonist Blocks H3R autoreceptors → ↑histamine release from TMN → ↑wakefulness; also increases ACh, DA, NE via H3 heteroreceptors Narcolepsy with or without cataplexy; excessive daytime sleepiness

Sedating vs non-sedating antihistamines — mechanism: First-generation H1 antihistamines (diphenhydramine) are lipophilic and readily cross the BBB → block H1R on cortical neurons (removing wake-promoting histaminergic tone from TMN) + block mAChR (causing delirium in elderly) + block α1AR (orthostasis). Second-generation agents (fexofenadine, bilastine) are substrates of P-glycoprotein (MDR1) at the blood-brain barrier and are actively effluxed from the CNS — hence non-sedating at therapeutic doses. Cetirizine and loratadine have intermediate CNS penetration.
