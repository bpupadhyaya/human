MMR Vaccine — M-M-R II / Priorix

Platform: Three live-attenuated viruses (measles + mumps + rubella)  |  Route: Subcutaneous  |  Schedule: Dose 1 at 12–15 months; Dose 2 at 4–6 years

Triple live-attenuated combination vaccine containing independently developed, separately propagated attenuated strains: measles (Edmonston B / Schwarz-Moraten), mumps (Jeryl Lynn B), and rubella (RA 27/3). First licensed in 1971; current M-M-R II formulation in 1978. Two-dose series achieves >97% seroconversion for all three components. Measles eliminated from the US in 2000; congenital rubella syndrome eliminated from the Americas in 2015. The MMR-autism claim (Wakefield, Lancet 1998) was fraudulent; the paper was fully retracted in 2010. More than 15 large cohort studies including Hviid 2019 (n=650,172, RR=0.93) show no association between MMR and autism spectrum disorder.

M-M-R II (Merck) Priorix (GSK) Tresivac (Serum Institute) MMRV (+ Oka VZV: ProQuad / Priorix-Tetra)

Overview

The MMR vaccine contains three live-attenuated viruses each with its own origin story:

Measles (Edmonston B / Schwarz-Moraten): Wild measles virus isolated by John Enders and Thomas Peebles in 1954 from David Edmonston (Fay School, Massachusetts). The Edmonston strain was serially passaged through human kidney cells, amnion cells, and chick embryo fibroblasts over years to produce attenuated variants. Merck M-M-R II uses Edmonston B; Priorix uses the Schwarz or Moraten derivatives — related strains with slightly different passage histories but equivalent immunogenicity.

Mumps (Jeryl Lynn B): Isolated by Maurice Hilleman in 1963 from his daughter Jeryl Lynn's throat swab during her mumps illness. Passaged in embryonated eggs and chick embryo fibroblast cells. The vaccine actually contains two genetic subpopulations (JL1 and JL2 at ~80:20 ratio). Jeryl Lynn remains the most widely used mumps strain globally with the strongest safety record.

Rubella (RA 27/3): Developed by Stanley Plotkin at the Wistar Institute, Philadelphia, from a fetal specimen during the 1964–65 rubella pandemic (RA = Rubella Abortus; 27/3 = 27th specimen, 3rd passage), passaged in WI-38 human diploid cells. The 1964–65 pandemic caused >11,000 fetal deaths and 20,000 infants born with congenital rubella syndrome (CRS) in the US alone. RA 27/3 replaced earlier rubella strains due to superior immunogenicity — high-titre IgG and mucosal sIgA against the E1 glycoprotein.

  1. Manufacturing: Each virus is independently propagated in cell culture (chick embryo fibroblasts for measles and mumps; WI-38 or MRC-5 human diploid cells for rubella); clarified and concentrated separately; quality-controlled for titer, identity, sterility, and absence of adventitious agents.
  2. Lyophilization: The three viral bulks are blended with stabiliser (sorbitol, hydrolyzed gelatin, mannitol) and lyophilized (freeze-dried) into powder; this extends shelf life and allows reconstitution immediately before use.
  3. Subcutaneous injection: Reconstituted with supplied diluent; 0.5 mL SC (not IM); outer upper arm or anterolateral thigh; SC avoids muscle inflammation and maximises lymphoid uptake via draining axillary/inguinal lymph nodes.
  4. Replication and antigen presentation: Attenuated viruses undergo limited replication for 5–14 days post-injection; sustained antigen presentation to DCs → MHC I (CD8&sup+ CTL) and MHC II (CD4&sup+ T helper) → germinal centers in draining lymph nodes.
  5. Protective immunity: Neutralising IgG against measles H/F proteins, mumps HN/F proteins, and rubella E1 glycoprotein; CD8&sup+ CTL memory (critical for measles clearance); CD4&sup+ Th1/Th2 memory providing B-cell help on re-encounter.

Immunogenicity & Herd Immunity

Measles — Neutralising IgG

Anti-measles IgG (anti-H + anti-F) ≥120 mIU/mL (ELISA) correlates with protection; ≥1:8 PRNT. Dose 1: 90–95% seroconversion; Dose 2: >97%. CD8&sup+ CTL memory is critical for viral clearance — measles drives strong Th1 + CTL responses. Memory persists for decades.

Mumps — Serological Response

Anti-mumps neutralising Ab ≥1:4 (approximate protective threshold; no WHO standard). Dose 1: 85–95% seroconversion; Dose 2: >90%. Mumps provides the most modest protection of the three components; waning immunity has contributed to mumps outbreaks in highly vaccinated college populations.

Rubella — Anti-E1 IgG

Anti-rubella IgG ≥10 IU/mL (HAI or ELISA) for CRS protection; ≥15 IU/mL (WHO recommendation). Dose 1: 95–99%; Dose 2: ~99%. RA 27/3 also induces mucosal sIgA — may explain superior protection vs. earlier rubella strains. Immunity durable; CRS eliminated from vaccinated populations.

Herd Immunity — Measles (R0 = 12–18)

Measles R0 is 12–18 — one of the highest of any human pathogen. Herd immunity threshold: ≥95% population immunity. Even pockets of 85–90% coverage can sustain transmission. The 2019 US outbreak (1,282 cases, largest since 1992) occurred in communities with coverage below 95% following Wakefield-driven hesitancy.

The Wakefield Fraud — Scientific Record

Wakefield 1998 Lancet: RETRACTED February 2010

In February 1998, Andrew Wakefield et al. published a case series in The Lancet (n=12 children) claiming MMR vaccination caused bowel inflammation leading to autism. The paper was fraudulent:

Data fabrication: Investigative journalist Brian Deer demonstrated through medical records that Wakefield misrepresented 11 of the 12 children's clinical data — altering dates of symptom onset, diagnoses, and clinical findings to create a false temporal association with MMR.

Undisclosed conflicts: Wakefield had received £55,000 (equivalent to ~£450,000 today) from plaintiff attorneys seeking evidence against vaccine manufacturers, without disclosing this to The Lancet or co-authors.

Ethics violations: The lumbar punctures and colonoscopies performed on the children were not clinically indicated and lacked proper ethical approval.

The Lancet issued a full retraction on February 2, 2010. The UK General Medical Council struck Wakefield from the medical register on May 24, 2010 (findings of serious professional misconduct). The BMJ published Deer's full investigation in 2011, calling it an "elaborate fraud."

The scientific evidence: More than 15 large epidemiological studies involving >1.5 million children have found no association between MMR and autism. The 2019 Danish cohort (Hviid et al., Ann Intern Med, n=650,172) — the definitive study — found RR=0.93 (95% CI 0.85–1.02) for autism among vaccinated vs. unvaccinated children: no increased risk.

Safety Profile

  • Common Low-grade fever + mild measles-like rash — Fever in 5–15% at days 5–12 post-dose (measles component replication); transient maculopapular rash in ~5%. Not contagious. Self-limiting <3 days.
  • Common (adult women) Rubella arthralgia / arthritis — Transient joint pain and stiffness in 10–25% of post-pubertal women (RA 27/3 rubella component); onset days 10–21; resolves <1 week; no long-term joint sequelae.
  • Uncommon Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) — Transient platelet reduction at ~1:30,000–40,000 doses; self-limiting (platelets typically >50,000/µL); treatment rarely needed; no chronic ITP attributable to MMR. The risk from wild measles infection (1:6,000) vastly exceeds vaccine-related risk.
  • Uncommon Febrile seizure — ~1:3,000 doses; associated with fever from measles component (days 5–12); no long-term neurological sequelae; higher with MMRV (~1:1,250) vs. MMR+V separately.
  • Very Rare Anaphylaxis — ~1–2 per million doses; gelatin or neomycin allergy most likely (not egg allergy — MMR is safe in egg-allergic individuals).
  • Serious (immunocompromised) Vaccine virus disease — Live viruses can cause severe progressive disease (measles inclusion body encephalitis, progressive rubella panencephalitis) in severely immunocompromised individuals. Contraindicated in SCID, hematologic malignancy with severe lymphopenia, high-dose immunosuppression.
  • No association Autism, IBD, Crohn's disease, SIDS, asthma, atopy — All definitively refuted by Cochrane review (57 studies), Hviid 2019 (n=650,172), and IOM 2011 causality review. Wild measles encephalitis (1:1,000 cases) and SSPE (1:25,000 cases; caused by persistent wild-type measles) are far more common and serious — MMR prevents both.

Administration

ParameterDetails
Dose 112–15 months; 0.5 mL SC; outer upper arm (deltoid region) or anterolateral thigh
Dose 24–6 years (school entry); ≥4 weeks minimum interval after dose 1 (catch-up)
Catch-up / outbreakAny unvaccinated individual ≥12 months may receive 2 doses ≥4 weeks apart; 3rd dose recommended in outbreak settings for those with prior 2 doses
StorageLyophilized powder: 2–8°C; protect from light (UV degrades live virus). Reconstituted vaccine: use within 8 hours at 2–8°C in dark. Discard unused reconstituted vaccine. Do not freeze reconstituted vaccine.
ContraindicationsSevere immunodeficiency (SCID, malignancy with severe lymphopenia, >2 mg/kg/day prednisone ≥14 days); pregnancy (avoid for 4 weeks post-MMR); prior anaphylaxis to MMR component. Egg allergy: NOT a contraindication.
Special situationsHIV: can receive if CD4 ≥200/µL; routine MMR in asymptomatic HIV. Post-exposure prophylaxis: MMR within 72h of measles exposure provides protection. Pregnant contacts: give MMR to non-immune contacts (not the pregnant person), provide measles IG if pregnant and unvaccinated.
