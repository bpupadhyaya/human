Clostridioides difficile

The spore-armoured hospital pathogen. Antibiotic-disrupted microbiome, two glucosyltransferase toxins, and a destroyed colonic epithelium — C. diff causes 500,000 US infections and 30,000 deaths annually.

Reclassified from Clostridium in 2016, this obligate anaerobe survives outside the host as alcohol-resistant endospores that persist on surfaces for months. Disease arises almost exclusively when antibiotics collapse colonisation resistance. TcdA and TcdB glucosylate Rho GTPases, destroying the colonic epithelium. The hypervirulent NAP1/B1/027 ribotype amplifies toxin output via a mutated negative regulator and adds binary toxin CDT, driving outbreaks with elevated mortality and recurrence.

Clostridioides difficile

Obligate anaerobic Gram-positive spore-forming rod; dominant cause of healthcare-associated infectious diarrhoea in high-income countries. The 19.6 kb pathogenicity locus (PaLoc) encodes TcdA (309 kDa enterotoxin) and TcdB (270 kDa cytotoxin) — both glucosyltransferases that inactivate Rho GTPases by UDP-glucose transfer onto Thr37 of RhoA, locking them in their GDP-inactive form and collapsing the actin cytoskeleton, tight junctions, and epithelial barrier. Disease arises almost exclusively in antibiotic-disrupted colonic microbiota. The NAP1/B1/027 hypervirulent ribotype carries a tcdC deletion (loss of negative regulation → 16–23× higher toxin output), binary toxin CDT (ADP-ribosylates actin → enhanced bacterial adhesion), and fluoroquinolone resistance — the combination driving early-2000s outbreaks. Spores resist alcohol hand gels and most disinfectants; only soap-and-water and bleach are effective.

Pathogenesis

1 · Antibiotic-CDI cascade — microbiome collapse

CDI requires antibiotic-disrupted colonisation resistance. Clindamycin, fluoroquinolones, 3rd-generation cephalosporins, and broad-spectrum penicillins (in descending order of risk) deplete Bacteroidetes and Lachnospiraceae. Clostridium scindens and related secondary bile acid producers are eliminated; primary bile acid taurocholate accumulates and stimulates spore germination. C. difficile colonises the vacated niche and produces toxins. Even a single perioperative antibiotic dose can precipitate CDI in susceptible hosts (>65 years, prior CDI, PPI use, IBD, immunosuppression).

2 · TcdB receptor binding — the dominant cytotoxin

TcdB (270 kDa) binds Frizzled receptors (FZD1, FZD2, FZD7), PVRL3 (nectin-3), and CSPG4 on colonocytes with 10–1,000× higher potency than TcdA in human colonic epithelium. Receptor-mediated endocytosis delivers TcdB to late endosomes; acidic pH (pH 4–5) drives conformational change of the hydrophobic loop → pore formation → translocation of the N-terminal glucosyltransferase domain into the cytosol. TcdA uses carbohydrate receptors with the same subsequent mechanism.

3 · Rho GTPase glucosylation — cytoskeletal collapse

Both TcdA and TcdB transfer UDP-glucose onto Thr37 of RhoA (and Thr35 of Rac1, Cdc42). This mono-O-glucosylation sterically blocks guanine exchange factor (GEF) binding, locking Rho GTPases in their GDP-inactive form. Downstream: F-actin depolymerisation, stress fibre loss, cell rounding, tight junction breakdown, and colonocyte apoptosis → epithelial barrier failure → diarrhoea. TcdB additionally activates the Pyrin inflammasome via RHOA glucosylation (Pyrin de-repression → IL-1β/IL-18 maturation), driving intense mucosal inflammation and neutrophil recruitment.

4 · Binary toxin CDT — actin ADP-ribosylation and enhanced adhesion (NAP1/027)

CdtA (ADP-ribosyltransferase) ADP-ribosylates actin at Arg177, sequestering G-actin and depolymerising F-actin. CdtB (binding component) docks on LSR receptor on colonocytes. CDT-mediated actin disruption paradoxically induces microtubule-dependent protrusions from the bacterial surface, dramatically increasing bacterial adhesion to colonocyte surfaces despite actin loss — explaining the higher virulence and colonisation persistence of CDT-positive strains.

5 · Immune evasion — spore biology and IgG threshold

Spores resist neutrophil and macrophage killing, alcohol hand gels, and standard disinfectants. Only soap-and-water mechanically removes spores from skin; bleach solutions decontaminate surfaces. Patients with low serum anti-TcdA/TcdB IgG titres develop symptomatic CDI; high titres (from prior exposure or natural immunity) correlate with asymptomatic colonisation. This immunological threshold is the rationale for bezlotoxumab (anti-TcdB monoclonal antibody) as adjunct therapy.

6 · NAP1/027 hypervirulence — tcdC deletion removes toxin brake

TcdC is an anti-sigma factor that negatively regulates TcdR-driven tcdA/tcdB transcription. In NAP1/B1/027, a 39-bp tcdC deletion plus a single-base frameshift produce truncated, non-functional TcdC, removing the negative regulatory brake and yielding 16–23× higher TcdB output in vitro. Combined with CDT and fluoroquinolone resistance (gyrA/gyrB mutations), this ribotype drove epidemic spread with elevated mortality and recurrence rates in healthcare facilities worldwide.

Treatment & Prophylaxis

Fidaxomicin (preferred 1st line)Narrow-spectrum macrocyclic lactone RNA polymerase inhibitor (distinct site from rifamycins); minimal impact on Bacteroidetes (preserves colonisation resistance recovery); reduces spore shedding vs. vancomycin; ~40% relative reduction in recurrence (pivotal RCTs); dose 200 mg PO twice daily × 10 days; extended pulse-taper regimen for recurrence; FDA-approved first and second line.
Vancomycin oral (alternative/fulminant)Non-absorbed glycopeptide; inhibits cell wall peptidoglycan cross-linking; 125 mg PO QID × 10 days (non-severe/severe); 500 mg QID for fulminant + IV metronidazole; vancomycin taper-pulse regimen (125 mg QID × 10d → taper over 6 weeks) for recurrence prevention.
Metronidazole IV (adjunct)Adjunct for fulminant CDI only (500 mg IV TID alongside oral/NG vancomycin); no longer recommended as monotherapy (inferior efficacy vs. vancomycin/fidaxomicin in trials).
FMT — Faecal Microbiota TransplantRestores colonisation resistance via screened-donor microbiome engraftment; ~85–90% cure rate for ≥2nd recurrence; routes: colonoscopy (highest efficacy), nasogastric tube, oral capsule (equivalent efficacy in RCTs). FDA-approved live biotherapeutics: SER-109 (oral spore-based product; 2023), RBX2660 (enema-based; 2023).
Bezlotoxumab (recurrence prevention)Human monoclonal IgG1 antibody targeting TcdB binding domain; single IV infusion 10 mg/kg concurrent with antibiotic therapy; reduces recurrence by ~40% in high-risk patients (≥65 years, NAP1/027 strain, severe CDI, prior CDI episode, immunosuppression); FDA-approved 2016.
Resistance profileMetronidazole MIC creep documented (reduced susceptibility without formal resistance); fidaxomicin resistance rare (rpoB mutations); vancomycin resistance not clinically significant in C. difficile; fluoroquinolone resistance common in NAP1/027 (gyrA/gyrB mutations — major contributor to epidemic spread).
Infection controlContact precautions (gown + gloves) for duration of diarrhoea; private room or CDI cohorting; soap-and-water handwashing (alcohol gel insufficient for spore removal); sodium hypochlorite (0.5% minimum) surface decontamination; prolonged precautions (≥48h after diarrhoea resolution — many guidelines recommend for full hospitalisation); antibiotic stewardship is the single most impactful prevention intervention.
