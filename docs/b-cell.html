B Cell

B lymphocyte — the antibody-producing cell of adaptive humoral immunity. Its surface IgM/IgD BCR binds antigen in native 3D conformation; with obligatory Tfh help (CD40L + IL-21), it enters germinal centers, undergoes somatic hypermutation and affinity maturation, and exits as long-lived plasma cells or memory B cells. The durability of vaccination depends entirely on this lineage.

Overview

The B lymphocyte is the antibody-producing cell of adaptive immunity and the cellular basis of humoral protection against extracellular pathogens. The "B" designation derives from the bursa of Fabricius in birds where B cells were first identified; in mammals, B cell development occurs in the bone marrow. Unlike T cells, B cells recognize antigen directly — in its native three-dimensional form — via the B cell receptor (BCR), a membrane-bound immunoglobulin. This capacity makes B cells exquisitely sensitive to the structural features of pathogens and vaccine antigens.

The central output of B cell biology is the production of high-affinity, class-switched antibodies — particularly IgG — that neutralize pathogens, opsonize bacteria, activate complement, and mediate antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). The durability of protection depends on two B cell descendants: long-lived plasma cells (LLPCs) in the bone marrow that continuously secrete antibody for years, and memory B cells that enable rapid re-expansion on re-exposure. Vaccination strategies are fundamentally designed to elicit potent germinal center reactions generating both populations.

Function — Germinal Center Reaction

The germinal center (GC) is the specialized microanatomical structure within secondary lymphoid follicles where B cells are refined into high-affinity antibody producers:

  1. Entry: Antigen-activated B cells interact with antigen-specific Tfh cells at the follicle-T zone border; ~10–20% seed germinal centers.
  2. Dark zone: B cells proliferate at high rate and undergo somatic hypermutation (SHM) — AID introduces point mutations in V region genes at ~10⁻³/bp/division, generating BCR variants with altered affinities.
  3. Light zone selection: B cells compete for limiting antigen on follicular DCs (FDCs); clones with higher-affinity BCRs capture more antigen, present more pMHC-II, receive more Tfh help (CD40L + IL-21), and proliferate preferentially — affinity maturation.
  4. Class-switch recombination (CSR): AID introduces double-strand breaks at switch regions; cytokines specify the isotype (IL-4 → IgG1/IgE; IFN-γ → IgG3; TGF-β → IgA).
  5. Exit: Selected B cells exit as plasmablasts (rapid antibody) or long-lived memory B cells.

Central tolerance: Autoreactive B cells at the immature B cell stage undergo receptor editing (secondary VJ recombination to change light chain) or clonal deletion. Peripheral tolerance: anergy or follicular exclusion for cells escaping bone marrow.

Peripheral maturation: Transitional B cells exit bone marrow to spleen for final maturation into follicular (FO) B cells (long-lived, GC-competent) or marginal zone (MZ) B cells (rapid T-independent IgM responses to encapsulated bacteria).

Pathology

X-linked Agammaglobulinemia (XLA)

Loss-of-function mutations in BTK (Bruton's tyrosine kinase) block B cell development at the pro-B/pre-B stage. Patients have virtually absent B cells and immunoglobulins; present at 6 months when maternal IgG wanes. Recurrent sinopulmonary infections (encapsulated bacteria: Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae), susceptibility to enteroviral meningoencephalitis. Treated with monthly IVIG replacement for life.

Common Variable Immunodeficiency (CVID)

Heterogeneous B cell dysfunction: B cells present but fail to differentiate into plasma cells; severely low IgG, IgA, and IgM. Genetic causes include ICOS, BAFFR, TACI, CD19, and CD21 mutations. Presents in young adults with recurrent respiratory infections, autoimmunity, and lymphoproliferation. Increased lymphoma risk. IVIG/SCIG replacement is lifelong standard of care.

Diffuse Large B Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)

Most common aggressive NHL; GC-B cell or activated B cell (ABC) origin. BCL-2 overexpression (t(14;18) in GCB type) or NF-κB hyperactivation (ABC type). BCL-6 translocations, MYC rearrangements ± BCL-2/BCL-6 "double-hit/triple-hit" lymphomas. R-CHOP (rituximab + cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) is frontline; CAR-T (tisagenlecleucel, axicabtagene) for relapsed/refractory disease.

EBV-driven B cell transformation

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infects naive B cells via CD21 (CR2) — which normally binds C3d to boost BCR signaling but also serves as the EBV receptor. Latent EBV drives B cell proliferation; immune control by EBV-specific CTLs normally prevents lymphoma. In immunosuppression (HIV, post-transplant): EBV+ diffuse large B cell lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma (c-Myc translocation), Hodgkin lymphoma (Reed-Sternberg cells).

B cell deficiency in HIV/AIDS

HIV-1 progressively depletes CD4+ T helper cells — including Tfh cells — which are obligatory for germinal center reactions. Without Tfh help, B cells cannot form productive GCs, cannot undergo class switching or affinity maturation, and fail to generate LLPCs or memory B cells. Patients on ART can partially restore B cell function, but pre-existing memory B cell deficits persist, explaining partial vaccine hyporesponsiveness.

T-Independent vs T-Dependent B Cell Responses

T-dependent (TD) responses

Protein antigens requiring Tfh help via CD40L + IL-21. Enable class-switch recombination (IgG, IgA, IgE), somatic hypermutation, affinity maturation, germinal centers, and memory B cells + long-lived plasma cells. These are the responses that vaccines aim to elicit. Quality (affinity) and durability both require T cell help — the reason CD4+ T cell depletion in HIV catastrophically impairs humoral immunity.

T-independent (TI) responses

Type 1 (TI-1): LPS and other TLR ligands directly activate B cells polyclonally + via BCR. Type 2 (TI-2): repetitive polysaccharide arrays crosslink BCR intensely + complement co-receptor activation. Both produce mainly IgM with minimal class switching or affinity maturation and generate little memory — critical limitation for plain polysaccharide vaccines (23vPPV), overcome by protein conjugation in PCV13/PCV15/PCV20, Hib, and MenACWY conjugate vaccines.
