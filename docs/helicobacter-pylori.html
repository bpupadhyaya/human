Helicobacter pylori

Half of humanity carries it. An IARC Group 1 carcinogen that colonises the gastric mucosa for decades, rewires acid secretion, and drives the Correa cascade toward gastric cancer — yet 80% of carriers are asymptomatic.

Gram-negative microaerophilic spiral rod; the only bacterium uniquely adapted to the pH 1–2 gastric lumen. Urease constitutes ~6% of total bacterial protein and generates an NH₃ micro-environment enabling motility through gastric mucus. In CagA+ strains (60% of Western, ~90% of East Asian isolates), the 40 kb cagPAI encodes a type IV secretion system that injects CagA oncoprotein into gastric epithelial cells. VacA pore-forming cytotoxin disrupts T cell IL-2 signalling and impairs macrophage phagosome maturation — creating an immunologically tolerant niche for decades-long persistence.

Helicobacter pylori

Gram-negative microaerophilic (5% O₂) spiral rod (~3 µm × 0.5 µm) with 4–6 unipolar sheathed flagella; colonises the gastric mucosa of ~50% of humanity, predominantly acquired in childhood via fecal-oral or gastric-oral routes. Urease (~6% of total protein; hexadimeric, Ni²³-containing) enables survival and chemotactic navigation through the pH 1–2 gastric lumen by generating local NH₃ to neutralise acid. BabA (Lewis b antigen adhesin) and SabA adhesins anchor colonisation to gastric epithelium. In CagA+ strains, the 40 kb cagPAI type IV secretion system (T4SS) injects CagA oncoprotein into gastric epithelial cells; host kinases phosphorylate CagA EPIYA motifs, activating SHP-2 → ERK/MAPK → ↑proliferation, ↓apoptosis, disrupted epithelial polarity, and nuclear β-catenin via E-cadherin disruption. VacA (vacuolating cytotoxin A; ~140 kDa pore-forming) inhibits V-ATPase, induces T-cell NFAT/IL-2 suppression, and impairs macrophage phagosomal killing. Long-term colonisation → Correa cascade (chronic gastritis → atrophic gastritis → intestinal metaplasia → dysplasia → adenocarcinoma); ~75% of non-cardia gastric cancers are attributable to H. pylori. MALT lymphoma uniquely responds to antibiotic eradication alone in 80% of cases.

Pathogenesis

1 · Urease — surviving gastric acid and establishing colonisation

The greatest barrier to gastric colonisation is the pH 1–2 lumen. H. pylori urease — a hexadimeric nickel-containing enzyme constituting ~6% of total bacterial protein — catalyses urea hydrolysis at ~1 µmol/min/mg protein: urea → NH₃ + CO₂. NH₃ reacts with H⁺ → NH₄⁺, neutralising the immediate bacterial micro-environment to pH 6–7 in the periplasm. This enables survival through the acid lumen and directed motility through the mucus viscosity gradient toward the near-neutral mucus gel overlying epithelial cells. BabA adhesin (binds Lewis b antigen) and SabA adhesin (binds sialyl-Lewis x on inflamed mucosa) anchor the organism to the gastric epithelium.

2 · CagA injection — the oncoprotein that rewires epithelial signalling

In CagA+ strains, the cagPAI-encoded type IV secretion system (T4SS) assembles a pilus that contacts α5β1 integrin on gastric epithelial cells. CagA protein is translocated directly into the epithelial cytoplasm. Host Src and Abl kinases phosphorylate CagA at EPIYA motifs (East Asian EPIYA-D > Western EPIYA-C in oncogenic potency, due to higher SHP-2 binding affinity). Phospho-CagA activates SHP-2 phosphatase (proto-oncogenic; gain-of-function analogous to Noonan syndrome mutations) → Ras/ERK/MAPK → ↑cell proliferation, ↓apoptosis, disturbed cell polarity ("hummingbird phenotype" via PAR1 kinase inhibition). Non-phospho-CagA disrupts E-cadherin/β-catenin junctions → nuclear β-catenin → Wnt target gene activation and potential intestinal transdifferentiation. CagA+ strains: significantly higher risk of PUD, gastric adenocarcinoma, and MALT lymphoma vs. CagA–.

3 · VacA — pore-forming cytotoxin with multiple immune targets

VacA (~140 kDa monomer) assembles into anion-selective channels in membranes. Its multiple pathological activities: (i) Vacuolation: forms pores in late endosomal membranes → inhibits V-ATPase → swollen, acidified vacuoles → epithelial cell death; (ii) Mitochondrial targeting: inserts into inner mitochondrial membrane → cytochrome c release → intrinsic apoptosis; (iii) T cell suppression: forms channels in T-cell plasma membranes → ↓Ca²⁺ signalling → inhibits NFAT transcription → ↓IL-2 → impaired CD4⁺ T-cell activation (paradoxical immune evasion despite ongoing inflammation); (iv) Macrophage interference: impairs phagolysosomal V-ATPase → ↓intracellular killing → intracellular bacterial survival. VacA allelic variation: s1/m1 (most cytotoxic) → highest PUD and cancer risk.

4 · Gastric acid dysregulation — the duodenal ulcer cascade

Antrum-predominant colonisation (most strains): CagA/OipA → IL-8 → neutrophilic antral gastritis. NH₃ from urease damages somatostatin-producing D cells in the antrum → ↓somatostatin → loss of paracrine inhibition of gastrin G cells → hypergastrinaemia → ↑ECL cell histamine → ↑parietal cell HCl secretion → hyperacidity → acid load in duodenum → duodenal gastric metaplasia (CagA+ especially) → duodenal ulceration. This explains the male predominance (M:F ~4:1) and anterior duodenal ulcer location.

5 · Correa cascade — gastric adenocarcinoma pathway

Corpus-predominant colonisation (CagA+ strains; ↑atrophy): extensive inflammation → parietal cell and chief cell loss → ↓acid (hypochlorhydria) → ↑bacterial overgrowth → nitrosamines from ingested nitrates. Correa cascade (sequential histological progression over decades): normal mucosa → superficial gastritis → chronic atrophic gastritis → intestinal metaplasia (IM; CDX2+ goblet cells replace gastric mucosa) → dysplasia (low-grade → high-grade) → intestinal-type gastric adenocarcinoma (Lauren classification). H. pylori eradication reduces gastric cancer risk by ~46% in prospective studies; eradication before development of atrophy/IM provides the greatest benefit.

6 · Immune evasion — Lewis antigen mimicry and flagellin concealment

Multiple layered immune evasion mechanisms sustain decades-long colonisation: (i) LPS molecular mimicry: Lewis x/y antigens on O-antigen mimic human blood group antigens; modified lipid A (acyl chain differences) reduces TLR4 stimulatory activity; (ii) Flagellar sheath (outer membrane-derived): conceals TLR5-stimulating flagellin from innate detection; (iii) VacA T-cell suppression: ↓NFAT/IL-2 → impairs CD4⁺ T-cell adaptive responses; (iv) CagA anti-apoptotic signalling: SHP-2/Bcl-2 prevents apoptosis of infected cells, sustaining the bacterial reservoir; (v) Urease NH₃: directly toxic to neutrophils (peroxynitrite-mediated oxidative damage) when recruited to the mucosa.

Treatment & Prophylaxis

Clarithromycin triple (declining)PPI (omeprazole 20 mg or equivalent) twice daily + clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g twice daily × 10–14 days. Recommended only where clarithromycin resistance <15%; eradication rates now <80% in many regions due to >20% resistance.
Bismuth quadruple (preferred where Clari-R high)Bismuth subsalicylate 525 mg four times daily + PPI twice daily + metronidazole 500 mg three times daily + tetracycline 500 mg four times daily × 10–14 days. Effective against clarithromycin-resistant and partially against metronidazole-resistant strains; first-line recommendation in high-resistance regions.
Concomitant quadruple (non-bismuth)PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin + metronidazole × 10–14 days; useful in areas with dual (clarithromycin + metronidazole) resistance where bismuth not available.
Rifabutin rescue (3rd line)PPI + rifabutin 150 mg twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g twice daily × 7–14 days; reserved for 3rd-line after two prior treatment failures; risk of myelosuppression (monitor CBC); avoid in TB-endemic areas (risk of Mycobacterium rifampicin resistance).
Culture-guided therapyEndoscopy → antral biopsy for culture and susceptibility testing; optimal approach when resistance testing available; especially in 2nd-line treatment failure patients; allows targeted regimen selection.
Eradication confirmationUrea breath test (UBT; ¹³C) or stool antigen test (SAT; monoclonal ELISA) ≥4 weeks after completing therapy and ≥2 weeks off PPI; serology (IgG) is NOT suitable for eradication confirmation (antibody titres decline slowly over months).
ResistanceClarithromycin resistance: 15–30% in many Western countries; 5–10% in Japan; mediated by 23S rRNA point mutations (A2142G, A2143G); not reversed by dose escalation. Metronidazole resistance: 20–40%; partially overcome by higher doses/extended duration or bismuth addition. Fluoroquinolone resistance rising (gyrA mutations); rifabutin and tetracycline resistance rare. No validated H. pylori vaccine in human use (multiple candidates in trials).
