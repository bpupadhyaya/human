HIV-1

The retrovirus that rewrote immunology. A 9.7 kb diploid ssRNA genome that permanently integrates into the host — and, untreated, dismantles adaptive immunity over years.

Human Immunodeficiency Virus type 1 is the dominant pandemic strain, responsible for >98% of HIV infections worldwide. It targets CD4+ T cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells — the very coordinators of the adaptive immune response — via gp120–CD4–coreceptor engagement, ultimately collapsing cellular immunity and opening the door to AIDS-defining opportunistic infections.

HIV-1

Enveloped retrovirus with a 9.7 kb diploid positive-sense ssRNA genome encoding 9 genes. Entry requires sequential binding of envelope glycoprotein gp120 to CD4 and a coreceptor (CCR5 or CXCR4), followed by gp41-mediated membrane fusion. Reverse transcriptase converts the RNA genome to dsDNA with an error rate (~3×10⁻⁵/site/replication) far exceeding any DNA virus — the molecular engine of antigenic diversity and drug resistance. The integrated provirus (cccDNA) establishes a long-lived latent reservoir in resting CD4+ memory T cells that is invisible to the immune system and inaccessible to current ART. Without treatment, progressive CD4+ depletion over 5–10 years culminates in AIDS: CD4+ <200/μL and susceptibility to life-threatening opportunistic infections.

Genome Structure — 9 Genes

gagGroup-specific antigen; encodes MA (matrix), CA (capsid), NC (nucleocapsid), p6. Capsid forms the conical core. p6 recruits ESCRT machinery for budding.
polEncodes protease (PR), reverse transcriptase (RT), and integrase (IN) — all three primary ART targets. Expressed as Gag-Pol polyprotein via ribosomal frameshifting.
envEncodes gp160 precursor; cleaved to gp120 (surface unit, receptor binding) and gp41 (transmembrane, fusion). Trimer of heterodimers on virion surface.
tatTrans-activator of transcription; binds TAR RNA hairpin at LTR; essential for processive viral transcription. Increases LTR-driven expression >1000-fold.
revRegulator of virion expression; shuttles unspliced/singly-spliced mRNAs from nucleus to cytoplasm via CRM1 exportin. Required for structural protein synthesis.
vifViral infectivity factor; counteracts host restriction factor APOBEC3G by targeting it for proteasomal degradation. Without Vif, APOBEC3G hypermutates the viral cDNA.
vprViral protein R; facilitates nuclear import of PIC in non-dividing cells (macrophage tropism); induces G2 arrest; degrades host SAMHD1 via DCAF1.
vpuHIV-1-specific (absent in HIV-2); degrades CD4 in ER; counteracts tetherin (BST-2) to enhance virion release from cell surface.
nefNegative factor (misnomer); downregulates CD4 and MHC-I to evade CTL killing; activates signalling pathways promoting T-cell activation and viral replication; major pathogenicity determinant.

Replication Cycle

1 · Attachment and fusion

gp120 binds CD4 with high affinity, inducing a conformational change that exposes the V3 loop for coreceptor engagement. CCR5 (R5-tropic; dominant in transmission) or CXCR4 (X4-tropic; associated with late-stage disease progression) binds the V3 loop, triggering gp41 heptad-repeat refolding and six-helix-bundle formation — driving viral and host membrane fusion. Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist) and enfuvirtide (fusion inhibitor targeting gp41 HR1) act here.

2 · Reverse transcription

The viral RT is an RNA-dependent DNA polymerase with intrinsic RNase H activity. It converts the ssRNA genome to dsDNA through an RNA:DNA hybrid intermediate. Error rate ~3×10⁻⁵ mutations/site/replication cycle — approximately one mutation per genome per round. This drives quasi-species diversity, immune escape, and ART drug resistance. NRTIs (e.g. tenofovir, emtricitabine) terminate chain elongation; NNRTIs (e.g. efavirenz, rilpivirine) allosterically inhibit RT.

3 · Integration — the provirus

Integrase (IN) catalyses 3' processing and strand-transfer of viral dsDNA into host chromatin — preferentially into actively transcribed genes. The integrated provirus (cccDNA analogue) is replicated with the cell's own DNA indefinitely. In resting CD4+ memory T cells, the provirus is transcriptionally silent, forming a long-lived latent reservoir established within days of primary infection. Latency is the central barrier to cure. INSTIs (dolutegravir, bictegravir) block strand transfer and are the backbone of modern ART.

4 · Latent reservoir — the cure problem

An estimated 10⁶–10⁷ resting CD4+ memory T cells carry integrated provirus. These cells can persist for decades (half-life ~44 months). ART suppresses viremia to undetectable (<50 copies/mL) but does not shrink the reservoir. Analytic treatment interruptions show viral rebound from this reservoir within 2–4 weeks of stopping ART in virtually all patients. "Shock and kill," "block and lock," and stem-cell transplant strategies are under investigation.

Antiretroviral Therapy — Drug Classes

NRTIs — Nucleoside / Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors

Chain terminators: incorporated by RT into the growing DNA chain, lacking a 3'-OH for elongation. Examples: tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), emtricitabine (FTC), abacavir (ABC), lamivudine (3TC). Standard backbone of most ART regimens; typically two NRTIs + one third agent.

NNRTIs — Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors

Allosteric RT inhibitors binding a hydrophobic pocket near the RT active site, inducing a conformational change. Examples: efavirenz, rilpivirine, doravirine. Low barrier to resistance (single K103N or E138K mutation). Rilpivirine-based combinations (Odefsey, Cabenuva) used in viral suppression maintenance.

INSTIs — Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors

Block integration by chelating Mg²+ at the integrase active site, preventing strand transfer. Examples: dolutegravir (DTG), bictegravir (BIC), cabotegravir (CAB). Very high barrier to resistance; first-line in virtually all current guidelines. CAB long-acting injectable (2-monthly) used for maintenance (Cabenuva = CAB + rilpivirine).

PIs — Protease Inhibitors

Prevent Gag-Pol polyprotein cleavage into functional viral proteins, producing immature non-infectious particles. Examples: darunavir (DRV, boosted with cobicistat or ritonavir), atazanavir. High barrier to resistance with boosting. Now largely second-line in resource-rich settings but central to many global programs.

Entry / Fusion Inhibitors and Others

Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist — only for R5-tropic virus confirmed by tropism assay); enfuvirtide (T-20, gp41 fusion inhibitor, SC injection, second-line); ibalizumab (CD4 post-attachment inhibitor, MDR HIV). Attachment inhibitor fostemsavir (pro-drug of temsavir, binds gp120).
