Glomerulus

Microscopic primary filtration unit of the nephron — a tight tuft of ~8–10 fenestrated capillary loops in the cup-shaped Bowman's capsule at the proximal end of each renal tubule. ~1 million glomeruli per adult kidney filter 180 L/day of plasma (GFR ~125 mL/min), producing primary urine that is subsequently modified by the tubule. Three-layer filtration barrier: (1) fenestrated endothelium with glycocalyx (charge selective), (2) glomerular basement membrane — GBM — collagen IV α3α4α5 chains, 250–400 nm thick (size and charge barrier), (3) podocyte foot processes with slit diaphragm (nephrin/NEPH1/podocin — final size-selective protein retention). Mesangial cells provide structural support and regulate GFR via contractility. Juxtaglomerular apparatus activates RAAS via renin secretion.

Overview

The glomerulus is the microscopic primary filtration unit of the nephron — a tight tuft of ~8–10 fenestrated capillary loops suspended within the cup-shaped Bowman's capsule at the proximal end of each renal tubule. Each human kidney contains approximately one million glomeruli, distributed throughout the renal cortex. Together they filter an astonishing 180 litres of plasma per day — nearly 125 mL per minute — generating the primary urine that is subsequently concentrated, modified, and reabsorbed as it traverses the nephron tubule.

The glomerulus is a precisely engineered tissue whose three-layer barrier discriminates between molecules by both size and charge: water, ions, glucose, amino acids, and urea pass freely; albumin (67 kDa, anionic) is retained to 99.97%; larger proteins are virtually completely excluded. Failure of glomerular barrier integrity — leading to proteinuria, nephrotic syndrome, and progressive kidney failure — is among the most clinically significant events in nephrology.

Anatomy — Three-Layer Filtration Barrier

Renal corpuscle structure: The glomerulus (capillary tuft) sits within Bowman's capsule, together forming the renal corpuscle. Bowman's capsule has a parietal epithelium (simple squamous, outer wall) and a visceral epithelium (podocytes, wrapping the capillaries). Bowman's space (urinary space) between them collects filtrate entering the proximal tubule.

Function — Ultrafiltration, Autoregulation, and RAAS

Net filtration pressure (Starling equation applied to glomerulus):

NFP = (Pcap − Pspace) − (πcap − πspace) = (60 − 18) − (32 − 0) = +10 mmHg
Pcap ≈ 60 mmHg (hydrostatic), Pspace ≈ 18 mmHg (tubular pressure), πcap ≈ 32 mmHg (oncotic), πspace ≈ 0 mmHg (protein-free filtrate). Filtration coefficient Kf = GFR / NFP ≈ 12.5 mL/min/mmHg.

Freely filtered: water, Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻, glucose, amino acids, urea, creatinine. Partially filtered: small peptides, β₂-microglobulin. Minimally filtered: albumin (~0.03%). Not filtered: red cells, platelets, large proteins, protein-bound drugs.

GFR autoregulation maintains GFR within ±10% over MAP 80–180 mmHg through: (1) Myogenic response — afferent arteriole smooth muscle reflexively constricts when stretched by rising BP; (2) Tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF) — macula densa cells in the distal tubule sense NaCl delivery; high NaCl → adenosine + ATP release → afferent arteriole vasoconstriction → ↓GFR. Beyond this range, GFR tracks blood pressure: hypertension → hyperfiltration → glomerular injury; hypotension → prerenal azotaemia.

RAAS activation via juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA): ↓renal perfusion pressure / ↓tubular NaCl (macula densa) / sympathetic β1 → juxtaglomerular cells secrete renin → cleaves angiotensinogen (liver) → angiotensin I (inactive decapeptide) → ACE (lung endothelium) → angiotensin II → vasoconstriction (AT1R, ↑SVR + ↑efferent arteriole tone) + aldosterone secretion (ZG: ↑Na⁺ reabsorption, ↑K⁺ secretion) + ADH release → blood pressure correction.

Pathology

Diabetic Nephropathy (Diabetic Glomerulosclerosis)

Most common cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) in developed countries. Hyperglycaemia → advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), reactive oxygen species, PKC activation → GBM thickening, mesangial expansion, glomerulosclerosis (Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules — pathognomonic nodular mesangial deposits). Microalbuminuria (30–300 mg/day albumin) → macroalbuminuria → ↓GFR. Glomerular hyperfiltration (early) → glomerular injury. Treatment: RAAS blockade (ACEi/ARB), SGLT2 inhibitors (↓hyperfiltration + renal protection), GLP-1 receptor agonists.

Nephrotic Syndrome (Glomerular Protein Loss)

Proteinuria >3.5 g/day + hypoalbuminaemia + oedema + hyperlipidaemia. Causes by age: children — minimal change disease (MCD: podocyte effacement on EM, no light microscopy changes; respond to steroids); adults — focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS: podocyte injury, scarring of some glomeruli; often steroid-resistant), membranous nephropathy (anti-PLA2R antibodies in 70% of primary; subepithelial immune complexes), diabetic nephropathy, amyloidosis. Podocyte loss → slit diaphragm disruption → massive proteinuria.

IgA Nephropathy (Berger's Disease)

Most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. Mesangial IgA₁ immune complex deposition (aberrantly glycosylated IgA₁ → IgA immune complexes → mesangial complement C3 activation) → mesangial inflammation → haematuria + proteinuria. Synpharyngitic haematuria (visible blood with upper respiratory infection). Oxford classification (MEST-C score). Treatment: RAAS blockade; immunosuppression for progressive disease; sparsentan (dual endothelin + angiotensin receptor antagonist) approved for IgAN.

Alport Syndrome

X-linked (COL4A5 mutations, most common), autosomal recessive (COL4A3/COL4A4) defects in GBM collagen IV α3α4α5 chain → GBM structural abnormality (thinning initially → lamellation on EM) → progressive haematuria + proteinuria → ESRD (typically 2nd–4th decade in males with X-linked form). Sensorineural hearing loss and anterior lenticonus. Treatment: ACEi/ARB to slow progression; gene therapy in development.

Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN)

Crescentic glomerulonephritis: parietal epithelial cell and macrophage proliferation fills Bowman's space (crescents) → renal failure within weeks. Three types: (1) anti-GBM disease (Goodpasture's — linear IgG on GBM, anti-α3 collagen IV; pulmonary haemorrhage), (2) immune-complex mediated (SLE lupus nephritis, post-streptococcal), (3) pauci-immune/ANCA-associated (GPA, MPA — c-ANCA/PR3 or p-ANCA/MPO). Treatment: pulse methylprednisolone + cyclophosphamide ± plasma exchange (anti-GBM); rituximab for ANCA-associated.
