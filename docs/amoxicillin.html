Amoxicillin (Amoxil)

Aminopenicillin beta-lactam antibiotic — covalently acylates the catalytic serine of penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), blocking peptidoglycan cross-linking and causing osmotic lysis in susceptible gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria.

The world's most prescribed antibiotic. WHO Access group essential medicine. First-line for CAP, streptococcal pharyngitis, H. pylori eradication, and acute otitis media. Combined with clavulanate (Augmentin) to overcome beta-lactamase resistance.

Amoxicillin

Class: Aminopenicillin / Beta-Lactam Antibiotic  ·  Route: Oral, IV  |  Brand: Amoxil, Trimox; co-amoxiclav (+ clavulanate) = Augmentin  |  Spectrum: Gram-positive + selected gram-negative

Amoxicillin is a semi-synthetic aminopenicillin (1972, Beecham) with superior oral bioavailability (~80–90%) vs ampicillin. The 4'-hydroxyphenyl side chain improves outer membrane penetration compared with benzylpenicillin, extending gram-negative coverage to H. influenzae, E. coli, and H. pylori. The beta-lactam ring covalently acylates the catalytic serine of penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs — DD-transpeptidases), permanently inhibiting peptidoglycan cross-linking → osmotic lysis. Primary resistance via beta-lactamase (overcome by clavulanate) or altered PBPs (overcome by high doses for intermediate-resistance pneumococcus). WHO Essential Medicine, Access group.

Beta-Lactam Mechanism — PBP Acylation

  1. Peptidoglycan cross-linking: Bacterial cell wall synthesis culminates in DD-transpeptidation — cross-linking of adjacent peptidoglycan strands by forming a peptide bond between the penultimate D-Ala and the ε-amino of DAP/D-Lys on an adjacent chain; this step is catalyzed by PBPs (class B penicillin-binding proteins: PBP1a/1b, PBP2, PBP3)
  2. Beta-lactam ring mimicry: The beta-lactam ring is a structural analogue of the D-Ala-D-Ala C-terminus of the peptidoglycan pentapeptide stem; it binds the PBP active site as a substrate mimic
  3. Covalent acylation: The catalytic serine (Ser-OH) of PBP attacks the carbonyl of the beta-lactam ring → nucleophilic ring-opening → stable covalent acyl-enzyme complex → PBP permanently inactivated; acylation is essentially irreversible under physiological conditions
  4. Cell wall failure: Cross-linking ceases → peptidoglycan becomes structurally weak → osmotic gradient across membrane exceeds wall strength → osmotic lysis; in gram-positives, this is the primary lethal event; additionally, autolysins (murein hydrolases) are released and degrade the weakened wall

Penicillin Allergy & Amoxicillin Rash

  • Reported penicillin allergy (~10% of patients): True IgE-mediated allergy ~1%; most "penicillin allergies" are non-immunological or mislabeled; allergy assessment + skin testing/graded challenge frequently de-labels patients safely
  • Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins: Historically overstated (~10%); actual rate ~1–2% for true IgE-mediated allergy; shared R1 side-chain between amoxicillin and cefadroxil/cefprozil — greater cross-reactivity than cephalosporins with dissimilar side-chains
  • Ampicillin/amoxicillin rash (non-allergic): ~5% of patients develop a maculopapular rash, usually starting day 5–7 of treatment; 80–100% of patients with infectious mononucleosis (EBV) develop this rash — mechanism uncertain (immune complex formation, not IgE); does NOT indicate penicillin allergy and is not a contraindication to future beta-lactam use
  • Anaphylaxis (1–4 per 10,000 courses): IgE-mediated; urticaria, angioedema, hypotension, bronchospasm; most reactions within 1–2 hours of oral dose; carry epinephrine if confirmed allergy
