Aspergillus fumigatus

The ubiquitous thermotolerant saprophyte that we all inhale daily — harmless to intact immune systems, 40–90% lethal when neutrophils fail. The leading mould pathogen of humanity.

A. fumigatus 2–3 µm conidia penetrate to the alveolus in every breath; the RodA hydrophobin surface masks β-glucan from Dectin-1, delaying innate recognition. In neutropenic hosts, germination to septate hyphae with 45° branching and angioinvasion produces haemorrhagic infarcts. Environmental azole resistance (TR34/L98H cyp51A) is spreading globally.

Aspergillus fumigatus

Ubiquitous, thermotolerant saprophytic mould found in decaying organic matter worldwide. Responsible for the vast majority of invasive aspergillosis. Conidia (2–3 µm) are inhaled daily by all humans and cleared efficiently by alveolar macrophages and neutrophils in immunocompetent hosts; in immunocompromised hosts, conidia germinate to hyphae (4–6 µm, septate, 45° dichotomous branching) which angioinvade the pulmonary parenchyma producing haemorrhagic infarction (CT halo sign). Key virulence: RodA hydrophobin masks β-glucan from Dectin-1 recognition on resting conidia; gliotoxin induces macrophage/neutrophil apoptosis and suppresses NF-κB in DCs; DHN-melanin quenches NADPH oxidase ROS; siderophores acquire host iron. First-line treatment: voriconazole (triazole, CYP51 inhibitor). Environmental azole resistance (TR34/L98H cyp51A mutation) is spreading via fungicide-exposed soil and poses a growing therapeutic threat.

Pathogenesis

1 · Conidial inhalation and alveolar clearance failure

Conidia (2–3 µm aerodynamic diameter) bypass the upper airway and mucociliary escalator, depositing in terminal bronchioles and alveoli. In immunocompetent hosts, alveolar macrophages phagocytose conidia via Dectin-1, TLR2/4, and complement receptors CR3/CR4 within 4–8 hours. Recruited neutrophils provide backup killing via NADPH oxidase ROS and NETs. In neutropenic or steroid-immunosuppressed hosts, this dual defence fails → germination proceeds.

2 · Conidial immune evasion

Resting conidia are coated by RodA hydrophobin — a self-assembling hydrophobic protein that physically masks immunogenic β-glucan and chitin from pattern recognition receptors, especially Dectin-1. This delays innate immune activation at the critical early time window. DHN-melanin (dihydroxynaphthalene-melanin) in the conidial wall quenches superoxide radicals from NADPH oxidase and inhibits phagosome acidification. Catalase (Cat1, Cat2) and SOD (SodA, SodB/SodC) neutralise residual H⊂2;O⊂2; and superoxide.

3 · Hyphal germination, tissue invasion, and angioinvasion

At 37°C, conidia swell and germinate → germ tube → hyphae within 6–12 hours. Exposed β-glucan on germinating hyphae triggers Dectin-1 — but neutrophil/macrophage effector function is impaired by immunosuppression. Hyphae secrete proteases (alkaline proteases Alp1/2, metalloprotease Mep) that degrade the alveolar basement membrane and ECM. Hyphae penetrate blood vessel walls (angioinvasion) → intravascular growth → thrombosis and haemorrhagic infarction. CT halo sign (ground-glass haemorrhagic halo around dense nodule) is the early diagnostic hallmark.

4 · Gliotoxin-mediated immunosuppression

Gliotoxin (secreted by hyphae from the gli biosynthetic cluster) is the primary active immunosuppressive mycotoxin: it induces caspase-dependent apoptosis in alveolar macrophages and neutrophils; inhibits NF-κB in dendritic cells, blocking upregulation of CD80/CD86 co-stimulatory molecules and IL-12 secretion → impaired Th1/Th17 priming; suppresses T-cell activation. Gliotoxin thus converts the lung into an immunologically tolerogenic environment permissive to hyphal expansion.

5 · Environmental azole resistance (TR34/L98H)

The TR34/L98H cyp51A mutation (tandem repeat in the CYP51A promoter + L98H point mutation) confers resistance to all medical triazoles (voriconazole, itraconazole, posaconazole, isavuconazole) by altering the drug-binding pocket. This mutation arises in the environment via selection pressure from agricultural azole fungicides (demethylation inhibitors) applied to tulip/flower bulbs, grain, and other crops. TR34/L98H is common in the Netherlands, India, and parts of sub-Saharan Africa. Patients in these regions may acquire azole-resistant IPA de novo without prior medical azole exposure, making empirical voriconazole potentially inadequate.

Host Immune Response

Alveolar macrophages (Dectin-1/TLR phagocytosis; primary early defence) Neutrophils (NADPH oxidase, MPO-halide, NETs; essential for hyphal killing) Mucociliary escalator (traps conidia >5 µm in upper airways) Complement (classical + alternative pathway opsonisation) Th1 (IFN-γ → macrophage activation; driven by DC-derived IL-12) Th17 (IL-17A/F → neutrophil recruitment; important in ABPA regulation) Th2 (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13: pathological IgE response in ABPA, eosinophilia) RodA/DHN-melanin: shield conidia from Dectin-1 and ROS Gliotoxin: kills macrophages/neutrophils; suppresses DC maturation and Th1/Th17 priming Neutropenia: the single strongest risk factor for IPA; neutrophil ANC <500 cells/µL

Treatment & Prophylaxis

VoriconazoleFirst-line IPA (triazole; CYP51 inhibitor; superior to amphotericin B in landmark 2002 trial). Therapeutic drug monitoring (trough target 1–5.5 µg/mL). Avoid if drug interactions. Visual disturbances and periostitis with prolonged use.
IsavuconazoleAlternative triazole; better GI tolerability and fewer drug interactions than voriconazole; active against some cyp51A non-TR34 mutations; preferred when azole interactions or hepatotoxicity are concerns.
Liposomal amphotericin B (L-AMB)Alternative first-line (3–4 mg/kg/day IV); preferred when drug interactions contraindicate azoles or in azole-resistant IPA; significant nephrotoxicity with conventional AmB-D formulation.
EchinocandinsCaspofungin or micafungin as salvage monotherapy or in combination with voriconazole; A. fumigatus β-glucan synthase (fks1) mutations conferring resistance are rare. Combination vori + anidulafungin showed borderline mortality benefit in one RCT.
ABPA treatmentSystemic corticosteroids (reduce eosinophilic inflammation) + itraconazole or voriconazole (reduce fungal burden and steroid requirement); monitor IgE levels and CXR/CT for response.
Prophylaxis in immunocompromisedPosaconazole oral suspension or delayed-release tablets; or voriconazole during prolonged neutropenia (>10 days) and GVHD treatment; environmental HEPA filtration during hospital construction; N95 masks during high-spore-exposure activities.
