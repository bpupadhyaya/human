Endothelial Cell

Squamous cells (~50–70 µm long) lining the entire cardiovascular tree and lymphatics (~350 m² surface, ~1 kg mass). Regulate vascular tone (eNOS/NO, PGI₂, ET-1), prevent thrombosis (thrombomodulin, TFPI, tPA, CD39), control permeability (VE-cadherin, claudin-5), gate leukocyte trafficking (E/P-selectin, ICAM-1, VCAM-1), and direct angiogenesis (VEGFR2 tip/stalk cell DLL4/Notch circuit). Weibel-Palade bodies (WPBs) are the defining secretory organelle — storing vWF and P-selectin.

Overview

The endothelial cell is the squamous epithelium of the vasculature — a single continuous monolayer of cells lining the luminal surface of every blood vessel and lymphatic channel in the body. The total endothelial surface area is approximately 350 m² with a mass of ~1 kg, making the endothelium one of the largest and most functionally diverse organs in the body.

Endothelial cells are dynamic signal-processing nodes that continuously sense haemodynamic forces (shear stress, cyclic stretch), blood-borne signals (hormones, cytokines, growth factors, metabolites), and direct cell-cell contacts, translating these inputs into real-time adjustments of vascular tone, permeability, haemostasis, and leukocyte trafficking. Endothelial dysfunction — loss of the normal anti-inflammatory, anti-thrombotic, and vasodilatory phenotype — is the earliest and most fundamental lesion in atherosclerosis, and a key mediator of tissue injury in sepsis, COVID-19, and diabetes.

Weibel-Palade bodies (WPBs) — the defining secretory organelle of endothelial cells; rod-shaped (~0.2 × 3 µm) storage granules containing: von Willebrand factor (large multimers in tubular arrays), P-selectin (rapidly mobilised to cell surface within seconds of thrombin/histamine/ROS stimulus), IL-8 (CXCL8), angiopoietin-2 (Ang2), and other mediators. WPB exocytosis is the rapid-response phase of EC activation (minutes), preceding transcriptional changes.

Mechanosensing: ECs align parallel to blood flow direction via primary cilia (polycystin-1/2), glycocalyx (0.5–3 µm thick; heparan sulfate/hyaluronan absorbs shear), PECAM-1 (CD31, junctional mechanosensor; phosphorylated by shear → eNOS activation), and integrins (αvβ3, α5β1) sensing matrix stiffness. Laminar shear stress upregulates KLF2/KLF4 → eNOS, thrombomodulin, PGI₂ synthase — the atheroprotective programme. Disturbed flow (bifurcations, curvatures) downregulates KLF2/4 and activates NF-κB — the atherogenic programme.

Leukocyte Recruitment Cascade

Activated endothelium (TNF-α, IL-1β → NF-κB) sequentially upregulates adhesion molecules to guide leukocytes from blood to tissue:

1. RollingP-selectin (WPB), E-selectin (transcription, 2–6 h)Minutes–hoursPSGL-1, CD44 (sialyl-Lewis X ligands)
2. Firm arrestICAM-1 (NF-κB), VCAM-1 (NF-κB)HoursLFA-1/Mac-1 (ICAM-1); VLA-4 (VCAM-1)
3. Chemokine-drivenCXCL8 displayed on glycocalyx heparan sulfateContinuousCXCR2 (neutrophil); inside-out integrin activation
4. TransmigrationPECAM-1 (CD31), JAM-A/B/C, CD99Minutes (paracellular)PECAM-1 homophilic; JAM-JAM interactions

VCAM-1 is particularly important in monocyte recruitment during atherosclerosis — it is expressed on EC overlying nascent lipid-rich plaques, is induced by oxidised LDL and disturbed flow, and is a critical step in foam cell accumulation. KLF2/4 transcriptionally suppresses VCAM-1 under laminar shear — explaining why atherosclerosis preferentially initiates at regions of disturbed flow.

Pathology

Atherosclerosis

Endothelial dysfunction at arterial bends and bifurcations (disturbed flow → ↓KLF2/4 → ↓eNOS, ↑NF-κB → ↑VCAM-1, ↑MCP-1) → LDL subendothelial retention → oxidation (oxLDL) → macrophage foam cell accumulation → fatty streak → fibrous plaque → SMC phenotype switching → advanced plaque with calcification and thin fibrous cap → rupture → acute MI or stroke. Statins, ACEi, and aspirin all partially restore endothelial function.

COVID-19 Endotheliitis

SARS-CoV-2 infects EC via ACE2/TMPRSS2 (particularly in lung, heart, kidney, gut) → direct cytopathic effect + NF-κB activation → cytokine storm → complement activation → microthrombi (platelet-fibrin and neutrophil extracellular traps) across multiple organs. Elevated D-dimer, vWF, and IL-6 in severe COVID-19 are endothelial markers. Anticoagulation (heparin) reduces mortality in moderate/severe disease. APOL1 risk genotypes → collapsing glomerulopathy via glomerular EC injury.

Hereditary Haemorrhagic Telangiectasia (HHT)

Loss-of-function mutations in ENG (endoglin, HHT1) or ACVRL1 (ALK1, HHT2) → impaired BMP9/10 → EC TGF-β signalling → abnormal AV malformations (AVMs) in lung, brain, liver, GI tract; mucocutaneous telangiectasias; recurrent epistaxis. Bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) reduces epistaxis and hepatic AVM blood flow. Liver transplantation for severe hepatic AVMs.

Septic Shock

LPS/PAMPs (bacteria) → endothelial TLR4 → NF-κB → ↑TF (DIC), ↓TM (thrombotic), ↑permeability (VEGF, thrombin) → capillary leak → oedema → multi-organ failure. Glycocalyx shedding (heparanase, matrix metalloproteinases) exposes endothelial adhesion molecules and worsens leak. Fresh frozen plasma and albumin partially restore the glycocalyx. eNOS-derived NO in high concentration from iNOS-expressing cells paradoxically contributes to vasodilatory shock.

Tumour Angiogenesis

VEGF-A overexpression by tumour cells (HIF-1α → VEGF) → pathological tip/stalk sprouting → immature, leaky, tortuous tumour vasculature (poor oxygen delivery paradoxically maintains hypoxia). Bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) and ramucirumab (anti-VEGFR2) normalise vasculature transiently, improving drug delivery. Notch inhibitors (γ-secretase inhibitors) shift stalk/tip balance, promoting AV shunts. VEGFR2-targeted kinase inhibitors (sunitinib, sorafenib, axitinib) are first-line in RCC and HCC.
