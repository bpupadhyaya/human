Acetylcholine (ACh)

The first neurotransmitter ever discovered — a quaternary ammonium ester (MW 182.65) controlling NMJ, autonomic, and CNS circuits through nicotinic and muscarinic receptors.

Synthesised by choline acetyltransferase (ChAT) from acetyl-CoA + choline. Terminated by acetylcholinesterase (AChE) in <1 ms — one of the fastest enzymatic reactions in biology. Two receptor superfamilies: nAChR (ionotropic, Na⁺/K⁺ channel, 5 major subtypes) and mAChR (GPCR, M1–M5). Loss of basal forebrain cholinergic neurons is the central neurochemical correlate of Alzheimer's disease.

Acetylcholine (ACh)

Class: Quaternary ammonium ester  ·  Neurotransmitter  ·  Neuromodulator  |  Biosynthetic enzyme: ChAT (CHAT gene)  |  Degrading enzyme: AChE (ACHE gene)  |  Storage: VAChT (SLC18A3)

Acetylcholine (ACh) is the founding member of the neurotransmitter family, identified by Otto Loewi in 1921 as "Vagusstoff." It is a simple choline ester carrying a permanent positive charge that binds nicotinic receptors (ionotropic, fast millisecond response) and muscarinic receptors (GPCR, slower modulation), mediating motor commands at the NMJ, ganglionic relay in the autonomic nervous system, and widespread neuromodulation of cognition, memory, and arousal in the CNS. Its inactivation by acetylcholinesterase (AChE) in under one millisecond makes it uniquely suited for precise rapid signalling at the motor endplate and parasympathetic effector organs. Pharmacological manipulation of ACh underpins treatment of myasthenia gravis, Alzheimer's disease, organophosphate poisoning, anaesthesia, COPD, and overactive bladder.

Overview & Structure

ACh is a simple ester: choline + acetate, linked through an ester bond (molecular formula C₇H₁₆NO₂⁺). The quaternary ammonium nitrogen bears a permanent positive charge at all physiological pH values, which anchors ACh in the binding pockets of both nAChRs and mAChRs via cation-π interactions with aromatic residues (Tyr, Trp). Unlike monoamine neurotransmitters, there is no reuptake transporter for intact ACh — signal termination is exclusively enzymatic.

Choline acetyltransferase (ChAT) catalyses: Acetyl-CoA + Choline → ACh + CoA-SH (ΔG ≈ −13 kJ/mol). ChAT is cytoplasmic in the presynaptic terminal and its expression is the defining molecular marker of cholinergic neurons. High-affinity choline reuptake via CHT1 (SLC5A7) is rate-limiting for ACh synthesis in high-demand states.

Synthesis, Vesicular Loading & Exocytosis Pathway

  Choline  ←─── recycled by CHT1 (SLC5A7) reuptake from synaptic cleft
       │         also from diet/phosphatidylcholine catabolism
       │
       │  ChAT (cytosolic)  +  Acetyl-CoA (from mitochondrial citrate)
       ▼
  Acetylcholine (ACh)
       │
       │  VAChT (SLC18A3) — vesicular ACh transporter
       │  Drives 2 H⁺ efflux per ACh loaded (V-type H⁺-ATPase gradient)
       │  ~10,000 ACh molecules per synaptic vesicle
       ▼
  Synaptic vesicle (ready-releasable pool at active zone)
       │
       │  Action potential → P/Q-type Cav2.1 (NMJ) opens
       │  Ca²⁺ binds synaptotagmin-1 (C2A/C2B domains)
       │  SNARE zippering: VAMP2 (vesicle) + SNAP-25 + syntaxin-1 (plasma membrane)
       ▼
  Synaptic cleft  →  ACh binds nAChR or mAChR
       │
       ├─── nAChR (ionotropic): Na⁺ influx + K⁺ efflux → depolarisation → AP
       │
       └─── mAChR M1/M3/M5 (Gq → PLC → IP3/DAG → ↑Ca²⁺/PKC)
            mAChR M2/M4  (Gi → ↓cAMP + GIRK K⁺ channel → hyperpolarisation)
       │
       ▼
  AChE (serine hydrolase, ~25,000 events/sec per active site)
       │  Ser203 → tetrahedral intermediate → acetyl-enzyme + choline released
       │  Water → deacylation → acetate + regenerated Ser203
       ▼
  Choline recycled by CHT1 → re-enters synthesis cycle

Botulinum neurotoxins (BoNT A–G) are Zn²⁺-metalloproteases that cleave SNARE proteins — BoNT-A/C/E cleave SNAP-25; BoNT-B/D/F/G cleave VAMP2 (synaptobrevin); BoNT-C also cleaves syntaxin-1. Result: irreversible block of ACh exocytosis → flaccid paralysis. Therapeutic uses: cervical dystonia, cosmetic (onabotulinumtoxinA, abobotulinumtoxinA), hyperhidrosis, chronic migraine prophylaxis, spasticity.

Myasthenia Gravis (MG) ↓Functional NMJ nAChR Anti-AChR antibodies (~85%; accelerate receptor internalisation, fix complement) or anti-MuSK antibodies (~10%; disrupt agrin/rapsyn clustering) Fatigable ptosis, diplopia, bulbar palsy, respiratory crisis; Tensilon test; tx: pyridostigmine, thymectomy, prednisolone/azathioprine, IVIG, efgartigimod (FcRn antagonist), eculizumab

Alzheimer's Disease ↓Cortical/hippocampal ACh (cholinergic hypothesis) Selective degeneration of nucleus basalis of Meynert cholinergic neurons → ↓cortical ACh → impaired attention and memory encoding Progressive amnestic dementia; AChEi (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) ± memantine (NMDA antagonist) — symptomatic benefit; amyloid/tau-targeting therapies (lecanemab) emerging
Organophosphate / Nerve Agent Poisoning ACh accumulation — cholinergic crisis Irreversible AChE phosphorylation (parathion, sarin, VX → Ser203-phosphate) → ACh overflow at all cholinergic synapses SLUDGE (muscarinic: salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI distress, emesis) + bradycardia, miosis, bronchospasm; nicotinic: fasciculations, weakness, paralysis; tx: atropine (large doses, titrate secretions dry) + pralidoxime (2-PAM)
Congenital Myasthenic Syndromes Genetic ACh pathway defects Mutations in CHT1, ChAT, AChE (ColQ), VAChT, or nAChR subunits Variable NMJ dysfunction from birth; ChAT mutations → episodic apnoea; AChE deficiency → paradoxical worsening with AChEi

Clinical key: SLUDGE = muscarinic excess (M1–M3). Fasciculations + paralysis = nicotinic (NMJ) excess. Treatment sequence in organophosphate poisoning: (1) remove from exposure, (2) atropine IV titrated until secretions dry — may require massive doses (10s to 100s of mg), (3) pralidoxime (2-PAM) within hours to reactivate AChE before irreversible "ageing" of the phosphorylated enzyme occurs.
