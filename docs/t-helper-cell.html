T Helper Cell (CD4+)

CD4+ T helper cells orchestrate adaptive immunity: Tfh cells drive germinal center B cell help and durable antibody; Th1 activates macrophages for intracellular pathogens; Th2 promotes IgE and anti-parasite responses; Th17 mobilizes neutrophils at mucosal barriers; Tregs enforce tolerance. Three signals required: TCR:pMHC-II, CD28:CD80/86, and APC-derived cytokines. Without all three, T cells anergize or die.

Overview

The CD4+ T helper cell is the master orchestrator of adaptive immunity — the lymphocyte subset that decides the character, magnitude, and duration of nearly every immune response. Named for their role in "helping" other immune cells, T helper cells provide the cognate signals essential for B cell class switching and affinity maturation, CD8+ T cell activation, macrophage polarization, and the establishment of immunological memory.

CD4+ T cells recognize antigen exclusively as peptide fragments presented on MHC class II — expressed only on professional antigen-presenting cells (dendritic cells, B cells, macrophages). This MHC-II restriction means T helper cells respond to extracellular pathogens and protein antigens, and are the primary responders to vaccine antigens delivered to the lymph node.

The importance of CD4+ T cells to vaccine-elicited immunity is demonstrated by HIV-1 infection: progressive CD4+ T cell depletion by a virus that preferentially infects these cells leads to AIDS — total collapse of both humoral and cellular adaptive immunity.

Memory formation: After primary response, ~90–95% of effector T helper cells die (contraction phase). Survivors become long-lived memory T cells: central memory (CCR7+CD45RO+, lymph-node resident) or effector memory (CCR7−CD45RO+, patrol tissues). Memory CD4+ T cells respond within hours of pathogen re-encounter and persist for decades.

Pathology

HIV/AIDS — CD4+ T cell depletion

HIV-1 preferentially infects and depletes CD4+ T helper cells via CCR5/CXCR4 co-receptors on CD4+ cells. Progressive depletion below 200 cells/µL defines AIDS; opportunistic infections (PCP, CMV retinitis, cryptococcal meningitis, MAC) become lethal. Without Tfh, germinal centers collapse and antibody responses fail. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) fully reconstitutes CD4+ counts and prevents AIDS progression.

Rheumatoid Arthritis — Th17 dysregulation

Th17 cells accumulate in the synovium of RA joints, producing IL-17A that drives neutrophil recruitment and macrophage IL-1β/TNF production, causing cartilage and bone destruction. HLA-DRB1 shared epitope is the dominant genetic risk factor — antigen-specific CD4+ T cell activation in pathogenesis. Anti-IL-17A (secukinumab), anti-IL-6R (tocilizumab), and anti-TNF biologics are effective.

Allergic asthma and atopy — Th2 excess

Th2-skewed CD4+ T cell responses to inhaled allergens produce IL-4 (drives IgE class switching in B cells), IL-5 (eosinophil survival), and IL-13 (goblet cell hyperplasia, mucus secretion, airway smooth muscle hyperresponsiveness). Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα, blocking IL-4 and IL-13 signaling) is highly effective in moderate-severe asthma and atopic dermatitis.

COVID-19 — CD4+ T cell lymphopenia

SARS-CoV-2 causes lymphopenia including CD4+ T cell depletion; viral RNA has been detected in CD4+ T cells from severe COVID-19 patients, potentially contributing to immunodysregulation. Inadequate early Tfh response correlates with poorer antibody maturation and worse outcomes. Defective IFN-γ-producing Th1 responses contribute to impaired macrophage activation.

Memory T Helper Cells — Subsets and Persistence

After the primary response, ~90–95% of effector T helper cells die (contraction). Survivors become long-lived memory T cells that persist for decades:

Memory longevity: Mathematical modeling of antibody decay kinetics and memory T cell homeostasis suggests that vaccine-elicited central memory T helper cells can persist for 50–75 years in the absence of further antigen stimulation, maintained by slow homeostatic proliferation driven by IL-7 and tonic TCR:self-pMHC-II contacts. This is the cellular basis for life-long protection conferred by measles, yellow fever, and smallpox vaccines.
