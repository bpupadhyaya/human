Lung

Paired gas-exchange organs: right (3 lobes) and left (2 lobes). Branching airways (23 generations, Weibel) connect trachea to ~300–500 million alveoli providing ~70 m² of surface area. Dual circulation: pulmonary (gas exchange) + bronchial (airway nutrition). TLC ~6 L; tidal volume ~0.5 L at rest. Primary target of respiratory pathogens including SARS-CoV-2 and influenza A.

Overview

The lungs are the paired organs of pulmonary gas exchange — the anatomical interface at which atmospheric oxygen loads into haemoglobin in erythrocytes and CO₂ produced by cellular metabolism unloads into expired air. Situated in the thoracic cavity on either side of the heart, they are enclosed by the pleural membranes and their mechanics are intimately coupled to the chest wall and diaphragm.

The right lung (3 lobes: upper, middle, lower) comprises approximately 57% of total lung volume; the left lung (2 lobes: upper, lower) is smaller to accommodate the leftward displacement of the heart. Together they contain 23 generations of branching airways, ~300–500 million alveoli providing ~70 m² of gas-exchange surface, and approximately 1,500 km of airways including bronchioles.

Conducting zone (generations 0–16): dead space, no gas exchange — ~150 mL total volume. This is the anatomical dead space that must be overcome with each breath. Respiratory zone (generations 17–23): gas exchange — ~2,500–3,000 mL at FRC (functional residual capacity).

Alveolar Units — Surfactant and Pneumocytes

Each alveolus is a ~0.2 mm air sac lined by two principal cell types:

Type I pneumocytes (AT1)

Flat, squamous cells covering ~95% of alveolar surface area. The gas-exchange cell: O₂ and CO₂ diffuse across the ultrathin (<0.3 µm) AT1 cell and basement membrane into pulmonary capillary blood. Cannot proliferate — injury requires AT2 transdifferentiation.

Type II pneumocytes (AT2)

Cuboidal cells covering ~5% of surface area. Produce surfactant (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine, DPPC + proteins SP-A, SP-B, SP-C, SP-D) — reduces alveolar surface tension, prevents collapse at end-expiration. Also the alveolar stem cell: proliferates and transdifferentiates to AT1 after injury.

Surfactant deficiency in preterm infants (<28 weeks, before AT2 maturation) → infant respiratory distress syndrome (IRDS/RDS). Treatment: exogenous surfactant instillation + positive-pressure ventilation. Antenatal corticosteroids accelerate AT2 maturation. In adults, AT2 dysfunction and surfactant loss contribute to ARDS pathophysiology.

Pleura and Vascular Anatomy

Each lung is enclosed in visceral and parietal pleura with a fluid-filled potential space (~5–20 mL at normal pressure ~−5 cmH₂O). This negative intrapleural pressure holds the lung "open" — the inward elastic recoil of the lung is balanced by the outward recoil of the chest wall at FRC. Loss of negative pressure (pneumothorax: air enters pleural space) → lung collapses toward its natural recoil state.

Gas Exchange Mechanics

Tidal breathing at rest: ~0.5 L tidal volume, 16–20 breaths/min = ~6–8 L/min minute ventilation. Alveolar ventilation subtracts the dead space:

V̇A = f × (VT − VD) = 16 × (500 − 150) = 5,600 mL/min
f = respiratory rate (breaths/min), VT = tidal volume, VD = dead space (~150 mL at rest)

Gas exchange follows Fick's law — flux is proportional to surface area × diffusion coefficient / membrane thickness. The diffusing capacity of the lung for CO (DLCO) is a clinical measure of the gas-exchange surface area × diffusivity product:

DLCO = V̇CO / (PACO − PcCO)
Falls in emphysema (lost surface area) and pulmonary fibrosis (thickened membrane)

At the alveolar-capillary interface: O₂ diffuses from alveolar air (PAO₂ ~100 mmHg) into pulmonary capillary blood (PvO₂ ~40 mmHg); CO₂ diffuses in the opposite direction (PvCO₂ ~46 mmHg → PACO₂ ~40 mmHg). Equilibration is normally complete within ~0.25 s — less than one-third of the ~0.75 s capillary transit time at rest, leaving substantial diffusion reserve.

Non-Respiratory Functions

The lung is not purely a gas exchanger — it serves metabolic, filtration, and immune roles:

Metabolic conversion

Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) on pulmonary endothelium converts angiotensin I → angiotensin II. The lung also degrades bradykinin, inactivates serotonin, and synthesizes prostaglandins.

Microemboli filtration

The pulmonary microvasculature traps small venous emboli, preventing systemic embolization to the brain and kidneys. Patent foramen ovale bypasses this filter — source of paradoxical embolism.

Immune surveillance

Alveolar macrophages (the primary phagocyte of the alveolus), dendritic cells, and mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) in airways provide continuous surveillance against inhaled pathogens and particles.

Mucociliary clearance

Pseudostratified ciliated epithelium + goblet cell mucus in conducting airways form the mucociliary escalator — clearing particles and microorganisms continuously upward to the pharynx. Impaired in CF, primary ciliary dyskinesia, chronic bronchitis.

Pathology

COPD / Emphysema

Proteolytic destruction of alveolar walls by neutrophil and macrophage elastase (triggered by cigarette smoke → oxidative stress → NF-κB → protease-antiprotease imbalance) → loss of alveolar surface area → ↓DLCO → airflow obstruction from loss of elastic recoil + mucus hypersecretion (chronic bronchitis component). Spirometry: FEV₁/FVC <0.70; air trapping → ↑RV, ↑TLC. 3.2 million deaths/year (WHO).

Asthma

Eosinophilic airway inflammation + mast cell activation → bronchospasm + mucus hypersecretion → reversible airflow obstruction. Triggers: allergens, exercise, cold air, viral infections, NSAIDs. Spirometry: variable FEV₁/FVC reduction that reverses with bronchodilator (>12% improvement). Lung parenchyma typically preserved. Treatment: ICS + β2-agonists; biologics (anti-IL-5, anti-IL-4Rα) for severe eosinophilic asthma.

Pneumonia

Infection-induced alveolar consolidation (exudate filling air sacs → ↓ventilation while perfusion maintained → V/Q mismatch → hypoxemia). Bacterial: Streptococcus pneumoniae most common community-acquired; Staphylococcus aureus in post-influenza. Viral: COVID-19 bilateral interstitial pneumonia; influenza primary viral pneumonia. Atypical: Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Legionella, Chlamydophila.

ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome)

Diffuse alveolar damage → alveolar flooding with protein-rich exudate (hyaline membranes) + destruction of AT1/AT2 cells → severe bilateral hypoxemia. Defined by: Berlin criteria PaO₂/FiO₂ <300, bilateral infiltrates, non-cardiogenic. Triggers: pneumonia (including COVID-19), sepsis, aspiration, trauma. Mortality 30–50% in severe ARDS. Treatment: lung-protective ventilation (6 mL/kg IBW, plateau P <30 cmH₂O), prone positioning, conservative fluid strategy.

Lung Cancer

Leading cause of cancer death worldwide (~1.8 million deaths/year). Non-small cell (~85%): adenocarcinoma (peripheral, EGFR/KRAS mutations, non-smokers included), squamous cell carcinoma (central, smoking-related), large cell. Small cell (~15%): highly aggressive, rapid growth, early metastasis, neuroendocrine origin, paraneoplastic syndromes. Smoking: attributable risk ~85%. Targeted therapies for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF mutations revolutionized advanced NSCLC.

Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)

Progressive fibrotic replacement of alveoli (UIP pattern: honeycombing + traction bronchiectasis on HRCT) → restriction (↓TLC, ↓FVC) → impaired DLCO → progressive hypoxemia → respiratory failure. Median survival 3–5 years. No disease-modifying therapy except anti-fibrotics (nintedanib, pirfenidone — slow progression) and lung transplantation.
