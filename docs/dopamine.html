Dopamine (DA)

The reward catecholamine — synthesised from L-DOPA in VTA and substantia nigra neurons, encoding reward prediction error and controlling motor, cognitive, and hormonal circuits via D1–D5 GPCRs.

MW 153.18 g/mol. Numerically rare (~500,000 neurons) yet projects to vast CNS territories. Synthesis: tyrosine → L-DOPA (TH, rate-limiting) → dopamine (AADC). Degradation: MAO-A/B → DOPAC; COMT → 3-MT; combined → HVA. Reuptake: DAT (SLC6A3), target of cocaine and amphetamine. Four CNS pathways: mesolimbic, nigrostriatal, mesocortical, tuberoinfundibular.

Dopamine (DA)

Class: Catecholamine  ·  Neurotransmitter  ·  Neuromodulator  ·  Adrenal hormone (minor)  |  Gene: TH (rate-limiting step)  |  Transporter: DAT (SLC6A3)  |  Precursor to: Norepinephrine, Epinephrine

Dopamine (DA; 3,4-dihydroxyphenethylamine) is a catecholamine neurotransmitter that serves as the brain's master valuation signal — phasic dopamine bursts encode reward prediction errors (Schultz 1998) that drive reinforcement learning across limbic and cortical circuits. Despite constituting only ~0.0003% of brain neurons, dopaminergic cells (VTA, SNc, arcuate nucleus) project to virtually every brain region via four canonical pathways. Degeneration of the nigrostriatal pathway causes Parkinson's disease; dysregulation of the mesolimbic pathway underlies addiction and schizophrenia; insufficient mesocortical dopamine produces the negative and cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia and contributes to ADHD and depression. Dopamine is also the precursor to norepinephrine (via DβH) and epinephrine (via PNMT), making it the hub of catecholamine biosynthesis.

Structure & Chemistry

Dopamine is a catecholamine: benzene-1,2-diol (catechol) ring + two-carbon aminoethyl side chain. Molecular formula C₈H₁₁NO₂; MW 153.18 g/mol. It bears a primary amine (NH₂) — unlike epinephrine (N-methyl) — giving it moderate α-adrenergic and β-adrenergic activity at high concentrations but preferential action at dopaminergic D1–D5 GPCRs at physiological concentrations in the CNS.

The catechol ring is essential for receptor binding and is also the site of COMT O-methylation (→ 3-methoxytyramine, 3-MT) in catabolism. The ethylamine sidechain is oxidised by MAO-A and MAO-B to produce DOPAC. The terminal degradation product is homovanillic acid (HVA), measured in CSF as a marker of central dopaminergic turnover.

Reward prediction error (RPE): DA neurons fire phasically for unexpected reward (positive RPE), are suppressed for omitted expected reward (negative RPE), and show no change for fully predicted reward. This temporal difference signal was established by Wolfram Schultz (1998) and is the cellular substrate of Pavlovian and instrumental conditioning.

Dopamine hypothesis update: Classic "DA excess" in schizophrenia applies only to the mesolimbic pathway. The mesocortical pathway shows DA hypofunction, explaining negative and cognitive symptoms that D2 blockers do not address. Aripiprazole, as a D2 partial agonist, theoretically reduces mesolimbic excess while preserving mesocortical tone — though clinical data show only modest advantages on negative symptoms.
