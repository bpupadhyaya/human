Connexin-43

Connexin-43 (Cx43, gene GJA1) — the primary gap junction protein of ventricular working myocardium. Six subunits form a hexameric connexon; two connexons dock end-to-end at the intercalated disc forming a ~1.4 nS aqueous pore that makes the myocardium an electrical syncytium. Cx43 density, distribution, and phosphorylation state are the molecular determinants of cardiac conduction velocity, anisotropy, and arrhythmia susceptibility.

Gene: GJA1 · 6q22.31 UniProt: P17302 Aliases: Cx43, GJA1, gap junction alpha-1 Scale: 03-Molecular

Overview

Connexin-43 (Cx43), encoded by GJA1, is the primary gap-junction protein of the ventricular working myocardium and the molecular structure that makes the heart a functional electrical syncytium. Six Cx43 subunits assemble into a connexon (hemichannel) in the plasma membrane; two connexons from adjacent cells dock head-to-head at the intercalated disc to form a complete gap junction channel with a single-channel conductance of approximately 1.4 nS.

These gap junction channels are aqueous pores (~1.5 nm internal diameter) wide enough to pass ions, metabolites, second messengers (cAMP, IP₃), and small molecules up to ~1,000 Da directly between the cytoplasms of adjacent cardiomyocytes — without traversing the extracellular space. This direct cytoplasmic coupling enables the depolarization wave from the SA node to propagate cell-to-cell across the myocardium at ~0.3–1 m/s in working muscle, ensuring synchronous contraction of billions of cardiomyocytes as a single coordinated unit.

Lateralization in heart failure: Cx43 is down-regulated, dephosphorylated, and re-distributed away from intercalated discs ("lateralized") in failing ventricles — reducing electrical coupling, slowing conduction, and creating the substrate for re-entrant arrhythmias.

Connexon Assembly and Plaque Formation

Six Cx43 subunits oligomerize in the ER/Golgi to form a hexameric connexon, which traffics to the plasma membrane via microtubule-dependent vesicular transport. At the intercalated disc, connexons from adjacent cells dock via ECL1–ECL2 hydrophobic interactions and disulfide bonds, forming a complete gap junction channel.

Thousands of channels cluster into plaques (0.1–1 µm diameter, visible by freeze-fracture EM as ordered particle arrays). Cx43 plaques co-localize with N-cadherin (adherens junctions) and desmoplakin (desmosomes), forming the mechanically and electrically integrated intercalated disc junction complex.

Channel Gating

Cx43 channels close (gate) in response to: low intracellular pH (acidosis → CT domain intramolecular interaction → channel closure; adaptive response to ischemia that limits injury propagation); high [Ca²⁺]i (same CT-mediated gating, Ca²⁺ overload closes channels); and phosphorylation (Src kinase and PKC → channel closure; CK1-mediated phosphorylation at Ser residues → channel stabilization). Voltage-dependent gating occurs above ±30 mV transjunctional voltage but is not physiologically limiting during normal cardiac rhythms.

Permeability to Signaling Molecules

Beyond ions, Cx43 passes cAMP (synchronizes PKA signaling across cell groups), IP₃ (coordinates Ca²⁺ waves), ATP (paracrine purinergic signaling via hemichannel release), and potentially small RNAs (controversial). This metabolic and signaling coupling is a secondary function that may become important during ischemic preconditioning and remodeling.
