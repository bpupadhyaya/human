# Atlas 01-Human · Scale 01: Subatomic

This is the deepest scale of the Human Atlas. It captures the particles and quantum-mechanical phenomena that constitute all matter — including biological matter — and constrain the chemistry of life at the atomic scale above.

## What the subatomic scale captures for biology

| Particle / phenomenon | Biological relevance |
|:---|:---|
| **Electrons** | All chemical bonding is electron sharing or transfer. Redox reactions are electron transfers and are central to metabolism (electron transport chain, NADH/FADH₂, ATP synthesis). Electronegativity differences produce the polar bonds essential to protein folding and ion-channel selectivity. |
| **Protons (H⁺)** | pH is proton concentration. The proton-motive force across the inner mitochondrial membrane (established by electron transport chain pumping protons) drives ATP synthase — powering every heartbeat. Acid-base balance regulates enzyme activity throughout the body. |
| **Neutrons** | Isotopic composition of elements. In biological research: radiocarbon (¹⁴C) from atmospheric nuclear tests enables **bomb-pulse dating** of cardiomyocyte DNA, the method that established adult human cardiomyocyte turnover rate (~1%/year). |
| **Photons** | Light energy drives spectroscopic measurement techniques (NMR, X-ray crystallography, fluorescence). In retinal cells (rhodopsin), photon absorption is the biological signal transduction event. In mitochondria, near-infrared photons can modulate cytochrome c oxidase function. |

## Quantum effects in biology

At this scale, quantum mechanics governs:

- **Electron tunneling** in enzyme catalysis and mitochondrial electron transport (long-range electron transfer across protein complexes)
- **Proton tunneling** in enzyme-catalyzed hydrogen transfers
- **Quantum coherence** — controversial but studied in photosynthetic energy transfer; potential relevance to enzyme catalysis
- **Wave-particle duality** — constrains the behavior of electrons in atomic orbitals, determining the geometry and energy of every chemical bond

These quantum phenomena constrain the atomic and molecular levels above: the shapes of atomic orbitals determine bond angles, which determine protein structure, which determines protein function.
