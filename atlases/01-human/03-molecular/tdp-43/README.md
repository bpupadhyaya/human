# TDP-43

## Overview

**TDP-43** (TAR DNA-binding protein 43, encoded by the *TARDBP* gene on chromosome 1p36.2) is a ubiquitously expressed, predominantly nuclear **RNA-binding protein (RBP)** that regulates multiple steps of RNA metabolism — including pre-mRNA splicing, mRNA stability, transport, and microRNA biogenesis. It was originally identified as a transcriptional repressor of the HIV-1 TAR element and was not associated with neurodegeneration until the landmark 2006 discovery by Neumann et al. [^neumann-2006-tdp43-discovery] that **hyperphosphorylated, ubiquitinated, C-terminally cleaved TDP-43** constitutes the major protein component of inclusion bodies in:

1. **Frontotemporal lobar degeneration with ubiquitin-positive inclusions (FTLD-TDP)** — the most common FTLD subtype (~45% of all FTLD)
2. **Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)** — >97% of all ALS cases, whether sporadic or familial

This discovery unified two previously separate diseases under the concept of **TDP-43 proteinopathy** and established TDP-43 as the defining pathological marker of ALS/FTLD. Subsequently, Sreedharan et al. [^sreedharan-2008-tardbp-mutation] identified missense mutations in *TARDBP* itself as causes of familial ALS (~4% of fALS), proving that TDP-43 dysfunction is not just a marker but a cause of motor neuron disease.

## Structure

### Protein structure

TDP-43 is a 414-amino acid, 43 kDa protein with:
- **N-terminal domain** (NTD, aa 1–76): dimerization; nuclear localization via interaction with importin-α
- **Nuclear localization signal (NLS)** (aa 82–98): required for nuclear import; mutated in some pathological contexts
- **RNA-recognition motif 1 (RRM1, aa 104–176)**: primary RNA-binding domain; binds UG-rich sequences and GGUG motifs; essential for RNA splicing regulation
- **RNA-recognition motif 2 (RRM2, aa 192–259)**: secondary RNA-binding; DNA binding; single-stranded nucleic acid interactions
- **Nuclear export signal (NES)** (aa 239–250): within RRM2; CRM1-dependent nuclear export (modest under basal conditions)
- **Glycine-rich C-terminal domain (CTD, aa 274–414)**: intrinsically disordered; **low-complexity domain (LCD)** with GXXXG and [G/S]Y[G/S] repeats; **prion-like domain** — drives liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) and, pathologically, irreversible aggregation; site of ALS-associated mutations; site of C-terminal fragments (CTFs, ~25 and 35 kDa) generated by caspase-3 cleavage

**ALS-causing TARDBP mutations:** >50 missense mutations identified, with ~90% clustering in the glycine-rich CTD (e.g., A315T, M337V, Q331K, G348C, N352S). These mutations increase CTD aggregation propensity without necessarily impairing RNA binding — consistent with a gain-of-toxic-function mechanism.

### Normal function: TDP-43 in RNA metabolism

Under physiological conditions, nuclear TDP-43 performs multiple RNA processing functions:

| Function | Mechanism | Key targets |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pre-mRNA splicing** | Binds UG repeats in introns → promotes or represses exon inclusion; component of splicing machinery | STMN2 (stathmin-2) exon skipping; UNC13A; CFTR exon 9; hundreds of neuronal transcripts |
| **mRNA stability** | Binds AU-rich elements in 3'UTRs → protects mRNA from decay | VEGF, GRN (progranulin), NF-M |
| **mRNA transport** | Associates with mRNA granules → facilitates dendritic/axonal transport | Local synaptic protein synthesis |
| **microRNA biogenesis** | Interacts with Drosha/DGCR8 complex → facilitates pri-miRNA processing | miR-9, miR-132 (neuronal miRNAs) |
| **Stress granule dynamics** | Reversibly localizes to cytoplasmic stress granules under stress; RRM1 required | SGs are proposed seeds for pathological inclusion formation |

**Critical neuronal RNA targets:**
- **STMN2 (stathmin-2):** Required for axon regeneration; nuclear TDP-43 promotes exon inclusion → functional STMN2 protein. When TDP-43 is lost from nucleus → aberrant exon inclusion → truncated, non-functional STMN2 → impaired axon repair. This is a major driver of ALS motor neuron degeneration and is the basis for ASO and gene therapy strategies (restored by antisense oligonucleotide PS-ASO 2023).
- **UNC13A:** Synaptic vesicle release factor; cryptic exon inclusion when TDP-43 lost from nucleus → UNC13A protein loss → impaired neurotransmitter release.
- **FUS, ATXN2, hnRNPs:** TDP-43 regulates splicing of other ALS-linked genes.

## Function

### TDP-43 phase separation and aggregation

TDP-43's glycine-rich CTD (prion-like domain) undergoes **liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS)** — a reversible condensation into liquid droplets that concentrate TDP-43 and its RNA partners. This phase separation:
- Is essential for normal stress granule formation and RNA granule dynamics
- Allows TDP-43 to concentrate with regulatory RNAs in specific nuclear condensates
- Is driven by multivalent, weak interactions between the prion-like domain repeats

Under pathological conditions (aging, oxidative stress, mutations, hnRNP disturbance), LLPS transitions to **liquid-to-solid phase transition** — TDP-43 droplets progressively gelify → form amyloid-like fibrils → constitute pathological inclusions resistant to cellular clearance.

### Pathological TDP-43 in ALS

The defining pathological features of TDP-43 proteinopathy:

**Nuclear clearance:** TDP-43 is lost from the nucleus of affected neurons — the earliest detectable change. This nuclear depletion causes loss of TDP-43's RNA processing functions → STMN2 and UNC13A cryptic exon inclusion → motor neuron dysfunction.

**Cytoplasmic mislocalization:** TDP-43 accumulates in the cytoplasm of the same neurons showing nuclear clearance. Cytoplasmic TDP-43:
- Is ubiquitinated (at Lys48, via TRAF6 E3 ubiquitin ligase)
- Is phosphorylated at Ser403/404 and Ser409/410 (pathological phospho-epitopes recognized by diagnostic antibodies)
- Is cleaved by caspase-3 to generate C-terminal fragments (CTF25, CTF35) — more aggregation-prone than full-length TDP-43
- Forms cytoplasmic inclusions that co-stain with p62/SQSTM1 (autophagy receptor) and optineurin — indicating autophagy-mediated clearance attempt

**Prion-like propagation:** Cell-culture and mouse model evidence suggests TDP-43 aggregates can be released extracellularly and taken up by neighboring cells, seeding aggregation in a prion-like pattern — consistent with the rostrocaudal clinical spread of ALS weakness from onset site.

## Mechanism

### TDP-43 vs. FUS/TLS — related RNA-binding proteins in ALS

| Feature | TDP-43 | FUS |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Gene | *TARDBP* (1p36) | *FUS* (16p11) |
| % familial ALS | ~4% | ~4–5% |
| % sporadic ALS | Pathological marker in >97% | Pathological marker in <10% |
| Domain structure | NLS + 2 RRMs + glycine-rich CTD | NLS + RRM + glycine-rich + RGG repeats + zinc finger |
| Pathological change | Nuclear clearance + cytoplasmic inclusions | Nuclear clearance + cytoplasmic inclusions |
| RNA targets | STMN2, UNC13A, hundreds of neuronal RNAs | Similar RNA metabolism roles |
| Stress granule | TDP-43 localizes to SGs transiently | FUS localizes to SGs; mutations cause irreversible localization |
| Therapeutic targeting | ASO restoring STMN2 (Phase 1/2 ongoing); TDP-43 nuclear retention strategies | Research phase; FUS-NLS mutations targetable by ASO in mice |

### Therapeutic strategies targeting TDP-43

1. **Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs):** 
   - STMN2-restoring ASO (PS-ASO): blocks the cryptic exon inclusion caused by TDP-43 loss → restores functional STMN2 → improves axon regeneration (Phase 1/2, Clene Nanomedicine/UCB); proof-of-concept: restores STMN2 in ALS patient iPSC-derived motor neurons
   - Direct TDP-43 targeting: reducing TDP-43 expression in SOD1 ALS paradoxically worsens — emphasizing that TDP-43 nuclear function must be preserved, not ablated

2. **Autophagy enhancement:** Rapamycin/everolimus → mTOR inhibition → enhanced autophagy → TDP-43 aggregate clearance; trehalose induces autophagy independently of mTOR; valproate (HDAC inhibitor/autophagy inducer) — limited clinical benefit in ALS trials

3. **Phase separation modulation:** Small molecules targeting the prion-like domain to prevent liquid-to-solid transition; YM3711 (TDP-43 aggregation inhibitor) in preclinical models

4. **ULF/nuclear retention:** Nuclear pore dysfunction (FG-nucleoporin aggregation) contributes to TDP-43 nuclear egress; importin-α/β restoration strategies under investigation

## Connections

**→ [ALS](../../07-system/als/)**: TDP-43 cytoplasmic inclusions are the defining pathological hallmark of >97% of ALS; TARDBP mutations cause ~4% of familial ALS; nuclear TDP-43 loss disrupts STMN2 and UNC13A splicing, driving axonal degeneration and synaptic failure in motor neurons.

**→ [mTOR](../mtor/)**: impaired autophagy delays TDP-43 aggregate clearance; mTOR inhibition (rapamycin) reduces inclusion burden in ALS cell models; p62/SQSTM1 and optineurin (autophagy receptors that are mTOR-regulated) are consistent components of TDP-43 inclusions, indicating failed selective autophagy.
