# Scale 03 — Molecular

The working parts of cells: proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, sugars, ions, and small molecules.

## What this scale captures

Molecules are where biology becomes chemistry that *does work*. A heartbeat is an army of myosin motors pulling on actin in step with calcium ions arriving from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. A signal from adrenaline reaches a chamber wall through a single G-protein-coupled receptor on each cell. Everything at higher scales — cells, tissues, organs — is built out of, and constrained by, the molecules at this level.

Entries at this scale describe **structure, mechanism, and interactions** — what the molecule looks like, what reactions it catalyzes or transmissions it carries, and which other molecules it talks to.

## Connections to other scales

- **Up to [04-cellular](../04-cellular/README.md)** — cells *express* molecules; a cell's identity is largely defined by which molecules it makes and at what level.
- **Up to [05-tissue](../05-tissue/README.md)** — extracellular matrix proteins (collagens, elastin, laminins) belong at this scale but operate at the tissue scale.
- **Down to [02-atomic](../02-atomic/README.md)** — every molecule is an arrangement of atoms; some entries at this scale will reference specific atomic-scale interactions (e.g., the catalytic zinc in a metalloprotease).

## Cross-atlas connections

Most pharmacology and immunology bridges happen at this scale:
- **Medicines** typically *target* a molecular entry. A β-blocker targets the β1-adrenergic receptor.
- **Pathogens** exploit molecular entries — receptors as entry points, kinases as hijacked machinery, ion channels as targets of toxins.
