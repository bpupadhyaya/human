# APP

## Overview

**APP** (amyloid precursor protein; gene *APP*, chromosome 21q21.3) is a **type I transmembrane glycoprotein** expressed ubiquitously in neurons, particularly at synapses, and is the source of the **amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides** that accumulate as extracellular plaques in Alzheimer's disease (AD). APP exists in several isoforms (695, 751, 770 amino acids) generated by alternative splicing; the neuronal isoform APP695 lacks the Kunitz protease inhibitor (KPI) domain.

The **amyloid cascade hypothesis** — formulated by Hardy and Higgins (1992) and refined by Hardy and Selkoe (2002) — holds that aberrant APP processing leading to Aβ42 overproduction or underclearing is the initiating event of AD [^hardy-2002-amyloid-hypothesis]. Genetic validation is compelling: trisomy 21 (Down syndrome) patients carry an extra copy of the *APP* gene on chromosome 21 and develop AD pathology nearly universally by their 4th–5th decade. Rare **familial AD (FAD) mutations** in *APP* (V717I/London; K670N/M671L/Swedish; E693G/Arctic; E693K/Italian) all elevate Aβ42 production or aggregation. Conversely, an *APP* variant in the Icelandic population (A673T) reduces BACE1 cleavage by 40% and confers protection against AD and cognitive decline in the elderly.

The field has come full circle with the 2023 FDA approval of **lecanemab (Leqembi)**, an anti-Aβ protofibril monoclonal antibody, which reduced amyloid burden and slowed clinical decline by 27% in early AD (CLARITY AD trial; HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.61–0.87) [^vandyck-2023-lecanemab], and **donanemab** (2024), which cleared plaques and slowed decline 35% in amyloid-positive MCI. These results vindicate the amyloid hypothesis as a disease-modifying target while underscoring the need for early intervention.

## Structure

APP695 (the neuronal isoform) is an 87-kDa glycoprotein (apparent MW ~120 kDa by SDS-PAGE due to glycosylation) with distinct functional domains:

**Extracellular N-terminal ectodomain:**
- **E1 domain (aa 1–189):** Heparin-binding domain and copper/zinc-binding domain; interacts with extracellular matrix components (laminin, collagen IV)
- **KPI domain (aa 190–288, absent in APP695):** Kunitz-type protease inhibitor; present in APP751/770; expressed more in non-neuronal cells
- **E2 domain (aa 289–500):** Central flexible linker; second heparin-binding region

**Aβ-containing transmembrane segment:**
- **Aβ sequence (aa 672–713):** Spans the luminal ectodomain–transmembrane boundary; Aβ40 ends at Val40, Aβ42 at Ala42 (within TMD); the two extra C-terminal residues of Aβ42 dramatically increase aggregation propensity
- **β-cleavage site (Met671/Asp672, BACE1 site — Swedish FAD hotspot):** Determines the N-terminus of Aβ
- **α-cleavage site (Lys687/Leu688, ADAM10 site):** Cleaves within the Aβ sequence → prevents Aβ production

**C-terminal intracellular domain:**
- **AICD (APP intracellular domain, aa 720–770):** Released after γ-secretase cleavage; nuclear signaling role (Fe65/Tip60 interaction); may regulate gene expression including neprilysin (Aβ-degrading enzyme)
- **YENPTY motif:** Clathrin-adaptor AP interaction site; mediates APP internalization (endocytosis — the amyloidogenic compartment)

## Function

**Normal APP function:**
APP has been implicated in **synaptogenesis, synaptic plasticity, neurite outgrowth, neuronal survival, and copper homeostasis**. The soluble ectodomain sAPPα (released by α-secretase) is neuroprotective — promotes neurite outgrowth, synaptic plasticity, and long-term potentiation; has neuroprotective activity against NMDA-mediated excitotoxicity and ischemia. Physiological function is partially redundant (APP knockout mice are viable but show memory deficits and increased locomotor activity).

**APP trafficking and amyloidogenesis:**
- **Non-amyloidogenic pathway (~90% of APP processing):** ADAM10 (or ADAM17) cleaves APP at the cell surface within the Aβ domain → sAPPα + C83; C83 is then cleaved by γ-secretase → harmless p3 peptide; predominant at surface
- **Amyloidogenic pathway (~10%, but produces all AD-associated Aβ):** APP internalized via YENPTY/clathrin → early endosomes (acidic pH → favorable for BACE1) → BACE1 (β-site APP cleaving enzyme 1) cleaves at β-site → sAPPβ + C99; C99 → γ-secretase complex (PSEN1/PSEN2 + nicastrin + APH-1 + PEN-2) → Aβ40 (major, ~90%) and **Aβ42 (minor, ~10%; more toxic and aggregation-prone)**

**Aβ aggregation and toxicity cascade:**
1. Aβ42 monomers → **dimers and small oligomers (2–12-mers)** — most synaptotoxic; inhibit LTP, activate kinases (CDK5, GSK-3β) → tau hyperphosphorylation; disrupt synaptic vesicle release; activate NMDA receptors → Ca²⁺ dysregulation
2. Oligomers → **protofibrils** — soluble aggregates; preferential target of lecanemab
3. Protofibrils → **mature amyloid fibrils → senile plaques** (neuritic plaques with tau-positive dystrophic neurites and activated microglia; diffuse plaques with fewer dystrophic neurites)
4. Plaques → microglial and astrocyte activation → NLRP3 inflammasome → IL-1β → neuroinflammation → tau propagation → neuronal death

## Mechanism

**BACE1 (β-secretase) — the rate-limiting amyloidogenic step:**
- Aspartyl protease; peak activity at pH 4–5 (endosomal); 9-transmembrane-spanning type I protein
- Swedish FAD mutation (K670N/M671L) dramatically increases BACE1 recognition (~6-fold) → elevated Aβ
- BACE1 is upregulated by oxidative stress, ER stress, and neuroinflammation — a convergence point amplifying amyloidogenesis
- BACE1 inhibitors (verubecestat, atabecestat, lanabecestat) failed Phase 3 AD trials despite Aβ lowering — suggesting irreversible downstream pathology at the treatment stage; early intervention may be required

**γ-secretase — the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio determinant:**
- Multiprotein intramembrane protease complex: PSEN1 (or PSEN2) as catalytic subunit + nicastrin (substrate docking) + APH-1 + PEN-2
- **Presenilin mutations (>300 known):** Alter γ-secretase processivity → elevated Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio → increased aggregation; most aggressive FAD
- γ-Secretase inhibitors failed AD trials due to Notch off-target effects and worsening cognition (γ-secretase processes >90 substrates including Notch)
- **γ-secretase modulators (GSMs):** Shift cleavage toward shorter Aβ peptides; in early development

**Anti-Aβ immunotherapy — validated therapeutic approach:**
- **Aducanumab (2021, FDA accelerated approval, withdrawn 2024):** Anti-Aβ aggregates mAb; cleared plaques but marginal clinical benefit; controversial approval
- **Lecanemab (Leqembi, 2023 FDA approval):** Anti-Aβ protofibrils IgG1; CLARITY AD: 27% slowing of CDR-SB decline, 26% reduction in ADCS-ADL, 65% plaque reduction (SUVr); biweekly IV infusion; ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities — ARIA-E and ARIA-H) in ~35% (mostly asymptomatic); symptomatic ARIA ~3% [^vandyck-2023-lecanemab]
- **Donanemab (Kisunla, 2024 FDA approval):** Anti-N3pG-Aβ (pyroglutamate form, plaque-specific); TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2: 35% slowing in early/mid AD; monthly infusion; treatment stops after plaque clearance
- **Genetic therapies:** BACE1 siRNA, APP ASOs targeting specifically toxic Aβ42-producing isoforms — in preclinical development

## Connections

APP FAD mutations (V717I, Swedish K670N/M671L) and trisomy 21 increase Aβ42 via β/γ-secretase cleavage; Aβ42 oligomers are synaptotoxic and seed amyloid plaques; lecanemab (anti-Aβ protofibrils) slows cognitive decline 27% in MCI and mild AD.

APOE4 impairs Aβ clearance from brain parenchyma via LRP1-mediated transport and microglial phagocytosis; APOE4 binds Aβ oligomers and promotes their aggregation; APOE4 homozygotes develop amyloid pathology ~10 years earlier than APOE3 carriers.

Aβ oligomers and amyloid fibrils activate the NLRP3 inflammasome in microglia → IL-1β and IL-18 → neuroinflammation; cholesterol crystals from lipid-laden plaques also activate NLRP3; IL-1β blockade reduces amyloid burden in mouse models of AD.

mTOR hyperactivation in Alzheimer disease impairs autophagy → Aβ and tau accumulation; rapamycin reduces amyloid burden in APP mouse models; mTOR is activated by Aβ oligomers via PI3K/Akt → positive feedback loop amplifying Aβ pathology.
