# Bradykinin

## Overview

**Bradykinin** (Arg-Pro-Pro-Gly-Phe-Ser-Pro-Phe-Arg; 9 amino acids, ~1.06 kDa) is a **vasoactive peptide** generated by the **kallikrein-kinin system** — not encoded as a prepropeptide like most peptide hormones, but cleaved from its precursor protein **high-molecular-weight kininogen (HMWK; gene *KNG1*, chromosome 3q27.3)** by plasma **kallikrein (KLKB1)**. A related peptide, **Lys-bradykinin (kallidin)**, is generated from low-molecular-weight kininogen (LMWK) by tissue kallikreins.

Bradykinin was discovered in 1949 by Rocha e Silva as a contractile agent in gut smooth muscle preparations — named for its "slow" (brady) onset relative to histamine. It is now understood as a principal **endogenous vasodilator and inflammatory mediator** whose physiological functions include vasodilation (via NO and PGI₂), increase in vascular permeability, pain sensitization, and smooth muscle contraction.

**Clinical significance of bradykinin:**
1. **ACE inhibitor cough** (10–15% of patients): ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) is identical to **kininase II** — it simultaneously converts Ang I → Ang II AND degrades bradykinin into inactive fragments. ACE inhibition → bradykinin accumulation → B2R on airway epithelial cells → PGE₂ and substance P → sensitizes vagal cough receptors → dry, persistent cough — a class effect of all ACE inhibitors; resolved by switching to ARB (which does not affect bradykinin)
2. **ACE inhibitor angioedema**: Bradykinin excess → B2R on dermal microvascular endothelium → increased permeability → angioedema; incidence 0.1–0.5%; higher risk in patients of African ancestry (RAAS-independent bradykinin accumulation); laryngeal involvement → potentially fatal airway obstruction
3. **Hereditary angioedema (HAE)**: C1-esterase inhibitor (C1-INH, encoded by *SERPING1*) normally restrains plasma kallikrein and Factor XIIa → C1-INH deficiency → unregulated kallikrein → bradykinin excess → episodic non-pitting, non-pruritic angioedema (NOT histamine-mediated; antihistamines and corticosteroids ineffective) [^zuraw-2010-hae-nejm]

**Bradykinin receptors — two opposing profiles:**

| Receptor | Gene | Expression | Coupling | Ligand preference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B2** | *BDKRB2* | Constitutive (endothelium, smooth muscle, CNS, kidney) | Gq (PLC → Ca²⁺) + Gi (eNOS → NO) | Bradykinin > des-Arg⁹-BK |
| **B1** | *BDKRB1* | Inducible by IL-1β, TNF-α, LPS (virtually absent in healthy tissue) | Gq + Gi | des-Arg⁹-bradykinin (carboxypeptidase metabolite) > bradykinin |

The B2 receptor mediates **acute vasodilation and vascular permeability** (ACE-I adverse effects, HAE attacks). The B1 receptor mediates **chronic inflammatory pain** and **persistent vascular pathology** (diabetes, inflammation), and is induced by NF-κB — making it a potential target for chronic inflammatory conditions.

## Structure

**Bradykinin (9 aa: Arg¹-Pro²-Pro³-Gly⁴-Phe⁵-Ser⁶-Pro⁷-Phe⁸-Arg⁹):**
- No secondary structure in solution (flexible nonapeptide); adopts β-turn-like conformation upon B2R binding
- Pro²-Pro³: conformational constraint; not cleaved by prolyl endopeptidases (explains relative protease resistance)
- Arg¹ and Arg⁹: positive charges essential for receptor engagement; des-Arg⁹ form (carboxypeptidase N cleavage of C-terminal Arg) has 1000× lower B2R affinity but high B1R affinity
- Phe⁵ and Phe⁸: hydrophobic residues involved in B2R transmembrane domain interaction

**Kallikrein-kinin system cascade:**
1. Factor XII (Hageman factor) activation: contact with negatively charged surfaces (collagen, heparin, polyphosphate, aggregated Aβ) → Factor XIIa (serine protease)
2. Factor XIIa cleaves prekallikrein (KLKB1 zymogen) → plasma kallikrein (active)
3. Plasma kallikrein cleaves HMWK at Lys₃₈₀-Arg₃₈₁ and Lys₃₉₈-Thr₃₉₉ → liberates bradykinin (9-aa) + two flanking fragments
4. Bradykinin acts locally (half-life ~17 seconds in blood due to rapid enzymatic degradation)

**Bradykinin degradation:**
- **ACE (Kininase II):** C-terminal dipeptide cleavage: Arg-Pro-Pro-Gly-Phe-Ser-Pro-Phe-**Arg** → removes Phe-Arg → Arg-Pro-Pro-Gly-Phe-Ser-Pro (BK 1-7) → then removes Ser-Pro → major inactivation pathway (>80% pulmonary clearance in first pass)
- **Neprilysin (NEP):** Endopeptidase; cleaves at Phe⁵-Ser⁶ bond → BK 1-5 and BK 6-9; neprilysin inhibition (sacubitril) also raises bradykinin — potential contributor to ARNI's vasodilatory effects
- **Carboxypeptidase N/M:** Removes C-terminal Arg⁹ → des-Arg⁹-bradykinin → B1R agonist (shifts acute response to chronic B1R-driven inflammation)
- **Aminopeptidase P:** Removes N-terminal Arg¹ → des-Arg¹-BK → inactive; minor pathway

**C1-esterase inhibitor (C1-INH; SERPING1; chromosome 11q12.1):**
- Serine protease inhibitor (serpin); covalently inhibits plasma kallikrein, Factor XIIa, Factor XIa, and complement C1r/C1s
- C1-INH deficiency (HAE) → unchecked kallikrein → excess bradykinin → recurrent angioedema attacks
- C1-INH is an acute-phase protein; levels increase with androgens (explains why danazol, an androgen, was historically used to raise C1-INH levels in HAE prophylaxis)

## Function

**Vascular effects (B2R on endothelium — vasodilation):**
1. Bradykinin → B2R → Gq → PLC-β → IP₃ → ER Ca²⁺ release → calmodulin → eNOS (Ser¹¹⁷⁷ phosphorylation) → NO production → smooth muscle cGMP → vasodilation
2. Bradykinin → B2R → Gi → PI3K → Akt → eNOS (additional phosphorylation) → sustained NO
3. Bradykinin → B2R → PLA₂ → arachidonic acid → COX → PGI₂ (endothelial) → IP receptor → cAMP → smooth muscle relaxation + platelet disaggregation
4. Bradykinin → B2R on microvascular endothelium → tyrosine phosphorylation of VE-cadherin → adherens junction loosening → increased paracellular permeability → edema formation (site of ACE-I angioedema and HAE attacks)

**Airway effects (ACE inhibitor cough mechanism):**
- Bradykinin accumulation → B2R on airway epithelial cells and C-fiber nociceptors
- → PLA₂ → arachidonic acid → COX-2 → **PGE₂** → EP receptors on vagal afferent C-fibers → sensitizes TRPV1 and TRPA1 → lowered cough threshold
- Bradykinin also directly activates TRPV1 on airway sensory neurons (bradykinin → B2R → PKC → TRPV1 Thr704 phosphorylation → channel sensitization)
- Result: innocuous stimuli (cold air, talking, smoke) trigger cough → dry, non-productive, persistent ("ACE-I cough")
- Resolves within days-weeks of ACE-I discontinuation; does NOT occur with ARBs (which do not affect bradykinin)

**Pain sensitization (B1R — inflammatory context):**
- In inflammation: IL-1β + TNF-α → NF-κB → *BDKRB1* promoter → B1R expression on peripheral sensory neurons
- des-Arg⁹-bradykinin → B1R → PLC → PKC → sensitization of TRPA1 and TRPV1 → allodynia and hyperalgesia
- B1R is a chronic pain target (in contrast to B2R's acute vasodilation role)

## Mechanism

**Hereditary angioedema (HAE) — pathogenesis and treatment [^zuraw-2010-hae-nejm]:**

**Types:**
- **HAE type I** (85%): *SERPING1* LOF mutation → low C1-INH protein and activity; autosomal dominant; C1 and C4 complement levels chronically low (diagnostic)
- **HAE type II** (15%): Normal C1-INH protein quantity but functionally inactive (conformational mutations); C1 normal, C4 low
- **HAE with normal C1-INH (type III):** Factor XII GOF mutations (F12 p.Thr309Lys); predominantly women; estrogen-dependent (oral contraceptives, pregnancy trigger attacks); C4 normal
- **Acquired angioedema (AAE):** C1-INH consumption by lymphoproliferative disease or anti-C1-INH autoantibodies; low C1 (distinguishes from HAE); associated with CLL, MGUS

**Triggers of HAE attacks:** Trauma, surgery, dental procedures, stress, estrogen (OCP, HRT, pregnancy); Factor XII activation at wound sites → kallikrein burst → bradykinin

**Treatment landscape:**
| Agent | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Icatibant (Firazyr)** | Selective B2R antagonist (peptidomimetic) | Acute HAE attack; SC injection |
| **C1-INH concentrate (Berinert, Cinryze)** | Replaces C1-INH → restores kallikrein inhibition | Acute attack (IV) and prophylaxis (Cinryze SC) |
| **Ecallantide (Kalbitor)** | Plasma kallikrein inhibitor | Acute attack (SC); anaphylaxis risk → REMS |
| **Lanadelumab (Takhzyro)** | Anti-kallikrein mAb → prevents kallikrein activation | Long-term SC prophylaxis (Q2W or Q4W) |
| **Berotralstat (Orladeyo)** | Oral plasma kallikrein inhibitor | Long-term oral prophylaxis; FDA approved 2020 |
| **Garadacimab** | Anti-Factor XIIa mAb | Phase 3 prophylaxis; blocks contact pathway upstream |

**ACE inhibitor angioedema — management:**
- Immediate discontinuation of ACE-I (switch to ARB)
- Acute severe angioedema (tongue, larynx): IV C1-INH or icatibant (evidence weaker than in HAE but used off-label); epinephrine for bronchospasm; antihistamines/corticosteroids are INEFFECTIVE (not IgE/histamine-mediated)
- High-risk patients (prior ACE-I angioedema, African ancestry, hereditary PAI-1 excess): prefer ARB de novo

## Connections

Bradykinin → B2R on endothelium → eNOS → NO and PGI2 → vasodilation; ACE inhibitors raise bradykinin → contribute to vasodilatory cardioprotective effects; angioedema (B2R-mediated) and dry cough are bradykinin-dependent adverse effects of ACE inhibitor therapy.

ACE (kinase II) cleaves both Ang I → Ang II AND degrades bradykinin; ACE inhibition simultaneously reduces vasoconstriction (Ang II ↓) and raises vasodilation (bradykinin ↑); bradykinin contributes ~20% of ACE-I antihypertensive benefit independent of RAAS blockade.

Bradykinin → B2R → Gq/Gi → Ca2+/calmodulin → eNOS activation → NO production; NO is the primary mediator of bradykinin vasodilation; in atherosclerosis and diabetes, impaired B2R/eNOS signaling reduces bradykinin-mediated cardioprotection and endothelial function.

Bradykinin → B2R on airway epithelium → PLA2 → arachidonic acid → COX-2 → PGE2 → sensitizes TRPV1 cough receptors → ACE inhibitor dry cough; bradykinin also releases PGI2 from endothelium → IP receptor → cAMP → vasodilation and platelet inhibition.
