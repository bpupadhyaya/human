# Perforin

## Overview

**Perforin (PRF1)** is a **pore-forming cytotoxic protein** that is the central molecular weapon of **cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD8+ CTL)** and **natural killer (NK) cells** — the professional killers of the immune system. Stored in specialized secretory organelles called **lytic granules** (modified lysosomes), perforin is released with granzyme serine proteases onto target cell membranes to execute programmed cell death with surgical precision [^voskoboinik-2015-perforin-review].

Encoded by *PRF1* on chromosome 10q22.1, perforin belongs to the **MACPF/CDC (membrane attack complex/perforin/cholesterol-dependent cytolysin)** protein superfamily — structurally related to the complement system's C9 and bacterial CDCs — revealing an ancient evolutionary origin of membrane attack mechanisms in both innate immunity and the complement system.

**Clinical significance:**
- **Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis type 2 (FHL2):** Biallelic loss-of-function mutations in *PRF1* → absent or dysfunctional perforin → failure to eliminate activated macrophages → uncontrolled macrophage activation → hyperferritinemia, cytopenia, hepatosplenomegaly, and neurological involvement; 100% fatal without HSCT
- **Aplastic anemia:** Autoreactive CD8+ CTL overexpressing perforin destroy HSCs in the bone marrow → severe aplastic anemia (SAA); perforin+ CD8+ T cells in the marrow predict treatment response
- **Cancer immunotherapy:** Perforin-mediated cytotoxicity is the primary mechanism by which anti-tumor CTL (generated by PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, CAR-T cells) kill cancer cells

## Structure

**Gene and protein:**
- *PRF1* gene: chromosome **10q22.1**; 17.7 kb; 3 exons (coding sequence in exons 2 and 3)
- **Protein:** 555 aa pre-protein; 21 aa signal peptide → 534 aa mature protein (~67 kDa); synthesized as a pro-protein and stored in lytic granule at mildly acidic pH (~5.5-6.0) in monomeric, inactive state
- **Domain architecture (N→C):**
  - **Signal peptide** (aa 1-21): ER targeting
  - **MACPF domain** (aa 38-374): Membrane attack complex perforin superfamily fold; two predicted transmembrane hairpin helices (TMH1, TMH2) that refold upon Ca²⁺ binding from α-helical to β-hairpin → membrane insertion; contains a central CH (complement homology) region conserved with C9
  - **EGF-like domain** (aa 376-421): C-type lectin-like fold; unclear function; structural role
  - **C2 domain** (aa 422-534): Ca²⁺-dependent phospholipid-binding domain; critical for Ca²⁺-regulated membrane targeting; structurally similar to PKC and synaptotagmin C2 domains; binds phosphatidylserine/phosphatidylcholine on target membranes

**Ca²⁺ requirement:**
- Perforin is **strictly Ca²⁺-dependent** for membrane binding and pore assembly
- At lytic granule pH (~5.5): perforin maintained in monomeric form by electrostatic repulsion
- Upon exocytosis into the neutral/calcium-rich intercellular cleft (~pH 7.2, [Ca²⁺] ~1 mM): C2 domain binds Ca²⁺ (2 Ca²⁺ ions) → conformational change → C2 domain inserts into target membrane phospholipids → MACPF domain is positioned for membrane penetration → TMH1/TMH2 hairpins unfold → β-sheet membrane insertion

**Pore assembly:**
- Membrane-associated monomers oligomerize (12-18 monomers) → circular pre-pore ring (~15 nm diameter) → concerted conformational change → β-barrel pores spanning the lipid bilayer
- Pore inner diameter: ~10-20 nm → sufficient for granzyme B (~3 nm Stokes radius) entry
- Ca²⁺-dependent membrane targeting is the key regulatory mechanism — ensures perforin only acts on external target membranes (not the CTL's own membrane which lacks external phosphatidylserine at neutral pH)

**Granzymes:**
- **Granzyme B (GZMB):** Primary cytotoxic granzyme; 32 kDa serine protease; cleaves after Asp (asp-ase); key substrates: Bid (→ tBid → MOMP → cytochrome c → caspase-9 → caspase-3), procaspase-3 (direct activation), ICAD (→ CAD nuclease release → DNA laddering)
- **Granzyme A (GZMA):** 26 kDa; Arg/Lys-ase; cleaves SET complex (SET, pp32, Ape1) → DNase NM23-H1 release → single-strand DNA nicks; caspase-independent cell death; more relevant to NK-cell killing of viral targets
- **Granzyme M, H, K:** Minor granzymes; species-specific roles
- Granzymes require perforin for cytosolic entry; without perforin, granzymes traffic to lysosomes and are degraded

**CTL self-protection:**
- CTL express CD107a (LAMP-1) on lytic granule membranes → surfaces are exposed during degranulation → CD107a masks phosphatidylserine from perforin C2 domain targeting
- Granule membrane PI(4,5)P₂ also protects the CTL membrane from perforin at the immunological synapse
- Serine protease inhibitor PI-9 (SERPINB9) in CTL cytoplasm → inhibits any granzyme B that escapes into the CTL → self-protection from fratricide

## Function

**Lytic granule biogenesis and secretion:**
- CTL and NK cells constitutively synthesize perforin and granzymes; stored in **secretory lysosomes** (lytic granules): acidic, Ca²⁺-low environment → perforin inactive
- TCR recognition of cognate MHC-I/peptide on target cell → **immunological synapse** formation: TCR + LFA-1/ICAM-1 ring → SMAC (supra-molecular activation cluster) at center; centrosome/MTOC polarizes toward synapse → lytic granules traffic along microtubules to synapse → Rab27a + Munc13-4 + Munc18-2 mediate granule docking and priming at plasma membrane → **SNARE complex assembly** (VAMP7-STX11-SNAP23) → granule fusion → perforin + granzyme exocytosis into the synaptic cleft

**Perforin pathway vs. Fas-FasL pathway:**
- CD8+ CTL can kill via: (1) **Perforin/granzyme pathway** (dominant; fast, ~30 min) and (2) **Fas-FasL pathway** (CD8+ T cell FasL → target Fas/CD95 → FADD → caspase-8 → apoptosis; slower, ~6-8h; important for killing Fas-expressing tumor cells and activated lymphocytes)
- NK cells also use **ADCC (antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity)**: FcγRIIIA (CD16) binds IgG-opsonized targets → perforin/granzyme release without antigen specificity

**FHL pathophysiology:**
- Perforin-deficient CTL and NK cells cannot eliminate activated macrophages after viral infection (e.g., EBV, CMV, influenza) → persistent macrophage activation → IFN-γ → CXCL9/10 → further T cell recruitment → cytokine storm → hemophagocytosis (macrophage engulfment of blood cells in bone marrow/liver/spleen) → cytopenias + hyperferritinemia + hypertriglyceridemia → multi-organ failure
- **FHL types:** FHL1 (unknown gene), FHL2 (*PRF1*), FHL3 (*UNC13D/Munc13-4*), FHL4 (*STX11*), FHL5 (*STXBP2/Munc18-2*) — all affect the lytic granule secretory pathway

## Mechanism

**HLH (Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis) treatment:**
- **HLH-94/HLH-2004 protocol:** Dexamethasone (immunosuppression) + etoposide (depletes activated T cells) + cyclosporine (calcineurin inhibitor, blocks T cell activation); intrathecal MTX for CNS disease
- **Emapalumab (Gamifant; anti-IFN-γ mAb; Swedish Orphan Biovitrum):** FDA Nov 2018 for primary HLH refractory/relapsed/intolerant to conventional therapy; IFN-γ is the central cytokine driver of macrophage activation in HLH; neutralizing IFN-γ → 65% ORR in pivotal trial
- **HSCT:** Only curative option for primary HLH (FHL); reduced-intensity conditioning (fludarabine + busulfan + ATG); 5-year OS ~60-65%
- **Secondary HLH (MAS/sHLH):** Often triggered by EBV, CMV, or systemic JIA/SLE (macrophage activation syndrome, MAS); treatment: anakinra (IL-1Ra) for MAS; ruxolitinib (JAK1/2i) for EBV-HLH; emapalumab increasingly used
