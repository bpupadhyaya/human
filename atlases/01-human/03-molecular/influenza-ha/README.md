# Influenza Hemagglutinin

## Overview

**Influenza hemagglutinin (HA)** is the principal surface glycoprotein of the influenza virus and the primary target of protective humoral immunity. HA simultaneously performs two essential functions in the viral life cycle: (1) **receptor binding** — attachment to sialic acid-containing glycoproteins on the surface of host respiratory epithelial cells; and (2) **membrane fusion** — pH-triggered conformational change in the endosome that drives merging of viral and endosomal membranes. These two functions are executed by structurally and functionally distinct subunits, **HA1** (receptor binding) and **HA2** (membrane fusion), generated by proteolytic cleavage of the precursor HA0.

HA is the primary antigen in all influenza vaccines — inactivated, live-attenuated, recombinant, and mRNA-based. Its hypervariable HA1 globular head undergoes continuous **antigenic drift** (accumulation of point mutations in antigenic sites A-E), requiring annual influenza vaccine reformulation guided by the WHO Global Influenza Surveillance and Response System. The conserved **HA2 stalk**, targeted by broadly neutralizing antibodies (BNAbs) such as CR6261 and MEDI8852, is the focus of universal influenza vaccine research aimed at cross-subtype protection [^ekiert-2009-stalk-mab].

The original atomic-resolution structure of influenza A hemagglutinin was determined by X-ray crystallography at 3 Å by Wilson, Skehel, and Wiley in 1981 [^wilson-1981-ha-structure] — one of the landmark structures in structural virology, establishing the molecular basis for sialic acid recognition and the mechanism of membrane fusion.

## Structure

### Domain architecture

HA is synthesized as a single precursor polypeptide (**HA0**, ~570 aa) that is cleaved by host serine proteases into two disulfide-linked subunits:

| Domain | Subunit | Location | Conservation | Primary function |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Globular head** | HA1 | Distal (away from membrane) | Hypervariable | Sialic acid receptor binding; major antibody target |
| **Stalk/stem** | HA2 (+ HA1 proximal) | Proximal (near membrane) | Highly conserved across subtypes | Membrane fusion; target of broadly neutralizing antibodies |
| **Fusion peptide** | HA2 N-terminus (aa 1–23) | Buried in pre-fusion; inserts into membrane post-fusion | Conserved | Initiates membrane merger |
| **Transmembrane anchor** | HA2 C-terminus | Viral membrane | Conserved | Anchors HA in viral envelope |

Three HA monomers non-covalently assemble into the **homotrimeric spike** projecting from the viral membrane. The virion surface contains ~450–750 HA trimers alongside ~100 neuraminidase (NA) tetramers.

### HA1 globular head — receptor binding domain

The HA1 globular head consists of a **jelly-roll β-barrel** flanked by loops forming the **receptor binding site (RBS)** — a shallow pocket on the membrane-distal tip of each monomer. Key RBS structural elements:
- **220-loop, 130-loop, 190-helix** — three structural rim elements contacting sialic acid
- **Conserved RBS base residues**: Tyr98, Trp153, His183 (H3 numbering) — invariant in all subtypes
- **Specificity-determining residues**: Asp190, Glu190, Gln226, Gly228 → determine α2,6-SA (human) vs α2,3-SA (avian) preference

**Antigenic sites (H3 numbering, Wilson 1981):** Five antigenic sites on the HA1 head surface defined by naturally occurring escape mutations and monoclonal antibody resistance:

| Site | Location | Variability | Immunodominance |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **A** | Surrounds RBS at head tip | Highest | Most immunodominant; key pandemic strain selector |
| **B** | HA1 head proximal to RBS; partially overlaps SA binding | High | Second most immunodominant |
| **C** | Lateral face of HA1 head | Moderate | Less immunodominant |
| **D** | Between site B and HA1-HA1 interface | Moderate | Partially cross-reactive |
| **E** | Bottom of HA1 head near stalk | Lower | Partially cross-reactive |

Analogous sites in H1 HA: **Sa, Sb, Ca1, Ca2, Cb** (Caton et al. 1982).

### HA2 stalk — membrane fusion machinery

The HA2 stalk is a coiled-coil heptad repeat structure that drives the irreversible conformational change of membrane fusion [^skehel-2000-ha-review]:

**Pre-fusion state:** Metastable, spring-loaded triple-stranded coiled-coil; fusion peptide (N-terminal 23 aa) buried hydrophobically between monomers; maintained at neutral pH.

**Post-fusion state (6-helix bundle):** pH-triggered refolding → three inner helices (HR1) + three antiparallel outer helices (HR2) → **six-helix bundle (6HB)** — the thermodynamically stable post-fusion hairpin that drives membrane apposition and merger. This 6HB mechanism is shared across all **class I viral fusogens** (RSV F, HIV gp41, SARS-CoV-2 S2, paramyxovirus F).

**Conserved stalk epitopes targeted by BNAbs:**
- **Site I (Ekiert 2009)**: HA2 stalk helix; bound by CR6261 (Group 1 HA), 12D1; blocks fusion peptide insertion
- **Site II**: HA2 membrane-proximal; bound by MEDI8852 (cross-group); ADCC mechanism
- **Pan-influenza A**: FI6v3 contacts both HA1 base and HA2 stalk → cross-Group 1+2 neutralization

**Protease cleavage of HA0 — required for infectivity:**
- **Monobasic cleavage site** (seasonal H1N1, H3N2): Cleaved by TMPRSS2, trypsin-like proteases in respiratory epithelium → restricts infection to airways
- **Polybasic cleavage site** (-RRKKR- motif; HPAI H5N1, H7N9): Cleaved by furin (ubiquitous, intracellular) → systemic infection; major virulence determinant

### Sialic acid receptor binding specificity

| Receptor linkage | Tissue distribution | Preferred by |
|:---|:---|:---|
| α2,6-sialic acid (Siaα2,6Gal) | Human upper respiratory (trachea, bronchus, nasal epithelium) | Human-adapted H1N1, H3N2 |
| α2,3-sialic acid (Siaα2,3Gal) | Avian intestinal epithelium; human lower respiratory (alveoli) | Avian strains; H5N1; H5N2 |

Pandemic emergence requires HA to switch from α2,3-SA (avian preference) to α2,6-SA (human upper respiratory preference). Key pandemic-enabling mutations:
- **1968 H3 pandemic**: Q226L + G228S (from avian H3 → human-adapted H3)
- **H5N1 ferret-transmissible mutants** (Imai 2012): Q226L/G228S + HA stabilization mutations — experimental; not observed in nature at scale

## Function

### Receptor binding and endocytosis

1. HA1 RBS contacts sialic acid on airway epithelium → initial attachment; multiple simultaneous HA-SA contacts per virion provide avidity
2. Receptor-mediated endocytosis via clathrin-coated vesicles
3. Endosomal acidification to pH 5–6 triggers HA2 conformational change

### pH-triggered membrane fusion

The HA2-mediated fusion mechanism is the paradigm for **class I viral membrane fusion** [^skehel-2000-ha-review]:

1. **Trigger**: Endosomal acidification → histidine protonation at HA1/HA2 interface → electrostatic repulsion releases HA1 clamp on HA2 stalk
2. **Extension**: HA2 B-loop extends → projects fusion peptide ~100 Å toward endosomal membrane → fusion peptide inserts into endosomal lipid bilayer
3. **Hairpin ("jackknife") formation**: HA2 folds back → brings viral and endosomal membranes into apposition → hemifusion stalk
4. **Pore opening**: 6-helix bundle completion drives full membrane merger → fusion pore expansion → vRNP release into cytoplasm
5. **M2 contribution**: M2 proton channel simultaneously acidifies virion interior → destabilizes vRNP-M1 contacts → facilitates vRNP nuclear import

### Hemagglutination and HI assay

HA crosslinks sialic acid on red blood cells (RBCs), agglutinating them — the basis of the **hemagglutination inhibition (HI) assay** (anti-HA antibodies block hemagglutination → HAI titer ≥1:40 is the classic surrogate correlate of ~50% protection against influenza). HAI titer is the regulatory endpoint for influenza vaccine licensure.

## Mechanism

### Antigenic drift and annual vaccine reformulation

HA1 head antigenic sites (A-E for H3; Sa/Sb/Ca/Cb for H1) accumulate point mutations under immune selection pressure, producing **antigenic drift**:
1. Point mutations at antigenic sites alter charge/shape → reduce binding by existing population antibodies
2. Drifted variants gain transmission advantage in partially immune populations
3. Accumulation of mutations at 4–6 antigenic sites → antigenically distinct cluster emergence → new dominant strain
4. **WHO GISRS** monitors drift biannually (February for NH; September for SH) → selects 4 strains (H1N1 + H3N2 + 2 B lineages) for the seasonal vaccine formulation

**Vaccine match impact**: Good match years → 50-60% efficacy; mismatch years (e.g., 2014-15 H3N2) → <20% efficacy.

### Antigenic shift and pandemic emergence

Reassortment of HA gene segments from animal influenza A (avian, swine) with human influenza A → novel HA subtype in humans → **pandemic** (no pre-existing population immunity against new HA):

| Year | HA change | Pandemic | Estimated deaths |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 1918 | Novel H1 (avian-origin) | Spanish flu | 50–100 million |
| 1957 | H2 HA reassortment | Asian flu | ~1–4 million |
| 1968 | H3 HA reassortment | Hong Kong flu | ~1–4 million |
| 2009 | Novel H1N1 (swine-origin) | Swine flu pandemic | ~150,000–575,000 |

H5N1 avian influenza (60% case fatality in confirmed human cases) remains a pandemic preparedness priority; does not yet sustain human-to-human transmission due to inability to efficiently switch to α2,6-SA binding.

### Broadly neutralizing antibody epitopes and universal vaccines

| BNAb | Target site | Subtype coverage | Mechanism |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| CR6261 | HA2 stalk Site I | Group 1 (H1, H2, H5, H6, H8, H9) | Blocks fusion peptide insertion |
| MEDI8852 | HA2 stalk | Group 1 + 2 (cross-group) | Stabilizes prefusion stalk; ADCC |
| FI6v3 | HA1 base + HA2 stalk | Pan-influenza A (all 16 subtypes) | Dual contact; blocks 6HB formation |
| 3.1 | HA2 Site II | Group 2 (H3, H7) | Membrane-proximal; ADCC |

**Universal influenza vaccine strategies targeting HA:**
- **Headless HA (stalk-only vaccines)**: Remove immunodominant HA1 head → focus immunity on conserved HA2 stalk; multiple academic and industry programs (NIAID, Janssen)
- **HA nanoparticle mosaic** (Kanekiyo 2019): Ferritin nanoparticles displaying multiple HA head epitopes from different subtypes → broad cross-reactive immunity; Phase 1 trials
- **mRNA influenza vaccines (investigational)**: Moderna mRNA-1010 (seasonal, 4 strains); Moderna mRNA-1073 (pandemic preparedness, H5+H1); both encode full-length HA with 2P stabilization; Phase 2/3 trials underway — potential to update HA sequences rapidly for pandemic response
- **Universal H3 mosaic mRNA** (Pfizer Phase 2): mRNA encoding H3 head mosaics targeting 12 distinct H3 antigenic clusters
