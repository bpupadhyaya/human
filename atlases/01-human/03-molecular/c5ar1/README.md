# C5aR1

## Overview

**C5aR1 (complement component 5a receptor 1; CD88)** is a **G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR)** that mediates the pro-inflammatory actions of **C5a** — the potent anaphylatoxin generated by C5 cleavage during complement activation [^ricklin-2010-complement-overview]. C5aR1 is expressed at high density on **neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, mast cells, and activated eosinophils** in the periphery, and at lower density on epithelial and endothelial cells in various tissues.

C5a–C5aR1 signaling is one of the most powerful amplifiers of innate immune activation — it drives neutrophil chemotaxis, degranulation, reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, and **neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation**. While essential for host defense against encapsulated bacteria and fungi, dysregulated C5a/C5aR1 signaling drives tissue injury in ANCA-associated vasculitis, lupus nephritis, PNH, and inflammatory conditions broadly.

The clinical significance of C5aR1 reached a landmark with FDA approval of **avacopan (TAVNEOS)** in October 2021 — a selective oral C5aR1 antagonist that achieved steroid-free remission induction in ANCA vasculitis, demonstrating that C5aR1 blockade can replace traditional high-dose glucocorticoid therapy [^nusshag-2020-c5ar1-anca-review].

## Structure

### Protein and gene

| Feature | Detail |
|:--------|:-------|
| Gene | *C5AR1*, chromosome 19q13.3 |
| Protein | 350 amino acids; ~37 kDa (glycosylated: ~42–55 kDa) |
| Class | Rhodopsin-family (Class A) GPCR |
| Topology | 7 transmembrane α-helices (TM1-TM7), extracellular N-terminus, 3 ECLs, intracellular C-terminus |
| G-protein coupling | Primarily Gαi (pertussis toxin-sensitive) → cAMP↓; also Gα16, Gβγ |

### C5aR2 (C5L2) — decoy receptor

C5aR2 (*C5AR2*, chr19q13.3) is a related receptor that binds C5a and the desarginated form C5a-desArg with high affinity but does **not** couple to G proteins (no signal transduction in most contexts). C5aR2 acts as a **decoy receptor**: sequestering C5a → limiting C5aR1 activation. C5aR2 also modulates inflammatory responses via β-arrestin 2 signaling. Loss of C5aR2 paradoxically worsens inflammation in some models.

### C5a — the ligand

C5a is a **74-amino-acid peptide** cleaved from the α-chain of C5:
- Generated by C5 convertase (classical: C4b2a3b; alternative: C3bBb3b; lectin pathway: MASP-2/C4b2a3b)
- Kd for C5aR1: ~1 nM (high affinity)
- Rapidly inactivated by carboxypeptidase B → C5a-desArg (~5-fold reduced potency at C5aR1)
- **C5a concentration increases dramatically** at sites of complement activation (trauma, infection, autoimmune injury, ischemia-reperfusion)

## Function

### Neutrophil activation cascade

C5a–C5aR1 is the primary signal that **converts circulating neutrophils into effector cells** at sites of complement activation:

1. **Chemotaxis:** C5a gradient → C5aR1 → Gαi → PI3K → Akt → cytoskeletal reorganization → directional migration toward higher C5a concentrations (complement locus = injury/infection)

2. **Degranulation:**
   - **Primary (azurophilic) granules:** Release elastase, PR3, MPO, defensins, cathepsin G
   - **Secondary (specific) granules:** Release lactoferrin, collagenase, cytochrome b558 (NADPH oxidase component)
   - **Tertiary granules:** Gelatinase, alkaline phosphatase
   - Surface upregulation of PR3 and MPO on neutrophil plasma membrane — the critical step enabling ANCA-mediated activation

3. **Respiratory burst (ROS):** C5aR1 → Gβγ → PKC → NADPH oxidase (NOX2; p67phox/p47phox/p22phox/gp91phox) → superoxide (O₂•⁻) → H₂O₂, HOCl → oxidative killing of pathogens (and tissue)

4. **NET formation (NETosis):** C5a-primed neutrophils release chromatin + granule contents (MPO, elastase, citrullinated histones) → NETs trap pathogens but also activate endothelium and trigger thrombosis; ANCA + C5a co-stimulation dramatically amplifies NET release

5. **Cytokine production:** IL-8, IL-1β, TNF-α release from C5aR1-activated neutrophils → amplifies the inflammatory cascade

### Mast cell and eosinophil activation

C5a/C5aR1 on **mast cells** triggers histamine degranulation and prostaglandin D2 release → anaphylactoid reactions. C5a/C5aR1 on **eosinophils** promotes survival and degranulation — relevant to EGPA (eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis; formerly Churg-Strauss).

## Mechanism

### C5aR1 signal transduction

C5a binds the N-terminal extracellular domain and the extracellular loops of C5aR1:
- Conformational change → Gαi activation → **adenylyl cyclase inhibition** (cAMP↓) → PKA↓
- Gβγ subunit release → **PI3Kγ** → PIP3 → Akt → cell survival and migration
- Gαq-like signaling (via Gα16 in some cells) → **PLCβ** → PIP2 → IP3 + DAG → Ca²⁺ release + PKC → ERK1/2 and p38 MAPK
- **β-arrestin recruitment** → receptor internalization + MAPK scaffold

### ANCA vasculitis pathomechanism involving C5aR1

The "two-hit" model for ANCA vasculitis activation:
1. **Priming (Hit 1 — C5a/C5aR1):** Subclinical complement activation (from infection, DAMPs, NET clearance failure) → C5a → C5aR1 on neutrophils → surface upregulation of PR3 and MPO, increased adhesion, reduced apoptosis threshold, enhanced superoxide production capacity
2. **Activation (Hit 2 — ANCA):** Anti-PR3 or anti-MPO IgG (ANCA) binds surface-translocated PR3/MPO → Fc receptor crosslinking (FcγRIIa) → NETosis, degranulation, ROS burst → endothelial injury → pauci-immune necrotizing vasculitis

**Why avacopan works:** Blocking C5aR1 prevents neutrophil priming → without priming, ANCA can still bind surface PR3/MPO but triggers much weaker effector responses → fewer NETs → less vascular injury.

### Avacopan — mechanism and clinical evidence

**Avacopan (TAVNEOS; Amgen):**
- Oral small-molecule C5aR1 antagonist (not anti-C5a antibody) → penetrates blood-brain barrier
- Does NOT inhibit C5b-9 (MAC) formation → pathogen defense at C5b-9 level preserved
- Mechanism: allosteric C5aR1 blockade → prevents C5a-induced neutrophil priming
- **ADVOCATE trial** (N=331, randomized, double-blind vs. prednisone taper + cyclophosphamide or rituximab) [^nusshag-2020-c5ar1-anca-review]:
  - **Remission at week 26:** 72.3% (avacopan) vs. 70.1% (prednisone) — non-inferior
  - **Sustained remission at week 52:** **65.7% vs. 54.9%** — avacopan SUPERIOR
  - eGFR recovery: better with avacopan; less cumulative glucocorticoid exposure
  - FDA approved **October 2021** for GPA and MPA; first steroid-sparing approach
