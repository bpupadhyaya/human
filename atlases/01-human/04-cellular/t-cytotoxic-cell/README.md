# Cytotoxic T Cell

## Overview

Cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs, CD8+ T cells) are the primary cellular effectors of adaptive immunity against intracellular pathogens and neoplastic cells. They constitute 20–35% of circulating CD3+ T lymphocytes in peripheral blood and are defined by surface expression of the CD8αβ co-receptor, which binds the α3 domain of MHC class I molecules.[^janeway-immunobiology] Because MHC class I is expressed on virtually all nucleated cells (~10¹³ in the human body), CTLs can in principle survey the entire organismal proteome for signs of viral infection, oncogenic transformation, or intracellular bacterial invasion.

CTLs are generated in the thymus by positive and negative selection of antigen-receptor diversity generated by somatic V(D)J recombination, producing a repertoire estimated at >10⁷ distinct specificities. Upon antigen encounter in peripheral lymphoid organs, naïve CD8+ T cells undergo clonal expansion (one cell → >10⁴–10⁶ effector progeny), mediate cytolytic effector function, and then contract — leaving 5–10% long-lived memory cells capable of rapid reactivation on secondary exposure.[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

## Structure

**Surface markers.** Mature CTLs express: CD3 (TCR signalling complex: CD3ε/δ/γ dimers + CD3ζζ homodimer, the ITAM-bearing signalling chains); CD8αβ heterodimer (co-receptor, binds MHC-I α3); TCRαβ (clonotypic, generated by V(D)J recombination); CD45 (tyrosine phosphatase; isoform CD45RA on naïve/effector-memory, CD45RO on effector/memory); CD28 (co-stimulatory receptor, binds CD80/CD86); CCR7 (lymph node homing in naïve/central-memory); CXCR3 (IFN-γ-induced, Th1 tissue homing).[^janeway-immunobiology]

**Activation/exhaustion markers.** Upon activation: CD69 (early, surface; tissue retention), CD44hi (memory marker), CD25 (IL-2Rα, early), CD122 (IL-2Rβ, memory). Inhibitory receptors (checkpoint molecules): PD-1 (CD279; upregulated on activation; ligands PD-L1/PD-L2 on tumour cells and APCs → ITIM-mediated SHP-1/2 phosphatase → dampened TCR/CD28 signalling); TIM-3 (galectin-9 ligand; co-expressed with PD-1 in exhaustion); LAG-3 (MHC-II ligand; synergistic exhaustion with PD-1); CTLA-4 (CD152; competes with CD28 for CD80/86).[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**Lytic granules.** CTLs store their killing machinery in specialised secretory lysosomes called lytic granules: perforin (PRF1, pore-forming protein, Ca²⁺-dependent); granzymes A, B, H, K, M (serine proteases); granulysin (antimicrobial peptide); serglycin (chondroitin-sulphate proteoglycan core for granule assembly); LAMP-1/LAMP-2 (CD107a/b; granule membrane markers expressed on CTL surface upon degranulation — activation marker).[^janeway-immunobiology]

## Function

**Three-signal activation paradigm.** Naïve CD8+ T cell activation requires:
1. **Signal 1 (antigen)**: TCR:CD3 recognition of cognate peptide:MHC-I on an APC, stabilised by CD8:MHC-I α3 engagement → ZAP-70 (Lck-phosphorylated ITAM → ZAP-70 recruitment) → LAT/SLP-76 → PLCγ1 → IP₃ (NFAT activation via calcineurin/NFAT pathway) + DAG (PKCθ → NF-κB + AP-1 activation).
2. **Signal 2 (co-stimulation)**: CD28 (on T cell) binds CD80 or CD86 (B7-1/B7-2) on professional APCs → PI3K/Akt → mTOR (survival, proliferation) + IL-2 production amplification.
3. **Signal 3 (inflammatory cytokines)**: IL-12 (from activated DCs/macrophages) → STAT4 → T-bet → IFN-γ production and effector CTL differentiation; type I IFNs (IFN-α/β) → STAT1/STAT2 → IRF7 → enhanced cytotoxic gene expression; IL-2 (from CD4+ Th cells and the CTL itself) → STAT5 → Bcl-2, proliferation, memory formation.[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**Cross-presentation.** For CTL priming against extracellular antigens (tumour-shed proteins, viruses that do not infect DCs), professional APCs — especially cDC1s (CD8α+ in mice; CLEC9A+/XCR1+ in humans) — internalise exogenous antigen and shuttle it into the cytosolic MHC-I antigen presentation pathway (cross-presentation). Mechanisms: phagosomal escape → proteasomal processing → TAP transport → ER peptide loading onto MHC-I. cDC1 cross-presentation is critically dependent on Batf3 transcription factor and is required for anti-tumour CTL responses.[^janeway-immunobiology]

**Immunological synapse formation.** CTL contacts a target cell; TCR scanning leads to recognition → MTOC (microtubule-organising centre) polarises toward the contact zone (supramolecular activation cluster, SMAC): cSMAC (TCR, CD8, LFA-1, PKCθ, CD28) and pSMAC (talin, integrin LFA-1 ring) and dSMAC (F-actin); lytic granules travel along microtubules to the cSMAC → directed degranulation into the synaptic cleft (focused, sparing bystander cells).[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**Cytotoxic killing mechanisms.**

*Perforin/granzyme pathway* (dominant): Perforin (Ca²⁺-dependent) inserts into target cell membrane and forms pores (MAC-like, membrane attack complex-like). Granzyme B (serine protease) enters via pore or endocytosis → directly cleaves caspase-3 (→ executioner apoptosis) and cleaves BID (→ truncated tBID → mitochondrial Bax/Bak pores → cytochrome c → apoptosome → caspase-9 → caspase-3/7) → intrinsic apoptosis. Granzyme A: cleaves SET complex (SET, Ape1, pp32, HMG-2) → NM23-H1 DNase → DNA nicks, caspase-independent cell death. Granzyme K: cleaves BID and SET complex.[^janeway-immunobiology]

*Fas/FasL pathway*: CTLs upregulate FasL (CD95L, TNFSF6) upon activation; target cells expressing Fas (CD95, TNFRSF6) are killed via DISC (death-inducing signalling complex: FADD + pro-caspase-8) → caspase-8 activation → caspase-3 → apoptosis; also important for activation-induced cell death (AICD) and for killing Fas-expressing non-professional APCs.[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

*TRAIL pathway*: CTLs express TRAIL (TNFSF10); tumour cells frequently upregulate DR4/DR5 death receptors → TRAIL-mediated apoptosis (important in tumour immunosurveillance).

*Cytokines*: IFN-γ (upregulates MHC-I on target cells improving CTL recognition, activates macrophages, antiviral); TNF-α (synergistic killing with perforin); LT-α (lymphotoxin).[^janeway-immunobiology]

## Lifecycle

**Thymic development.** Bone marrow-derived HSCs seed the thymus as early T-lineage progenitors (ETPs, CD44+CD25−DN1). Progression through DN1→DN2→DN3 stages involves commitment to T-lineage (Notch1/DL4 signalling, TCF7/GATA3 TFs) and TCRβ V(D)J recombination. Productive TCRβ rearrangement → pre-TCR signalling → β-selection → proliferation → DN4 → DP (CD4+CD8+) cells; TCRα V(J) rearrangement → complete αβ TCR.

DP thymocytes undergo positive selection: cortical thymic epithelial cells (cTECs, expressing unique proteasome β5t subunit producing MHC-I-binding peptides) test TCR:MHC-I/II avidity → intermediate affinity → survival and CD8 SP (or CD4 SP) fate; too-low affinity → death by neglect; too-high affinity → negative selection in medulla (mTECs via AIRE-expressed tissue-restricted antigens + dendritic cells eliminate autoreactive cells by clonal deletion or Foxp3+ Treg conversion). CD8 SP cells emigrate to peripheral blood and secondary lymphoid organs via S1PR1:S1P gradient.[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**Peripheral naïve phase.** Naïve CD8+ T cells recirculate through blood and lymph nodes (CCR7/CXCR4-driven entry via HEVs), scanning DC surfaces. They require tonic TCR:MHC-I signals and IL-7 (via CD127) for homeostatic survival and very slow proliferation (~10 cycles/year).[^janeway-immunobiology]

**Effector differentiation.** Upon TCR stimulation: transcription factor cascade: T-bet (TBXI21, Th1 master TF) → perforin, granzyme B, IFN-γ gene expression; ID2; Blimp-1 (PRDM1) → terminal effector differentiation (↓IL-7R, ↓CCR7, ↑KLRG1); Eomes → memory precursor effector cells (MPECs, ↑CD122/IL-2Rβ → IL-15 responsiveness for long-term survival).[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**Memory formation and subsets.** Following antigen clearance, ~90–95% of effector CTLs die (contraction phase); 5–10% persist as memory:
- **T_CM (central-memory)**: CCR7+CD62Lhi, lymph node-resident; superior self-renewal (IL-15-driven); low immediate effector function but rapid re-expansion potential.
- **T_EM (effector-memory)**: CCR7−, CX3CR1+; patrol blood and non-lymphoid tissues; higher immediate effector function.
- **T_RM (tissue-resident memory)**: CD69+CD103+; permanently reside in barrier tissues (lung, gut, skin, brain, female genital tract); first line of defence on re-challenge; IL-15 and TGF-β maintain them; critical in mucosal anti-viral immunity.[^janeway-immunobiology]

**Exhaustion.** Chronic antigen stimulation (HIV, HBV/HCV, LCMV-clone 13, solid tumours) → progressive epigenetic and transcriptional reprogramming of CTLs: TOX (transcription factor) drives exhaustion; PD-1hi, TIM-3hi, LAG-3hi, CTLA-4hi surface expression; reduced proliferative capacity, cytokine production (IFN-γ, TNF-α), and cytotoxicity. PD-1:PD-L1/2 blockade (nivolumab, pembrolizumab, atezolizumab) partially reinvigorates exhausted CTLs — major basis of checkpoint immunotherapy.[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

## Pathology

**HIV/AIDS.** HIV-1 infects and depletes CD4+ T helper cells; without CD4 help (signal 3 deficiency), CD8+ CTL priming and maintenance are impaired. HIV-specific CTLs are initially generated but become exhausted; immune evasion via MHC-I downregulation (Nef protein). CD8+ T cell counts and functional capacity are key correlates of protection in HIV controller phenotypes.[^janeway-immunobiology]

**Chronic viral hepatitis.** HBV and HCV drive CTL exhaustion; virus-specific CTLs are PD-1hi/TIM-3hi and fail to clear hepatic infection. PD-1 blockade partially restores HCV-specific CTL function; combination with NS5B inhibitors may enable immune-mediated HBV cure.[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**Tumour immunology and CAR-T therapy.** Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are often PD-1hi and functionally exhausted. Checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4) and adoptive CTL transfer (TIL therapy, TCR-engineered T cells) harness CTL cytotoxicity. CAR-T cells (chimeric antigen receptor, e.g., anti-CD19 for B-ALL, anti-BCMA for myeloma) redirect CTLs to surface tumour antigens in an MHC-independent manner, achieving deep remissions.[^janeway-immunobiology]

**Autoimmune CTL pathology.** CD8+ T cells infiltrate and destroy islet β-cells in type 1 diabetes (HLA-A2-restricted insulin B chain peptides); contribute to CD8+ T cell synovitis in rheumatoid arthritis; mediate allograft rejection (allorecognition of donor MHC-I peptides).[^alberts-mol-cell-biology]

**CD8+ T cell lymphomas.** Rare; include extranodal NK/T cell lymphoma (nasal type, EBV+), subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma (SPTCL, αβ CTL phenotype), aggressive NK-cell leukaemia; characterised by CD3+CD8+TIA-1+ cytotoxic immunophenotype.[^janeway-immunobiology]
