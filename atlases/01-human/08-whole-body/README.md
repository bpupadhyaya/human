# Atlas 01-Human · Scale 08: Whole Body

This is the highest scale of the Human Atlas — the organism level at which disease presents, treatment is experienced, and medicine is evaluated. At this scale, the model captures **emergent properties** that arise from the integration of all seven scales below: physiology that cannot be reduced to any single organ, system, or molecule, but requires their coordinated interaction.

## What the whole-body scale captures

| Domain | Examples |
|:---|:---|
| **Cardiovascular coupling** | Cardiac reserve, Frank-Starling, exercise physiology, Fick principle (VO₂ = CO × a-vO₂ difference) |
| **Autonomic regulation** | Heart rate variability (HRV) as a window into whole-body homeostasis; baroreflex; chemoreceptor reflexes |
| **Neurohumoral integration** | Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) as a whole-body pressure/volume regulator; vasopressin; natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP) |
| **Metabolic demands** | VO₂max as an integrative measure of cardiac, pulmonary, and muscular capacity; substrate switching (rest: fat; exercise: glycolysis) |
| **Whole-body homeostasis** | pH, temperature, osmolality, blood volume — all maintained by multi-organ coordination |
| **Disease at the organism level** | Hypertension, shock, heart failure syndrome, exercise intolerance — emergent phenotypes of multi-scale dysfunction |

## Why this scale matters

Every molecular, cellular, tissue, and organ entry in this atlas ultimately constrains what happens at the whole-body level. Conversely, whole-body phenotypes (BP, VO₂max, HRV, BNP level) are the observables that medicine actually measures and that patients actually experience. A multi-scale model that cannot predict whole-body behavior is incomplete; an entry at this scale makes the integration explicit.
