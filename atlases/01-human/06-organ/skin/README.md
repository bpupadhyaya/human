# Skin

## Overview

The skin is the **largest organ of the human body**, covering approximately 1.5–2.0 m² of body surface area and weighing 4–5 kg (including subcutaneous tissue). It forms the continuous outer boundary between the human body and its environment — providing **mechanical, chemical, microbial, and UV radiation barrier** functions while also serving as a sensory, thermoregulatory, metabolic, and immune organ [^kolarsick-2011-skin-anatomy].

The skin is organized into three primary layers:
1. **Epidermis** — stratified squamous epithelium; the outer barrier
2. **Dermis** — dense connective tissue containing vasculature, nerves, and appendages
3. **Hypodermis (subcutaneous fat)** — adipose tissue providing thermal insulation and mechanical cushioning

The epidermis is entirely renewing tissue: keratinocytes produced in the **stratum basale** migrate outward over approximately 28 days, progressively differentiating and ultimately shedding as dead cornified squames from the surface. This continuous renewal — ~30,000–40,000 cells shed per hour — creates a self-refreshing barrier.

## Structure

### Epidermis

The epidermis is a stratified squamous epithelium composed of five layers (from deep to superficial) [^proksch-2008-skin-barrier]:

| Layer | Cells/Characteristics | Key events |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Stratum basale** | Keratinocyte stem cells; single layer; anchored to basement membrane by hemidesmosomes (integrin α6β4); mitotically active | Continuous cell production; location of melanocytes (DOPA+ cells) between basal keratinocytes |
| **Stratum spinosum** | Polygonal keratinocytes; connected by desmosomes (keratin tonofilaments); "prickle cells" | Lamellar granule (Odland body) formation begins; synthesis of filaggrin precursors and involucrin |
| **Stratum granulosum** | Flattened keratinocytes; keratohyalin granules (profilaggrin/loricrin); onset of programmed cell death | Lamellar granule exocytosis → lipid bilayers between cells (skin barrier lipids); cornified envelope assembly begins |
| **Stratum lucidum** | Present only on palmoplantar skin (palms/soles); transitional layer; anucleate | Eleidin replaces keratohyalin; additional barrier reinforcement |
| **Stratum corneum** | 10–20 layers of flat, anucleate, keratin-filled cornified squames; surrounded by lamellar lipid bilayers (ceramides, free fatty acids, cholesterol) | Barrier function; desquamation (shedding of outermost squames via serine proteases KLK5/KLK7) |

**Skin barrier lipids**: The intercellular lamellar bilayer of the stratum corneum (analogous to mortar between bricks) consists of approximately 50% ceramides, 25% cholesterol, and 15% free fatty acids — a unique non-bilayer lipid composition that creates a nearly impermeable barrier to water loss and pathogen entry [^proksch-2008-skin-barrier].

### Specialized Epidermal Cells

| Cell type | Location | Function |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Keratinocytes** | All layers | Structural; barrier; antimicrobial peptide secretion (defensins, cathelicidin LL-37) |
| **Melanocytes** | Stratum basale (1:10 keratinocytes) | Synthesize melanin in melanosomes → transfer via cytoplasmic dendrites to surrounding keratinocytes → UV protection (perinuclear melanin caps shield DNA) |
| **Langerhans cells** | Stratum spinosum | Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells; tissue-resident antigen-presenting cells; express CD1a, langerin (CD207); MHC-II presentation of epidermal antigens to T cells in lymph nodes |
| **Merkel cells** | Stratum basale (fingertips, lips, hair follicle isthmus) | Slowly-adapting mechanoreceptors for light touch/texture; synapse with sensory nerve endings; high density in fingertips underlies tactile acuity |

### Dermis

The dermis is a dense irregular connective tissue compartment (~1–4 mm thick) divided into:

- **Papillary dermis** (superficial): Loose connective tissue; capillary loops project into dermal papillae; Type III collagen predominates; rich in sensory receptors (Meissner corpuscles for fine touch).
- **Reticular dermis** (deep): Dense Type I collagen bundles in a woven pattern; elastin fibers; fibroblasts; Type IV collagen basement membrane zone at the dermoepidermal junction.

**Dermal appendages** (derived from epidermis, extend into dermis):
- **Hair follicles**: Cyclically active (anagen/catagen/telogen); bulge region contains epithelial stem cells; dermal papilla (specialized fibroblasts) controls follicle cycling; arrector pili smooth muscle causes piloerection.
- **Sebaceous glands**: Holocrine secretion of sebum (triglycerides, wax esters, squalene); anti-microbial; lubricate hair/skin; androgenic stimulation → acne (C. acnes) in sebum-rich follicles.
- **Eccrine sweat glands**: Merocrine secretion; primary thermoregulatory organ (up to 10 L sweat/day in extreme heat); distributed across entire body surface (~3–4 million glands).
- **Apocrine glands**: Axillary/inguinal; become active at puberty; odorless secretion metabolized by bacteria → body odor; vestigial function.
- **Nails**: Highly cornified keratin plates; protect distal digits; enable precision grip.

**Sensory receptors in dermis/hypodermis:**
- Meissner corpuscles (papillary dermis): fine touch, two-point discrimination
- Pacinian corpuscles (deep dermis/hypodermis): vibration, deep pressure
- Ruffini endings: sustained pressure, skin stretch
- Free nerve endings: pain, temperature (throughout dermis)

### Hypodermis

The hypodermis (subcutis) is composed of adipocytes organized in lobules separated by fibrous septa. Functions: thermal insulation, mechanical cushioning, energy storage, hormone conversion (androstenedione → estrone via aromatase in adipocytes).

## Function

### Barrier Function

The skin's barrier serves multiple defense modalities:

1. **Physical barrier**: Stratum corneum cornified squames + lamellar lipid bilayers prevent transcutaneous water loss (normal TEWL ~5–10 g/m²/h) and resist mechanical abrasion.
2. **Chemical barrier**: Acidic surface (pH 4.5–5.5 — the "acid mantle") from secreted fatty acids and lactic acid inhibits alkaline-preferring pathogens; sebum fatty acids are antimicrobial.
3. **Antimicrobial barrier**: Keratinocytes secrete defensins (β-defensin 1/2/3), cathelicidin (LL-37), and S100 proteins — broad-spectrum antimicrobial peptides that disrupt bacterial membranes.
4. **UV protection**: Melanin absorbs UV-B (280–315 nm) → prevents DNA damage (thymine dimer formation → melanoma risk); darker skin has higher eumelanin content in larger melanosomes.
5. **Immunological barrier**: Langerhans cells and dermal dendritic cells provide continuous surveillance; pattern recognition by TLRs in keratinocytes initiates innate immune responses.

### Thermoregulation

The skin is the primary thermoregulatory organ:
- **Heat dissipation**: Eccrine sweating (evaporative cooling); cutaneous vasodilation (increased blood flow to skin → radiation/conduction); the skin can redirect up to 8 L/min of cardiac output for cooling.
- **Heat conservation**: Cutaneous vasoconstriction (arteriole constriction in dermis reduces heat radiation); piloerection (vestigial; traps air layer in furry animals).
- **Core temperature maintenance**: Hypothalamic thermostat → sympathetic nervous system → eccrine sweat gland activation and cutaneous vascular tone.

### Vitamin D Synthesis

The skin is the primary site of vitamin D₃ (cholecalciferol) synthesis: UV-B radiation converts **7-dehydrocholesterol** (in stratum spinosum/basale) → **previtamin D₃** → thermal isomerization → **vitamin D₃**. Hepatic 25-hydroxylation → renal 1-α-hydroxylation → active 1,25(OH)₂D₃ (calcitriol). Dark skin requires ~3–6× longer UV-B exposure than light skin to produce equivalent vitamin D₃.

### Sensory Function

The skin is the body's sensory organ for touch, pressure, vibration, pain, and temperature — integrating input from 4 mechanoreceptor types, free nerve endings (nociceptors/thermoreceptors), and specialized structures to provide the continuous environmental map essential for motor control and social interaction.

## Pathology

### Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

**~15–20%** of children and **~1–10%** of adults globally. Driven by filaggrin (FLG) loss-of-function mutations → defective stratum corneum barrier → increased transepidermal water loss → allergen penetration → Th2-skewed immune response (IL-4, IL-13, IL-31) → itch-scratch cycle → secondary Staphylococcus aureus colonization. Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα) has transformed moderate-severe management.

### Psoriasis

**~2–3%** of global adults. Immune-mediated (Th17/IL-17 axis); accelerated keratinocyte proliferation (turnover 3–5 days vs. normal 28) → thickened plaques with silvery scale; Auspitz sign (pinpoint bleeding on scale removal). Nail pitting, psoriatic arthritis in ~30%. TNF-α, IL-17A, IL-23/p19 inhibitors highly effective.

### Melanoma

Rising incidence globally; UV exposure (cumulative and episodic sunburn) drives BRAF V600E mutations in melanocytes → malignant transformation. BRAF V600E present in ~50% of cutaneous melanomas; BRAF + MEK inhibitors (vemurafenib + cobimetinib) dramatically improve survival. Anti-PD-1 immunotherapy (pembrolizumab, nivolumab) achieves durable responses in advanced disease.

### Wound Healing

Four overlapping phases:
1. **Hemostasis** (0–24 h): Platelet aggregation, fibrin clot formation; PDGF/TGF-β release from alpha-granules.
2. **Inflammation** (1–4 days): Neutrophil → macrophage influx; debridement; M1 → M2 macrophage polarization switch signals transition to repair.
3. **Proliferation** (4–21 days): Keratinocyte migration (re-epithelialization), fibroblast proliferation/collagen synthesis, angiogenesis (VEGF), myofibroblast contraction.
4. **Remodeling** (21 days–2 years): Collagen III → collagen I; tensile strength recovers to ~80% of unwounded skin; scar maturation.

Chronic wounds (diabetic foot, venous ulcers, pressure injuries) represent failure of normal healing transitions.

### Skin Infections

| Type | Pathogen | Disease |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Bacterial** | *S. aureus*, *S. pyogenes* | Impetigo, cellulitis, necrotizing fasciitis |
| **Viral** | HSV-1/2, VZV, HPV, molluscum | Cold sores, shingles, warts, molluscum contagiosum |
| **Fungal** | *Trichophyton*, *Candida*, *Malassezia* | Tinea (ringworm), candidal intertrigo, seborrheic dermatitis |
| **Parasitic** | *Sarcoptes scabiei*, *Pthirus pubis* | Scabies, crabs |
