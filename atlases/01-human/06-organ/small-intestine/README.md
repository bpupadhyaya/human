# Small Intestine

## Overview

The small intestine is a 6–7 m convoluted tube that constitutes the principal site of nutrient digestion and absorption in the human body [^guyton-hall]. Spanning from the pyloric sphincter to the ileocaecal valve, it is divided into three anatomically and functionally distinct segments: the duodenum (25–30 cm), jejunum (~2.5 m), and ileum (~3.5 m). Despite its modest luminal diameter (2.5–3 cm), the small intestine achieves a total absorptive surface area of approximately 250 m² — roughly the size of a singles tennis court — through three hierarchical surface amplifications: the circular folds of Kerckring (plica circulares), villi, and microvilli (brush border).

The small intestine is not merely an absorptive tube. Its enteroendocrine system constitutes the largest endocrine organ in the body by cell number, secreting more than 20 peptide hormones — including CCK, secretin, GIP, GLP-1, GLP-2, and PYY — that coordinate digestion, appetite, and metabolic homeostasis [^guyton-hall]. Its immune apparatus (Peyer's patches, intraepithelial lymphocytes, lamina propria lymphocytes) provides the intestinal mucosal firewall against luminal pathogens while maintaining tolerance to dietary antigens.

## Structure

### Gross Anatomy

**Duodenum** (25–30 cm, C-shaped, fixed retroperitoneal): Four parts. The superior (first) part — the duodenal cap — is most vulnerable to peptic ulceration. The second (descending) part receives bile and pancreatic juice via the major duodenal papilla (ampulla of Vater, controlled by the sphincter of Oddi) and the minor papilla (accessory pancreatic duct). The fourth part passes the ligament of Treitz — the landmark separating upper from lower GI bleeding. Brunner's glands in the submucosa secrete alkaline mucus (pH 8.1–9.3) to neutralise acid chyme.

**Jejunum** (~2.5 m, proximal mobile small bowel): Wider lumen, thicker wall, more prominent plica circulares and villi. Majority of carbohydrate, protein, and fat absorbed here. Fewer goblet cells, more villi per cm² than ileum.

**Ileum** (~3.5 m, distal mobile small bowel): Narrower lumen, less prominent folds. Specialised functions: vitamin B12-intrinsic factor complex absorption (cubilin/amnionless receptor at apical membrane of enterocytes → transcytosis → transcobalamin II transport in portal blood); bile acid reabsorption (ASBT/SLC10A2 apical transporter → IBABP intracellular carrier → OSTα/β basolateral export → portal circulation → hepatic NTCP reuptake), ~95% recovery per cycle; 20–30 Peyer's patches (organised secondary lymphoid follicles) in terminal ileum.

### Wall Structure

| Layer | Key features |
|:---|:---|
| Mucosa | Epithelium (enterocytes, goblet, Paneth, enteroendocrine, tuft, M-cells) + lamina propria (capillaries, lacteal, immune cells) + muscularis mucosae |
| Submucosa | Meissner's plexus; Brunner's glands (duodenum) |
| Muscularis externa | Inner circular + outer longitudinal; Auerbach's (myenteric) plexus; ICCs of Cajal as pacemakers |
| Serosa | Visceral peritoneum (absent over retroperitoneal duodenum) |

### Epithelial Cell Types

The intestinal epithelium turns over completely every 3–5 days from LGR5+ intestinal stem cells (ISCs) at crypt bases [^guyton-hall]:

| Cell type | Proportion | Location | Function |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Enterocytes | ~80% | Villus surface | Absorption; brush border enzymes (lactase, sucrase-isomaltase, peptidases); SGLT1, GLUT5, PepT1 |
| Goblet cells | ~15% | Villi + crypts | MUC2 mucin secretion → gel-forming mucus layer |
| Paneth cells | Rare | Crypt base only | α-defensins (cryptdins), lysozyme, REG3γ — antimicrobial; support ISC niche |
| Enteroendocrine cells | ~1% | Villi + crypts | Hormone secretion (CCK, secretin, GIP, GLP-1/2, PYY, serotonin) |
| Tuft cells | ~0.4% | Villi | Chemosensory; IL-25 → ILC2 → Th2 immunity against helminths/protozoa |
| M-cells | Rare | Follicle-associated epithelium | Antigen sampling for Peyer's patches; entry site for some pathogens |

**Villus architecture:** Each villus is a finger-like projection (0.5–1.5 mm) containing a central lacteal (blind-ended lymphatic capillary, absorbs dietary fat as chylomicrons) surrounded by fenestrated capillaries (absorbs water-soluble nutrients → portal vein) and a core of lamina propria immune cells and smooth muscle.

## Function

### Carbohydrate Digestion and Absorption

Salivary and pancreatic amylase cleave starch → dextrins and maltose → brush border enzymes complete hydrolysis [^stryer-biochemistry]:
- **Lactase** (LCT gene): lactose → galactose + glucose; primary lactase deficiency (autosomal recessive) affects ~70% of world adults (non-Caucasian populations)
- **Sucrase-isomaltase** (SI gene): sucrose → glucose + fructose; maltose/isomaltose → glucose
- **Glucoamylase:** maltooligosaccharides → glucose

Absorption transporters:
- **SGLT1** (SLC5A1): Na⁺-coupled glucose/galactose uptake (apical, high affinity, against concentration gradient, driven by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase basolateral gradient)
- **GLUT5** (SLC2A5): facilitated fructose uptake (apical, concentration gradient)
- **GLUT2** (SLC2A2): basolateral export of all three monosaccharides → portal blood

### Protein Digestion and Absorption

Gastric pepsin initiates protein cleavage → pancreatic endopeptidases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase) and exopeptidases (carboxypeptidase A/B) → oligopeptides and amino acids [^stryer-biochemistry]. Final hydrolysis by brush border peptidases. Absorption:
- **PepT1** (SLC15A1): di/tripeptide uptake (H⁺-coupled, high capacity, clinically exploited by prodrug design — valacyclovir, enalapril)
- **Amino acid transporters:** multiple SLC families (rBAT/b⁰,+AT for cationic, ASCT2 for neutral, SLC6A19/B0AT1 for neutral — mutated in Hartnup disease)

### Fat Digestion and Absorption

Dietary triglycerides → emulsified by bile salts (micelle formation) → pancreatic lipase + colipase → fatty acids + 2-monoacylglycerol [^stryer-biochemistry]. Absorption:
- Passive diffusion of fatty acids across enterocyte apical membrane (and via CD36/FAT for long-chain)
- Re-esterification in ER → triglycerides → assembled into **chylomicrons** (TG core + ApoB-48, ApoA-I/IV, ApoE, ApoC-II; MTP [microsomal triglyceride transfer protein] is essential; abetalipoproteinaemia = MTP mutation → fat malabsorption)
- Chylomicrons exocytosed basally → enter lacteal → thoracic duct → left subclavian vein (bypass portal circulation entirely)

Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are absorbed with fat; impaired in cholestasis, pancreatic insufficiency, or small bowel resection.

### Vitamin and Mineral Absorption — Specialised Sites

**Duodenum:**
- **Iron:** Dietary non-haem Fe³⁺ reduced by DCYTB → Fe²⁺ → apical DMT1 (SLC11A2) uptake; haem-Fe via HCP1; basolateral export via ferroportin (SLC40A1) + hephaestin oxidation to Fe³⁺ → transferrin binding; regulated by hepcidin (hepatic)
- **Calcium:** TRPV6 (apical, calcitriol/1,25-(OH)₂D₃-induced) → calbindin-D9k (intracellular buffer) → PMCA1b/NCX1 (basolateral export); also vitamin D-independent paracellular absorption
- **Folate:** PCFT/SLC46A1 (proton-coupled folate transporter); methylenetetrahydrofolate from diet → reduced folate carrier into cells
- **Zinc, Magnesium:** ZIP4 (Zn²⁺), TRPM6/7 (Mg²⁺)

**Ileum:**
- **Vitamin B12:** cubilin/amnionless (CUBAM) receptor mediates IF-B12 complex endocytosis → transcytosis → portal blood (transcobalamin II)
- **Bile acids:** ASBT/SLC10A2 (apical); IBABP (intracellular); OSTα/β (basolateral); drives enterohepatic circulation

### Enteroendocrine Function

The small intestinal enteroendocrine system secretes hormones that regulate digestion, satiety, and systemic metabolism [^guyton-hall]:

| Hormone | Cell | Site | Stimulus | Target/Action |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| CCK | I-cell | Duodenum/jejunum | Fat, protein | Gallbladder contraction + pancreatic enzyme/HCO₃⁻ secretion; satiety |
| Secretin | S-cell | Duodenum | Luminal acid (pH <4) | Pancreatic HCO₃⁻, ↓gastric acid |
| GIP | K-cell | Duodenum/jejunum | Glucose, fat | Incretin — insulin release in response to glucose (glucose-dependent); also ↓gastric motility |
| GLP-1 | L-cell | Ileum/colon | Glucose, fat, SCFAs | Potent incretin; ↓glucagon; ↑satiety; slow gastric emptying; target of GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide) |
| GLP-2 | L-cell | Ileum/colon | Nutrients | Intestinal trophism, ↑blood flow, ↑epithelial proliferation |
| PYY | L-cell | Ileum/colon | Fat, protein | Ileal brake — ↓appetite, ↓gastric motility |
| Serotonin (5-HT) | EC-cell | Throughout | Mechanical, chemical | Peristaltic reflex initiation via 5-HT3/5-HT4 on ENS neurons |

### Motility

Two patterns coordinate digestion [^guyton-hall]:

1. **Segmental contractions (fed state):** Rhythmic, non-propagating contractions of circular muscle → mixing of chyme with digestive secretions, repeated contact with absorptive surface
2. **Peristalsis:** Aboral propagating contractions (ascending excitation → descending inhibition, via myenteric plexus) → net movement of chyme aborally

**Migratory Motor Complex (MMC) — fasted state:**
Cyclic (~90 min) activity complex:
- Phase I (quiescence, ~45 min)
- Phase II (irregular contractions, ~30 min)
- Phase III ("housekeeper wave," 5–10 min): intense, high-amplitude contractions sweep residue, bacteria, and sloughed cells from stomach → ileum; driven by motilin (duodenum, every 90 min during fasting) and 5-HT. Loss of MMC (e.g., opioids, scleroderma) → SIBO.

## Connections

- **Part of:** [Digestive System](../../07-system/digestive-system/README.md) — primary site of nutrient digestion and absorption; ~250 m² surface absorbs sugars, amino acids, fats, vitamins, and minerals; enteroendocrine cells secrete CCK, GLP-1, GIP, and secretin.
- **Contains:** [Intestinal Epithelium](../../05-tissue/intestinal-epithelium/README.md) — villi and crypts of Lieberkühn line the lumen; LGR5+ stem cells at crypt bases renew the epithelium every 3–5 days; Paneth cells defend the stem cell niche.
- **Modulates:** [Liver](../liver/README.md) — delivers absorbed nutrients via portal circulation (first-pass metabolism); duodenal iron absorption and ileal bile acid reabsorption (~95% recovery per cycle) directly regulate hepatic iron stores and bile acid pool.
- **Modulates:** [Pancreas](../pancreas/README.md) — duodenal I-cells secrete CCK → pancreatic enzyme secretion; S-cells secrete secretin → pancreatic HCO₃⁻; GIP from K-cells acts as an incretin on pancreatic β-cells, potentiating glucose-stimulated insulin release.

## Pathology

### Coeliac Disease (Gluten-Sensitive Enteropathy)

Immune-mediated enteropathy in genetically susceptible individuals (HLA-DQ2 [~90%] or HLA-DQ8 [~5%]) triggered by gluten (gliadin from wheat, rye, barley) [^guyton-hall]. Tissue transglutaminase (tTG) deamidates gliadin → enhanced binding to HLA-DQ2/DQ8 → CD4+ T cell activation → Th1 response → mucosal inflammation.

**Histology:** Villous atrophy (Marsh III: flat mucosa), crypt hyperplasia, increased intraepithelial lymphocytes (IELs). Results in malabsorption of fat (steatorrhoea), iron (anaemia), calcium (osteoporosis), B vitamins, and fat-soluble vitamins. Serology: anti-tTG IgA (>90% sensitivity/specificity); anti-endomysial IgA; anti-deamidated gliadin peptide (DGP) IgG. Treat: lifelong strict gluten-free diet → villous restoration within 2 years. Complications: refractory coeliac, enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL), small bowel adenocarcinoma.

### Crohn's Disease (Small Bowel)

Transmural, granulomatous, chronic inflammatory bowel disease that can affect any GI segment but has predilection for terminal ileum (ileitis) and ileocolon (ileocolitis) [^guyton-hall]. Pathogenesis: genetic susceptibility (NOD2/CARD15, ATG16L1, IL23R) + dysbiosis + mucosal barrier defect → aberrant innate and adaptive immune activation → transmural inflammation.

**Features:** Skip lesions (normal mucosa between diseased segments), cobblestone mucosa, deep fissuring ulcers, non-caseating granulomata (path hallmark), strictures (from transmural fibrosis), fistulae (enterocutaneous, enteroenteric, enterovesical, perianal). Complications: SBO (strictures), malabsorption (B12, fat, bile acid depletion with terminal ileal disease), fistulae, abscesses. Treat: 5-ASA (mild), steroids (acute), azathioprine/6-MP (maintenance), anti-TNF (adalimumab, infliximab), anti-integrin (vedolizumab), anti-IL-12/23 (ustekinumab).

### Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO)

Overgrowth of colonic-type bacteria in the small intestine (>10⁵ CFU/mL on jejunal aspirate, or H₂ breath test). Causes: anatomical (strictures, fistulae, Roux-en-Y, small bowel diverticula), motility disorders (diabetes autonomic neuropathy, scleroderma — MMC loss), achlorhydria, immune deficiency. Consequences: bacterial deconjugation of bile acids → fat malabsorption, diarrhoea; bacterial consumption of B12 → megaloblastic anaemia; bacterial production of folate; gas production → bloating, distension. Treat: antibiotics (rifaximin first-line; metronidazole, ciprofloxacin), treat underlying cause.

### Carcinoid Tumour / Small Intestinal Neuroendocrine Tumour (NET)

Most common primary small intestinal malignancy. Arise from enterochromaffin cells (EC cells), predominantly in distal ileum. Often multifocal. Secrete serotonin (5-HT), substance P, bradykinin → carcinoid syndrome (flushing, diarrhoea, right-sided valvular heart disease [carcinoid plaques], bronchospasm) — occurs when hepatic metastases secrete directly into systemic circulation (bypassing hepatic inactivation). Diagnose: plasma chromogranin A, 24h urine 5-HIAA, somatostatin receptor scintigraphy (¹¹¹In-DTPA-octreotide) or ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET. Treat: surgical resection; somatostatin analogues (octreotide/lanreotide) for carcinoid syndrome; PRRT (peptide receptor radionuclide therapy) for metastatic disease.

### GI Stromal Tumour (GIST)

Mesenchymal tumour arising from ICC precursors. Most common in stomach (50–60%), small intestine (30–35%). KIT (c-Kit, CD117) gain-of-function mutation in 80% → constitutively active RTK signalling; PDGFRA mutation in 10%; rare: NF1, SDH, BRAF. Diagnose: CD117 + DOG1 IHC. Treat: imatinib (tyrosine kinase inhibitor, Gleevec) — revolutionary in recurrent/metastatic; surgical resection for localised disease.
