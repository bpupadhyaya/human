# Platform 03 — Recombinant Subunit Vaccines

### Protein-based vaccines with adjuvant

**[← Vaccine Atlas](../README.md)** · **[← Atlases index](../../README.md)**

</div>

---

Recombinant subunit vaccines deliver a **purified pathogen protein** (not a live or whole pathogen) combined with an **adjuvant** to boost the immune response. The antigen is produced in a heterologous expression system — mammalian cells (CHO), insect cells (Sf9/baculovirus), yeast, or plant cells — then purified, formulated with adjuvant, and injected.

**Key properties:**
- **No live pathogen or nucleic acid** — perceived as low-risk by vaccine-hesitant populations
- **Highly stable** at 2–8°C — standard cold-chain compatible
- Adjuvant choice profoundly shapes immune character (Th1 vs Th2, IgG subclass, CD8 induction)
- Long track record: Shingrix (RZV), Engerix-B (hepatitis B), Gardasil (HPV) are among the most successful vaccines of the modern era

**Key limitations:**
- Generally weaker T-cell (CD8⁺) responses than live/vectored platforms
- Response depends critically on adjuvant and protein conformation; subunit antigens without adjuvant are weakly immunogenic
- Manufacturing requires antigen production in expression system, purification, and quality testing — more complex than mRNA
