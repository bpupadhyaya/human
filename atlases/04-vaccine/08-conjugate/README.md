# Platform 08 — Conjugate Vaccines

Polysaccharide capsular antigens from encapsulated bacteria individually conjugated to a carrier protein (CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, or protein D) to convert T-cell-independent (TI-2) antigens into T-cell-dependent (TD) antigens, enabling germinal center formation, affinity maturation, memory B-cell generation, and effective immunisation of infants.

## Platform Characteristics

- **Antigen:** Purified bacterial capsular polysaccharide (CPS), serotype-specific
- **Carrier proteins:** CRM197 (most common; non-toxic G52E diphtheria toxin mutant); tetanus toxoid (TT); diphtheria toxoid (DT); protein D (*H. influenzae* outer membrane protein, used in Synflorix PCV10)
- **Adjuvant:** Aluminium salts (aluminium phosphate or aluminium hydroxide); some conjugate vaccines lack exogenous adjuvant (Hib-CRM197)
- **Route:** Intramuscular
- **Cold chain:** 2–8°C; do not freeze (freezing denatures protein-polysaccharide conjugate)
- **Mechanism:** CPS-carrier conjugate is processed by APCs; CPS binds BCR directly; carrier protein peptides are presented via MHC II to pre-primed CD4+ T helper cells (carrier-primed, e.g., by prior diphtheria/tetanus vaccination); T-cell help enables germinal center reaction, affinity maturation, class-switch to IgG, and memory B-cell formation
- **Key correlate:** Opsonophagocytic killing assay (OPKA) titre; anti-CPS IgG ≥0.35 µg/mL (approximate; serotype-dependent)

## Historical Context

The conjugate vaccine principle was established by **John Robbins** and **Rachel Schneerson** at NIH in the 1980s for *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib). Hib was the leading cause of bacterial meningitis in children under 5 before vaccine introduction. Hib conjugate vaccine (Hib-CRM197, PRP-T, PRP-OMP) nearly eliminated invasive Hib disease within 10 years of introduction. The same principle was then applied to *Neisseria meningitidis* (meningococcal conjugate vaccines) and *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (pneumococcal conjugate vaccines, PCV7→PCV13→PCV15/PCV20).
