> *"The vaccine, the antibody, the antibiotic — each is a designed bridge between the two atlases."*

The Medicine Atlas catalogs those bridges — every one humanity has ever built. Modern or traditional. Pharmaceutical, herbal, or food. Famous or forgotten.

---

## Why this atlas?

The [Human Atlas](../01-human/README.md) models the body. The [Pathogen Atlas](../02-pathogen/README.md) models the threats and microbes that interact with it. The **Medicine Atlas** is the *interface* — the entire vocabulary of substances and interventions humanity uses to push biology toward health.

That vocabulary is much larger than a typical pharmacology textbook admits. It must include:

- **Modern medicine** — the pharmaceuticals, biologics, vaccines, and molecular therapies of the last two centuries.
- **Traditional medicine** — systems like Ayurveda and Traditional Chinese Medicine, refined over thousands of years across cultures.
- **Food as medicine** — the spices, fermented foods, dietary patterns, and micronutrients we already consume — many with measurable therapeutic effects.

The Medicine Atlas treats them all as legitimate objects of study. **Modern science gets no automatic priority; tradition gets no automatic pass.** Every entry is held to the same standard: *what is it, what does it do, how does it work, what is the evidence, what does it interact with?*

## The three streams

### Stream 01 — Modern Medicine

The pharmacological and clinical knowledge of the last two centuries: small molecules, biologics, vaccines, and the increasingly precise tools of molecular medicine.

| Category | Examples | Scope |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pharmaceuticals** | Aspirin, statins, metformin, mRNA-LNP | Small-molecule drugs and lipid carriers |
| **Antibiotics** | Penicillin, vancomycin, carbapenems | Anti-bacterial therapies |
| **Antivirals** | Acyclovir, remdesivir, paxlovid | Anti-viral therapies |
| **Vaccines** | Polio, MMR, mRNA, virus-like particles | Trained adaptive immunity |
| **Biologics & Gene Therapy** | Monoclonal antibodies, CRISPR, cell therapy | Macromolecular and cellular therapies |
| **Cancer Therapeutics** | Chemotherapy, immunotherapy, CAR-T, targeted | Oncology |
| **Cardiovascular** | Beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, statins | Heart and vessels |
| **Mental Health** | SSRIs, SNRIs, antipsychotics, ketamine | Brain and behavior |
| **Hormones & Endocrine** | Insulin, thyroxine, sex steroids, GLP-1 | Endocrine signaling |

### Stream 02 — Traditional &amp; Cultural Medicine

Healing systems developed and refined over millennia in cultures around the world. Catalogued seriously, and cross-referenced with what modern evidence supports or refutes.

| Category | Origin | What's catalogued |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Ayurveda** | India, 3,000+ years | Herbs, formulations, diagnostic categories, lifestyle prescriptions |
| **Traditional Chinese Medicine** | China, 2,500+ years | Materia medica, acupuncture, formulations, theory |
| **Western Herbalism** | Europe / North America | Botanical medicine and clinical herbalism |
| **Indigenous Medicine** | Every continent | Native traditions, ethnobotany, sacred plants |

> **Notable traditions to add in subsequent revisions:** Tibetan Sowa Rigpa, Unani, Siddha, Kampo, traditional African medicine, Latin American curanderismo, and many more. *The atlas should reflect humanity's full medical inheritance.*

### Stream 03 — Food &amp; Nutrition Medicine

The medicines we already eat. Where dietary biology meets pharmacology — often more powerfully than either is given credit for.

| Category | Examples | Scope |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Food as Medicine** | Mediterranean diet, intermittent fasting, micronutrients | Dietary patterns and clinical nutrition |
| **Spices &amp; Adaptogens** | Turmeric, ginger, ashwagandha, garlic | Bioactive culinary compounds |
| **Probiotics &amp; Microbiome** | Fermented foods, FMT, postbiotics, prebiotics | Microbial therapies |

## The connection to the other atlases

Every entry in the Medicine Atlas is **cross-referenced** with the other two:

- **Into the Human Atlas:** which scale does this medicine act on? Receptor (molecular)? Cellular signaling? Whole-organ physiology? Systemic immune response?
- **Into the Pathogen Atlas:** which threats does it target, prevent, or interact with? Which microbes does it disturb (intended or unintended)?

These cross-references are what turn the atlas from a list into a **navigable knowledge graph** — useful as both a training corpus for AI agents working on the next therapy, and as a reference for human clinicians, researchers, and students worldwide.

## How an entry will look (planned schema)

Each entry in the Medicine Atlas will eventually carry a consistent, structured profile:

```
# <Name>

- Class / tradition
- Origin and history
- Mechanism of action — across the Human Atlas scales it touches
- Targets — which Pathogen Atlas entries, if applicable
- Clinical evidence — level, references, jurisdictions where approved
- Traditional indication — and the evidence base for it
- Interactions, contraindications, safety profile
- Cultural context
- Open questions for further research
- References (DOI / PubMed / textbook / oral tradition source)
```

This structure makes the atlas useful as both an **AI training corpus** and a **clinical / scholarly reference**.

## Help build the Atlas

The Medicine Atlas needs contributors from every part of the world and every medical tradition. There is no minimum contribution — a referenced sentence, a translated entry, a corrected dosage range, an under-represented tradition surfaced — all of it matters.

### Roles especially needed

- **Pharmacologists, clinical pharmacists, physicians** &mdash; for modern medicine entries
- **Ayurvedic, TCM, Tibetan, Unani, Siddha, indigenous, and folk practitioners** &mdash; for traditional medicine entries
- **Ethnobotanists and medical anthropologists** &mdash; for context, lineage, and historical accuracy
- **Food scientists, nutritionists, dietitians** &mdash; for the food-as-medicine stream
- **Translators** &mdash; to make the atlas truly global
- **AI / ML researchers** &mdash; to structure data so it is useful as a training corpus

### How to contribute

1. **Open a [Discussion](https://github.com/bpupadhyaya/human/discussions)** to propose new categories, entries, or structural changes.
2. **Open an [Issue](https://github.com/bpupadhyaya/human/issues)** for specific gaps you want filled.
3. **Submit a Pull Request** with new entries or revisions.
4. **Get in touch directly** &mdash; **[bpupadhyaya@gmail.com](mailto:bpupadhyaya@gmail.com?subject=Medicine%20Atlas%20%E2%80%94%20I'd%20like%20to%20contribute)** &mdash; especially if you represent a medical tradition currently under-represented.

The atlas will always be free. The medicines belong to humanity.
